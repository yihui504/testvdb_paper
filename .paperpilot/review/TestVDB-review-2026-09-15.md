## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper reports an experience study of an LLM-based pipeline (TestVDB) that mines documentation--implementation bugs in three production vector database systems. It has three parts. First, a mining campaign: 81 adjudicated submissions across Milvus, Qdrant and Weaviate, of which maintainers confirmed 51 as real bugs and fixed 23 through merged PRs, reported as a submission-and-adjudication ledger rather than a detection rate. Second, an audit of the material the pipeline's confirmation stage reads: every one of 134 (constraint, cited-page) pairs in the per-case evidence packages was checked against the page it cites, giving 18 supported as cited, 58 re-anchored from a source file or landing page, and 58 with no support on the cited page; the packages were rebuilt, and two leaks of maintainer-cognition material into the judge were stripped and the affected cases re-judged. Third, a census that classifies the deployed confirmation stage's 243 judgments (81 cases, three runs each) by the clause of the aggregation protocol that closes each one, measured over twelve judge configurations on two model backbones. The census reports that the refuting clause carrying no verbatim-evidence requirement closes 50 judgments and is wrong 24 times, all 24 on maintainer-confirmed bugs, while the clause guarded by a verbatim-intent requirement closes 19 and is right 17 times; it replays the protocol with the unguarded clause routed to human review rather than closing, reports the resulting change under the deployment's counting convention and under forced verdicts, and reports that the judge's four-perspective organization changes which cases are decided rather than how many.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The clause census is built to survive the paper's own instrument defects: the perspectives are identified by the vocabulary their recorded cells actually carry rather than by the letter printed in the dispatch, and the load-bearing count is checked for invariance across the two aggregation rules the dispatch prints, across both leak repairs, and against maintainer labels alone — see 2.1.
- **S2:** The paper prices its counting convention everywhere and demonstrates that its headline counterfactual is an effect of the convention rather than of better judging ("nothing under the forced reading") — see 2.2, 4.2.
- **S3:** The audit-and-rebuild discipline, together with the report of four defects the authors found in their own dispatches (none of them by their own audit), makes this a model of how to report on a pipeline whose confirmation stage is the object of study — see 3.1, 3.4.
- **S4:** A three-system mining campaign with maintainer-validated yield, reported with an unusually honest account of what it is not: the voided detection-ability experiment, the submission-filtered pool, and the one unjudgeable case — see 1.1, 1.2.
- **S5:** The printed statistics recompute exactly from the text, and the artifact ships the frozen per-run verdicts, the packages the study read, the dispatch texts and five analysis scripts — see 4.1, 4.2.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The 134-pair audit's rates are presented as a property of what the judge reads, but §4.3 describes pairs that were then re-anchored and packages that were then rebuilt, while §4.1 and Data Availability say the study read the post-rebuild generation — so the referent of the headline "43.3% unsupported" is ambiguous. Clarify which generation the split describes and report the post-rebuild residual — see 2.3, Q2.
- **W2:** The paper's two headline findings — 43.3% of cited pairs unsupported, and the unguarded contract clause carrying 80% of incorrect False-Positive closures — are reported in adjacent sections and never joined. Because the unguarded clause is a mechanical containment check on exactly the material the audit found defective, the census may be a materials result rather than a protocol result, and the paper's advice ("put the evidence guard where the error mass is") depends on which — see 2.4, Q1.
- **W3:** The census is established on the primary backbone only: on the second the pattern reverses, and the deployed dispatch's double definition of the perspectives (C and D exchanged) leaves the letters non-comparable there, so the paper's main insight does not generalize to the pipeline's own second deployment — see 2.6, 3.5.
- **W4:** Related Work omits four closely related works that sit in the project's own bibliography: CASCADE (FSE 2026), the source-of-authority SLR (2026), Sondhi et al. (ICST 2019) and MeTMaP (2024) — the first two especially leave the paper's delta unstated against the closest prior work — see 3.3, Q4.
- **W5:** The pair audit is a single-reader classification of a headline contribution number, with no second coder and a row-level accounting the paper reports as not reconciling — see 2.5, Q3.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Importance & Scope** — Excellent
   - **1.1** [strength] Section 2 establishes the target class carefully and usefully: it defines documentation--implementation inconsistency as what is accepted, rejected, returned and how state evolves, explicitly separates that from vector-search correctness (§2, "Correctness asks whether a returned result is right in the mathematical sense"), and grounds the separation in two dedicated prior studies of VDBMS defects plus the community roadmap. The canonical instance (Milvus #49823, `nprobe` documented as an integer in [1, 16384] but accepted as 0 with HTTP 200) makes the class concrete in one paragraph, and §2's "reverse shape" paragraph (all-or-nothing operations that apply partially) correctly pre-empts the objection that the class is trivial. The problem is important and the context is well established: VDBMSs sit under retrieval-augmented applications, their dominant defect class is silent wrong results, and the one dedicated fuzzer is crash-oracle by construction.
   - **1.2 [minor, fixable]** The pool's representativeness is bounded but not fully discussed. §6 (Threats) states the pool is "submission-filtered subset of one pipeline's output", and §4.2 gives per-system breakdowns (Milvus 43, Qdrant 28, Weaviate 10), yet the paper never says in which direction the submission filter could shape the recall contrasts — e.g., whether the 30 negatives are cases the pipeline itself was least confident about, which would make the judge's job easier than it looks. One sentence in §6 pointing the reader at the expected direction of that bias would close the gap.
   
2. **Insights & Evidence** — Adequate
   (The measurement work here is stronger than most experience papers: frozen packages, twelve configurations, paired exact tests, both readings reported. What holds the tier to Adequate is that two links between the paper's headline numbers and the objects those numbers describe are left unclosed — the referent of the audit's rate (2.3) and the relationship between the audit and the census (2.4) — so a reader cannot yet tell whether the paper's two central measurements describe the same material or the same mechanism.)
   - **2.1** [strength] The census is executed as an instrument designed to survive its own protocol's defects, which is exactly what an experience paper about a fallible pipeline should do. §4.5 identifies each perspective by the vocabulary its cells actually carry across all 243 judgments ("A is Neutral 172, Refuted 50, Confirmed 21; B is Neutral 146, Confirmed 80, Refuted 17; C is Confirmed 72, Neutral 71, Weak-Refuted 59, Refuted 41") rather than trusting the letter, and it then tests the load-bearing number four ways: invariance across the two aggregation rules the dispatch prints (which disagree on the insufficient-evidence default), invariance across both leak repairs (pre-repair 47 and 20 closures versus post-repair 50 and 19, with the 24 invariant), computation on maintainer-confirmed labels only, and survival of the re-judged cells. I checked the decomposition arithmetically: the seven clause rows sum to 243 judgments, the 153 true-bug judgments give the pool base rate of 63.0% that the catch-all discussion uses, and 24 of the 30 incorrect False-Positive closures is 80% as printed.
   - **2.2** [strength] The counterfactual is priced honestly at both readings and its limits are stated rather than glossed: §4.5 reports that routing contract refutation moves recall 39/51 to 46/51 and suppression 21/30 to 12/30 under the convention, that under forced verdicts "the same replay changes nothing", that fifteen cases are closed by the clause in at least two of three runs with seven of them true bugs (so seven is a case-level ceiling), and that "what cannot be computed from frozen data is whether a judge re-adjudicating those refutations under a verbatim-evidence guard would reach the same place". Refusing to claim the fix works, while pricing what the convention would pay for it, is the correct disposition of this evidence.
   - **2.3 [major, fixable]** The audit's rates and the material the judge read are not tied to the same generation. §4.3 presents 134 pairs with 58 "re-anchored" and 58 "no support on the cited page", then says "Every package was then rebuilt against version-pinned documentation and all rows re-anchored"; §4.1 and the Data Availability statement say the study read "the post-rebuild, cognition-stripped generation the dispatches name". The paper never states which state the 134-pair split describes. If it describes the as-found pairs (the natural reading of "were re-anchored"), then the headline "43.3% of what the confirmation stage reads is unsupported by its own citations" describes material that was subsequently repaired, and the residual rate on the packages the judges actually read — the number a reader would use to judge the census — is missing. This is measurement validity, not a wording nit: the claim and the metric must refer to the same object, and this paper's own audit discipline ("a specification read from the wrong documentation version tests the wrong system", §3.2) makes the mismatch conspicuous.
   - **2.4 [major, fixable]** The two headline results are never joined, and the mechanism behind the census is left undecided. The unguarded clause is defined in §3.5 as a mechanical containment check ("A --- contract. Does the quoted contract text actually ground the assertion? Run as a mechanical containment check"), and §4.3 shows that 43.3% of the cited pairs have no support on the page they cite. So the clause that the census blames for 24 of 30 incorrect False-Positive closures is a clause testing material the audit found defective; the two results are plausibly one phenomenon. §5 and §7 present them side by side — "Audit what your oracle reads" and "Put the evidence guard where the error mass is" — without ever cross-tabulating the A=Refuted closures against the pair-audit verdicts. If the wrong closures concentrate in cases with unsupported (or unfixably unsupported) contract rows, the correct advice is to fix the material, and adding a guard to the clause would leave the error mass in place. A single cross-tab, computable from the shipped pair-audit verdicts and the recorded cells, settles it.
   - **2.5 [minor, fixable]** The audit is a single-reader classification of one of the paper's three contributions, disclosed as such ("classified by a single reader without a second coder", §4.3; Threats). Disclosure is right, but the number is load-bearing for the Discussion's first takeaway, and the paper also reports that the row-level accounting of the rebuild "does not fully reconcile in our own report and we flag it as an open item" while the pair-level rates "do reconcile". A second coder on a sample of pairs, with an agreement figure and the residual post-rebuild rate, would let a reader calibrate 58/134.
   - **2.6 [minor, unfixable]** The census's generalization is bounded by its data, and the paper says so. On the second backbone the pattern reverses — contract refutation closes 56 judgments, by-design closes 50 and is wrong 22, and the unguarded clause supplies 24 of 48 incorrect closures (50% rather than 80%) — and the deployed dispatch's double perspective definition with C and D exchanged leaves the letters possibly denoting different perspectives there (the D cell carries the source vocabulary in 217 of 243 second-backbone judgments against 98 of 243 on the primary). The paper scopes the claim to the primary backbone and states what it cannot say about the second; the weakness is inherent to the data, not to the reporting, and I mark it unfixable on that basis.
   - **2.7 [minor, fixable]** Table 4's accuracy column is computed over 243 judgments that are not independent: 81 cases contribute three runs each, and a case's runs share the same package, the same source clone and the same backend. The paper gives case-level figures where they matter most (the counterfactual's fifteen cases and seven bugs), but the clause accuracies (0.92, 0.81, 0.52, 0.89) and the "50 of 243 judgments" denominator have no case-level or clustered counterpart. A case-level version of the same table — or one sentence stating that the judgment-level counts over-weight repeat-closed cases — would make the headline share robust to the obvious objection that a handful of cases fired the clause three times each.
   
3. **Perspective** — Adequate
   - **3.1** [strength] The report of the authors' own four dispatch defects (§4.1, §5) is the paper's most transferable contribution to practice: eight of twelve arms declare a binary `verdict` field while their text mandates three values; the deployed dispatch prints two aggregation rules with opposite defaults and defines the four perspectives twice with C and D exchanged; the output schema declares the source vocabulary for a perspective the text calls maintainer cognition, which 98 of 243 recorded cells use. The statement that "none of the four was found by our own audit; all were found by reviewers reading the shipped texts" is a lesson about self-audit's limits that other pipeline builders can act on immediately.
   - **3.2** [strength] The paper's characterizations of the competitors I checked hold, numbers included. VDBFuzz is described as crash-oracle by construction with its released-runner oracle firing on 5xx: VDBFuzz's own text labels the oracle "Crash as Oracle", says "crashes present the most direct and observable oracle", and self-reports that it "currently only performs fuzzing for crash-related bugs" — and its Qdrant template set is the 205 templates the paper ran. Metamon's "precision 0.722 at recall 0.480" is verbatim from that paper, and the asymmetry the paper draws (its falsifier is another LLM question) is accurate. Calling Ma et al. "the closest work" is fair: that study measures four bias types across multi-agent debate and meta-judging and finds debate amplifies biases after the initial round while meta-judging resists — bias, not accuracy, exactly as this paper says. MASTOR is correctly characterized as taking the implementation as its authority (its oracle agents read only source-verified fields, and it self-reports that it cannot detect violations not reflected in code), and LogicHunter as consulting and executing the implementation freely while treating retrieved documentation as the statement of intent. The deltas claimed in §7 are therefore deltas against work the authors have read correctly.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** Related-Work coverage has four gaps, all of them works the project's own bibliography already lists but the text never cites. (i) CASCADE ("Detecting Inconsistencies between Code and Documentation with Automatic Test Generation", FSE 2026) targets the same documentation--implementation inconsistency class with the same inversion — documentation supplies the expectation — and, like this paper, uses an independent artifact as the falsifier; its falsifier is a regenerated implementation, i.e. still model-derived, which is precisely the distinction the paper draws against Metamon (§7) but never draws against CASCADE. (ii) Mughal and Bilal's systematic literature review ("LLM-Based Test Oracles: Source-of-Authority Taxonomy", 2026) codes 83 studies by where an oracle's authority comes from — the same organizing axis as this paper's Table 1 — and is the natural instrument for the paper's "to our knowledge none reports" claims. (iii) Sondhi et al. ("Testing for Implicit Inconsistencies in Documentation and Implementation", ICST 2019) has the paper's exact target in its title years before LLM oracles; I verified the metadata, not the full text. (iv) MeTMaP (AIware/FoSE 2024) applies metamorphic testing to false vector matching in vector databases, and belongs in §7's "Testing vector databases" paragraph, which currently cites only VDBFuzz, the roadmap and the bug study, even though §2's Table 1 lists metamorphic relations as a family. The first two are what hold this criterion at Adequate: without them the paper's delta over the closest 2026 work is asserted rather than positioned.
   - **3.4** [strength] The Discussion's lessons are concrete, non-obvious and transferable beyond this pipeline: audit what your oracle reads rather than only what it concludes; locate the evidence guard where the error mass is; price both the deferral channel and the contrast rather than the level; check the dispatch before re-architecting the judge; and self-audit a stage against its own printed rules, which §5 demonstrates is "a five-line script" whose replay found 22 departures, 10 of them on real bugs. Each lesson is tied to a measurement in the paper, not asserted.
   - **3.5** [minor, unfixable] The reach of the headline lesson is bounded by the single-backbone census, and the paper's own mitigation is scoping rather than repair ("the rule is advice about where to look rather than a property of the protocol", §5). That is the honest construction, but a practitioner deciding where to put a guard learns only that this protocol on this backbone had the guard on the clean clause; the second backbone's reversal is what stops the lesson from being a general design rule, and no revision of the current data can change that.
   
4. **Verifiability** — Excellent

### Questions for Authors

- **Q1:** Can you cross-tabulate the A=Refuted closures against the pair-audit verdicts — i.e. of the 15 cases (and 24 judgments) the unguarded clause closes to False-Positive on true bugs, how many have contract rows that the audit classified as unsupported on their cited page, or as unsupported after the rebuild? If the answer is "most of them", item 2.4's rating would move up because the paper's headline finding becomes one mechanism (defective material mechanically failing a containment check) rather than two adjacent ones, and the prescription would need to change from guarding the clause to fixing the material.
- **Q2:** Which generation does Table 3's 134-pair split describe — the packages as found before the rebuild, or the post-rebuild packages the judges read? If it is the former, restating the claim as "43.3% as found, X% residual after re-anchoring" would move item 2.3 up, because the headline number would then refer to the material the census actually rests on.
- **Q3:** Could you report a second-coder agreement figure on a sample of the 134 pairs (or a pre-registered re-classification of the 58 "no support" pairs)? A concrete agreement number would move item 2.5 up, since single-reader classification is currently the only undisclosed-quality step in the paper's second contribution.
- **Q4:** Was the omission of CASCADE (FSE 2026) and the source-of-authority SLR (2026) from Related Work deliberate — for instance because a fuller comparison is deferred to another paper? Positioning the delta against them (system-level untagged prose versus method-level tagged prose; a real implementation falsifier versus a regenerated-implementation falsifier) would move item 3.3 up, since both currently make the paper's "what this paper adds" paragraph look unpositioned against the closest work.
- **Q5:** Is Table 4's accuracy column stable at the case level — recomputed over the 81 cases by each case's majority closing clause, does the unguarded clause still supply about 80% of the incorrect False-Positive closures? If yes, item 2.7's rating would move up, because the census would then be immune to the objection that a few cases closed three times each drive the share.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

This is an experience report on a documentation–implementation defect-mining pipeline for vector database management systems (VDBMSs), organized as three results. First, a mining campaign: across Milvus, Qdrant and Weaviate the ledger records 132 rows, of which 81 submissions were adjudicated — 51 maintainer-confirmed and 30 false positives adjudicated by the authors — with 23 of the confirmed bugs fixed by merged PRs; the paper states that this is a record of submission and adjudication rather than a detection rate, because the runs that would have measured per-version detection were voided for dispatch-discipline violations. Second, an audit of the evidence the confirmation stage reads: every (constraint, cited-page) pair a judge's package carries was re-checked against the page it cites, giving 18 of 134 supported as cited, 58 mis-anchored (a source file or a landing page; re-anchored to version-pinned documentation) and 58 unsupported on the cited page; two leakage channels found during the same audit (ten packages with an embedded maintainer-cognition section; cognition files referencing the candidates' own issue numbers) were stripped and the affected cases re-judged, and all reported rates are computed on the cleaned pool. Third, a clause-level census of the confirmation stage: the deployed judge's 243 judgments are classified by the first aggregation clause that closes them. Contract refutation, which the protocol requires no evidence for, closes 50 judgments and 24 of those closures fall on maintainer-confirmed bugs (accuracy 0.52), while the verbatim-guarded by-design clause closes 19 and is right 17 times; replaying a routing rule in place of contract refutation recovers seven true bugs for nine false-positive interceptions under the deployment's counting convention and nothing under the forced reading. A twelve-configuration study (two model backbones, three runs per case, frozen packages) supplies the judgments and prices both readings; the paper reports that the census does not reproduce on the second backbone, that the four-perspective organization changes which cases are decided rather than how many, and that most of the measured gain of its aggregation rule is deferral.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The clause-level error census is a new instrument, and its construction is unusually careful: the four perspectives are identified by the vocabulary their cells actually carry, the load-bearing count rests on maintainer labels, and the count is invariant across the two aggregation rules the dispatch prints. — see 2.1
- **S2:** The pair-level audit of documentation-derived oracle material, with the two leakage repairs it forced and the expectation-framing check, is the paper's most portable contribution to the documentation-oracle line, and the paper marks it as a measurement of this distiller rather than of LLM distillation in general. — see 2.4
- **S3:** The reporting discipline is a contribution in itself: two counting conventions priced at every contrast, a Holm analysis of the printed p-value family, negative results reported as findings (the judge's organization; the failed second-backbone replication), and the crash-oracle baseline reported with its own bound rather than as a defeat of the family. — see 2.7, 1.2
- **S4:** The lessons are concrete and cheap to act on for anyone building an LLM-judge oracle pipeline, and each is tied to a measurement in this paper rather than to general exhortation. — see 3.1, 3.3

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The paper's central claim — the error budget sits in the one refuting clause the protocol does not guard — is established on the primary backbone only; the second backbone reverses the pattern and the cross-backbone comparison is confounded by the authors' own dispatch defect, so the generality of the prescription remains unresolved. — see 2.2
- **W2:** The headline causal claims ride on bundled contrasts (the four-perspective contrast changes five things at once), the isolation steps are under-powered at the recall level, and the paper's own honest price of the deployed change is +3, which it calls "suggested, not established". — see 2.3
- **W3:** Related-work positioning omits neighbours that already sit in the paper's own bibliography: the systematic review that taxonomizes LLM test oracles by source of authority, and the two closest code–documentation inconsistency works. — see 3.2
- **W4:** The declared artifact's own README does not document the prompt directory the paper cites or the second backbone's three other configurations, and several load-bearing numbers are printed but not scripted. — see 4.2, 4.3

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Importance & Scope** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The residual the paper targets — silently accepted inputs and silently wrong behaviour that violate a VDBMS's own API documentation, invisible to crash oracles — is a real and practically important problem, and Section 2 anchors it accurately in the sources it names: I checked the citations against the works themselves (cached summaries). The bug study does attribute the dominant share to functional failures (57.3%, with crash at 15.1%), the roadmap does name oracle definition as the open problem for the residual, and VDBFuzz is indeed crash-oracle by construction with its released Qdrant template set at 205 templates. The framing is neither inflated nor stale.
   - **1.2** The scope discipline is exemplary for an experience paper. Section 4.2 states that the ledger is "not a per-run detection rate, and we cannot supply one" and explains what was voided and why; Section 4.5 states that the census is "narrower than the claim sounds" and confines it to the primary backbone; Section 4.1 names the one unjudgeable case and quantifies how the counting convention credits it; Section 6 collects the residual threats. Claims are matched to evidence throughout, including the negative ones.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The campaign's submission-selection policy is not described. Section 4.2 says the ledger "carries our own screening and submission decisions" and Section 6 calls the pool "submission-filtered subset of one pipeline's output", but the reader never learns how the 132 rows were selected from the pipeline's output, so "51 of 81 adjudicated submissions" floats without a surrounding denominator. One sentence pointing at the artifact's pool-assembly and ledger directories (and the screening rule) would close this.

2. **Insights & Evidence** — Adequate
   The method is fit for the question and the reporting is unusually candid; the tier is Adequate rather than Excellent because the evidence is partial in the specific places where the paper's own claims are strongest (2.2–2.3), with two further data-quality limits (2.5–2.6).
   - **2.1** The census instrument (Section 4.5, Table 4) is the part of the paper that will be reused, and it is built carefully. The authors classify all 243 judgments of the deployed stage by the first clause in the printed aggregation sequence that decides them, verify that each lettered perspective's cells carry its own vocabulary and no other (A: Neutral 172/Refuted 50/Confirmed 21; B: 146/80/17; C: Confirmed 72/Neutral 71/Weak-Refuted 59/Refuted 41), read the 19 C-refutation judgments back to their evidence (15 comment or docstring, 2 a named server validation rule, 2 code structure) and surface the two weakest rather than hiding them, and show the load-bearing count (24 of 50 contract refutations on confirmed bugs) is computed on maintainer labels and is invariant across the two aggregation rules the dispatch prints. Calling the guarded clause "the middle of the three clauses that assign False-Positive" and noticing it is the accurate one is a genuinely non-obvious finding.
   - **2.2 [major, fixable]** The census's generality is unresolved, and it bounds the paper's significance. Section 4.5 reports that on the second backbone the pattern reverses: contract refutation closes 56 judgments, by-design closes 50 of which 22 are wrong, and the unguarded clause supplies 24 of 48 incorrect False-Positive closures — 50% rather than 80%. The paper then states that the deployed dispatch defines the four perspectives twice with C and D exchanged and that the D-cell vocabulary split differs sharply between backbones (217/243 vs 98/243), so the letters may not denote the same perspective and "the two censuses may not be comparable". The counterfactual is a replay of recorded values, not a re-adjudication, and the paper says it cannot compute whether a judge re-adjudicating those refutations under a verbatim-evidence guard would land in the same place. As written, the claim is correctly scoped and survives; but resolving whether the error mass is backbone-specific or an artifact of the dispatch defect needs one re-dispatched arm per backbone with the corrected text (or with the guard actually applied), and without it the "put the guard where the error mass is" prescription is bounded to a deployment the reader cannot recognise a priori.
   - **2.3 [major, fixable]** The paper's causal claims are carried by bundled contrasts and modest honest effects. Section 4.4 states that the four-perspective contrast changes five things at once (the perspectives, the aggregation-rule text, the red-line set, the declared verdict field, and cognition access) and gives the resulting null with an interval that excludes only effects larger than about eleven points, explicitly "do not establish equivalence". The deployed change decomposes only on the primary backbone, where the schema-repair step (recall 30→35; forced 25→29) and the rule step (35→39 at the recall level, not significant; 39→51 on the confirmed set, significant) are individually under-powered at the recall level. Under the paper's own hand-adjudicated pricing the whole change is +3 (32 vs 29) and the deployed stage's own price is +4 — the paper calls this "suggested, not established", which is the right reading. Fix: run the two missing isolation cells — a perspectives-only arm whose aggregation text, red lines and declared field are held identical to the flat+aggregation control, and a schema-repaired control on the second backbone — so each factor is priced one at a time on both backbones.
   - **2.4** The evidence-package audit (Section 4.3) is a real measurement of a step that the documentation-oracle line performs without reporting. The three-way split (18 supported / 58 mis-anchored / 58 unsupported of 134 pairs) is complemented by a case-level treatment of the 12 main assertions (5 supported, 3 dropped, 2 rewritten, 2 recorded as weak evidence; all acted on), by the two dominant mechanisms (version drift, conceptual-only documentation), and by the leak repairs with before/after numbers stated (Section 4.1: without the cognition repair the deployed stage would read 41/51 with a confirmed set of 41+6 rather than 39/51 and 39+9, and the affected true/false-positive sets are reported for both controls). The expectation-framing check — zero of 81 packages contain a sentence framed as an expectation, with the 19 hits inspected and all verbatim server responses — is exactly the right leak test for this design, and the paper is explicit that the rates are this distiller on these three vendors' documentation, not a property of LLM distillation in general.
   - **2.5 [minor, fixable]** Two data-quality limits sit under the audit and are only partly mitigated. The pair classification is "a single reader without a second coder" (Section 6), and the row-level accounting of the rebuild "does not fully reconcile in our own report"; the paper prints the pair-level rates because they do reconcile, but the reader cannot see how large the unreconciled row-level discrepancy is. A second coder on a sample of the 134 pairs, plus either a reconciled row-level account or dropping that accounting from the artifact note, would harden the paper's second headline.
   - **2.6 [minor, fixable]** The suppression and interception readings that price the arms and the counterfactual inherit the 30 negatives "adjudicated by us against the maintainers' disposition where available" (Section 4.1), with no agreement measure reported for those labels; the independent adjudicator pass that is reported (agreement on 4, 5 and 6 of 20 commonly ruled cases; κ = −0.01, 0.08, 0.11) covers the routed-queue rulings, not the negatives. Since "seven true bugs for nine false-positive interceptions" moves with the negatives, report an agreement statistic or a second coder for that set.
   - **2.7** The statistical and reporting discipline is strong and should be said. Both McNemar levels are reported for every contrast and the paper notices where they disagree ("the rule step is significant at the confirmed-set level and not at the recall level, and we privilege neither"); the unadjusted p-value family is analysed with a Holm correction and the reader is told which four of ten recall-level tests survive and that everything larger should be read descriptively; the routing-rate/convention identity is stated rather than dressed up; the VDBFuzz baseline run of 205 templates and 0 anomalies is bounded to "the released configuration's reach", with the same logs' triple-count artefact (152 lines reporting three anomalies from a dictionary-valued return) disclosed rather than left to be found. The yield is maintainer-adjudicated, and the claims that fall outside the shipped artifact (the 23-fix characterisation; the hand-priced joint readings) are marked as such.

3. **Perspective** — Excellent
   - **3.1** The lessons are concrete, transferable and each backed by a measurement here: audit what the oracle reads (Section 4.3's 43.3% and the 13%-supported figure); put the evidence guard where the error mass is (Section 4.5's clause table); price the deferral channel and the contrast, not just the level (Section 4.4's +9 by convention vs +3 hand-priced); check the dispatch before re-architecting the judge (Section 5's four defects, none found by the authors' own audit, one of which was worth five of the headline nine bugs); self-audit the stage against its own printed rule (the 22 forward departures, 17 in the forbidden direction, 10 on real bugs, "a five-line script"). These generalise past this pipeline even where the magnitudes do not, and each carries its bound.
   - **3.2 [minor, fixable]** Within my specialty, the paper's characterisations of the competitors it names are accurate — I verified METAMON's published profile (precision 0.722 at recall 0.480, and its falsifier is indeed another LLM question), MASTOR's source-as-authority design (its own limitations section states it cannot detect violations of intent not reflected in code), SATORI's per-response-field OpenAPI anchoring, AGORA+'s trace anchoring, and TRACE's documentation-plausible/implementation-drift asymmetry. The positioning gap is elsewhere: the paper's own bibliography already holds its closest neighbours, and they are never cited in the text. The systematic literature review that taxonomises LLM test oracles precisely by source of authority (mughal26; 83 studies) concludes that hallucination is "named far more often than measured" — which supports the paper's novelty claim rather than threatening it, and citing it would let the audit inherit a field-level trend instead of standing alone. Likewise, the sentence on LLM-era comment–code successors cites DocChecker and C4RLLaMA but not CASCADE (cascade26), which detects code–documentation inconsistencies with automatic test generation and whose dual-check FP suppression and shared-documentation-ambiguity residuals parallel this paper's design, or Doc2OracLL (doc2oracll25) on documentation's impact on LLM oracle generation. All three are already in TestVDB.bib; positioning them costs one sentence each.
   - **3.3** The paper's strongest lesson is also its most uncomfortable finding, and it is framed as a lesson rather than buried: on this pool, what an LLM judge confirms is decided less by how it is organized than by how it accounts for the cases it refuses to decide (forced-verdict recall moves only 25→27 and 23→26, while the convention-level numbers move by 9 and 14). Practitioners can check this in their own pipelines with the paper's own arithmetic, which is what an experience paper should leave behind.

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate
   The link is declared and reachable and the package is rich, but two paper-vs-package mismatches (4.2) leave parts of the second-backbone evidence unconfirmed, and several load-bearing numbers remain unscripted (4.3).
   - **4.1** The artifact is declared and reachable: I resolved the anonymous URL and its README loads (8.1 KB), documenting the pool, the frozen packages, the per-arm dispatches and verdicts, the analysis scripts, the voided runs, and the RQ3 baseline logs and analysis sources. The paper names its five analysis scripts with paths and states which numbers they recompute; the raw generations ship beside the cleaned ones so either pool can be reconstructed; the two judging prompts are said to ship as English renderings under rq2/prompts/.
   - **4.2 [major, fixable]** Two mismatches between the paper's Data Availability and the artifact's own README need resolving. (i) The README's rq2 layout lists materials/, dispatches/, verdicts/, pool/ and analyses/ — no rq2/prompts/, which the paper (Section 4.1) cites as the location of the two English judging prompts. (ii) The README's arm table enumerates the shipped arms and includes only one second-backbone arm (source-only, run_donlyq*), while the paper reports four second-backbone configurations (flat, flat+aggregation, full, source-only) and states the package holds "the frozen per-run verdicts of all twelve configurations". Either the README is stale or the snapshot lags the paper; since the second-backbone replication (27→41, p=0.0001) and the perspectives penalty (−11 true bugs) are load-bearing contrasts, confirm and refresh the package so a reader can check them.
   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** The paper discloses that the joint prices and the per-arm routed-queue counts behind them, the catch-all composition, the expectation-framing check, the C row's evidence classification and the per-perspective cell vocabularies "are printed but not yet scripted". The raw verdicts do ship, so these are checkable in principle, but the +3 hand-price is one of the paper's most consequential numbers and deserves a script before the rest. The row-level accounting of the rebuild is flagged as unreconciled; the earlier rebuilt generations are archived separately and do not ship, so the audit's re-anchoring decisions cannot be re-inspected from the package (the pair-audit verdicts do ship, which partly compensates).
   - **4.4 [minor, fixable]** The artifact README's own title ("Detecting Logic Bugs in Vector Database Management Systems via LLM-Derived Behavioral Specifications") is an older title of this paper. A reader arriving from the paper may briefly wonder whether the link points at a different submission; updating the README title to match the submitted one removes the doubt.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   The structure is sound and the tables carry their weight; the tier is held down by terminology drift across arms and by a handful of passages whose antecedents need re-reading (all fixable).
   - **5.1** The paper is information-dense but well organized: the three contributions are stated up front and each gets its own measured section, the oracle-family table, configuration table and census table are load-bearing rather than decorative, and every cross-reference I followed resolved to a real quantity. The prose is careful about scope to the point that the honesty itself is a reading aid.
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** Naming drift across the arms costs the reader. The same configuration is "the deployed stage", "the full stage" and "the deployed configuration"; "rule-bearing" is used both for the flat+aggregation control and, in "the bundled contrast ... flat judge to rule-bearing judge", in a way that invites confusion with the full stage, which also carries the rule; and "the second backbone" is left unnamed in the prose entirely — the backbone identifiers GLM-5.3-Flash and Qwen3.8-Flash appear only in Table 2 — so the reader must keep the mapping in mind across Sections 4.4–4.5. A one-paragraph arms glossary with fixed labels would remove most of the re-reading this paper otherwise imposes.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** A few sentences need their antecedents fixed: "The ledger records 132 rows ... It carries our own screening and submission decisions" (what does "it" refer to — the ledger's content? the campaign?); "The re-judged cells were merged into those runs and the pool reduced as before" (reduced how?); and "the no-aggregation arm confirms 33 with 3 leaked false positives (suppression 0.900, precision 0.917)" only parses if "confirms 33" is read as "confirms 33 true bugs" (33/36 = 0.917), which the sentence does not say.

## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

*Tier basis (how the recommendation follows): no criterion below Adequate, no substance criterion Weak, and two substance criteria at Excellent (Importance & Scope, Perspective) → Accept. I am the reviewer without a literature survey; the two Excellents rest on the paper's own content, and the field-context element of Importance & Scope is marked provisional under 1.3.*

### Summary

The paper targets documentation--implementation bugs in vector database management systems: cases where the system silently accepts an input, or returns a shape or state, that its API documentation forbids. It reports three objects. A mining campaign over Milvus, Qdrant and Weaviate whose ledger holds 132 rows, in which maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs and 23 were fixed by merged PRs. An audit of the (constraint, cited-page) pairs that the pipeline's confirmation stage reads, finding 18 of 134 supported as cited, 58 mis-anchored to a source file or landing page, and 58 with no support on the cited page, which forced a rebuild and two leak repairs. And a clause census of the confirmation stage: 243 judgments, 81 cases times three runs, classified by the aggregation clause that closes each one.

The twelve judge configurations that supply the census all re-adjudicate the same frozen pool of 81 cases, three runs each, with each control described as a single traceable edit of the arm it extends. The paper's main results are that the error budget sits in the one refuting clause carrying no evidence requirement (contract refutation closes 50 judgments, 24 of them real bugs, accuracy 0.52) while the verbatim-guarded by-design clause closes 19 and is right 17 times; that this pattern does not reproduce on the second backbone (50% rather than 80%); that routing the unguarded clause to human review would recover seven true bugs for nine intercepted false positives under the deployment's counting convention and nothing under the forced reading; and that the four-perspective organisation changes which cases are decided but not how many (forced recall 27 vs. 27 and 26 vs. 26, with the confirmed sets overlapping on 22 and 23 cases). The paper also reports four defects in its own dispatches, found by reviewers reading the shipped prompts.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The census is built to be checkable from the paper alone, and it checks out: letters identified by the vocabulary of their own cells, cross-tabs that close on 243, a load-bearing count computed only on maintainer labels. — see 2.2, 4.2
- **S2:** The paper prices its own instrument everywhere the price applies: the counting convention at two places, the second leak repair in both directions, the Holm correction against the four contrasts that survive it, and four dispatch defects reported rather than repaired. — see 2.3, 3.3, 3.5
- **S3:** The pair audit is a cheap, transferable practice with a measured number attached (58 of 134 unsupported, a further 58 mis-anchored), applied to a step the paper argues no pipeline reports. — see 2.7, 3.1
- **S4:** The twelve-configuration instrument over frozen packages gives real comparative leverage, and the "organisation changes which cases, not how many" result is non-obvious and well supported. — see 2.1, 2.3, 3.4
- **S5:** Scope discipline is applied where the affected number appears, not only in a threats section: the voided detection runs, the unjudgeable case, the single-reader audit, the bundled contrasts and the second-backbone non-reproduction are all disclosed in situ. — see 1.2, 2.4, 2.9

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The clause census --- the paper's central claim by its own description --- is one 243-judgment measurement on one model backbone; the second backbone reverses the pattern and is confounded by the paper's own C/D dispatch duplication, so the regularity rests on a single observation. — see 2.4
- **W2:** The prescription is priced as a replay of recorded cells rather than as a re-adjudication, and is a no-op under the forced reading; the paper cannot show that a guarded judge would reach the same place. — see 2.5
- **W3:** The headline contrast bundles the verdict field, the aggregation rule and routing; the individual steps are non-significant at the recall level while the bundle is significant, and the hand-priced value the paper calls honest is "suggested, not established". — see 2.6, 2.8
- **W4:** The audit's headline rests on a single reader with no second coder, and the row-level accounting of the rebuild does not reconcile in the paper's own report. — see 2.7
- **W5:** The results are not tabulated: twelve configurations' outcomes live in roughly sixty in-text numbers, under labels that do not match Table 2, which carries no numeric column. — see 5.2

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Importance & Scope** — Excellent
   - **1.1** Sections 1--2 build the case concretely rather than by assertion. The paper argues that VDBMS failures mostly do not crash (citing the systematic bug study and the community roadmap), that the one dedicated VDBMS fuzzer fires on 5xx or a failed request and so cannot reach them by construction, and it carries two running instances: Milvus issue \#49823, where \texttt{nprobe} is documented as an integer in $[1,16384]$ yet \texttt{nprobe=0} returns HTTP 200 and answers the query, and Qdrant issue \#10369, where the \texttt{lookup\_from} vector-size constraint is bypassed and becomes observable only after a delete-and-recreate transition. Table~\ref{tab:oracles} then walks the candidate oracle families and states, for each, the structural reason it anchors somewhere other than untagged system-level behavioural prose. The residual is shown to be real, and shown not to be covered by construction.
   - **1.2** The scope is stated where it matters rather than deferred to a limitations section. The abstract itself calls the ledger "a record of submissions and adjudication, \emph{not} a per-run detection rate" and says the detection-ability runs were voided; Section~\ref{sec:method} states the pool's construction, the unjudgeable case and the counting convention; Section~\ref{sec:census} opens by bounding its own census to the primary backbone; Section~\ref{sec:audit} states that its rates are this distiller on these three vendors. A reader always knows which object a number describes and what it is not.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The campaign reports confirmation counts and a disposition breakdown but no severity grading, and it does not interpret the disposition pattern. Section~\ref{sec:yield} gives 51 confirmed as 23 fixed, 18 acknowledged-open, 7 closed without a fix and 3 duplicates, and grades the bugs only by noting that one is a crash-or-panic defect and the other 50 are silent accepts, wrong results, or poor diagnostics; so roughly half the confirmed bugs were left unfixed, and the paper does not discuss whether maintainers' decisions to leave them open bear on how much the class matters, nor how many are low-consequence input-validation gaps. Section~\ref{sec:yield} also says "a substantial share of the 81 adjudicated submissions sits on versions that experiment covered" without quantifying it. My rating on this criterion is provisional in the sense my role requires: it rests on the paper's own positioning of the residual against the oracle families, which I did not survey.

2. **Insights & Evidence** — Adequate
   - **2.1** The instrument is well matched to the question. Section~\ref{subsec:overview} names the two design features that make the pipeline measurable --- each stage writes a structured artifact, and per-case evidence is frozen so every configuration concludes from the same material --- and Section~\ref{sec:method} makes each control "a traceable edit of the arm it extends" (the schema-fix arm changes one output-schema line; the source-withheld arm drops the \texttt{source=} path from every per-case material line). Table~\ref{tab:configs} shows the twelve configurations differing only in what the judge is given and which rules it applies. For a paper whose claim is about judge organisation, holding the materials and the discipline clauses fixed is exactly the right control, and it is the control that the finding the paper cites from Bodicoat et al.\ (Section~\ref{sec:related}: supplied context and prompting technique dominate model choice in oracle accuracy) demands.
   - **2.2** The census is self-checking, and its arithmetic closes. Section~\ref{sec:census} identifies each lettered perspective by the vocabulary its own cells carry and no other (A: Neutral 172, Refuted 50, Confirmed 21; B: 146, 80, 17; C: Confirmed 72, Neutral 71, Weak-Refuted 59, Refuted 41), each summing to 243; Table~\ref{tab:census}'s rows (61+21+7+50+19+16) leave exactly the 69 catch-all judgments, and the catch-all's split into 37 source-vocabulary and 32 cognition-vocabulary cells reproduces the table's 45 true bugs (28+17). The load-bearing count of 24 is computed only on maintainer-confirmed labels, and the paper shows it is invariant across the two aggregation rules its dispatch prints. I verified these sums; they hold. For a count whose object is a text-closing clause, this is the right way to make it checkable without opening the artifact.
   - **2.3** The handling of things that went wrong is evidence practice rather than a weakness. Section~\ref{sec:method} prices the second leak repair numerically and in both directions ("without it the deployed configuration would read 41/51 with a confirmed set of 41+6 rather than the 39/51 and 39+9 we report"; the controls' suppression falls 0.467$\to$0.400 and 0.933$\to$0.900 and "none moves away"); the counting convention is priced at two places and the forced reading reported beside every contrast; the Holm paragraph names the four contrasts that survive a family correction and tells the reader that the deployed stage's own recall-level advantage over the flat judge should be read descriptively. The "organisation changes which cases, not how many" result (Section~\ref{sec:buys}: forced 27 vs.\ 27 and 26 vs.\ 26, intersecting on 22/27 and 23/26) is a genuine, non-obvious finding stated in the currency of decisions rather than counts.
   - **2.4 [major, unfixable]** Section~\ref{sec:census} --- which Section~\ref{sec:buys} itself calls "the paper's claim" --- is measured on one backbone: 243 judgments, one model family, one pool. The paper reports that the same census does not reproduce on the second backbone (contract refutation closes 56, by-design closes 50 of which 22 are wrong, and the unguarded clause supplies 24 of 48 incorrect false-positive closures, 50\% rather than 80\%), and that the second census is itself uninterpretable as a comparison because the deployed dispatch defines the four perspectives twice with C and D exchanged and the D cells' vocabulary differs sharply between backbones (217 of 243 against 98 of 243). The central regularity therefore rests on a single measurement whose only attempted replication is both negative and confounded. This is major because it bounds the reach of the headline finding, not because the paper overstates it: every statement of the finding carries the scope, and the second-backbone negative is reported in the abstract, the introduction, the census, the discussion, the threats and the conclusion. The hedges are why this criterion is Adequate rather than Weak; what they cannot do is make a one-backbone regularity a property of the protocol.
   - **2.5 [major, fixable]** The prescription is a replay, not an experiment. Section~\ref{sec:census} is explicit that "what cannot be computed from frozen data is whether a judge re-adjudicating those refutations under a verbatim-evidence guard would reach the same place", and that the gain "is a property of the convention rather than of the stage's decisions" --- under forced verdicts the replay changes nothing (27/51, 27/30, identical confirmed sets). So the paper locates where errors land and prices what the counting rules would pay if those closures became abstentions; it does not show that a guard would produce better decisions. This is fixable inside a revision: the paper knows the fifteen cases the clause closes in at least two of three runs, that seven of them are the case-level maximum, and that all seven are currently unconfirmed.
   - **2.6 [major, fixable]** The headline contrast is bundled. Section~\ref{sec:method} concedes that three contrasts change more than one thing and that the four-perspective contrast changes five; the decomposition the paper does give (flat$\to$flat+schema 30$\to$35, $p{=}0.0625$; $+$routing 35$\to$39, $p{=}0.2891$; bundle 30$\to$39, $p{=}0.0039$) has the shape of a significant bundle built from non-significant steps at the recall level, and the paper reports that the two levels disagree for the rule step and privileges neither. That bundle is the paper's headline story --- deferral, not organisation, moves the outcome --- so the gap between the instrument's design and the claim it carries is not incidental. It is a power problem, and the paper's own interval ($\pm0.11$ at $n{=}81$) says what would fix it.
   - **2.7 [minor, fixable]** The pair audit is classified by a single reader without a second coder, and Section~\ref{sec:threats} records that the row-level accounting of the rebuild "does not fully reconcile in our own report" as an open item. The audited rates --- 58 of 134 unsupported and a further 58 mis-anchored --- appear in the abstract, the contribution list, the discussion and the conclusion. The paper marks them as this distiller on these three vendors' documentation and does not generalise them, and the claim is directionally robust to moderate misclassification, which is why I rate the item minor; but the paper's second most quoted number carries no reliability measure, and a second reader on a sample is a cheap addition.
   - **2.8 [minor, fixable]** The reading the paper prefers has no independent support. Section~\ref{sec:method} reports that an independent adjudicator on the same materials agrees with the author-executed pass on 4, 5 and 6 of the 20 commonly ruled cases ($\kappa = -0.01$, $0.08$, $0.11$), and that two of the three independent passes land at the forced floor. The paper handles this correctly by reporting the joint reading "as a bound rather than a measurement" and refusing to claim establishment, so this bounds rather than undermines; the residual point is that the hand-priced reading is nevertheless the source of Section~\ref{sec:buys}'s $+3$/$+4$ and of the discussion's "worth nine, falls to three", and it rests on one non-blind pass whose agreement with independent work is at chance.
   - **2.9 [minor, fixable]** Pool construction limits what the recall levels can mean, and most of it is disclosed: the 30 negatives are "adjudicated by us against the maintainers' disposition where available"; there is no inter-annotator study; one case (\texttt{milvus\_001}) is unjudgeable and, under the convention, credited to eleven of the twelve configurations; and Section~\ref{sec:yield}'s ledger carries no denominator for how many candidates the pipeline produced, so no screening rate can be read from it. Because the load-bearing census count uses only maintainer labels, the paper survives the two most serious of these. But the 51 include 7 cases "closed without a fix" and 3 duplicates, and the paper does not say whether those closures were "will not fix" (a confirmation of the behaviour) or "not a bug" (a refutation); if some are the latter, the recall denominator shifts.
   - **2.10 [minor, fixable]** Section~\ref{sec:baseline} prices the crash-oracle baseline as 0 oracle anomalies from the released 205-template configuration (1,127 mutation stages, 11,629 response-status lines, no 5xx), then immediately bounds it ("a bound on \textbf{the released configuration's} reach into the silent majority rather than on the crash-oracle family"). That is an honest floor rather than a contest, and the paper says so; the weaker part is the follow-up --- a hand-guided probe built on the pipeline's boundary reasoning that "submits a value that vocabulary cannot reach" whose "output was not retained, so no execution result is claimed". A one-sentence non-result of this kind is worse than either finishing the measurement or dropping it, because it tells the reader the demonstration exists without showing it.

3. **Perspective** — Excellent
   - **3.1** Section~\ref{sec:discussion}'s first lesson --- audit what your oracle reads, not only what it concludes --- is the paper's most transferable contribution. It is a practice (compare a sample of constraint--page pairs against the pages they cite), it is priced (Section~\ref{sec:audit}'s three-way split), it names the two mechanisms it found (version drift from an augmentation script drawing every vendor's constraints from one fixed version; "conceptual-only" constraints such as \texttt{nprobe} $\in [1, \texttt{nlist}]$ that exist as prose but not as documented values), and it is checked against the failure the practice invites (zero of 81 rebuilt packages contain a sentence framed as an expectation, with all 19 phrasing hits inspected and accounted for as verbatim server responses).
   - **3.2** The second lesson --- put the evidence guard where the error mass is --- is a design rule argued from the census and stated with its own bound ("advice about where to look rather than a property of the protocol"). The underlying observation that only explicit intent evidence may refute, while the clause that fires most carries no requirement at all, is an asymmetry a practitioner can check in their own protocol without reproducing this pool.
   - **3.3** The third lesson --- check the dispatch before re-architecting the judge --- is demonstrated rather than asserted: four defects in the shipped prompts, none found by the authors' own audit, one of which (the schema line) "moved five of the headline nine bugs". Its generalisation is real for anyone evaluating an LLM judge, whose instrument text is part of the treatment.
   - **3.4** The pricing lessons give a transferable methodological point: with a three-valued judge, any metric that credits abstention makes the rule's effect and the routing rate the same quantity, so a contrast must be priced at the contrast rather than at the level, and the counting convention must itself be priced. Applied here it reorders the arms across readings (forced recall inside $[23,31]$ of 51 against a convention span of $[27,48]$), which is a caution that travels to any LLM-judge evaluation with a "needs review" verdict.
   - **3.5** The final lesson --- replaying a printed rule over the stage's own recorded values, "a five-line script", which found 22 forward departures from the appended rule, 17 in the direction the red lines forbid, 10 of them on real bugs --- is paired with the paper's stance that an audit which only reports what it finds in the target is not an audit. Reporting its own instrument's defects, all four of which were surfaced by reviewers reading the shipped texts rather than by the authors' own audit, is an unusual and useful posture for an experience paper, and it is the part of this paper I would most expect other authors of LLM-pipeline evaluations to reuse. (Provisional: I did not survey the field, so I make no claim about the priority of these lessons against related work; what I can judge is that they are tied to measurements made here and are stated at the reach those measurements support.)

4. **Verifiability** — Excellent

All three recommendations land on Accept, so the unanimous shortcut decides outright and the consensus-tier count is not reached; it would return the same verdict, there being no consensus Poor and no consensus Weak.
