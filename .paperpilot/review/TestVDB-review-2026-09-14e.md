## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets what it calls documentation–implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): inputs silently accepted, or behaviors silently produced, that contradict constraints stated only in natural-language API documentation — the paper's canonical example is a parameter documented as an integer in [1, 16384] being accepted with the value 0 and HTTP 200. It argues, through a table that walks each standard oracle candidate by where its expectation anchors, that no deterministic-oracle family derives an expectation from system-level, untagged behavioral prose, which leaves an LLM as the practical oracle for the residual. The proposed system, TestVDB, is a four-stage LLM-agent pipeline: behavioral documentation is distilled into structured, source-verified constraints; attack agents bound to a pre-registered strategy registry turn constraints into executable probes; probes run against a Docker-pinned version with raw HTTP logging; and a confirmation stage separates evidence assembly from cross-examination, with a five-section evidence chain checked mechanically and read from four perspectives (contract, objective physical constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition), the implementation source acting as the falsification anchor.

The paper reports two bodies of evidence. First, an operational ledger accumulated across Milvus, Qdrant and Weaviate: of 81 adjudicated submissions, maintainers confirmed 51 as real bugs and fixed 23 via merged PRs. Second, a re-adjudication study over those 81 candidates on frozen evidence packages that were audited, rebuilt against version-pinned documentation, and run three times per configuration: a contract-only core (0.157 recall at 0.967 suppression), the full stage (0.765 recall at 0.700 suppression under the deployment's counting convention, 0.647 recall at 0.917 precision when routed cases are hand-adjudicated, 0.529 under forced verdicts only), a source-only arm including a second backbone, and a flat single-prompt judge (0.588/0.867). A bidirectional probe compares the crash-oracle fuzzer VDBFuzz's reach with TestVDB's on Qdrant.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Real, independently adjudicated field yield: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs and 23 merged fix PRs across three production VDBMSs, with the submission ledger broken out per system and the acceptance and fix-nature figures reported in aggregate — 50 of 51 carry an official bug label, all 23 fixes modify implementation code and none is documentation-only — plus the two per-system acceptance cells the paper does give (Milvus 25 of 29, Qdrant 5 of 14); all re-verified as of September 2026 — see 1.1, 1.2.
- **S2:** An unusual audit-and-rebuild discipline over the paper's own materials: the shipped evidence packs were audited (43.3% of cited (constraint, page) pairs unsupported), rebuilt, re-adjudicated, and two further leakage channels (pack provenance notes; candidate-anchored maintainer-cognition entries) were removed with the affected arms and runs re-executed — see 3.2.
- **S3:** The measurement apparatus is reported completely enough to be re-derived: I reproduced the Wilson intervals, the exact McNemar p-values from the reported discordant pairs, and the table arithmetic; and the RQ3 baseline's zero-crash result is interrogated rather than trusted (the released runner's counters are shown to be non-diagnostic) — see 3.1, 3.3.
- **S4:** The oracle-exclusion analysis and its positioning are verifiable and accurate: against the named competitors I checked, the paper's characterizations hold, and the claimed delta (the anchor inversion against MASTOR) is real — see 2.1, 2.2.
- **S5:** A declared, reachable replication package and a self-contained protocol appendix (both judge prompts, the aggregation rule, the dispatch asymmetry) — see 4.1, 4.2.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** RQ1's headline ledger is not attributable to the system as described and measured: it is cumulative across earlier mining rounds and pipeline generations, the reported configuration contributed none of the 81, author-side screening and the submission decision sat in the loop, and no end-to-end detection recall is ever measured for the pipeline — see 3.4.
- **W2:** The "structure pays" result is single-backbone and carried by the routing channel: forced-verdict accuracy is statistically indistinguishable from the flat judge (p=0.34), the recall-only comparison fails Holm correction (0.090), the primary-family arms are effectively tied on Youden's J (0.465 vs. 0.455), and the second-family re-run does not separate (p=0.58) — see 3.7, 3.8.
- **W3:** The deployment-faithful joint reading (0.647 recall / 0.917 precision) rests on the authors adjudicating the routed cases non-blind, and nothing bounds the resulting optimism — see 3.5.
- **W4:** The evaluation pool's ground truth is misaligned with the paper's target class in places: at least five of the 51 "true bugs" are outside the assertion framework by construction, three of the 30 negatives were closed as not-reproducible rather than as documented-acceptable behavior, and four of the twelve misses are reclassified as adjudication disagreements rather than judge errors — see 3.6.
- **W5:** Missing related work: LogicHunter (ISSTA 2026) frames the same oracle-ambiguity problem and builds a documentation+source+runtime oracle for silent semantic failures, in an adjacent domain; it is uncited and unpositioned — see 2.3.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The problem is real and well motivated against sources I checked: the cited empirical bug study (fetched) reports functional failures at 57.3% of VDBMS defects against crashes at 15.1%, and the paper's §"Background and Motivation" (subsec:consistency) draws the consistency-versus-correctness boundary explicitly, so the class it targets — silent acceptance or silent behavior that contradicts documented constraints — is the shape that the dedicated crash fuzzer (VDBFuzz) cannot observe and that schema-based oracles do not bound.
   - **1.2** The impact evidence is strong and concrete for an SE venue: the ledger of Table 2 (tab:yield) is maintainer-adjudicated, 50 of 51 confirmed bugs carry an official bug label, 23 have merged fix PRs, the fix-nature classifier is validated against the one candidate the authors themselves disproved (Qdrant #9149), and two further defects were fixed upstream within the following releases (RQ1 subsection, "External corroboration"). Bug-finding papers rarely carry independently merged fixes at this rate.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** Two scope statements would sharpen the significance claim. First, the three targets are the native VDBMSs that prior VDBMS-testing work already covers (the cited bug study spans 15 systems; VDBFuzz evaluates these three among others), so the paper should say what it believes generalizes beyond them rather than leaving the reader to infer it from the limitations paragraph. Second, the prevalence shape the paper leans on (the Silent-Accept / Type-Coercion / Silent-Ignore family = 72.5% of Table 3, tab:bug-patterns) is a property of the authors' own submitted ledger, not a population estimate; the external anchor is the cited bug study's broader functional-failure share, which is a larger class.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** Table 1 (tab:exclusion) is a genuine contribution of the problem-analysis kind: it organizes oracle candidates by where the expectation comes from, states for each family why it misses prose-anchored behavioral conformance, and separates the authors' own objective-constraint catalogue from an independent oracle because it supplies only a falsifier, not an expectation. The row distinctions are drawn precisely and — importantly — the paper discloses that rows 2–6 are argued from each family's anchoring mechanism rather than exercised experimentally.
   - **2.2** I checked the delta against the named competitors, and each characterization holds: MASTOR (fetched) grounds oracle generation in implementation source, demotes OpenAPI claims it cannot substantiate into `pending` fields that the oracle agents never read — so the paper's claim that it "records a doc-versus-source discrepancy signal ... but never turns that signal into an oracle" is accurate, and MASTOR's own stated limitation ("cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code") is exactly the gap TestVDB claims. CASCADE's falsifier is code regenerated from the same documentation, and its residual-false-positive analysis — quoted verbatim in the paper — is accurately reported. Metamon's reported profile (precision 0.722 / recall 0.480 on 9,482 pairs) matches its paper. SATORI's oracles are response-field properties read off OpenAPI metadata, with the response-side spec mismatches the paper concedes; VDBFuzz is crash-oracle by design, as described. The claimed conceptual move — inverting the anchor so the prose is ground and the implementation is only the falsifier — is a real delta over this line.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** Missing related work: LogicHunter ("Testing LLM Agent Frameworks with an Agentic Oracle", ISSTA 2026; arXiv:2607.06195, same research group as the cited VDBFuzz) is uncited. It motivates itself with the same oracle-ambiguity argument — crash oracles cannot see silent semantic failures — and answers with a specification-aware generator plus an agentic oracle that retrieves documentation, navigates source code, and inspects runtime state (reported 30 confirmed and 26 fixed bugs; oracle precision ≈ 91%). The domain (LLM agent frameworks) and the artifact class (a fuzzer) differ from TestVDB's, so this is a positioning gap rather than a novelty collision, but the Related Work's oracle-source discussion should place TestVDB's anchor inversion against it, especially as it is contemporaneous and from the same community.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The statistical reporting reproduces exactly. I recomputed the Wilson intervals of the headline cells (39/51 → [0.632, 0.860]; 8/51 → [0.082, 0.280]; 29/30 → [0.833, 0.994]) and the exact McNemar p-values implied by the reported discordant pairs (16/2 → 0.0013; 15/3 → 0.0075; 11/2 → 0.0225; 5/0 → 0.0625; 8/4 → 0.39; 15/5 → 0.041, Holm ×2 = 0.083); every table row in Tables 4–6 (tab:rq2-matrix, tab:rq2-single, tab:scorings) sums to 81, and the Holm family definition and multipliers are stated and internally consistent (0.0013 × 4 = 0.0052 for the flagship; ×7 = 0.0092 under the maximal-ten variant).
   - **3.2** The audit/rebuild/re-adjudication program is a methodological strength, not just a disclaimer. The paper reports what its own materials got wrong (43.3% of 134 distinct (constraint, cited-page) pairs unsupported; 32% of rows addressing an unrelated endpoint; four packs carrying assembler provenance annotations), traces every defect to the assembler rather than the confirmation stage, rebuilds the packs (674 of 2,106 rows survive; 22 explicit, 652 inferred), then discovers and removes a cognition-anchor channel and re-adjudicates the affected cases in all thirteen affected runs. Each layer (original packs / rebuilt packs / aligned protocol) isolates one variable, which is exactly what makes the three-layer attribution readable.
   - **3.3** The RQ3 baseline treatment is careful in a way that is rare for comparison sections: rather than trusting VDBFuzz's reported zero, the paper shows the released runner's summary counters are non-diagnostic (a template counts as success when its process exits zero; one failure marker is emitted nowhere in the code, the other matches no message the templates emit), substitutes a per-template liveness oracle, and reports a measured zero over 23,258 logged responses across 96 endpoints — then bounds that zero from the released mutation vocabulary. I verified the load-bearing artifact claims in the released repository at the revision the paper names (`7a41449`): the integer boundary set tops out at 65,536 and the dimension candidates at 10,000 (the two citations resolve to those lines), the Qdrant template set contains exactly the 205 templates the paper says `generation_summary.json` reports, and exactly three released templates issue `wait=false` upserts. The two marker properties also check out in the released code: the "unrecognized arguments" string occurs only inside its own marker definition in `run.py`, and no template in the released Qdrant set emits the connectivity message the other marker matches.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** Attribution and the missing end-to-end measurement. RQ1's headline ledger is, by the paper's own statements, cumulative across mining rounds and pipeline generations ("the pool ... spans earlier pipeline generations whose per-generation configuration we do not itemize"), carries author-side novelty screening and the submission decision, and the reported configuration "contributed none of the 81" submissions (abstract; RQ2 subsection; Limitations). The one complete run's funnel is reported "at its two measured points, not as a complete chain of subtractions" (RQ1 subsection, "Process ledger of one complete run"). The consequence is that the paper never measures the detection effectiveness of the pipeline it describes: RQ1's numbers are the project's human–machine ledger, and RQ2 measures only the confirmation stage over a fixed pool. A reader cannot learn what fraction of real documentation-implementation bugs the described pipeline finds per run — the question RQ1 nominally asks. Two revision paths would fix this: itemize per-round provenance for the 81 (the ledger has per-case data, so the mapping exists in the artifact) and/or carry the reported run's five registrations through to whatever maintainer outcomes exist.
   - **3.5 [major, fixable]** The human-review channel is exercised by the authors, non-blind. The joint reading (0.647 recall / 0.917 precision) — which the paper calls "the deployment-faithful one" and uses to argue that the counting convention overstates and forced scoring understates — is produced by the authors adjudicating both arms' routed cases against their pack materials while knowing the candidates and the arm ("(non-blind; disclosed)", RQ2 subsection). The disclosure is honest but no measurement bounds the bias. The paper's own evidence cuts both ways: the authors rejected 4 of the full stage's 15 routed confirmations even though all are maintainer-confirmed upstream, which suggests the pass was strict — but that is an argument, not a bound. A blind adjudication of a subset (or an arm-blinded ordering) would settle it.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** Pool/ground-truth alignment. The RQ2 pool's "51 true bugs" are maintainer-confirmed submissions, and the paper itself states that the Result-Incorrectness cluster (5 bugs) sits "outside the assertion framework by construction" and entered the ledger on maintainer adjudication alone; four of the twelve misses are then reclassified as "ground-truth-adjudication disagreements rather than judge errors". Separately, 3 of the 30 negatives were adjudicated false positives "as not reproducible" (RQ1 subsection, "Bug status") rather than as documented-acceptable behavior, which is a different negative class from the one the confirmation stage is asked to intercept. The recall and suppression denominators therefore mix in cases that are not quite the paper's target class. Reporting recall restricted to in-class candidates (or the pool's class composition) would let a reader see the headline undiluted.
   - **3.7 [minor, fixable]** Run-to-run stability of the primary arm is reported but not interpreted. The full stage's three-run case agreement is 67.9% (55/81) against the contract core's 96.3%, and the per-run rows diverge materially (run 1: 40 TP / 8 leaked; run 3: 35 / 11, Table 4). Majority voting is a legitimate design, but the deployment implication — a single-run deployment would see different rates, and the paper's own protocol-vs-judgment-convention conflicts concentrate in this variance — deserves a sentence, since the paper's claim is about the protocol as a deployable mechanism.
   - **3.8 [minor, fixable]** The flagship statistic's unit and the "leads on both backbones" sentence. The primary paired test is over confirmed sets, so a "leak" counts as confirmed and the net +14 includes its five additional false-positive confirmations; the paper states this and reports the recall-only test's failure to survive Holm. Two consequences should be visible where the claim is first made: (i) the abstract's "on metrics that price a confirmed false positive against a confirmed true positive, the full stage still leads on both backbones" is accurate for F1 (0.788 vs. 0.706) and for net TP−FP (30 vs. 26), but on Youden's J the two primary-family arms are effectively tied (0.465 vs. 0.455) — the near-tie belongs next to the other caveats the abstract already carries; (ii) the separation is single-backbone, with the second-family pair at p=0.58 and lower routing on both arms (10.3%/19.8%), which the paper does state in the body but which a reader of the abstract could miss.
   - **3.9 [minor, fixable]** RQ3 wording: the abstract says the baseline was "run to natural completion on the instance where our silent-accept defects are live", whereas the RQ3 subsection says what ran to completion was "its template-mutation stage" and that the API-sequence-mutation stage "was not run". The body gives the structural argument for why the omission cannot change the reach result (a crash oracle cannot fire on a 2xx silent accept), and the paper separately concedes that the later-search panic surface "we have not measured" — so the abstract should simply say the full released template set was run, rather than implying the whole tool was.

4. **Verifiability** — Excellent
   - **4.1** The Data Availability statement declares a replication package, and it is reachable: I fetched `README.md` from the artifact (HTTP 200), which enumerates the pipeline definitions, the strategy registry, per-version knowledge bases and specifications, all scripts with raw HTTP logs, one evidence chain per candidate, the submission ledger, the frozen and rebuilt RQ2 packs with the audit records and per-case judge outputs for every arm and run, the RQ3 baseline reports with per-template logs, and the analysis scripts. The README's RQ3 accounting reproduces §RQ3's numbers (23,258 logged requests / 22,540 mutation iterations in `full-coverage-v3`; the 205-template set; the 65,536 / 10,000 vocabulary bounds), which is a strong sign that the shipped materials are the ones the paper describes.
   - **4.2** The protocol is documented in the text itself: Appendix A (app:prompts) gives the full-stage and flat-judge prompts, the fixed aggregation order, the four perspectives with their weight constraints, and the deliberate difference between the dispatches (the flat prompt is given the protocol's red lines and four of the seven objective-constraint classes but no perspectives or routing aggregation), with the measured consequence of the narrower enumeration (the flat judge reaches 13 of the 16 objective-constraint-driven confirmations). Ground-truth protocol, counting conventions, and the three readings are all defined in the RQ2 subsection before use.
   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** The declared URL's landing path does not resolve usefully: `https://anonymous.4open.science/r/TestVDB_artifact-EC36/` redirects to the service's repository listing, which returned HTTP 401 `{"error":"not_connected"}` in my check, while file paths under `/api/repo/.../file/` return content normally (a nonexistent repository name returns 404 `repo_not_found`, so the difference is real). A reviewer who clicks the given link may conclude the artifact is dead; the Data Availability note should give a working entry path.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1** The structure is sound and the running example (Qdrant #10369) is threaded through all four stages of the Approach section, which makes an intricate multi-agent pipeline followable; Table 6 (tab:scorings) is the right device for the three-reading result, and the ledger and ablation tables are compact and complete.
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** At 522 words with a 108-word sentence, the abstract carries the entire caveat stack (three readings, five net false-positive confirmations, the recall-only Holm failure, single-backbone status, the second-family non-separation) and is much denser than typical for the venue; several RQ2 sentences pack three claims into nested parentheticals and semicolon chains (the joint judge-plus-human paragraph is the worst offender). Trimming the abstract and moving the qualification chain into the contributions and RQ2 prose would raise readability without losing any disclosure.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** One arithmetic-basis ambiguity: the joint full-versus-flat comparison reports "discordant 8/2, exact McNemar p=0.11" immediately after recalls 0.647 and 0.569, but the recall difference is 4 cases while the discordant net is 6 — the test is on confirmed sets (36 vs. 30, i.e. including the arms' false-positive confirmations), per the convention introduced earlier. Stating the unit in that sentence would save readers a recomputation and remove the appearance of an inconsistency.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** For a paper this dense there is a single figure (Figure 1, the pipeline overview). The recall/suppression trade-off across the five arms and the bidirectional RQ3 probe are inherently graphical results currently confined to tables; one trade-off plot would carry the multi-reading headline better than six table rows.
   - **5.5 [minor, fixable]** Small consistency items: the arm the text calls "contract core" appears as "core" in Table 4's row labels; Table 5's caption omits the "Human-Review counts as confirmed" statement that Table 4's caption gives; and the small-caps verdict \textsc{False-Positive} versus "false-positive candidate" distinction is introduced inside a long sentence in the RQ2 subsection and is easy to lose by the time the tables arrive. A mechanical scan found no typos, repeated words, or unescaped characters.

### Questions for Authors

- **Q1:** The flagship paired test operates on confirmed sets (so false-positive confirmations count toward the difference) and the recall-only variant fails Holm correction (0.090), while Youden's J ties on the primary family (0.465 vs. 0.455). What justified making the confirmed-set unit the primary statistic, and what do the conclusions look like under a utility-weighted metric (e.g. a paired test on F1 or on J)? — if the primary unit is better justified, or if an F1/J-based test also separates, item 3.8's rating would move up; if not, the claim that structure "pays" should be stated in metric terms.
- **Q2:** Could the joint reading (0.647 recall, 0.917 precision) be re-measured with the human adjudicator blinded to the arm and to the candidate's prior outcome (even on a 20–30 case subset)? — a blind estimate would bound the non-blind channel and would move item 3.5 up; a large shift would move it down and would need to be carried into the abstract.
- **Q3:** Which of the 51 confirmed bugs were produced by the configuration evaluated here (pre-bound strategies + the four-perspective confirmation protocol), and what is that configuration's end-to-end detection recall on a version for which maintainer adjudication exists? — answering would move item 3.4 up by closing the attribution gap between the described system and the headline ledger.
- **Q4:** Five of the 51 "true bugs" are, by the paper's own statement, outside the assertion framework by construction, and three of the 30 negatives were "not reproducible" rather than documented-acceptable. What are the stage's recall and suppression restricted to in-class candidates? — a class-restricted reporting would move item 3.6 up and would let readers compare the headline against the class the paper actually targets.
- **Q5:** How does TestVDB's confirmation design differ from LogicHunter's agentic oracle (documentation retrieval + source navigation + runtime state inspection, ISSTA 2026)? — positioning the delta explicitly (implementation source as falsifier of a prose-derived claim, versus documentation as oracle authority for a fuzzer's findings) and citing the work would move item 2.3 up.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets *documentation-implementation bugs* in vector database management systems: cases where a VDBMS silently accepts an input or produces a behavior that its natural-language API documentation forbids. It first argues, family by family, that no deterministic oracle class derives its expectation from system-level untagged prose (Table 1, §2.3), leaving an LLM-derived oracle and importing its false-positive problem. It then presents TestVDB, a four-stage pipeline: documentation is distilled into source-verified typed constraints (§3.2); pre-bound strategies route each constraint to an attack agent that emits executable probes with inline oracle lines (§3.3); probes run in a sandbox against a Docker-pinned version with raw HTTP logging (§3.4); and a confirmation stage splits evidence assembly from cross-examination, building a five-section evidence chain and auditing it against four perspectives (contract, objective physical constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition) under a fixed aggregation with a three-valued verdict (§3.5).

The evaluation has three parts. §4.2 reports a cumulative submission ledger across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate: 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions maintainer-confirmed, 23 fixed by merged PRs, grouped into eight bug patterns (Tables 2–3). §4.3 re-adjudicates that same 81-candidate pool (51 bugs, 30 adjudicated false positives) three times per configuration on audited, rebuilt evidence packs, comparing a contract core, the full stage, a source-only arm, a flat single-prompt judge, and a second-backbone rerun (Tables 4–6); headline results are 0.157 recall / 0.967 suppression for the core and 0.765 / 0.700 for the full stage under the deployment's counting convention (Human-Review counted as confirmed), against 0.588 / 0.867 for the flat judge, with forced-verdict and hand-adjudicated joint readings reported alongside. §4.4 probes the two oracles' reach bidirectionally against VDBFuzz on Qdrant: VDBFuzz's released template set run to completion on v1.18.0 reaches none of TestVDB's confirmed defects, while a hand-guided TestVDB-style boundary probe reaches VDBFuzz's integer-overflow crash on v1.4.0.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The paper's central originality claim — *anchor inversion*, prose as the ground of the expectation and the implementation source only as its falsifier — survives verification against the competitors it names, and the Related-Work characterizations are unusually accurate for the area — see 2.1, 2.2.
- **S2:** Exceptional measurement hygiene: the authors audited their own frozen evidence packs, found 43.3% of cited (constraint, page) pairs unsupported, rebuilt all 81 packs against version-pinned documentation, and then found and cleaned four further contamination channels while shipping the pre-cleanup verdict files — see 3.1.
- **S3:** Statistical reporting is complete enough to check and reproduces: three independent runs per arm with per-run rows, Wilson intervals on every headline rate, exact McNemar with a fully enumerated test family plus a family-size sensitivity, and a separately reported null result on the second backbone — see 3.2.
- **S4:** The detection claim is backed by independently visible outcomes rather than by the tool's own notion of a bug: maintainer adjudication, official bug labels, merged fix PRs, and a file-level classification showing that all 23 merged fixes modify implementation code — see 1.1.
- **S5:** The paper reports its own null results rather than burying them — the forced-verdict collapse of the full-vs-flat gap, the recall-only comparison failing Holm correction, the second backbone failing to replicate, and equal-treatment adjudication for both arms' routed cases — see 3.3, 3.5, 3.7.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The only comparison that survives correction is the counting convention the paper itself calls an overstatement (Human-Review counted as confirmed), while both the forced-verdict and the deployment-faithful hand-adjudicated readings are statistically indistinguishable between the two judges, and five of the fourteen net cases are extra false-positive confirmations — see 3.5.
- **W2:** The deployment-faithful "joint judge-plus-human" reading — the one the paper calls the truth between its two bounds — was produced by the authors themselves, non-blind, acting as the human in the loop, with a single adjudicator and no agreement statistic — see 3.6.
- **W3:** The paper's named mechanism (the four-perspective evidence-chain protocol) is never isolated: the full stage is statistically indistinguishable from the much simpler source-only arm (p=0.39), so what is measured is escalation behavior rather than the protocol itself; the objective-constraint class set that drives 16 of the 39 confirmations was codified from the same mining experience that produced the evaluated pool — see 3.7.
- **W4:** The 51-bug ledger that carries the paper's real-world impact comes from earlier pipeline generations and versions, and the evaluated run contributed none of it, so no part of the paper measures the detection yield of the system as described — see 1.3.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The impact evidence is concrete and independent of the authors' own judgments of severity. §4.2 reports the submission ledger (Table 2): 43 Milvus, 28 Qdrant, and 10 Weaviate submissions adjudicated, 51 confirmed, 23 fixed through merged PRs, 50 of 51 carrying an official `bug` label; it separately reports that 27 of the 30 adjudicated false positives were closed as intended behavior and 3 as not reproducible, and it decomposes the 51 into 23 fixed / 18 open with acknowledgment / 7 closed without a fix / 3 duplicate-tracked. The "Fix nature" check — every one of the 23 merged fix PRs modifies implementation code and none is documentation-only, with the classifier validated against the #9149 PR that the authors' own re-probe showed to be test-only — is exactly the right kind of check against report inflation. This is a real, checkable footprint in three production systems.
   - **1.2** The oracle-exclusion analysis (Table 1, §2.3) is a useful contribution independent of the implementation: it organizes the field by *where each oracle family obtains its expectation*, and the row-by-row argument (crash signals, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property-based testing, structured sources, doc-derived oracles) gives the reader a way to locate the residual. The paper is careful to distinguish what it exercised (rows 1 and 7) from what it argues structurally (rows 2–6), which I take to be a fair reading. The audit result in §4.3 (Layer 0) — 43.3% of the 134 distinct cited (constraint, page) pairs with no support on the page — is a significant negative result about LLM-derived specification pipelines generally, not just about this one.
   - **1.3 [major, fixable]** §4.2's headline ("maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions") is attributed throughout to a pipeline whose measured behavior is never reported for that purpose: the abstract states the ledger "predates the run reported here, which contributed none of the 81", the Table 2 caption repeats it, §4.3 opens by excluding the reported run from the pool, and §5 discloses the serving split (GLM-5.2 for the pool, GLM-5.3-Flash for the reported run). The reported run's own contribution to the detection claim is the 32-candidate anchor of §4.3 (19/32 re-confirmed on the original packs under the pre-audit protocol). The consequence is that no result in the paper measures the end-to-end detection yield of the system as described and evaluated — RQ2 and RQ3 measure the confirmation stage and the oracle comparison on a pool that earlier generations produced, and the two generations are explicitly not itemized. The disclosure is thorough, so this is a positioning rather than a truthfulness problem; but it is a positioning problem on the paper's largest number. Fix: state the RQ1 claim as the pipeline family's cumulative ledger consistently from the abstract onward, and make the reported run's own measured yield (the anchor plus RQ2 and RQ3) the claim that the evaluated system supports.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** I checked the delta against the paper's named closest work, MASTOR — named as such in §6's "Oracles derived from structured sources" paragraph, and the source of the Table 1 row-5 claim in §2.3 (fetched; cached stem `mastor a multi agent approach to semantic test oracle generation for restful apis (corr 2026)`, arXiv:2606.10465). The paper's characterization holds: MASTOR's oracles are grounded in the Java implementation source, its OpenAPI claims not substantiated in source are demoted to `pending` fields, oracle agents read only source-verified fields, and it adds a ChallengerAgent that inspects generated oracles and drives one targeted regeneration. Its own stated limitation — "MASTOR infers semantics from implementation behavior. It cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code" — is precisely the gap that TestVDB's "anchor inversion" addresses: prose as ground, source only as falsifier. The paper's further claim that a second LLM reviewing a generated oracle is not new here, and that cross-request behavioral oracles are not unique, is honest and consistent with what MASTOR does.
   - **2.2** The other named competitors' characterizations in §6 and Table 1 also check out against their cached full texts: SATORI (ASE 2025) generates unary per-response-field oracles from OpenAPI field descriptions and their NL descriptions, with its real bugs living in specification–response mismatches — so the paper's "response-field rather than input-acceptance/state properties" is accurate; AGORA+ (TOSEM) induces invariants from observed traffic ("expected-behavior coverage is bounded by the input distribution" per its own threats section); CASCADE (FSE 2026) uses code regenerated from the same documentation as the falsifier and its residual false positives are attributed by its own analysis to the generated code and tests sharing the documentation's underspecification — the paper's quoted explanation is a fair match; Metamon (LLM4Code 2025) makes an LLM question the arbiter, and its reported precision 0.722 / recall 0.480 on 9,482 pairs matches the paper verbatim. Every characterization I checked within my specialty was accurate as written.
   - **2.3** The RQ2 measurement design is the part I would call methodologically new in this line: audit the pipeline's own evidence packs against the pages they cite, rebuild them version-pinned, re-adjudicate the whole pool three times per configuration, and attribute the movement across three layers (assembler, protocol alignment, pool), with a seeded anchor (32 never-submitted candidates, 19/32 re-confirmed) to bound the submission filter. The nearest work I found in the specialty — Molinelli et al.'s leakage-controlled oracle benchmark (ASE 2025) and Konstantinou et al.'s controlled oracle study (2024) — controls for training-data contamination and for actual-vs-expected drift respectively, but neither audits the *artifacts fed to the judge* and re-measures after repair.
   - **2.4** (Tier rationale, not a finding.) The verified picture places the criterion at Adequate rather than Excellent: the anchor inversion is a real, non-obvious delta over the closest fetched prior work, and the RQ2 measurement design is new in this line, but the delta is incremental — multi-agent pipelines, source grounding, cross-request behavioral oracles, and second-agent oracle review all pre-exist, as §6 itself states — and the paper's own decomposition removes forced-judgment quality from the set of things its structure improves.
   - **2.5 [minor, fixable]** One related-work gap, settled at abstract level: RustC4++ (TOSEM 2026, DOI 10.1145/3800689, "Improving Rust Code-Comment Inconsistency Detection via Hybrid LLM and Static Analysis") belongs in the comment–code inconsistency line the paper enumerates in §6 (iComment, DocRef, DocPrism, DocChecker, C4RLLaMA) and, unlike those, verifies with static analysis and SMT rather than at runtime. It matters twice: it is a straightforward missing citation, and it slightly over-broadens the paper's sentence that the doc-derived line "keeps the derived oracle as the final arbiter, validating it only through runtime behavior". Add it to the line (or state why comment-level Rust static verification is out of scope).

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The measurement hygiene in §4.3 is the strongest part of the paper and is, in my experience, rare. The authors audited the frozen packs that fed their own confirmation stage and found 43.3% of 134 distinct (constraint, page) pairs unsupported, two over-strong, 32% of constraint rows addressing the wrong endpoint, and four packs carrying assembler provenance notes; they rebuilt all 81 packs from version-pinned documentation and the tested version's OpenAPI artifact, and reported the row arithmetic transparently (2,106 → 674 surviving rows; 22 `explicit`, 652 inferred-from-behavior; 21 packs with no covering row). They then found and cleaned four further channels — provenance notes, expectation-framing scan (19 hits, all inside verbatim server responses), ten packs carrying an inherited maintainer-cognition section, and seven candidate-anchored cognition entries — re-adjudicating the affected cases in every affected arm and run and shipping the pre-cleanup verdict files. This is the discipline that the "expectation-framing bias" and "observation leakage" threats in §5 demand, actually executed rather than merely disclosed.
   - **3.2** The statistics are reported at a level that lets a reader check them, and they check out. I verified the internal identities (every row of Tables 4–5 satisfies TP+FP+FN+TN = 81; Table 6's rates reproduce from the majority rows of Tables 4–5), recomputed four Wilson intervals (8/51 → [0.082, 0.280]; 29/30 → [0.833, 0.994]; 39/51 → [0.632, 0.860]; 19/32 → [0.423, 0.745]), and reproduced the exact McNemar tail probabilities for the flagship pair (16/2 → 0.00131), the true-positive-only pair (11/2 → 0.0225), the joint pair (8/2 → 0.109), and the second-family pair (8/5 → 0.581), including the Holm multipliers in footnote 1 and the maximal-ten-test sensitivity (0.0092). Reporting run-level instability (full stage 40/38/35 true positives across runs; 67.9% case-level agreement) rather than only the majority row is the right call.
   - **3.3** The stratified decomposition in §4.3 ("Where recall lives") is internally consistent with the aggregate in a way that makes it usable: the 37/3/11 strata sum to 51, and the per-stratum confirmation counts sum to the 39 convention confirmations, the 27 forced confirmations, and the 33 joint confirmations respectively. Its conclusion is a negative one — the evidence-absent stratum does not lead under any scoring (0.727 convention, 0.273 forced, 0.545 joint), and the contract core is silent on 11 of 11 — and the paper says so plainly. The per-perspective attribution (7 contract, 16 objective-constraint, 2 cognition, 14 no forced driver) is the kind of decomposition that makes the "routing, not accuracy" conclusion follow from data rather than from argument.
   - **3.4** (Tier rationale, not a finding.) Taken together I read this as Adequate rather than Weak: each core claim is stated with the number and the hedge that the number supports, the null results are reported rather than omitted, and the internal arithmetic reproduces — so the evidence does not outrun the claims. It is not Excellent: the surviving comparison is the metric the paper itself calls an upper bound, the deployment-faithful reading is underpowered, the human channel is author-run, and the architecture's distinctive component is unvalidated — notable, disclosed gaps rather than undisclosed overreach.
   - **3.5 [major, fixable]** The comparison that survives Holm correction is the *confirmed-set unit under the deployment's counting convention*, and the paper itself characterizes that convention as an upper bound that "overstates the joint system" (§4.3). Under the two readings the paper calls more faithful, the two judges are not distinguishable: forced verdicts give 0.529 vs. 0.490 (discordant 7/3, p=0.34) and the hand-adjudicated joint reading gives 0.647 vs. 0.569 (discordant 8/2, p=0.11). The surviving difference is also partly composed of the full stage confirming *more false positives* than the flat judge — the abstract's own "five of its fourteen net cases are additional false-positive confirmations" — so the recall-only comparison correctly fails correction (Holm-adjusted 0.090). My concern is not with the honesty of the reporting, which is exemplary; it is that the abstract's first-order claim ("the structured full stage reaches 0.765 recall at 0.700 suppression ... against 0.588") is the least faithful of the three numbers and is the one a reader carries away, and that §4.3's ordering claim ("core < flat < source-only ≈ full ... survives correction under every family size we test") is a claim about a metric the paper simultaneously argues overstates the system. Fix: lead the RQ2 conclusion with the joint reading, present the convention-based test explicitly as an upper-bound statistic, and state in the abstract which unit the surviving p-value is computed on.
   - **3.6 [major, fixable]** The human-review channel is the mechanism the paper credits for the separation, and its measurement is single-rater, non-blind, and author-executed. §4.3's paragraph opening "The decomposition, however, locates the mechanism precisely" says the authors "then adjudicated the routed cases of *both* arms against their pack materials only ... playing the channel's designated human role (non-blind; disclosed)"; the abstract repeats the disclosure. This produces both the deployment-faithful recall (33/51, 0.647) and the joint precision (33/36, 0.917) and suppression (27/30, 0.900), i.e., the numbers the paper presents as the measured truth between its two bounds. A single non-blind adjudicator who also built the packs is the weakest possible instantiation of the channel, and no agreement statistic is given for it. Equal treatment of both arms' routed cases and the pack-materials rule are good design, but they do not substitute for independence. Fix: have at least a second independent adjudicator re-adjudicate the 21 full-stage and 11 flat-stage routed cases (or a sample), report agreement alongside the joint rates, or demote the joint reading from "the deployment-faithful one" to an illustration.
   - **3.7 [major, fixable]** The paper's named architectural mechanism is not isolated by any arm. The full stage and the source-only arm — which is "the confirmation stage's source-forensics step operating alone", with no cognition access and a mechanical mapping of four source verdicts to a binary verdict — are indistinguishable (§4.3: "full-vs-source-only is indistinguishable (p=0.39 ...)"), so whatever the four-perspective protocol and its aggregation add over a single source-forensics step is unmeasured, and the paper's own decomposition places the measured effect in the routing channel rather than in the perspectives. Two related exposures are disclosed but not resolved: the objective-constraint class set that drives 16 of the 39 confirmations was codified "from the same mining experience that produced the evaluated pool" (§5; excluding those confirmations lowers recall to 0.451, and §5 concedes the flat judge admits no symmetric exclusion), and the flat prompt receives only four of the seven constraint classes (Appendix A.2) — the authors do measure the consequence (the flat judge reaches 13 of the 16 objective-driven confirmations, two of the three misses being classes its prompt lacks), which is the right instinct, but the flat arm remains the weaker-specified of the two on the rule axis. Fix: add an arm that isolates the perspectives (e.g., full stage with the contract/cognition perspectives ablated, or source-only plus routing) and either freeze the objective-class set on a different round's pool or report the recall with the class set reduced to what the flat prompt sees.
   - **3.8 [minor, fixable]** One counting convention favors the system on the false-positive side. §4.3: "a returned case is never adjudicated a false positive, so it still counts as intercepted in the joint system and the joint suppression closes at 27/30 (0.900)". A case the human could not resolve on the materials is thereby scored as a successful interception (worth 1/30, ≈3.3 points of suppression), while on the recall side a returned case is *not* counted as confirmed. The asymmetric treatment is defensible for a deployment but should be reported in both directions: give the suppression with the returned case counted as unresolved next to the 0.900.
   - **3.9 [minor, fixable]** §4.4's reach claim is slightly stronger than the paper's own disclosures support. The paragraph on the reverse direction concedes that "the panic the defect produces on a later search is a separate surface whose observability to VDBFuzz's oracle class we have not measured" (#9045, v1.18.0), yet the next paragraph asserts of the unrun API-sequence-mutation stage that "its omission cannot change the reach result". The structural argument is sound for the silent accept *at the point of acceptance*; it does not cover a composition in which a later operation produces the crash the defect is by then known to produce. The footnote's careful bound — a hand-reproduced crash on v1.4.0 is a recovered panic (HTTP 500) that their substituted liveness oracle would not record, so only service death falls within the substituted oracle's reach — is exactly the right level of care; the earlier sentence should be restricted to that same scope.

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate

Shipping the verbatim dispatch files with the English renderings is the right arrangement.
   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** Some headline numbers cannot be recomputed from the text alone — the per-case verdicts, the per-perspective attribution of the 39 confirmations, the human adjudication rationales, and the identity of the 16 objective-constraint-driven confirmations all live in the artifact. I could not reach the anonymous URL from this review environment (network access blocked; the review protocol also forbids cloning or running the artifact), so reachability should be confirmed at submission time; if the link is live, this criterion moves to the top of the range.
   - **4.4 [minor, fixable]** §4.3 (in its paragraph on the hand-adjudicated pass) refers to "the round-13 adjudications recorded under the identical protocol", and §4.2's process ledger refers to a "pre-review protocol" whose verdict vocabulary predates the Human-Review channel; Appendix A mentions the asymmetry only by pointer, as "(the asymmetry quantified in Section 4.3)". ## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets *documentation--implementation* bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where a system silently accepts an input or behaves in a way that contradicts its natural-language API documentation (the canonical example is `nprobe=0` accepted where the documentation declares the range [1, 16384]). It argues that no deterministic-oracle family anchors its expectation in system-level, untagged prose (Table 1), leaving an LLM-derived oracle as the practical option, and it builds TestVDB, a four-stage multi-agent pipeline -- behavioral specification extraction, test-script generation under pre-bound strategies, sandboxed execution against Docker-pinned versions, and a confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly from chain-auditor cross-examination across four perspectives, with the implementation source as the falsification anchor (Figure 1, §3.5).

The empirical work has two halves. RQ1 is an operational ledger of 81 adjudicated upstream submissions across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, of which maintainers confirmed 51 as real bugs and fixed 23 (Table 2). RQ2 is a controlled re-adjudication of the same 81 candidates (51 true bugs, 30 maintainer-adjudicated false positives) on audited, rebuilt evidence packs, in which five judge configurations -- contract core, full stage, source-only, flat single-prompt judge, and source-only on a second model family -- are run three times each (Tables 4–6). The full stage reads 0.765 recall at 0.700 suppression under the deployment's counting convention, 0.647/0.900 under a hand-adjudicated joint reading, and 0.529 under forced verdicts, against the flat judge's 0.588/0.867. RQ3 probes the two oracles' reach bidirectionally against VDBFuzz on Qdrant (§4.4).

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** A large, adversarially checked real-bug yield: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 with merged fix PRs whose changed files are classified so that documentation-only "fixes" cannot count — see 1.1
- **S2:** The oracle-gap claim is measured, not just asserted: the crash-oracle baseline, run to full template coverage on the instance where the paper's silent-accept defects are live, reaches none of them, and the paper audits the baseline's own counters before trusting its zero — see 1.2, 3.2
- **S3:** The paper's internal accounting reconciles — I re-derived table marginals, the paired-test discordant nets, the forced→joint→convention scoring ladder, the evidence strata, and the Wilson intervals without finding a discrepancy — and it reports the evidence against its own headline comparison — see 3.1, 3.3, 4.1
- **S4:** The confirmation-stage design (builder/auditor split, four perspectives, three-valued verdict with explicit routing) is a legible, reusable mechanism, and the finding that its measured value lies in routing rather than forced accuracy is a useful result in its own right — see 2.1, 3.4

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The decisive full-vs-flat comparison survives only on a counting convention the paper itself calls an upper bound; under the joint (deployment-faithful) reading it is not significant (p=0.11), and it does not replicate on the second model family (p=0.58) — see 3.4, 2.3
- **W2:** No end-to-end automated measurement is reported: RQ1's ledger measures a human-screened, unblinded deployment and RQ2's rates are pool-internal, so neither headline number describes the automated system on an unscreened stream — see 1.3, 3.5
- **W3:** Readability: RQ2 is a ~9-page subsection without subheadings, and the abstract front-loads conditional figures and undefined terms; a reader can lose the paper's central twist (three readings of one measurement) — see 5.2, 5.3
- **W4:** A few reported quantities stop one step short of reconstructible in the paper: the relation between "registrations," candidates, and the five defect numbers; the per-run identity of the four affected flat-arm runs in the cleanup accounting; the undefined "round-13" — see 4.3, 5.4

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** [strength] Table 2 and §4.2: 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions maintainer-confirmed (63.0%), 23 fixed, split 29/14/8 confirmed across Milvus/Qdrant/Weaviate. §4.2 adds the status accounting (23 fixed + 18 open with acknowledgment + 7 closed without fix + 3 duplicate-tracked = 51) and a "Fix nature" check in which all 23 merged fix PRs modify implementation code, 15 also add regression tests, and none is documentation-only. Real defects in real systems, carrying the strongest external signals (official labels, merged PRs), are the paper's core asset; the problem's importance does not need arguing.
   - **1.2** [strength] §2.1 and §4.4: the paper first argues structurally that crash-oracle fuzzing cannot reach non-crashing accepts, then measures it — a full-coverage run of VDBFuzz's released Qdrant template set (205 templates, 22,540 mutations, 23,258 logged responses across 96 endpoint paths) yields no hit on the defects live on that instance, while a hand-guided reverse probe reaches VDBFuzz's crash class from boundary reasoning. This bidirectional evidence is exactly what a reader can act on, and it is a clean, useful negative result.
   - **1.3 [major, fixable]** §4.2 and §5: the impact claim and the measurement sit at different levels. The ledger "measures the human--machine pipeline rather than the automated stage alone" (author-side screening; unblinded operation), and RQ2 re-adjudicates the pool that ledger produced with pool-internal recall; the one complete automated run's public yield is a process ledger registering five new defect numbers (§4.2) at ≈67 active hours and ≈70M input tokens (§4.1). A reader therefore cannot say what the system would find, at what cost, if run without the authors in the loop. Fixable by stating the deployment-level claim explicitly (the paper is close to this) and/or by reporting one measurement of the automated stage on an unscreened stream.
   - **1.4 [minor, fixable]** The cost profile (§4.1) and the run's yield (§4.2) appear in different subsections and are never combined; one sentence ("≈67 hours and ≈70M tokens for five new registrations on Qdrant v1.18.0") would let readers judge adoptability.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate (provisional: I did not survey the field; assessed from the paper's own positioning)
   - **2.1** [strength] §3.5, §6, and Table 1: the "anchor inversion" — documentation prose as ground truth, implementation source only as falsifier — is a crisp delta against the works the paper names: MASTOR (whose authority is the implementation), Metamon (an LLM judge stabilized by the same LLM), CASCADE (code generated from the same documentation), SATORI/AGORA+/MASTEST (structured sources), and the doc-derived oracle line whose derived oracle remains the final arbiter. The four-perspective confirmation protocol with three-valued routing and a bounded rebuild loop is a concrete system design, not a slogan.
   - **2.2 [minor, fixable]** §6 concedes that ingredients are individually known ("a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is therefore not new here, and cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to TestVDB"); the delta therefore rests on the anchor inversion plus the measured routing mechanism. §1 should state that in one sentence, naming the closest competitor, rather than leaving it to §6.
   - **2.3 [minor, unfixable]** The empirical novelty — that the structured protocol's advantage is escalation, not forced accuracy — is bounded by its own non-replication on the second backbone (§4.3; p=0.58). No revision can widen that evidence; only the claim's scope can be set correctly (which the paper largely does).

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** [strength] I checked the arithmetic reachable from the tables and it adds up. Table 2's rows and columns sum to 81/51/23/30; Table 3's eight patterns sum to 51 (acceptance family 25+8+4 = 37 = 72.5%); every cell pair in Tables 4–5 satisfies TP+FN=51 and leak+TN=30; each McNemar discordant net quoted equals the confirmed-set difference implied by the tables (full 48 vs. flat 34 ↔ 16/2; core 9 vs. full 48 ↔ 1/40); the scoring ladder in §4.3/Table 6 closes in both directions (39 − 33 = 2 material-insufficient + 4 rejected; 27 forced + 9 hand-confirmed − 3 already majority = 33 joint); the strata (37/3/11 packs) decompose to exactly the same true positives under all three scorings (29+2+8=39; 24+0+3=27; 26+1+6=33); and the Wilson intervals reproduce to three decimals (8/51 → [0.082, 0.280]; 19/32 → [0.423, 0.745]).    - **3.2** [strength] §4.4's treatment of the baseline: the authors distrust its counters, discover they cannot fail (a dead-port run reports exit 0 and 100% success), substitute a liveness oracle, report the applied mutation space and the HTTP-status distribution, and then bound their own conclusion ("Both bounds are properties of the released configuration rather than of crash oracles as a class"). The reverse probe is honestly labeled hand-executed with knowledge of the mechanism.
   - **3.3** [strength] The paper reports disconfirming evidence against itself: the recall-only McNemar does not survive Holm correction (0.090), the joint reading is not significant (p=0.11), and the second-family re-run of the flagship pair does not separate (discordant 8/5, p=0.58; forced-only 0.510 vs. 0.451).    - **3.4 [major, fixable]** §4.3 and Table 6: "the structured protocol recalls 0.765 where a flat single-prompt judge reaches 0.588" is true only on the convention that counts Human-Review as confirmed — a reading the paper itself calls an upper bound ("the convention overstates the joint system, forced scoring understates it, and the measured truth sits in between") — and the paper's own decomposition says the separation "is carried by the routing channel, not by forced-verdict accuracy" (forced: 0.529 vs. 0.490, p=0.34). This residual exposure is inherent to a single-pool study and cannot be removed by revision.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** §4.2/§4.3/§5: both the numerator and the miss block contain adjudication judgment calls — 4 of the 12 misses are cases the paper keeps as ground-truth true positives while saying the stage's literal-contract refutation is correct, and 3 of the 9 leaks are declared adjudication disagreements. A one-line sensitivity (full-stage recall with those four excluded from the ground-truth pool: 39/47 ≈ 0.83) would make the dependence visible.

4. **Verifiability** — Excellent
   - **4.1** [strength] The Data Availability section declares a replication package whose inventory maps one-to-one onto the claims: agent roles and the strategy-trigger registry, per-version knowledge bases and specifications, all scripts with raw HTTP logs, one evidence chain per candidate, the ledger, the original and rebuilt packs with audit records and per-case outputs for every arm and run, the RQ3 baseline reports, and analysis scripts that recompute every rate, interval, and paired test. §A ships both judging prompts.    - **4.2 [minor, fixable]** §A: the dispatch files actually used are Chinese and authoritative only in the artifact; the appendix gives English renderings.
