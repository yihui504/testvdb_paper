## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): inputs a system silently accepts, or behaviors it exhibits, that contradict its natural-language API documentation. It argues that no deterministic oracle family derives its expectation from behavioral prose and instantiates TestVDB, a four-stage LLM-agent pipeline: documentation distilled into source-verified structured specifications; pre-bound-strategy generation of executable probes; sandboxed execution against a Docker-pinned version with raw HTTP logging; and a confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly from cross-examination, where a five-section evidence chain is read from four perspectives with the implementation source as the falsification anchor. Two empirical bodies of work are reported separately: an operational ledger of 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs and 23 merged fixes across Milvus, Qdrant and Weaviate (the sum of sixteen per-version campaigns that predate the run reported here); and a re-adjudication study over the 81-candidate pool those campaigns produced (51 real bugs, 30 maintainer-adjudicated false positives), using evidence packs that were audited, rebuilt against version-pinned documentation, and re-judged three times per configuration across eight judge configurations.

That re-adjudication reports the contract-only core at 0.157 recall / 0.967 suppression, the structured full stage at 0.765 / 0.700 under a counting convention that credits human-review routing as confirmation, a flat single-prompt judge at 0.588, and a set of decomposition arms: a flat judge bound to the same aggregation rule matches the full stage's recall exactly (p = 0.61), so the four-perspective organization's increment is not established on this pool; withholding the implementation source raises convention recall to 0.941 and cuts suppression to 0.467; and the full-vs-flat pair does not separate when re-run on a second model family (p = 0.58). A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz finds the crash-oracle baseline reaching none of the silent-accept defects on Qdrant v1.18.0, while a hand-guided boundary probe built on TestVDB's reasoning reaches VDBFuzz's own integer-overflow case on v1.4.0.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** A genuinely under-tested problem class, a real maintained yield (51 confirmed, 23 fixed), and an oracle-space analysis that is honest about which of its rows are argued rather than exercised — see 1.1, 2.1, 2.3
- **S2:** Measurement discipline that makes the paper's own negative results credible: pack audit and rebuild, frozen packs, three runs per arm, contamination cleanups followed by re-adjudication, and statistics that recompute exactly (I re-derived every Wilson interval, every exact McNemar p-value cited, and the Holm multipliers) — see 3.1, 4.1
- **S3:** The component controls do the work the paper claims for them, and the paper follows them wherever they lead — including to its own architecture — and prices the source's role with a withholding control rather than asserting it — see 3.2, 3.3, 3.6
- **S4:** Competitor characterizations are accurate on every one I verified, and the two load-bearing artifact claims of the RQ3 comparison check out against the baseline's own released code — see 2.1, 2.2

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The surviving claim is never stated plainly. On the paper's own numbers the full-vs-flat gap exists only under the counting convention; the forced reading is null (p = 0.34), the joint reading is null (p = 0.11), and the comparison does not replicate on a second family. The contribution list and abstract should be re-centered on what did survive — see 1.2, 3.2, 3.5
- **W2:** The mechanism the paper identifies as load-bearing (the seven objective-constraint classes, 16 of 39 confirmations, 0.451 recall without them) cannot be excluded symmetrically from the control arm, and the classes were codified from the same mining experience that produced the pool; the causal comparison is therefore not identified — see 3.3
- **W3:** The declared replication package does not yet contain what it promises for the newest results: the `rq2/analyses/` directory is absent and there are no per-case judge outputs for the five arms that carry the decomposition (flat, flat + schema fix, flat + aggregation, full without source, source-only-Qwen) — see 4.2
- **W4:** Two internal accounting inconsistencies sit in the RQ2 subsection's supporting numbers, including the composition of the full stage's minority confirmations and a clause attribution that sums to 236 against a stated 243 — see 3.4

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The problem selection is well grounded rather than assumed. §2.1 and §1 rest the motivation on the two empirical studies of VDBMS defects the paper builds on, and I checked both: the bug study's cached summary reports functional failures as the dominant symptom class (57.3% vs 15.1% crash, with the "stays up but silently returns wrong results" phrasing the paper quotes), and the roadmap's own text — retrieved in this review via a scoped search rather than from the cache — categorizes incorrect-behavior bugs as its largest symptom class (43.0%) and names oracle definition as its key open challenge for that residual. The paper's own ledger agrees with the same picture — one of the 51 confirmed bugs produces a crash (§1), and the pattern table is dominated by silent-acceptance shapes (37 of 51 in the Silent-Accept / Type-Coercion / Silent-Ignore family). This is a real, documented gap, and 51 maintainer-confirmed defects with 23 merged fix PRs is a substantial yield for the target class.
   - **1.2 [major, fixable]** What the paper now claims, and what it should claim. The contribution list (§1) describes the pipeline, then concedes in the same bullet that the comparison's separation is carried by routing, that adding the aggregation rule alone to the flat judge recovers the recall exactly (p = 0.61), and that the four-perspective organization's increment "is not established on this pool". Read together with §4.3's second-family non-replication and the joint-reading null, the paper's methodological yield is three transferable findings — (i) prose-derived expectations can be operationalized at this scale, (ii) explicit escalation beats forced closure, (iii) the implementation source is a suppressor rather than a detector — plus a negative result about judge-harness structure. That is worth publishing, but the reader has to assemble it from five scattered hedges while the abstract still leads with a comparison the paper itself declines to stand behind. State the surviving claims in one place, in the order of their evidential strength, and subordinate the rest.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The paper refers throughout to "the deployment" and to "the deployment's own reading", which reads as an arm's-length production user (e.g. §5.3's "the deployment reports what it routes", §4.2's "the deployment reports what the protocol routes"). In fact the pipeline, the protocol, the counting convention and the adjudication are all the authors'. The disclosure is present ("with author-side screening and submission", §1), but the framing does rhetorical work the evidence does not: rename it "the operational protocol" and keep "convention" as the metric label.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** I checked the delta against the five competitors the contribution actually rests on, from their cached full texts: MASTOR (source as the oracle's authority; its `pending` fields hold OAS items the source does not substantiate and are deliberately never turned into oracles), CASCADE (regenerated-implementation-as-falsifier from the same documentation), Metamon (LLM judge on generated assertions, stabilised by metamorphic queries), LogicHunter (agentic oracle that retrieves documentation but also reads and executes the implementation) and VDBFuzz (crash oracle). The paper's characterizations are accurate in each case, including the generous ones: it states that "a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is therefore not new here, and cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to TestVDB; what is new is the anchor inversion". The inversion is real — MASTOR's authority is the implementation, TestVDB's is the prose, and the implementation enters only through clauses that can yield a not-defect verdict — and the VDBMS domain application is new. The delta is real but incremental, which is what the paper itself claims.
   - **2.2 [minor, fixable]** One overstatement in the argument that supports the RQ3 comparison. §4.4 and the README text state that "neither failure marker can fire---one is emitted by no code path". The first marker, `"error: unrecognized arguments:"`, is argparse's own message and fires whenever a template receives an argument it does not declare; I fetched the released runner and a template and confirmed only that neither marker appears in the template's own text (whose failure path logs a Chinese message through a broad `except Exception`). The claim should be "does not fire in this configuration". The conclusion — that the run-level success/failure counters are uninformative and the per-template oracle is the usable signal — is untouched, and I verified its supporting mechanics (exit-code-based success at `run.py:292`).
   - **2.3** The treatment of TestVDB's own deterministic component is a strength worth naming: the objective-constraint catalogue is included as row 7 of the exclusion table, against the paper's interest, with the reasoning that "omitting it would overstate the residual". Together with the footnote inventory of the subsection's Holm family and the explicit statement that rows 2–7 of the table are argued from each family's anchoring mechanism rather than exercised, this is the kind of positioning that makes a novelty claim checkable rather than rhetorical.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** Related-work coverage is the strongest I have seen in this area (66 distinct cited works, including work from the same months, with correct one-line deltas). My scoped coverage searches — VDBMS testing, documentation-implementation inconsistency detection, prose-derived oracles for REST APIs, metamorphic testing of vector search, missing-input-validation detection from documentation — surfaced no highly-related uncited work; the nearest uncited neighbours (APITestGenie, LLM2Alloy) are adjacent rather than competing. One optional addition, offered provisionally (I did not fetch a source for it and it is not a competitor in the doc-oracle space): the abstention / selective-prediction and human-in-the-loop triage line, which is exactly what the paper's surviving mechanism claim ("escalate rather than force") is about. Either position against it explicitly or state why the VDBMS deployment setting makes the trade different.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The measurement design is unusually disciplined, and I could verify much of it arithmetically. §4.3 audits its own materials (43.3% of the 134 distinct (constraint, cited-page) pairs unsupported; 32% of constraint rows addressing an unrelated endpoint; four packs with assembler provenance notes), rebuilds the packs, and then re-adjudicates every affected pack in every affected arm and run after each contamination discovery (13 runs for the cognition-section cleanup; seven cases for the candidate-anchored cognition entries). Pool arithmetic is exact (81 = 51 + 30; 43 + 28 + 10 across targets; 23 + 18 + 7 + 3 = 51; the pattern table sums to 51; the pack-rebuild funnel 2106 − 674 − 463 = 969, 969 − 302 = 667, 667 + 7 = 674, 674 − 22 = 652). Every Wilson interval recomputes to three decimals (8/51 → [0.082, 0.280]; 29/30 → [0.833, 0.994]; 39/51 → [0.632, 0.860]; 37/51 → [0.591, 0.829]; 19/32 → [0.423, 0.745]); every exact McNemar p-value cited recomputes (16/2 → 0.0013; 8/4 → 0.39; 11/2 → 0.0225; 5/0 → 0.0625; 15/3 → 0.0075; 15/5 → 0.041; 2/14 → 0.0042; 6/1 → 0.125; 7/3 → 0.34; 8/2 → 0.11; 9/6 → 0.61; 2/18 → 0.0004; 8/5 → 0.58); the Holm multipliers match a standard step-down; and the derived metrics recompute (F1 0.788/0.706 and 0.698/0.651; Youden's J 0.465/0.455 and 0.422/0.363; the strata 8/37, 0/3, 0/11 and 29/37, 2/3, 8/11; the RQ3 status census 23,258 = 14,022 + 7,520 + 1,512 + 204 with no 5xx). Numbers of this density that survive recomputation are a real signal of care.
   - **3.2 [major, fixable]** The headline comparison is convention-carried, and the paper's own fair readings of it are null. In §4.3: full 0.765/0.700 vs flat 0.588/0.867 under a convention where a routed case counts as confirmed; forced verdicts 0.529 vs 0.490 (p = 0.34); joint judge + human 0.647/0.917 vs 0.569/0.967 (p = 0.11); and an independent adjudicator with the full deployed rules reaching 0.588 — the same value as the flat judge's convention recall, though measuring a different quantity. In other words, the one reading in which the two systems differ materially is the one that credits a declined decision as a detection, which is a property of the metric before it is a property of the system. The paper discloses this ("the convention overstates the joint system, forced scoring understates it"), but the abstract and contribution list still present 0.765 vs 0.588 as *the* RQ2 result with the nulls as "sensitivities". Lead with the joint reading and its interval, state plainly that the between-arm difference is not established on this pool, and keep the convention number where it belongs — as the operational reading of a rule that routes rather than closes.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The mechanism claim is not identified. §4.3 reports that 16 of the full stage's 39 confirmations are driven by the objective-constraint perspective and that excluding them drops recall to 0.451, while "the flat judge admits no symmetric exclusion, since its single-prompt design records no perspective attribution"; the same section concedes the classes "were codified from the same mining experience that produced the evaluated pool, so absolute recall cannot fully separate protocol quality from protocol fit". The flat control does receive four of the seven classes as prompt examples (the paper quantifies the resulting difference carefully), but with no attribution it cannot be re-scored under the same exclusion. As measured, therefore, "the full stage beats a flat judge" is equally compatible with "the full stage's advantage is one extra rule plus three extra enumerated classes", which is a prompt-content effect rather than a confirmation-architecture effect — and since the flat + aggregation control already reproduces the recall exactly, the residual uncertainty is precisely about the classes that carry 41% of the confirmations. The honest fix is instrumentation, not prose: record per-clause attribution in the control arms too, and run the exclusion on both sides.
   - **3.4 [minor, fixable]** Two internal accounting inconsistencies in §4.3's supporting numbers. (i) The composition of the full stage's 39 majority confirmations — "23 are Confirmed in all three runs; the remaining 16 each carry a minority verdict, 15 of them a Human-Review component ... and one a minority False-Positive" — does not reconcile with the ablation table's per-run rows under the stated ≥2/3 majority with Human-Review counted as confirmed: per-run true positives sum to 40 + 38 + 35 = 113 over 39 majority confirmations, which forces exactly 4 of those 39 to have only two confirming runs and hence to carry a False-Positive label; with 23 unanimous cases, that means at least four of the 16, not one, carry a minority false positive. (ii) The clause attribution in §4.3 ("19 ... 50 ... 16 ... 21 ... 61 ... and 69 routed by the catch-all") sums to 236, not the 243 case judgments it claims to cover, and its 69 catch-all (routed) judgments conflict with the 48 routing verdicts reported two paragraphs earlier ("48 of the 243 case judgments pooled across the three runs") and with the 21-case routed queue. The qualitative conclusions — 17 of the 19 source-closed judgments are adjudicated false positives; the source channel buys suppression at a recall cost — survive intact, but that paragraph is the one offered as locating the mechanism "precisely", so the numbers should reconcile with the tables.
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** The "leads on both backbones" framing overstates a thin margin. §1 and §4.3 note that "on metrics that price a confirmed false positive against a confirmed true positive, the full stage still leads on both backbones (30 vs. 26 net ...)". The "net" statistic is the raw difference of counts (39 − 9 vs 30 − 4), and the only balanced measure reported, Youden's J, is effectively tied on the primary family (0.465 vs 0.455 — one point on a 51/30 pool); the paper does disclose that, but the leading sentence does not. Given that the second family's F1 gap (0.698 vs 0.651) is likewise small and the paired test there is null, report an interval on J or drop the net-of-counts framing.
   - **3.6** The RQ3 comparison is bounded in exactly the places a reviewer would push. The paper states what was not run (VDBFuzz's API-sequence stage), what was not measured (which templates reach a zero-length vector), that the v1.4.0 confirmation was a hand-executed probe chosen with knowledge of the overflow mechanism from the baseline's own case study, and that the substituted liveness oracle cannot record a recovered panic. It then draws the narrow conclusion. I verified the two load-bearing artifact numbers against the released baseline: `vdbfuzz/mutator.py:247` is the dimension-candidate list with maximum 10,000, the integer boundary set (in `vdbfuzz/keywords/__init__.py`) tops out at 65,536, and `templates/qdrant/` holds exactly 205 templates — so "the released mutation space cannot construct the input class VDBFuzz's own study reports crashing Qdrant" is well founded at code level.
   - **3.7 [minor, fixable]** §4.3 opens by describing the subsection as re-adjudicating "under blinding and with no human in the loop", yet the joint reading's recall depends on an author-executed adjudication of 21 + 11 routed cases and the protocol routes 19.8% of verdicts precisely so a human will decide them. The automated arms are indeed human-free, but the sentence reads as covering the whole subsection; say "no human in the automated arms".

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets a class of vector-database defects that produce no crash: cases where a system accepts an input or returns a result that contradicts its own natural-language API documentation (its canonical example is a search API that accepts `nprobe=0` where the documentation declares the range $[1,16384]$). It argues that every deterministic oracle family anchors its expectation elsewhere—in a crash signal, another implementation, an output relation, a schema—so the expectation has to be read out of the prose, which in practice means an LLM, which imports the false-positive problem. The proposed pipeline has four stages: documentation is distilled into typed, source-verified constraints; attack agents bound to a pre-registered strategy table turn constraints into probes; a Docker-pinned sandbox executes them and logs raw HTTP; and a confirmation stage separates an evidence builder from a chain auditor that cross-examines a five-section evidence chain from four perspectives, with the implementation source as the falsification anchor.

The empirical content has two layers. RQ1 reports an operational deployment across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate: 81 adjudicated submissions, 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 merged fixes, plus a bidirectional reach probe against the crash-oracle fuzzer VDBFuzz. RQ2 re-adjudicates the frozen evidence packs behind that ledger after an audit found 43.3% of their cited (constraint, page) pairs unsupported by the cited pages; on rebuilt, version-pinned packs, eight judge configurations are each run three times over the same 81-case pool (51 real bugs, 30 false positives). Under the deployment's counting convention (a `Human-Review` verdict counts as a confirmation), the pack-only contract core recalls 0.157 at 0.967 suppression, the full structured stage 0.765 at 0.700, and a flat single-prompt judge 0.588 at 0.867. The paper then decomposes that gap with component controls—a schema correction to the flat dispatch, the full stage's aggregation rule added to it, and a source-withheld variant of the full stage—reports the same comparison under forced and joint judge-plus-human readings, and locates the recall gain in the routing rule rather than in forced-verdict accuracy or in the four-perspective organization, a conclusion it states against its own interest.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The bug yield is real and carefully validated: 51 maintainer-confirmed defects with 23 merged fixes, every fix PR classified by changed files to exclude documentation-only and test-only repairs — see 1.1.
- **S2:** The paper audits its own frozen evidence packages, finds 43.3% of their cited pairs unsupported, rebuilds every package against version-pinned documentation, and re-adjudicates every affected case and run after three contamination channels in its own materials are found and removed — see 3.1, 4.1.
- **S3:** The source-withheld control plus the per-judgment clause audit together establish what the falsification anchor is *for*—refusal, not discovery—a specific and non-obvious result, and the audit even reports two cases where the anchor's own red line was violated — see 3.2.
- **S4:** The statistical reporting is verifiable by recomputation: every Wilson interval and every exact McNemar $p$ I recomputed from the paper's own discordant counts reproduces exactly, and every table row reconciles on both class axes — see 4.1.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The flagship full-stage-vs-flat-judge $p$-value is computed against a flat dispatch the paper itself documents as internally inconsistent; once that is repaired the gap closes by more than half, and the full-vs-repaired-baseline paired test is never reported — see 3.3.
- **W2:** The component control that carries the causal claim is a two-change bundle, and the paper both declares the partition "not identified" and then partitions it anyway; no arm isolates the four perspectives without the aggregation rule, so "the advantage belongs to the routing rule rather than to the four perspectives" is stronger than the design supports — see 3.4.
- **W3:** The per-judgment clause audit, which is the evidence for the paper's central mechanistic claim, does not reconcile: the six listed clauses sum to 236 of the stated 243 judgments, and the catch-all's 69 judgments exceed the 48 `Human-Review` verdicts the same subsection reports — see 3.6.
- **W4:** The joint judge-plus-human reading (0.647), one of the three headline numbers and called "the measured truth" in text, is not reproducible by the paper's own independent adjudicator ($\kappa \le 0.11$ on 20 cases, 0.588), and no uncertainty is attached to the $\kappa$ estimates — see 3.5.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The RQ1 ledger is a genuine contribution: 81 adjudicated submissions across three production VDBMSs (Table 2), 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 fixed via merged PRs, with the fix count protected against inflation by classifying every merged PR by its changed files (all 23 modify implementation code; 15 add regression tests; none is documentation-only), and with the classifier itself validated against the one candidate the authors later disproved. The concentration of the ledger in silent-accept/type-coercion/silent-ignore shapes—37 of 51, 72.5% (Table 3)—substantiates the paper's premise that this bug class is real and reachable at production scale. This is the part of the paper whose impact does not depend on the RQ2 protocol arguments.
   - **1.2 [minor, fixable]** The advertised contribution is narrower than the abstract suggests. Contribution bullet 2 (Section 1) leads with "a four-stage, multi-agent pipeline whose confirmation stage grounds every verdict in evidence sources outside the LLM", but Sections 4.3 and 7 report that the pipeline's distinguishing organizational element—the four-perspective cross-examination—has no established increment on this pool, and that the recall gain belongs to a routing rule which a flat single-prompt judge reproduces. Given that the authors reached that honest conclusion, the contribution statement should lead with what survives (the anchor inversion, the routing discipline, the audit-and-rebuild method) rather than with a pipeline whose measured value is carried by one clause of its aggregation rule.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The significance of the RQ2 result is bounded by a counting convention that the paper inherits rather than chooses. Under the mechanically cleanest reading—forced verdicts only—the full stage's 0.529 does not separate from the flat judge's 0.490 (discordant 7/3, $p{=}0.34$), so a practitioner adopting this protocol should expect a change in queue composition, not a detection gain. The paper reports this, but its significance discussion never states plainly that the deployment's *measured* advantage over a flat judge is a routing-policy difference, i.e. a difference in how often each arm declines to decide.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** Checked the delta against MASTOR (fetched, in its own §4.2–4.3): the paper's claim that MASTOR's authority is the implementation, and that its doc-versus-source signal (the `pending` fields holding OAS-declared items the source does not substantiate) never becomes an oracle, is verified verbatim in MASTOR's own text, as is its self-stated limitation that it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code". The anchor inversion is a real, correctly attributed delta, and the paper concedes the overlaps it cannot defend (cross-request behavioural oracles and LLM-reviewed oracle generation are "not new here").
   - **2.2** Checked the delta against Metamon (fetched): its falsifier is the LLM judge itself, stabilised by metamorphic prompt transformation and self-consistency over 9,482 pairs, with no non-LLM evidence source; and against CASCADE (fetched), whose falsifier is a second LLM-generated artifact derived from the same documentation, with both of CASCADE's residual false positives attributed by CASCADE's own authors to code and tests "made the same false assumption over something underspecified in the documentation". Both characterizations hold, and the quoted clause is verbatim in CASCADE's text. "Its falsifier is another LLM question—precisely the self-reference TestVDB breaks" is a fair summary of the closest deployment of the same division of labour.
   - **2.3** Checked the structured-source row (SATORI, fetched; AGORA+ at summary level): SATORI's oracles are per-response-field and OAS-anchored, its own limitation statement confirms unary-only coverage, and its reported bugs do include response-side specification–response mismatches—so the paper's claim that the input-acceptance and state-conformance faces remain unreached is correct. LogicHunter (fetched) is correctly characterized: its oracle's tools include reflective source search and sandboxed code execution, so it does consult the implementation before reaching a verdict, whereas this paper admits the implementation only as a falsifier. Each of these is a real, non-obvious delta, and no competitor characterization I checked is inflated in the paper's favor.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** The delta shrinks once the paper's own ablation lands. The ingredients it lists as new—multi-agent generation, cross-operation oracles, an LLM reviewer over generated oracles, cross-examination of an evidence chain—each exist in named prior work, and the one element that is structurally distinctive here (four-perspective cross-examination) is shown by the paper's own control not to drive the measured result. What remains new is the direction of oracle authority plus the routing discipline. That is enough for a paper, but the originality claims in Sections 1, 4.3 and 7 should be re-anchored to it (see also 1.2).
   - **2.5 [minor, fixable] (provisional—my search backends failed, so this is a coverage note, not an assertion about the literature)** Section 1 states that "a multi-perspective panel does not fix this: every judge reads the same ambiguous documentation, so the ambiguity is shared, not broken." That is a claim about a whole family of panel designs (multi-agent debate, judge ensembles), and the paper's own design is a panel with an aggregation rule. The judge-reliability citations cover self-preference, self-inconsistency and prompt sensitivity but not the panel/ensemble line, and positioning the four-perspective design against it would sharpen what the §4.3 ablation does and does not rule out.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The measurement design in §4.3 is unusually disciplined, and it is the paper's best methodological contribution. Layer 0 audits the shipped packs and finds 43.3% of 134 distinct (constraint, cited-page) pairs unsupported, 32% of constraint rows addressing an unrelated endpoint, and four packs leaking assembler provenance notes; Layer 1 rebuilds all 81 packs from version-pinned documentation and the tested version's own OpenAPI artifact (the 2,106 → 674 row accounting closes exactly); Layer 2 realigns the judging protocol after finding that the first re-adjudication still carried two pre-deployment rules that suppressed recall by construction (bare structural inference accepted as by-design refutation; "validation present" counting as non-defect evidence). Section 5 then works through four judgment-side channels, removing three of them and measuring the fourth: expectation framing is measured rather than removed (a mechanical scan of the rebuilt packs finds 19 expectation-flavored phrases, all inside verbatim server responses, and zero framing added by assembly, so no sanitization is applied); four assembler provenance notes are removed before re-adjudication; ten packs carrying an embedded maintainer-cognition section whose dispatch forbids reading cognition are cleaned, with the ten affected cases re-adjudicated in every affected arm and run (thirteen runs across both backbones); and seven candidate-anchored entries in the cognition materials themselves—referencing the pool's own issue numbers—are removed, with those seven cases likewise re-adjudicated. This is a stronger validity regime than most bug-finding papers apply to themselves.
   - **3.2** The source-withheld control is a genuinely informative intervention and the paper reads it correctly. Withholding only the source clone—every rule sentence, perspective, aggregation and cognition material intact—raises convention recall from 0.765 to 0.941 and cuts suppression from 0.700 to 0.467 (discordant 2/18, $p{=}0.0004$): the source channel is what refuses candidates, not what finds them. The per-judgment clause audit sharpens this to a specific count (the source-anchored perspective closes 19 judgments as `False-Positive`, 17 of them adjudicated false positives and 2 adjudicated true bugs), and the paper reports that two of the 19 rest on bare structural inference where its own red line requires routing—including one that closed a genuine bug. Reporting violations of one's own red line with counts is the right behavior; see 3.5 and 3.6 for where the surrounding accounting does not close.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The flagship comparison is measured against a baseline the paper knows to be defective, and the repaired baseline is never paired against the full stage. In §4.3 ("What in the protocol carries the gap"), both component arms first correct an inconsistency in the flat dispatch—its output-schema line declared a binary verdict field while its supplement mandates three-valued verdicts—so the flat judge could not reliably route, and routing is exactly what the convention counts as confirmation. With the schema line corrected the flat arm rises from 30 to 35 true positives (0.588 → 0.686), i.e. more than half of the reported 9-case recall gap; the paper reports this and even bounds it ("the schema-only arm accounts for at most $+5$ of it and the remainder is the rule's"). But the headline $p$-values in the abstract, Section 1 and §4.3 (discordant 16/2, $p{=}0.0013$, Holm-corrected 0.0052) are computed against the *unrepaired* flat arm, and the obvious robustness check—the paired test of the full stage against the schema-corrected flat arm, whose confirmed sets are 48 vs 39, net $+9$—is reported nowhere. Appendix A also asserts that because both judges share the rule content "the comparison is conservative: whatever separates the full stage from the flat judge is structure, not rules"—a claim the schema correction's $+5$ partly falsifies, since the shared rules were not in fact equally in force in both dispatches. This does not touch the mechanism finding (which rests on the flat$+$aggregation arm equalling the full stage), but it does mean the paper's most-quoted number is measured over a contrast the authors have themselves shown to be partly an artifact of their own baseline's prompt.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The control that carries the causal claim is a two-change bundle, and the causal language outruns what the design can support. Section 4.3 states that adding the aggregation rule reaches 0.765 and is "indifferent on the confirmed set" to the full stage ($p{=}0.61$)—the paper's central decompositional result, and a well-founded one. But two things are bundled: the schema correction is present in the aggregation arm too, and the paper both declares the partition "not identified" and then performs it ("the remainder is the rule's"), which requires an additivity assumption that a 2×2 design missing its fourth cell (perspectives without the aggregation rule) cannot license. Worse for the causal claim, the rule-only control is *worse* than the full stage on the axes the recall metric does not price: 12 leaks against 9, and routed-queue precision 0.600 (15/25) against 0.714 (15/21), with a routing rate 43% higher (28.4% vs 19.8%)—itself evidence that the "same" aggregation rule, re-expressed in the flat judge's vocabulary, was not the same rule operationally. The paper's careful sentences ("the organization's own increment is not established on this pool") are correct; the conclusion's unhedged "the advantage belongs to the routing rule rather than to the four perspectives", and Section 1's "adding the aggregation rule alone to the flat judge" (summary paragraph and contribution bullet 2), are not, since on suppression the point estimates run the other way and no test exists for the missing cell.
   - **3.5 [major, fixable]** The joint judge-plus-human reading is presented with more authority than its reproducibility supports. Section 4.3 replaces the convention with a hand adjudication of both arms' routed queues and obtains 0.647 recall at 0.917 precision, described as "the measured truth sits in between" the convention's 0.765 and forced scoring's 0.529. The same subsection then reports that re-running the same 25 routed cases through an independent adjudicator under three successively fuller statements of the deployed protocol gives agreement with the authors' own pass of $\kappa = -0.01$, $0.08$ and $0.11$ (on 20 cases carrying an author ruling) and 2, 0 and 4 confirmations where the authors confirm 11, for substituted recalls of 0.529, 0.529 and 0.588. Two problems follow. First, agreement of $\kappa \le 0.11$ on $n{=}20$ means the human pass is not reproducible even by a second reader given the same materials and the full rules, so calling its output "the measured truth" overstates it; the paper's own rescue ("the monotone trend says the protocol statement carries real weight") also does not hold as written for the confirmations, which run 2, 0, 4. Second, no confidence interval or standard error accompanies any $\kappa$; at that sample size the estimates are close to uninformative, and the paper should either attach uncertainty or report raw agreement counts alone. The forced reading is mechanical and survives this intact, which is why the weakness is not fatal—but one of the three headline readings in the abstract rests on a pass the paper's own evidence shows a second adjudicator cannot reproduce.
   - **3.6 [major, fixable]** The per-judgment clause audit—the evidence for the mechanistic claim—does not reconcile with the totals the same subsection reports. Section 4.3 says the audit "locates it precisely" by recording which clause of the fixed aggregation decided each of the full stage's 243 case judgments, then gives: 19 closed as `False-Positive` by the source-anchored perspective, 50 by a refuted contract assertion, 16 by maintainer cognition, 21 by a confirmed contract assertion, 61 by an objective-constraint violation, and 69 routed by the catch-all. Those six numbers sum to 236, not 243, with no stated residual. Independently, the catch-all count cannot exceed the number of `Human-Review` verdicts, which the same subsection reports as 48 of 243—yet the audit assigns 69 judgments to the catch-all. One of the two figures must be wrong, and since the paper's central claim (the separation is carried by the routing channel) is exactly what this audit is adduced to prove, the discrepancy needs resolving before the audit can carry that weight. The same accounting problem appears twice more: in the leak attribution, where the objective-constraint (18), contract-driven (8) and no-forced-driving (19) buckets cover 45 of the 48 majority confirmations with the residual unstated; and in the per-case breakdown of the 39 confirmations (23 unanimous; of the remaining 16, ten with a `Human-Review` majority), which appears to imply a forced recall of 29/51 where 27/51 is reported.
   - **3.7 [minor, fixable]** One claim is calibrated above its own audit. Contribution bullet 2 describes a confirmation stage that "grounds every verdict in evidence sources outside the LLM". By the paper's own audit, 69 of 243 case judgments (or 48, if the figure in 3.6 is the correct one) are decided by the catch-all and carried to confirmation by the routing convention—i.e. by no evidence clause at all; routing is a deferral, not a ground. The body of Section 4.3 says this correctly and repeatedly ("what the routing rule buys is not better forced guessing but honest escalation"); only the contribution sentence needs the correction.

## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets a class of bugs in vector database management systems (Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate) that produce no crash: cases where the system silently accepts an input, or returns a result, that contradicts what its API documentation prescribes. It argues (Table 1) that each standard oracle family anchors its expectation somewhere other than the natural-language prose — crash signals, another implementation, output-output relations, machine-checkable properties, structured schemas, or method-level tagged prose — leaving an LLM-derived oracle as the practical option for the residual, and that putting an LLM in the oracle role imports a false-positive problem. TestVDB is a four-stage pipeline (documentation distillation into source-verified specifications, attack-agent test generation under pre-bound strategies, sandboxed execution with raw HTTP logging, and bug confirmation). Its confirmation stage splits evidence assembly from cross-examination: an evidence builder assembles a five-section evidence chain and a chain auditor applies four mechanical checks plus four perspectives (contract, objective/physical constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition), grounded in the implementation source, returning a three-valued verdict that may route a case to human review instead of forcing a closure.

The evaluation has two parts. RQ1 (Section 4.2) reports a cumulative ledger of 81 maintainer-adjudicated submissions, of which 51 were confirmed as real bugs and 23 fixed by merged PRs, accumulated over sixteen per-version mining campaigns. RQ2 (Section 4.3) audits and rebuilds the 81 frozen evidence packages that fed the confirmation stage (finding 43.3% of cited constraint-page pairs unsupported by the cited pages), then re-adjudicates the whole pool three times per configuration across eight judge configurations. The contract-core arm reaches recall 0.157 at suppression 0.967; the full structured stage 0.765 at 0.700; a flat single-prompt judge reading the same packs and sources 0.588 at 0.867. Withholding the implementation source from the full stage raises recall to 0.941 and cuts suppression to 0.467; and a flat judge given only the full stage's aggregation rule recovers the full stage's recall exactly (0.765), so the four-perspective organization's own increment is reported as not established on this pool. RQ3 (Section 4.4) probes the two oracles' reach bidirectionally against the VDBFuzz crash-oracle fuzzer on Qdrant, first showing that the released runner's own success counters are uninformative, then measuring a completed template sweep with a substituted liveness oracle.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The paper audits, rebuilds, and re-adjudicates its own judge inputs rather than treating them as given, and discloses in both directions — including the payload-shape boundary that weakens four of its own observations — see 3.1, 3.2
- **S2:** Component controls that are allowed to return a negative result for the paper's own architecture (the aggregation-only arm matches the flagship exactly; the second backbone does not separate) — see 3.3, 3.4
- **S3:** Statistical reporting is unusually disciplined: three independent runs per configuration, majority reduction, exact McNemar p-values with an explicitly constructed correction family, Holm multipliers shown per test, and Wilson intervals — the reported p-values and intervals that I re-derived from the reported counts all reproduce — see 3.4, 4.1
- **S4:** RQ3 does not trust the baseline's own summary counters; it demonstrates they cannot fire, substitutes a per-template oracle, and bounds the zero with a measured reach argument from both directions — see 4.2
- **S5:** The declared artifact is enumerated in enough detail (packs, per-case judge outputs for every arm and run, ledger, prompts) that the measurement is in principle checkable outside the text — see 4.1

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The hand-adjudicated joint reading (0.647, advertised in the abstract and the contribution list) is inconsistent with the paper's own "pack materials only, without consulting the issue trackers" rule for the three group-by routed cases, and 33 − 3 = 30/51 = 0.588 is exactly the figure the paper reports from the independent adjudicator and treats as a strict lower bound — see 3.5
- **W2:** The per-judgment clause audit, which is what "locates [the source channel] precisely," does not close: the six counts sum to 236, not the 243 judgments being decomposed, and the catch-all count (69) contradicts the 48 routed verdicts reported in the same subsection (69/243 = 28.4% is the flat+aggregation control's routing rate) — see 3.6
- **W3:** The source-withheld control is reported but never scored on the net/F1 metrics the paper uses to claim a lead; on those metrics it outranks the flagship configuration, and under the paper's own stated error-pricing that control is cheaper — see 3.7
- **W4:** Several interlocking counts cannot be reconstructed or reconciled from the text (the "0.593" submitted-pool figure, the routed-queue reconciliation, the anchor protocol's self-contradictory parenthetical) — see 3.10, 3.11, 4.4, 5.4

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The yield claim is concrete and externally adjudicated. Table 2 reports 81 maintainer-adjudicated submissions: 51 confirmed (Milvus 29/43, Qdrant 14/28, Weaviate 8/10), 23 fixed by merged PRs. The paper then defends the count against the obvious inflation objection by classifying every merged fix PR by its changed files (all 23 modify implementation code, 15 add regression tests, none is documentation-only) and by running the classifier on the one candidate the authors themselves later disproved, where it correctly returns "test-only" and matches their seven-version re-probe (Section 4.2, "Fix nature"). Getting 51 logic bugs acknowledged and 23 repaired in three production systems is clear practical impact, and the self-check on the classifier is the kind of evidence that makes the number usable by others.
   - **1.2** The problem statement is careful about scope. Section 2.2 explicitly separates consistency (what the system accepts/errors on/returns, and how state evolves) from correctness (ANN recall, ranking), states that result correctness "remains open and is not our claim," and returns to that boundary in Section 4.2 when it reports the in-class recall (38/46) alongside the headline (39/51). This keeps the significance claim inside what was measured rather than inflating it across the whole class of "wrong results."
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** Two later restatements of the ledger drop the scope qualifier the abstract carries. The abstract is careful — "in an operational human--machine deployment with author-side screening and submission, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs and fixed 23 via merged PRs," followed by the note that the ledger "predates the run reported here, which contributed none of the 81" — and the introduction's results paragraph adds that the ledger "measures the human--machine pipeline rather than the automated stage alone" (Section 4.2 likewise discloses that "submission decisions were made per candidate by the authors after novelty screening and manual review (no deterministic submission rule), so the 81-candidate pool is a submission-filtered subset of the pipeline's confirmed output rather than a random sample of it"). Contribution 3 in Section 1, however, announces "51 maintainer-confirmed real bugs (23 fixed) across three production VDBMSs" with no scope, and the conclusion repeats "maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions and fixed 23" bare. The quantity is genuinely bounded: the only evidence about what the automated stage would find unaided is the 32-candidate anchor from one Qdrant run, whose readings (0.594 under the forced A/B/D protocol, 0.969 under the deployed one) the paper correctly refuses to call recall because those candidates were never submitted, so a reader of the contribution list alone cannot tell what fraction of the 51 would exist without the author-side screening and submission decisions. Carry the abstract's qualifier into the contribution bullet and the conclusion.
   - **1.4 [minor, fixable]** The measured mechanism is narrower than the paper's framing. The title and abstract lead with "LLM-Derived Behavioral Specifications," but the controlled comparison in Section 4.3 attributes the entire measurable separation to the confirmation stage's aggregation rule ("adding the aggregation rule alone ... recovers the full stage's recall exactly"), and the abstract itself concedes that the four-perspective organization's increment is not established. If the durable contribution is a confirmation-stage routing rule rather than an extraction method, the framing should follow the evidence rather than the pipeline's first stage.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate — *provisional: I did not survey the field; every statement below about prior work is the paper's own characterization of works I did not fetch.*
   - **2.1** *(provisional)* From the paper's own positioning, the novelty delta is stated clearly and is not trivial: Sections 2.3 and 6 argue an "anchor inversion" in which the documentation prose supplies the expectation and the implementation is admitted "only to falsify the claim already read from the prose." The paper names the works it is distinguishing itself from and gives each a specific verdict as it characterizes it: AGORA+ anchors in execution traces, SATORI in OpenAPI fields, MASTOR in source (with the explicit caveat that a source-anchored oracle "cannot report a documentation-code gap as a defect"), CASCADE's falsifier is another LLM artifact sharing the documentation's ambiguity, and LogicHunter's agentic oracle "freely consults and executes the implementation before returning a verdict; ours does not let the implementation arbitrate at all." I did not fetch any of these works, so I cannot check the characterizations; taken at face value the delta is real and the paper is unusually willing to name what is *not* new.
   - **2.2** *(provisional)* **[minor, fixable]** Contribution 1 claims "We formalize documentation-implementation consistency testing for VDBMSs," but Section 2.2 delivers a definitional scope statement (consistency vs. correctness; the observable surface: accepts, errors, returns, state evolves) with no formal object, no definition of the consistency relation over that surface, and no property or theorem. "Define" would be accurate and would not weaken the contribution. Reading the two sections together, the durable novelty is empirical (a maintained, maintainer-adjudicated corpus plus a controlled, audited ablation) more than mechanical: the paper itself notes that "a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is therefore not new here, and cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to TestVDB" (Section 6).

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The audit-and-rebuild of the evidence packs (Section 4.3, "Measurement design") is the strongest methodological move in the paper. A mechanical assembly pass plus per-pair verification found 43.3% of the 134 distinct (constraint, cited-page) pairs unsupported, two over-strong pairs, and 32% of constraint rows addressing the wrong endpoint; all traced to the assembler, not the confirmation stage. The paper then rebuilt all 81 packs (674 surviving rows out of 2,106, of which only 22 are verbatim-explicit), removed four provenance leaks, and — after finding three further contamination channels — re-adjudicated the affected cases in every affected arm and run (thirteen runs with the embedded cognition section; seven cases for the candidate-anchored cognition entries) and replaced the verdicts. Papers rarely re-open their own judge inputs this way; that the paper reports the number its own rebuild reduced (652 of 674 surviving rows are behavioral inference) is what makes the contract-core result legible rather than embarrassing.
   - **3.2** The payload-shape disclosure is a strength of the same kind. Section 4.3 reports that four candidates reproduce the filed report's parameter shape rather than the shape the API consumes (three with grouping knobs inside a `groupParams` object that is not a field of the REST v2 request; one with a top-level `shardsNum`), so "the recorded observation shows that drop rather than the validation gap the report describes." The paper then says which of them survive on what basis (the three group-by cases rest on the maintainers' corrected-payload reproduction and merged fix, not on our observations; the `shardsNum` case is the counted leak) and re-tests the four on clean packs in a dedicated paragraph. This is a case where the paper voluntarily downgrades its own evidence.
   - **3.3** The comparison design isolates structure from rules rather than bundling them silently. Appendix A states that each judge receives the same per-case bundle and may read the same source clone, that the discipline clauses are identical, and that the deliberately different parts are the plus-arm's output-schema correction and the aggregation rule; the flat-judge prompt retains the protocol's red lines (verbatim intent for by-design refutation; objective-constraint acceptance) with four of the seven classes enumerated. Appendix A additionally reports the effect of the narrower enumeration (the flat judge "reaches majority confirmation on 13 of the 16 objective-constraint-driven confirmations the full stage makes"; of the three it misses, two are driven by a class the prompt does not enumerate and one is a numeric-lower-bound case it does — "an execution difference on those cases, not a rule-set difference"), so the reader can see that shared rules are not the difference. Section 4.3 reports the forced-verdict collapse (0.529 vs. 0.490, p = 0.34), the full-vs-source-only indistinguishability (p = 0.39, with the underpowering named), the second-family non-replication for the full-vs-flat pair (p = 0.58) explicitly framed as "a non-detection rather than as evidence that the effect is absent there," and the honest admission that the flagship survives Holm "on the confirmed-set basis ... rather than on the recall-only comparison alone" (0.0225 raw, 0.090 adjusted). Section 4.3 states that the authors adjudicated both arms' routed cases "against their pack materials only, without consulting the issue trackers, playing the channel's designated human role (non-blind; disclosed)," and that this yields a joint judge-plus-human recall of 33/51 (0.647). Section 5 then explains three of those cases the other way: "On three of the group-by candidates the first-pass judge found the premise itself unbound ... and routed on that ground rather than confirming or refuting; the human pass overrode the routing and confirmed, resting on the maintainer's own corrected-payload reproduction and the interface asymmetry it exposed, and the merged fix PR bears that out." The maintainers' corrected-payload reproduction is tracker knowledge, and Section 4.3 itself says the three group-by confirmations "rest on that reproduction and fix rather than on our own observations." If those three adjudications are removed as outside the pack materials, the joint reading falls to 30/51 = 0.588 — exactly the figure the paper reports from the independent adjudicator and presents as the strict bound ("an independent adjudicator reading the full deployed rules and the same materials reaches 0.588, not 0.647"). Section 4.3 ("What the implementation source is for") reports that removing the source clone raises convention recall to 0.941 (48/51) and cuts suppression to 0.467, a swing of p = 0.0004. Applying the same metrics to the source-withheld control: net TP−FP = 48 − 16 = 32 (above the flagship's 30), F1 = 0.835 (above the flagship's 0.788), J = 0.408 (below). This connects to the paper's stated error pricing in Section 5 — "a false positive costs a maintainer one triage pass, whereas a suppressed true positive is a defect that stays in the shipped system, and for a bug-finding deployment the two are not symmetric" — under which a channel that sacrifices nine true positives to refuse seven false positives is a net loss. The paper's answer is presumably that the source's role is epistemic (breaking the extraction-judgment self-reference) rather than rate-based, and it does say "the source is not the mechanism that finds the bugs; it is the mechanism that refuses them"; but the evaluation as printed invites the opposite reading and never confronts it. No submitted-pool quantity in the paper equals 0.593; the deployed-protocol reading of the submitted pool is 0.765 (Table 6 / Section 4.3), and the nearest published figures are the flat judge's 0.588 and the anchor's own 0.594. ### Meta Recommendation
**ACCEPT**

All three recommendations land on Weak Accept, so the unanimous shortcut decides outright; the consensus-tier count reaches the same verdict independently, there being no consensus Poor and no consensus Weak. What is notable is not the verdict but the profile: every criterion sits at Adequate unanimously, with no Excellent anywhere. That is a uniform step down from the previous round, and the reviewers' reasons converge rather than scatter — none of them disputes the yield (51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 merged fixes) or the measurement discipline, and all three independently recomputed the statistics and found the tables, intervals, McNemar values and Holm multipliers reproducing exactly. The downgrade is about **what the paper can still claim over those measurements**. Three reviewers working separately reached the same conclusion: after the component controls, the flagship comparison survives only under a counting convention the paper inherits rather than chooses, and the surviving contribution has not been restated to match. R1 puts it as "the surviving claim is never stated plainly"; R2 as "the contribution statement should lead with what survives"; R3 as the impact belonging to the human–machine pipeline rather than to the system. That is a fixable writing problem, and it is the single highest-value revision in this round. Compounding it, two reviewers independently reproduced the same accounting failure in the paragraph the paper offers as locating its mechanism "precisely", and one reviewer verified by HTTP that the declared replication package does not yet contain the analysis scripts or the per-judgment outputs for the five arms that carry the decomposition — a declaration the paper makes in its own Data Availability section and that is currently untrue of the artifact. None of this is a soundness collapse: the numbers that survive recomputation are unusually clean, and two of the three reviewers explicitly recorded that as a strength. But a paper whose centre of gravity has moved twice under its own controls has to be re-centred on the page, and this round is the first to say so unanimously.

### Priority Revisions
Ranked by impact on the verdict; the first three are the ones more than one reviewer reached independently.

1. **State the surviving claim in one place, and re-centre the abstract and contribution list on it.** R1 rates this `[major, fixable]` at 1.2 and 3.2; R2 at 1.2 and 1.3; R3 at 1.4.
