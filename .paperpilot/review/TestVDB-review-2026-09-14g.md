## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets *documentation-implementation bugs* in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs): cases where Milvus, Qdrant, or Weaviate silently accepts an input or produces a behaviour that contradicts its own natural-language API documentation (the paper's canonical instance of the class is Milvus accepting `nprobe=0` while documenting the range as `[1, 16384]`; the bug it threads through all four stages as a running example is Qdrant \#10369, a `lookup_from` vector-dimension bypass). The authors argue structurally, via a seven-row table of candidate oracle families (Table 1), that no deterministic-oracle family derives its expectation from system-level untagged prose, so an LLM-derived oracle is the practical option for this class — and that putting an LLM there imports a false-positive problem. Their answer is TestVDB, a four-stage multi-agent pipeline: behavioural specification extraction (crawl + distill documentation into typed, source-verified constraints), pre-bound strategy-based test generation, Docker-pinned sandboxed execution with raw HTTP logging, and a bug-confirmation stage that splits an evidence builder (five-section chain, including implementation-source grounding) from a chain auditor that cross-examines it under four perspectives (contract, objective physical constraints, behavioural elegance, maintainer cognition) and returns Confirmed / False-Positive / Human-Review.

Empirically the paper reports two layers. RQ1 is an operational ledger: 51 of 81 maintainer-adjudicated submissions confirmed as real bugs, 30 adjudicated false positives, 23 fixed via merged PRs, accumulated over sixteen per-version mining campaigns with author-side screening and the submission decision in the loop. RQ2 re-adjudicates that 81-candidate pool from scratch on rebuilt, version-pinned evidence packs under five judge configurations, three runs each, with three scoring conventions reported side by side: the contract-only core recalls 0.157 at 0.967 suppression; the full structured stage reaches 0.765 at 0.700 under the deployment's counting convention (Human-Review counted as confirmed), 0.647 under a hand-adjudicated joint reading, and 0.529 under forced verdicts; a flat single-prompt judge reading the same packs and sources reaches 0.588. The paper attributes the full-vs-flat separation to the routing channel rather than to forced-verdict accuracy, reports that the pair does not separate on a second backbone, and reports that an independent adjudicator of the routed queue agrees with the authors' hand pass only slightly beyond chance. RQ3 runs VDBFuzz's released Qdrant template set to completion on the same instance (zero anomalies) and, in the reverse direction, hand-executes a boundary probe that reaches VDBFuzz's integer-overflow crash on v1.4.0.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The pack audit-and-rebuild is the methodological standout of the paper: a mechanical audit of the stage's own shipped evidence packs found 43.3% of cited (constraint, page) pairs unsupported, and the paper rebuilt all 81 packs, found and removed three further contamination channels, re-executed thirteen affected runs, and re-adjudicated everything — with arithmetic that reconciles exactly on the page. — see 3.1, 4.2
- **S2:** The negative results are reported rather than buried, though not all in the same places: the recall-only Holm non-survival (adjusted 0.090) reaches the abstract, the contribution list, and RQ2; the second-backbone non-replication ($p{=}0.58$) and the independent adjudicator's 0.588 reach the abstract and RQ2; the joint reading's non-significance ($p{=}0.11$) appears only in RQ2 and the caption of the closing results table; and the independent adjudicator's $\kappa$ values ($-0.01$, 0.08, 0.11) appear only in RQ2 — so the paper's sharpest disclosures are made in RQ2, with some but not all of them carried into the abstract. — see 3.2, 3.3
- **S3:** The RQ1 yield is concrete and verified: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs across three production systems, 23 with merged fix PRs, a fix-nature classifier whose output is checked against the paper's own retracted candidate (\#9149, correctly identified as test-only). — see 1.1
- **S4:** I verified the novelty delta against the five named competitors — MASTOR, Metamon, CASCADE, LogicHunter, and VDBFuzz — reading the shared cache's structured summary for each and escalating to its cached full text where a specific claim needed more, and found no material mischaracterization of any of them; several concessions (that a second-LLM oracle review and cross-request behavioural oracles already exist) are accurate and costly to the paper's own framing. — see 2.1, 2.2, 2.5
- **S5:** RQ3 is the best-executed baseline comparison in the paper, including falsification of the baseline's own harness: the released VDBFuzz runner reports exit code 0 and a 100% success rate against a port with no server, so the authors substitute a liveness oracle and report 22,540 mutations over 23,258 HTTP responses with the status distribution. — see 3.6

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The paper's central architectural claim — that the implementation source is the falsification anchor that breaks the extraction–judgment self-reference — is never isolated by an ablation, and the paper's own per-perspective attribution assigns source evidence *zero* of the full stage's 39 confirmations; the arms that exist (core = pack only; source-only = source forensics alone; flat = pack + source) do not separate the source's marginal contribution. — see 2.3, 3.4
- **W2:** Table 1's necessity argument omits a candidate the paper itself measures: a documentation-free catalogue of seven objective constraint classes (perspective B) drives 16 of 39 confirmations at the highest precision of the three forced-driving perspectives (0.889), and the paper's defence that B "supplies only a falsifier … and no expectation about which behaviors to exercise" is asserted rather than measured. — see 2.4
- **W3:** The flagship full-vs-flat significance result ($p{=}0.0052$) holds only under the counting convention that treats a routed case as confirmed; the full stage routes 19.8% of verdicts against the flat judge's 8.6%, so the confirmed-set unit rewards escalation propensity. Under both readings that remove the convention the difference is non-significant (joint 0.647 vs. 0.569, $p{=}0.11$) or collapses (forced 0.529 vs. 0.490, $p{=}0.34$). — see 3.2, 3.3
- **W4:** "Structure" bundles two things the experiment does not separate: the four-perspective organization (the paper's named contribution) and the mandatory route-when-unsettled aggregation rule. The paper's own decomposition credits the routing rule, so the ablation as run cannot show that the perspectives carry any part of the gap — and because the metric counts escalation as success, the conclusion is close to definitional. — see 3.5
- **W5:** The ledger's unit and the paper's named bug class are not coextensive: 14 of the 51 confirmed bugs sit in packs carrying no surviving contract row that covers their endpoint (the evidence-absent and weak-evidence strata, 11 and 3 respectively), and the claim that documentation-implementation inconsistency is a "prevalent subset" of the non-crashing majority is not established by the cited empirical studies, whose functional-failure category is dominated by result-correctness shapes the paper explicitly excludes. — see 1.3, 3.8

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The RQ1 ledger (Table 2, §RQ1) reports 81 adjudicated submissions across Milvus (43), Qdrant (28) and Weaviate (10), with 51 confirmed and 23 fixed via merged PRs; every submission's state was re-verified as of September 2026, per-case status and duplicate flags ship in the artifact, and the fix column excludes the three duplicate-tracked cases. The fix-nature classification ("every one of the 23 modifies implementation code, 15 also add regression tests, none is documentation-only") is exactly the check a sceptical reader wants on a bug-count headline, and validating the classifier against the paper's own retracted \#9149 (test-only PR, correctly flagged) is a strong move. Concrete, actionable impact on real infrastructure.
   - **1.2** The oracle-exclusion analysis (Table 1) plus RQ3 converts "existing oracles miss this class" from an assertion into a delimitation with a mechanism per row, and the paper is explicit that only rows 1 and 7 are exercised directly while rows 2–6 are argued from each family's anchoring mechanism. Together with the bidirectional VDBFuzz probe this gives the area a usable statement of where the boundary lies.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** §Background motivates the target class as "a prevalent subset of this silent majority", citing the bug study and the roadmap. From the cached summary of the bug study (`toward understanding bugs in vector database management systems (arxiv preprint 2025)`): functional failure 57.3%, crash 15.1%, performance degradation 7.3% — the paper's characterization of those two sources is accurate. But the 57.3% class is populated by inaccurate query results, missing matches, and inconsistent indexing, i.e. the result-correctness shapes the paper explicitly disclaims ("result correctness of vector search remains open and is not our claim"). The cited studies therefore do not establish the prevalence of the documentation-implementation subset; the only supporting evidence is the authors' own ledger, which is itself selected by the pipeline's target class and by author screening. Softening the prevalence claim, or citing the ledger as its evidence with that selection stated, would fix it. The impact itself does not depend on the prevalence claim — see 1.1.
   - **1.4** The cost and scale profile (§4.1: $\approx$67 active hours over five calendar days, $\approx$860 sandboxed executions, $\approx$70M input / 4.9M output tokens for the orchestrating session alone, flagged as a lower bound) is the kind of operational reporting this area usually lacks and makes the pipeline's real bottleneck ("source grounding and live re-probes rather than raw LLM latency") legible to a practitioner audience.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** I checked the anchor-inversion claim against the shared cache's entry for MASTOR (`mastor a multi agent approach to semantic test oracle generation for restful apis (corr 2026)`), the paper's own "closest to this work". MASTOR uses the OpenAPI spec as the authoritative endpoint list, places OAS-declared items not substantiated in source into `pending` fields, and lets oracle agents read source-verified fields only; its stated limitation is that it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code". The paper's characterization — "it records a doc-versus-source discrepancy signal in its `pending` fields, but never turns that signal into an oracle" — is exactly what MASTOR does, and the inversion TestVDB claims (prose as ground, implementation only as falsifier) is a real, non-obvious delta that MASTOR's own limitation forecloses. Claim holds.
   - **2.2** Checked Metamon, CASCADE, LogicHunter, and VDBFuzz the same way. Metamon's judge is the same LLM that TestVDB names as the self-reference; its reported profile is quoted verbatim-correctly (precision 0.722 at recall 0.480 on 9,482 pairs — the paper prints the pair count only) and correctly labelled "not a head-to-head baseline"; two details I add from Metamon's own text, not the paper's, are that the judge is stabilised by self-consistency over metamorphic prompt variants and that the 9,482 pairs are oracles produced by injecting first-order mutants into a fixed-version Java project. CASCADE's residual-false-positive quote ("the generated code and tests made the same false assumption over something underspecified in the documentation") matches CASCADE's own manual analysis in substance, and CASCADE's falsifier is indeed another LLM artifact derived from the same documentation. LogicHunter's 30 confirmed / 26 fixed and its oracle tooling (reflective `code_search`, sandboxed `run_code`) are accurate, and the identity claim — that TestVDB does not let the implementation arbitrate, whereas LogicHunter's ReAct oracle freely consults and executes it — holds against TestVDB's deployed aggregation, where source evidence can only refute (perspective C). VDBFuzz's three-stage structure, its API-sequence stage's stated aim ("logical inconsistencies or invalid state transitions"), its 120-minute-per-tool protocol, and its 205 released Qdrant templates all check out; the one narrow divergence is that "crash-oracle by design" is a simplification of its own reported results, which are 13 crash vulnerabilities plus 6 runtime exceptions (see 3.6). No material mischaracterization found in any of the five.
   - **2.3 [major, fixable]** The delta is thinner than the contribution list implies. §Related Work concedes — correctly — that "a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is therefore not new here, and cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to TestVDB"; against MASTOR that leaves the architecture largely paralleled (multi-agent pipeline, per-oracle reviewer agent with targeted regeneration, cross-operation behavioural oracles). What remains distinct is (a) the anchor inversion and (b) the routing-on-unresolvable aggregation. The latter is a design choice none of the closest competitors is barred from adopting, and the paper's own decomposition makes it the carrier of the reported advantage (§RQ2). The originality is real and recognizable, but it is a repositioning plus one rule, not a new technique — and the repositioning's value is measured on one backbone only.
   - **2.4 [major, fixable]** The "LLM is forced" delimitation — the paper's contribution 1 — is argued by elimination in Table 1 over crash, differential, metamorphic, property-based, structured-source, and doc-derived oracles, but the table has no row for the oracle the paper itself measures highest on precision: perspective B's catalogue of seven objective constraint classes, which "constitute violations *without contract endorsement*" and which the paper's own attribution credits with 16 of 39 confirmations (§RQ2). The introduction does address this ("It is not an independent oracle, because it supplies only a falsifier for what a system must not do … and no expectation about which behaviors to exercise"), but the premise that those probes cannot be generated without first reading prose is asserted, not measured — and §RQ3 shows a boundary probe reaching a crash from the class, not from a contract row. If a contract-free objective-constraint fuzzer reaches a large share of the observed confirmations, the residual the LLM is "forced" to cover is narrower than the table reads. Fixable by adding the family as a row with the same anchoring argument applied honestly to it, or by an arm.
   - **2.5** Related-Work coverage is complete on every axis I could search: two metadata-only surveys and four scoped scripted queries plus two scoped web searches over VDBMS testing (2025–2026) and LLM-oracle-generation-from-documentation surfaced no uncited highly related work; the only VDBMS-testing papers in the area (VDBFuzz, the roadmap) are cited, and the DBMS-oracle, REST-API-oracle, comment-code-inconsistency, and judge-reliability lines are densely covered (65 cited works, with no citation in the text missing from the bibliography and none unused). No missing-related-work finding.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The measurement design (§RQ2) is the strongest part of the paper and I could not fault its arithmetic. The pack rebuild reconciles exactly on the page: 2,106 assembled rows $-$ 674 endpoint-filtering $-$ 463 evidence-verification $=$ 969 survivors; 969 $-$ 302 archaeology rows $=$ 667, plus 7 retained single-assertion rows $=$ 674; 22 `explicit` + 652 inferred $=$ 674. Two further contamination channels found after re-adjudication (ten packs carrying embedded cognition sections; seven candidate-anchored cognition entries) were removed, the thirteen affected runs re-executed, and the pre-cleanup verdict files shipped. I have rarely seen a paper this willing to invalidate and re-run its own headline measurement, and reporting the pre-cleanup files alongside is the right call.
   - **3.2** The statistics are disciplined and I verified them: arm matrices each sum to 81; 39/51 = 0.765, 21/30 = 0.700, 39/48 = 0.812, $F_1$ 0.788 vs. 0.706 ($2{\times}39/(2{\times}39{+}9{+}12)$ and $2{\times}30/(2{\times}30{+}4{+}21)$), Youden $J$ 0.465 vs. 0.455 ($0.765{+}0.700{-}1$ and $0.588{+}0.867{-}1$); the Holm multipliers track the ranks within a family of seven (rank 4 $\rightarrow$ $\times$4 giving $0.0013{\times}4{=}0.0052$; rank 5 $\rightarrow$ $\times$3 giving $0.0075{\times}3{=}0.023$) and the maximal ten-test sensitivity (0.0092) is reported. Pre-declaring the family by construction, publishing the discordant nets (16/2, 0/25, 8/4), and reporting that the recall-only test does *not* survive (adjusted 0.090) is exemplary.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The full-vs-flat separation is significant only in the reading that counts a routed case as confirmed, and the full stage routes 19.8% of verdicts against the flat judge's 8.6%, so the confirmed-set unit prices escalation propensity as accuracy. The paper states each component — "the convention overstates the joint system, forced scoring understates it" — and labels the convention as the deployment's operating point, which is a defensible operational definition ("a routed case goes upstream unless a reviewer finds it egregiously wrong"). But it never states the joint inference, and its own independent-adjudicator result undercuts it: an adjudicator given the frozen packs, the pinned clones and the full deployed rules reaches 0.588 and agrees with the authors' hand pass at $\kappa = -0.01/0.08/0.11$ — i.e. the routed queue's confirmability is itself contested at close to chance, so the convention's confirmation credit is an assumption, not a measurement. The abstract leads with 0.765-vs-0.588 and the significance test rides on that unit; re-anchoring the headline on the joint reading (or reporting the primary statistic on the forced set and the convention as the deployment's sensitivity) would remove the problem.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** §Approach states that the confirmation stage's "falsification power … comes from grounding the falsification in the implementation source", and contribution 2 says the stage "grounds every verdict in evidence sources outside the LLM---the implementation source above all". The paper's own per-perspective attribution contradicts the "above all": of the full stage's 39 majority confirmations, 7 are contract-driven, 16 objective-constraint-driven, 2 cognition-driven, and 14 carry no forced-driving perspective; source evidence drives none. Nor does any arm remove the source from the full stage — the five configurations vary what the judge sees and which rules it applies (contract core = pack only; source-only = pack plus source; flat = pack plus source in a single prompt; full = pack plus source plus maintainer cognition) but none holds everything else fixed while dropping the clone — and §RQ2 records that the Layer-2 alignment change removed two pre-deployment rules, both source-side ones under which a source finding could defeat a real defect: by-design refutation on bare structural inference ("no validation is present") without verbatim intent evidence, and "validation present" counting as non-defect evidence even where no contract row covered the observed face. The source's marginal contribution to the measured result is therefore unquantified, and full-vs-source-only is indistinguishable ($p{=}0.39$) — a comparison the paper calls underpowered, which may well be true, but it is the only evidence bearing on the source's role.
   - **3.5 [major, fixable]** Appendix A.2 states that the flat judge keeps the protocol's judgment red lines and loses "the four-perspective organization and its routing aggregation", and §RQ2 concludes that the routing channel carries the separation. The experiment therefore cannot separate the paper's named contribution (the four perspectives) from the escalation rule; and since the scoring convention counts escalation as confirmation, "the structure pays" is close to a restatement of "the arm with a mandatory routing rule routes more". A flat-judge arm with the same mandatory route-when-unsettled instruction, or a full-stage arm with routing disallowed, would isolate the two; without it the causal claim in contribution 2 is not established.
   - **3.6** RQ3 is unusually careful for a baseline comparison. The template-mutation stage is run to completion under the baseline's own published protocol with the time limit removed (50.8 minutes against a 120-minute allowance, 205 templates, 22,540 mutations, 23,258 responses with a status breakdown); the paper first falsifies the released harness's own success counters (exit code 0 and a reported 100% success rate against a port with no server) and substitutes a liveness oracle; the scope is stated precisely (the API-sequence stage was not run; a crash oracle cannot fire on a 2xx silent accept regardless of mutation strategy; the reverse-direction probe was hand-executed with the value chosen from VDBFuzz's own case study, and the paper does not claim the automated strategy would have generated it). Both bounds are correctly attributed to the released configuration rather than to crash oracles as a class.
   - **3.7 [minor, fixable]** RQ1 and RQ2 measure different protocol generations: the RQ2 headline describes a configuration aligned *after* the 81-candidate pool was produced, and the deployment's own confirmation rate on that same pool is 48/81 = 0.593, against the re-adjudicated 0.765. The paper says the two layers are not complements, but it never reports the deployed configuration's suppression on the same pool, and no arm isolates the Layer-2 rule change (bare structural by-design refutation; "validation present" as non-defect evidence) whose removal the paper says restored recall "by construction". Since that change moves the headline in the favourable direction, an arm carrying the pre-alignment rules on the rebuilt packs is the ablation a sceptical reader will ask for.
   - **3.8 [minor, fixable]** Class boundaries are looser than the label: the stratification (§RQ2, "Where recall lives") puts 37 of the 51 true bugs in evidence-present packs, 3 in weak-evidence packs, and 11 in evidence-absent packs (the latter two strata making up the paper's "14 of the 51" that carry no surviving contract row covering their endpoint) — i.e. for more than a quarter of the ledger there is no documentation covering the observed face, so those cases are objective-robustness or routing outcomes rather than documentation violations. Table 3's eight patterns do not distinguish which are grounded in a documented row and which are not, so a reader cannot recover the class composition of the headline yield. Annotating the pattern table by grounding source (documented row / objective class / routing) would close this.
   - **3.9 [minor, fixable]** Intervals are reported per arm (Wilson) but never on the paired difference; the discordant counts are given (16/2 for the flagship, 8/5 on the second family), so a difference interval or an exact CI on the paired risk difference would be a small addition that makes the headline comparison readable at a glance.

- **Q5:** How many of the 51 confirmed bugs would a documentation-free tester reach by probing the seven objective constraint classes generically — and, of those 16 objective-constraint-driven confirmations, how many had a probe whose target parameter was selected by reading prose rather than by the class catalogue? — [intended effect: this is the boundary of contribution 1; a measured answer either narrows Table 1 (lowering 2.4 to a presentational fix) or closes it, and would let 2.4 be judged on evidence rather than on the paper's assertion.]

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems: cases where the system silently accepts an input or returns a result that contradicts its natural-language API documentation (the running example is a `nprobe=0` search that Milvus documents as out of range, returns HTTP 200, and answers anyway). It argues structurally — by asking where each oracle family gets its expectation — that crash signals, differential testing, metamorphic and property-based testing, structured specs, and existing prose-derived constraint inference all anchor outside system-level untagged prose, leaving an LLM as the practical oracle, and therefore importing the LLM's false-positive problem. TestVDB is a four-stage pipeline (specification extraction with source verification, pre-bound strategy generation with attack agents, Docker-pinned sandboxed execution, and a confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly from cross-examination) in which the implementation source is used as a falsification anchor rather than as the authority for what correct behavior is.

The evaluation has three parts. RQ1 reports an operational ledger of 81 adjudicated submissions across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate — 51 maintainer-confirmed and 23 fixed by merged PRs — accumulated over sixteen per-version campaigns. RQ2 audits and rebuilds the frozen evidence packs that fed the confirmation stage, then re-adjudicates the 51-true-bug / 30-false-positive pool three times per configuration across five judge arms; it reports the contract core (0.157 recall at 0.967 suppression), the full structured stage (0.765/0.700), a flat single-prompt judge over the same packs and sources (0.588/0.867), a source-only arm, and a second-backbone replication. The three scoring conventions — forced verdicts, joint judge-plus-human, and the deployment convention that counts a routed Human-Review verdict as a confirmation — are applied to the full stage and to the flat judge; the contract core and the source-only arm are scored under the deployment convention alone. RQ3 runs VDBFuzz's released Qdrant template set to completion against the same version, records a zero across 22,540 mutations, and shows the zero is measured rather than an artifact of a dead harness.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The paper audits its own evidence pipeline and rebuilds it rather than defending the shipped one: it found 43.3% of the (constraint, cited page) pairs in its own frozen packs unsupported by the pages they cite, traced every defect to the pack assembler, rebuilt all 81 packs against version-pinned documentation, removed leaked provenance notes and candidate-anchored cognition entries, and re-ran every affected arm — see 3.1.
- **S2:** Statistical execution is unusually clean and every rate recomputes from the paper's own tables; it states its Holm family, reports raw and adjusted values, and gives both sides of trade-offs it could have hidden (the contract core's near-perfect suppression at 0.157 recall; the second backbone's failure to separate) — see 3.2, 4.2.
- **S3:** The novelty claims I checked against the competitors the paper names hold up: MASTOR's authority really is the implementation (its own limitations section says it cannot detect requirements not reflected in code), SATORI's oracles really are response-field properties, Metamon's falsifier really is the same LLM judge, and CASCADE's falsifier really is code generated from the same documentation — see 2.1, 2.2, 2.3.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The central full-stage-vs-flat-judge comparison is a comparison of routing *volume*, not of judgment quality: both arms route at essentially the same precision (full: 15 true bugs of 21 routed; flat: 8 of 11), the full stage simply routes about twice as often, and the reported convention prices each routed case as a confirmation — see 3.3.
- **W2:** The paper's own decomposition attributes the separation to the routing channel, yet the judging-prompt appendix asserts the comparison "isolates" structure from rules on the grounds that the recall-affecting rules are shared — the routing aggregation rule is precisely not shared between the two prompts — see 3.4.
- **W3:** The flagship separation is a single-backbone result that does not replicate on the second family (0.588 vs 0.529 over three runs, p=0.58), and the paper's own mechanism story (routing rate falling from 19.8% to 10.3% across backbones) makes a prompt-compliance explanation live — see 3.5.
- **W4:** Verified against the paper's own named competitors, the "anchor inversion" delta is real but thin: LogicHunter already anchors intent in retrieved documentation and merely *also* consults source, and the paper's source-only arm (0.706 against the full stage's 0.765, p=0.39) shows that where the recall lives is implementation forensics, not the prose anchor — see 2.5.
- **W5:** Missing related work within the API-oracle line the paper positions against: RESTOR (ISSTA 2026) generates executable assertions from a request-response pair and is not cited, though the paper cites the sibling trace-anchored work (AGORA+) — see 2.6.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The problem is real, sourced, and well delimited. Section 2 grounds it in two empirical studies of VDBMS defects (functional failures dominate; crash bugs are a minority) and in the testing roadmap that names oracle definition as the open challenge, and Section 1 gives the canonical instance (`nprobe=0` accepted with HTTP 200, Milvus issue #49823). The practical payoff is not hypothetical: the submission ledger (Table 2) reports 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs and 23 merged fixes across three production systems, with 50 of 51 carrying an official `bug` label. That is a genuine contribution to the reliability of infrastructure LLM applications depend on.
   - **1.2** Table 1's exclusion analysis is the right shape of argument for this problem: it organizes prior oracle families by *where the expectation comes from* rather than by feature comparison, and the paper is scrupulous about which rows are exercised by experiment (rows 1 and 7) and which are argued from anchoring mechanism (rows 2–6). The concession that SATORI's reported bugs do include specification–response mismatches — i.e. that the residual is narrower than "documentation-derived oracles miss documentation bugs" — makes the scope claim credible rather than inflated.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The impact evidence and the method are not the same object. Section 4.3 states that the ledger "is the sum of sixteen per-version campaigns rather than one configuration's output" and that per-session strategy and rule configuration "cannot be reconstructed retroactively"; Table 2's caption repeats that the reported run contributed none of the 81. The paper discloses this in three places, but contribution 3's phrasing ("51 maintainer-confirmed real bugs (23 fixed) across three production VDBMSs") reads as a yield of the described pipeline, when it is a yield of a family of sixteen differently configured rounds of it.
   - **1.4 [major, fixable]** The significance of the second contribution rests on an assumption the paper leaves undefended. The value proposition of the structured confirmation protocol, as measured, is that it forwards more unsettled cases to a human (19.8% of verdicts, against the flat judge's 8.6%) and that forwarding is scored as success. Under the paper's own strictest reading of the same data — the joint judge-plus-human pass, where routed cases are actually adjudicated — the advantage over the flat judge is 0.647 vs 0.569 with p=0.11, and under forced verdicts it is 0.529 vs 0.490 with p=0.34. So the method's impact is real but conditional on a deployment policy that treats "sent for review" as "found"; that assumption should be stated where the impact is claimed, not only in Section 4.3.
2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** I checked the anchor-inversion claim against MASTOR (cached: `mastor a multi agent approach to semantic test oracle generation for restful apis (corr 2026)`). The paper's characterization — "MASTOR's authority is the implementation" — holds: MASTOR generates oracles from a source bundle, uses the OpenAPI spec only to enumerate endpoints, and its own limitations section states that it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code", and that if the implementation is internally consistent but behaviorally incorrect against unstated requirements, its oracle will accept the behavior. The delta the paper claims for itself is therefore real, and MASTOR's ChallengerAgent review genuinely does mean that "a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is not new here" — a concession many papers would not make.
   - **2.2** The SATORI (`satori static test oracle generation for rest apis (ase 2025)`) characterization holds on the cached full text: per-response-field oracles, 17 unary oracle types matching AGORA+'s unary invariant set, and real bugs confined to string formats, enum values, numeric ranges, and binary oracles — response-side properties, not input-acceptance or state-conformance. The same summary characterizes AGORA+ as the dynamic/trace counterpart, which corroborates indirectly (I did not read AGORA+ itself) the paper's clause that AGORA+ induces invariants from observed traffic and leaves behaviors absent from that traffic unconstrained.
   - **2.3** The two "self-reference" claims hold verbatim. Metamon (`metamon finding inconsistencies … (llm4code 2025)`) captures behavior via generated regression tests and then asks the same LLM, stabilized by metamorphic negation and self-consistency, whether the assertion agrees with the documentation — the paper's "its falsifier is another LLM question" is exact, as are the numbers (precision 0.722 at recall 0.480 on 9,482 pairs). CASCADE (`cascade detecting inconsistencies between code and documentation … (2026)`) regenerates the implementation from the same documentation as its falsifier, and the quoted explanation of its residual false positives ("the generated code and tests made the same false assumption over something underspecified in the documentation") is CASCADE's own.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** One imprecision in an otherwise accurate characterization of MASTOR, in the direction of attributing *more* to the competitor: Table 1's row 5 says MASTOR "records a doc-versus-source discrepancy signal in its `pending` fields". In the full text, `pending` holds "OAS-declared items the source code does not substantiate (for example, error status codes returned only by a framework-level exception mapper)" — an evidence-locatability set, since a framework exception mapper does return those codes, not a set of confirmed doc/source divergences. The conclusion (downstream agents read only source-verified fields, so the signal is never turned into an oracle) is correct either way.
   - **2.5 [major, fixable]** The verified competitor set narrows the novelty corner more than the paper's framing admits. LogicHunter (`logichunter testing llm agent frameworks with an agentic oracle (issta 2026)`) treats retrieved documentation as the statement of intent and gives its ReAct oracle `code_search` (Python reflection) and sandboxed `run_code` tools — so the family already contains a documentation-anchored oracle; what TestVDB adds is the *discipline* that the implementation may refute but not arbitrate. That discipline is a real design position, but the paper never measures its cost or benefit: its own source-only arm — the confirmation stage's source-forensics step operating alone on the same pack and clone — reaches 0.706 (36/51) against the full stage's 0.765, p=0.39, so on this pool the incremental value of "the implementation only falsifies" over "the implementation is consulted" is indistinguishable from zero. Either measure that delta (e.g. an arm allowed to read source but forbidden to let it confirm) or state explicitly that anchor inversion is a protocol property with no demonstrated recall advantage over the nearest documentation-anchored-with-source-consultation design.
   - **2.6 [minor, fixable]** Missing related work in the line the paper positions against: RESTOR ("Automated Test Oracle Generation for RESTful APIs via Reinforcement Learning", ISSTA 2026 research track) generates executable assertions from a single request-response pair and reports an industrial deployment; it belongs to the same trace-anchored oracle family as AGORA+ that Table 1 row 5 covers, and its absence from a related-work paragraph that cites AGORA+, SATORI, MASTOR, and MASTEST is conspicuous. (Abstract-level check via the ISSTA 2026 program listing and a secondary index; I did not fetch it, so treat the specifics as provisional.) A sentence positioning the anchor class against it would close the gap.
3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The paper's handling of its own defects is the strongest part of the evaluation. Section 4.3 reports that an audit of the originally assembled 81 packs found 43.3% of 134 distinct (constraint, cited-page) pairs unsupported on the page cited, two over-strong pairs, 32% of constraint rows addressing an unrelated endpoint, and four packs carrying assembler provenance notes that leaked adjudication-adjacent annotations; it then rewrote the assembler, rebuilt every pack (674 surviving rows out of 2,106; 22 `explicit` against 652 inferred), and re-adjudicated the whole pool. It next found and cleaned two further contamination channels (ten packs with embedded cognition sections; cognition entries referencing seven of the pool's own issue numbers) and re-ran the affected arms. The payload-shape boundary in the same subsection — four candidates whose filed reports used a parameter shape the API never binds (`groupParams` nesting, top-level `shardsNum`) — is disclosed with the correct conclusion: three remain true positives because maintainers reproduced them with corrected payloads, and one is a leak.
   - **3.2** The quantitative apparatus is sound and I re-derived it. All three Wilson intervals I recomputed (8/51 [0.082, 0.280]; 39/51 [0.632, 0.860]; 21/30 [0.521, 0.833]) are exact two-sided score intervals; every reported exact McNemar p-value reproduces (16/2 → 0.0013; 11/2 → 0.0225; 5/0 → 0.0625; 7/3 → 0.34; 8/2 → 0.11; 8/5 → 0.58; 15/5 → 0.041; 15/3 → 0.0075); the Holm multipliers match a seven-test family ranked by p-value (0.0052, 0.023, 0.083) and the ten-test sensitivity (0.0092) is arithmetically right; the stratum recalls sum correctly under all three conventions (29+2+8=39 convention; 24+0+3=27 forced; 26+1+6=33 joint); and the per-perspective attribution closes (16+7+2+14=39 confirmations; 18/8/3/19=48 with 2/1/1/5=9 leaks, giving the reported precisions 0.889/0.875/0.737).
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The flagship comparison measures routing volume, and the routed queue's composition is the missing number. The paper states that the full stage routes 19.8% of case judgments (48/243; 21 unique cases) and the flat judge 8.6% (21/243; 11 unique cases), and that under the convention every routed case counts as confirmed; it also states that "the decomposition attributes the convention-based separation to its explicit human-review routing". The scoring-conventions table's caption (Table 6) then gives the routed-queue contents: 21 = 15 confirmations + 6 false positives for the full stage, 11 = 8 + 3 for the flat judge. Those are precisions of 0.714 and 0.727 — the two arms route equally well and differ only in how often they decline to decide. Because the convention converts volume into recall one for one, an arm that routed every case would score recall 1.0 under it; the paper's defence ("a routed case goes upstream unless a reviewer finds it egregiously wrong") is a deployment policy, not evidence that routed cases are defects, and the paper neither engages nor cites the standard selective-prediction treatment of this failure mode — report accuracy at coverage, and do not score an abstention as a correct answer — even though routing is by its own account the load-bearing mechanism. Fix: report routed-queue precision for both arms, and add a routing-matched control — the flat judge is already instructed to use Human-Review "when a case cannot be settled", so a variant told to route at ~20% is a cheap, decisive arm separating "the structure judges better" from "the structure abstains more".
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The ablation's stated interpretation contradicts its own design. Appendix A claims the design "isolates the protocol's *structural* contribution — the four-perspective organization and its routing aggregation — from its *rule* content, which both judges share; since the shared rules are the ones that most directly affect recall, the comparison is conservative: whatever separates the full stage from the flat judge is structure, not rules." But the routing aggregation is the mechanism Section 4.3 credits for the separation, and the aggregation rule appears only in the full-stage prompt (its "Aggregation (fixed)" clause); the flat prompt explicitly carries "no … aggregation rule". The variable that carries the measured effect is therefore an unshared rule, and the comparison bundles perspective vocabulary, aggregation, and the resulting routing behaviour. Either run the routing-matched flat arm of 3.3 (which would isolate the four-perspective organization from the routing mandate) or restate Appendix A to say plainly that the comparison measures the paired effect of perspectives-plus-routing and that the routing component is the load-bearing one.
   - **3.5 [major, fixable]** The flagship separation is one backbone's property, and the paper's mechanism story predicts why. Re-running full and flat on Qwen gives 0.588 vs 0.529, discordant 8/5, p=0.58, with routing rates falling from 19.8% to 10.3% (full) and 8.6% to 7.8% (flat). Since the primary-family effect is a routing-rate effect (3.3), a backbone that routes less will show less separation *whether or not it judges better* — the second-family null is not merely underpowered, it is confounded with the mechanism. The paper reports this as "a property of that backbone under this protocol, not a law of the protocol", which is fair, but the claim is still carried in the abstract and contribution 2 on one family. A third family, or a routing-rate-matched cross-family comparison, is what would settle it; short of that, condition the headline on the primary backbone wherever it appears.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** The joint reading is conceptually the paper's most credible number and empirically its least reproducible. An independent adjudicator given the same packs, clones, and the full deployed rules agrees with the authors' hand pass at κ = 0.11 (the best of three conditions) and confirms 4 of 20 comparable routed cases where the authors confirm 11, giving 0.588 rather than 0.647. The paper reports this fully and brackets the readings honestly; what it does not do is weight them — contribution 2 still lists 0.647 as one of three co-equal "readings", and the abstract leads with 0.647 in the same sentence as the independent 0.588. Since 0.588 is the value an independent party reproduces, and it coincides with the primary-family flat judge's 0.588, the paper should say which number a third party should expect.
   - **3.7 [minor, fixable]** The Limitations claim that "published LLM-derived oracles operate at substantially higher false-positive rates than ours" is not supported by the paper's own citation set. The nearest same-kind work (Metamon, 0.722 precision → a 0.278 false-positive rate against the full stage's 0.188) does not make "substantially", and the comparison crosses benchmarks, units, and ground truths — the paper itself says Metamon is "not a head-to-head baseline". Worse, LogicHunter — which the paper's related-work section (§6) cites, not the Limitations passage making this claim — reports an oracle precision of 91.17%, above the paper's convention-scored 0.812 (though below its joint 0.917, and measured on a validity-filtered anomaly set rather than a blind candidate pool). The supportable version is that no comparable pooled false-positive rate exists for the closest systems, which is why the paper reports suppression as a property of its arms instead.
   - **3.8 [minor, fixable]** The full stage's three runs drift monotonically while no other arm does: confirmations 40 → 38 → 35 and leaked false positives 8 → 9 → 11 across runs 1–3, a 5-TP spread against a 9-TP gap to the flat judge. The paper reports per-run rows but never comments on the trend or states whether the runs were temporally ordered (a learning or ordering effect, or drift in the judge materials, would matter for the majority-vote construction). A note on run ordering, or more repetitions, would close it.
   - **3.9 [minor, unfixable]** Two disclosed boundaries cap the absolute numbers rather than the comparisons. The 81-candidate pool is the pipeline's own submission-filtered output, and the objective-constraint classes were codified from the same mining experience that produced it; the paper quantifies the exposure (excluding objective-constraint-driven confirmations drops recall to 0.451) and anchors the filter's direction with the 32-case never-submitted sample (0.594 floor under the stricter A/B/D protocol, 31/32 under the deployed one). No revision can supply an independently sourced holdout; the disclosure is adequate, but readers should treat every absolute rate in RQ2 as a fit statistic on the developers' own pool.
4. **Verifiability** — Excellent
   - **4.1** The replication package is declared with an inventory specific enough to be checked against the claims: agent role definitions and the strategy-trigger registry, per-version knowledge bases and specifications, all generated scripts with raw HTTP logs, one evidence chain per candidate, the submission ledger, the original and rebuilt RQ2 packs with the pack-audit verdict records, per-case judge outputs for every arm and run on both backbones, the RQ3 baseline reports with per-template logs, and the analysis scripts that recompute every rate, interval, and paired test. ## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets a class it calls *documentation-implementation bugs* in vector database management systems: cases where a VDBMS silently accepts an input or behaves in a way that violates its natural-language API documentation, producing no crash and therefore escaping crash-oracle fuzzers. Its argument is that every deterministic oracle family anchors its expectation somewhere other than system-level prose, so an LLM-derived oracle is the practical option, which imports the LLM's false-positive problem. TestVDB answers with a four-stage pipeline (specification extraction, test-script generation under pre-bound strategies, sandboxed execution against a Docker-pinned version, and a bug-confirmation stage that separates evidence assembly from cross-examination), in which the implementation source serves as the falsification anchor and a three-valued verdict routes unsettled cases to human review.

The evaluation has two halves. RQ1 reports an operational ledger of 81 adjudicated submissions to Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, of which maintainers confirmed 51 as real bugs and fixed 23 via merged PRs. RQ2 audits the frozen evidence packs that had fed the confirmation stage, finds 43.3% of their cited (constraint, page) pairs unsupported, rebuilds every pack against version-pinned documentation, and re-adjudicates the full 81-candidate pool three times per configuration across five judge configurations. It reports the confirmation stage under three scoring conventions (forced verdicts 0.529 recall, hand-adjudicated joint reading 0.647, deployment counting convention 0.765; contract-only core 0.157 recall at 0.967 suppression; flat single-prompt judge 0.588 under the convention). RQ3 probes oracle reach bidirectionally against VDBFuzz on Qdrant.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The paper audits and rebuilds its own measurement instruments, then reports the result as a three-layer attribution rather than a single number — an unusually strong self-correction that makes the RQ2 numbers interpretable — see 3.1, 3.2.
- **S2:** Nearly every printed quantity I could recompute closes exactly: Wilson intervals, exact McNemar p-values, F1/Youden, the stratification sums, and the pack-rebuild arithmetic all reproduced to the printed precision — see 4.2, 3.2.
- **S3:** Internal validity threats are disclosed with specificity and then actually measured (pack contamination, cognition anchoring, expectation framing, self-disproved candidate #9149), including one result that narrows the paper's own headline (no separation from the flat judge on a second backbone) — see 3.3, 3.6.
- **S4:** The comparison against the flat judge is deliberately designed to isolate *structure* from *rule content*, and the residual rule difference is quantified rather than left hanging — see 2.2.
- **S5:** The RQ3 baseline comparison is instrumented rather than taken on trust: the authors show the released runner's counters cannot fire and substitute their own oracle — see 3.7.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The flagship full-vs-flat separation is carried by the counting convention (routed cases counted as confirmed); under forced verdicts the two judges are indistinguishable and it does not replicate on the second backbone — see 3.3.
- **W2:** The submission-filter bias argument in RQ1 compares two different protocol readings and draws a conclusion the paper's own later section contradicts — see 3.5.
- **W3:** Two metrics behind headlined numbers ("case agreement", "routed") are never defined, and the paper's decomposition of the full stage's 39 confirmations does not reproduce from its own declared run outputs (23 all-CONFIRMED and 10 HR-majority, versus the printed 24 and 12) — see 3.4.
- **W4:** Two reproduction paths that produce headlined numbers (the independent-adjudicator substitution, and the per-perspective precision attribution) are not specified precisely enough to be checked from the text — see 4.3, 4.4.
- **W5:** The abstract is roughly double the customary length and drops caveats the body is careful to attach; the RQ2 prose is dense enough to defeat a first read — see 5.2, 5.3.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The demonstrated yield is real and independently verifiable in kind: §4.2's ledger (Table 2: 81 adjudicated submissions, 51 maintainer-confirmed, 23 fixed) is backed by acceptance signals the projects themselves emit (50 of 51 carry a `bug` label; 25 of 29 Milvus bugs carry `triage/accepted`) and, more tellingly, by a *fix-nature* classification of every merged fix PR — all 23 modify implementation code, 15 add regression tests, none is documentation-only. The authors even adversarially validate the classifier by pointing it at the fix PR of the candidate they had themselves disproved (#9149) and showing it identifies that PR as test-only, matching their seven-version re-probe. For a bug-finding paper this is the right thing to check and it is checked.
   - **1.2** The conceptual contribution in §2.3 and Table 1 is genuinely useful beyond this system: an oracle candidate is classified by *where its expectation anchors*, not by what technique it uses, and the table states for each family why the documentation-implementation residual is out of its reach. That criterion is portable and it is the part of the paper most likely to be cited by people who never run TestVDB.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The impact claim is assumption-laden in a way the abstract does not fully carry. The 51/23 yield is a *human–machine deployment's* cumulative output with author-side novelty screening and the submission decision in the loop (hedged in §4.2 and §4.3, where the paper says "it measures the human--machine pipeline rather than the automated stage alone"), and the automated component measured alone spans 0.157 (contract core) to 0.765 (deployment convention). A reader who scores only the automated component would land one tier lower; a single sentence in the abstract naming the assumption would remove the ambiguity.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate *(rated provisional — I did not survey the field; I assessed the delta only from the paper's own positioning, which names its closest competitors)*
   - **2.1** The claimed delta is stated crisply and is not oversold: §6 concedes that "a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is therefore not new here, and cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to \system{}," and locates the contribution in what it calls the *anchor inversion* — MASTOR's authority is the implementation, whereas TestVDB takes the prose as ground and admits the implementation only as falsifier. Naming the nearest work and conceding the overlapping parts is the honest way to claim a thin delta, and the concession is made in Related Work rather than buried.
   - **2.2** The flat-judge control is designed, not incidental: Appendix A states that both prompts carry the same red lines (verbatim-intent requirement; the objective-constraint principle with four of seven classes as examples) and differ only in perspective vocabulary, chain sections, and the aggregation rule, and it then quantifies the residual rule difference (the flat judge reaches 13 of the 16 objective-constraint-driven confirmations; the narrower enumeration accounts for two of the three it misses). This is what makes the comparison a test of *structure*.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** The novelty of the *structured protocol itself* is not isolated by any ablation in the paper. The full stage is statistically indistinguishable from the source-only arm (confirmed sets of 48 vs. 44, discordant 8/4, $p{=}0.39$; §4.3), and the authors' own decomposition says of the eight cases the full stage adds over source-only, "six are forced \textsc{Confirmed} majorities delivered by the perspectives" with the rest routing. A reader therefore cannot tell what the four-perspective organization buys over the source-forensics step it contains; as measured, the demonstrated advantage belongs to source grounding plus the routing rule, not to the panel structure.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** §4.3's "Measurement design: audit, rebuild, protocol alignment" is the strongest part of the paper. The Layer 0 audit (a mechanical assembly pass plus per-pair verification) is reported with its own error classes — a per-vendor fixed-version contract substitution, parameter-name rather than endpoint matching, implementation-private constants read as contract — and the rebuild arithmetic closes exactly: 2,106 assembled rows $-674$ endpoint-filtered $-463$ verification failures $=969$ survivors; $969=667+302$; $667+7=674$ with 22 `explicit` and 652 inferred. Because each layer isolates one variable, the resulting three-layer attribution (assertion-framework floor vs. structure vs. routing) is a measurement rather than a narrative.
   - **3.2** The stratification in §4.3 ("Where recall lives") closes exactly and its conclusion is the opposite of the one the paper originally wanted: 37/3/11 packs give convention recall 0.784/0.667/0.727 (29+2+8 = 39 confirmations), forced 0.649/0.000/0.273 (24+0+3 = 27), joint 0.703/0.333/0.545 (26+1+6 = 33), and the core reads 0.216/0.000/0.000 (8/37, 0/3, 0/11). Reporting that the evidence-absent stratum does *not* lead, and that the human-review channel is what keeps it off zero, is the kind of result many papers would have quietly dropped.
   - **3.3 [minor, fixable]** The headline comparison rests on the counting convention, and the abstract says so: its sentence reads "the structured full stage reaches 0.765 recall at 0.700 suppression under the deployment's counting convention---against 0.588 for a flat single-prompt judge reading the same packs and sources," and the forced-verdict collapse (0.529 vs. 0.490) appears two sentences later. What a reader must still assemble from three separate places is that the gap is absent under *both* stricter readings (forced $p{=}0.34$; joint 0.647 vs. 0.569, $p{=}0.11$) and on the second backbone ($p{=}0.58$). The claim as scoped ("Routing, not forced accuracy, is where the structure pays") is supported; the residual risk is that 0.765-vs-0.588 gets quoted as an accuracy gap — which the abstract's next clause ("the other two readings we report are sensitivities around it") mostly, but not entirely, forecloses.
   - **3.4 [minor, fixable]** I re-derived the full stage's three-run aggregates from the declared run outputs rather than from the text (`rerun_v3/run_full{1,2,3}/verdicts_batch*.jsonl` with the seven `verdicts_coganchor_rejudge.jsonl` overrides, scored against `gt_81.json`; ground truth there is 51 T / 30 F, matching Table 2). The per-run rows reproduce exactly (40/8/11/22; 38/9/13/21; 35/11/16/19), the majority row reproduces exactly (39/9/12/21; $n_1{=}9$, $n_2{=}12$, $n_3{=}36$), and 48 of the 243 verdicts are Human-Review exactly as printed. The 67.9% reproduces exactly as well — as 55 of 81 cases in which all three runs return the *identical verdict label*; under the binary reading (all three agree on confirmed-vs-not) the figure is 74.1% (60/81). The three printed quantities are therefore mutually consistent and no off-by-one should be sought among them. What survives is that "case agreement" is never defined, three instances are printed (67.9%, 96.3%, 56/81), and the reader cannot tell which of two plausible definitions produced them. Recomputing also exposes a discrepancy in the prose describing this pool: §4.3 says "Of its 39 confirmations, 24 are Confirmed in all three runs and 15 carry at least one Human-Review component---12 routed by the majority of their three runs and 3 confirmed two-to-one on ordinary votes." The verdict vectors give 23 cases with three CONFIRMED verdicts (the 39th majority confirmation is 2$\times$CONFIRMED + 1$\times$FALSE-POSITIVE), 15 carrying $\geq$1 HR vote, of which 10 are HR-majority and a further 2 are (CONFIRMED, FALSE\_POSITIVE, HUMAN\_REVIEW) triples — so "12 routed" is reached only if those 2 are counted as routed, and "by the majority of their three runs" does not cover them. "Routed" is likewise never defined operationally: on an HR-majority basis the queue holds 15 cases (10 true bugs, 5 false positives), not the 21 (15 + 6) that the caption of Table 6 reports. Both counts may be right under a definition I could not reconstruct; the fix is to define both metrics and reconcile the totals.
   - **3.5 [major, fixable]** §4.2 and §4.3 state incompatible things about the same comparison. §4.2 argues the submission filter did not select stronger candidates because "the stage's own confirmation rate on the submitted pool is 48/81 (0.593), within a thousandth of the anchor [0.594], so on a stage-to-stage basis the submitted and never-submitted streams look alike." But the 0.594 is the anchor's *stricter* A/B/D binary force-closed reading, while 48/81 is the full stage under the *deployment* convention — and §4.3 says both that "the anchor was also measured on the original packs before the audit, so its absolute value is not comparable to the rebuilt-package numbers below" and that re-running the same 32 packs "under the deployed protocol … gives 31/32". The protocol-matched pairs are therefore 0.594 vs. the submitted pool's strict numbers (forced 0.529 / joint 0.647) and 0.969 vs. 0.593 (deployment convention) — neither is "within a thousandth." The sentence's conservative conclusion may survive a corrected comparison, but as printed the supporting arithmetic is a protocol mismatch, and it sits under the RQ1 precision claim.
   - **3.6** The threats-to-validity treatment (§5) is unusually complete for a paper of this kind and is measured rather than merely confessed: expectation framing (19 hits, all inside verbatim server responses, zero added by assembly), pack contamination (13 affected runs re-adjudicated and replaced, with pre-cleanup files shipped), cognition anchoring (candidate-anchored entries removed, seven cases re-adjudicated), and adjudication disagreement (the approximate-count family runs *against* the stage, and the nine leaks include three adjudication disagreements rather than contract failures). The paper also names the two directions a skeptical reader would take — protocol–pool co-evolution, and its own quantification that removing the objective-constraint class lowers recall to 0.451 with no symmetric exclusion available for the flat judge.
   - **3.7** §4.4's handling of the baseline is methodologically careful. Rather than reporting VDBFuzz's zero-crash summary at face value, the authors test the counters (pointing the released runner at a dead port yields exit code 0 and a 100% success rate), substitute a per-template liveness oracle, and confirm the run was live (23,258 responses across 96 paths, statuses summing to 23,258). They then bound the zero from two directions — the released mutation space cannot construct the crashing input class, and a recovered panic with a live server would not trip a liveness oracle either — and explicitly decline the broader claim that crash oracles as a class cannot reach it. The reverse direction's limitations (a hand-executed probe, the value chosen with knowledge of the overflow mechanism, the 65,536 ceiling of the released boundary set) are stated in the same paragraph.

### Questions for Authors

- **Q1:** How are "case agreement" and "routed" defined? The verdict vectors reproduce 67.9% (55/81) only as agreement on the *verdict label* (the confirmed-vs-not reading gives 74.1%), and on an HR-majority reading the full stage's routed queue holds 15 cases (10 true bugs, 5 false positives), not the 21 reported in Table 6's caption — which count of the 39 confirmations is authoritative underneath it? — *Intended effect: defining both metrics and reconciling 24/12 with the 23/10/3/2/1 the vectors give would move 3.4 from a flagged inconsistency to a strength, since every other aggregate on this pool reproduces exactly.*
- **Q2:** Which protocol-matched pair do you intend in §4.2 when you write that the submitted pool's 0.593 is "within a thousandth of the anchor"? §4.3 says the anchor's absolute value is not comparable to the rebuilt numbers and gives 31/32 for the same sample under the deployed protocol. — *Intended effect: a corrected, explicitly protocol-matched comparison would restore 3.5 and strengthen the submission-filter argument, which currently reads as supporting the opposite of what the numbers imply under the deployment protocol.*
- **Q3:** Given that the full stage is statistically indistinguishable from the source-only arm ($p{=}0.39$), what does the four-perspective organization contribute beyond the source-forensics step it contains — and can that contribution be isolated without an additional arm? — *Intended effect: an answer that isolates the perspectives would move 2.3 and sharpen the novelty delta; if the honest answer is "nothing measurable on this pool," the paper should say so, since its own decomposition already points that way.*
- **Q4:** On which basis are the per-perspective attribution counts computed — the 39 confirmations or the 48, and are the attribution sets exclusive? — *Intended effect: this moves 4.4, and it is the only thing standing between the reader and the paper's claim that the objective-constraint class is the most precise of the three forced-driving perspectives.*
- **Q5:** Would it strengthen or weaken the paper to lead with the joint reading (0.647 vs. 0.569, $p{=}0.11$) and present the convention as the operational reading rather than the headline? — *Intended effect: this bears on 3.3; if the authors can argue that the deployment's convention is the *only* honest reading of a deployment that literally routes cases to a human queue, the current framing stands; if not, the strict reading is where a skeptical PC member will land.*

### Priority Revisions
The main problems the author must fix, ranked by impact on the verdict. Two issues drew a `[major, fixable]` from two different reviewers and are the revisions that matter most; the rest are single-reviewer majors or the consensus minor cluster.

1. **Separate the four-perspective organization from the mandatory routing rule, and report the routed queue's precision for both arms.** R1 rates this `[major, fixable]` at 3.3 and 3.5, R2 at 1.4, 3.3 and 3.4; R3 reaches the same place at 3.3 as a minor. The reviewers converge on a specific mechanism: the flagship separation survives Holm correction only under the counting convention, the full stage routes 19.8% of verdicts against the flat judge's 8.6%, and under both readings that price escalation honestly the difference is non-significant (joint 0.647 vs. 0.569, $p{=}0.11$) or collapses (forced 0.529 vs. 0.490, $p{=}0.34$). R2 supplies the number that makes the point sharpest and that the paper never prints: both arms' routed queues run at essentially the same precision (full 15/21 = 0.714; flat 8/11 = 0.727), so on this pool the two arms route equally well and differ in how often they decline to decide. A flat-judge arm instructed to route at a matched rate would settle whether the structure judges better or merely abstains more — and would isolate the paper's named contribution (the four perspectives) from the escalation rule that the paper's own decomposition credits. This is also where the reviewers diverge in framing and the author should note it: R2 (3.4) reads Appendix A's "isolates" sentence as a contradiction, since the aggregation rule is not shared between the two prompts; R1 (3.5) makes the same revision request without alleging a contradiction. The appendix's own wording lists "the four-perspective organization **and its routing aggregation**" on the structure side, so R2's contradiction charge is the stronger reading of the two, but the revision both reviewers want is unaffected either way.

2. **Quantify the implementation source's marginal contribution, or stop claiming it carries the result.** R1 rates this `[major, fixable]` at 2.3 and 3.4, R2 at 2.5; R3 reaches it at 2.3 as a minor. Contribution 2 says the confirmation stage "grounds every verdict in evidence sources outside the LLM---the implementation source above all", but the paper's own per-perspective attribution assigns the source-anchored perspective (C) **zero** of the full stage's 39 majority confirmations, no arm holds everything else fixed while dropping the source clone, and full-vs-source-only is indistinguishable ($p{=}0.39$). R2 adds a competitor-side reason to care: LogicHunter already anchors its intent in retrieved documentation and merely also consults source, so what TestVDB adds is the discipline that the implementation may refute but not arbitrate — a real design position whose cost and benefit the paper never measures. Either add an arm that reads source but forbids it from confirming, or state plainly that anchor inversion is a protocol property with no demonstrated recall advantage on this pool.

3. **Condition the flagship claim on the primary backbone, or address the mechanism confound.** R2 rates this `[major, fixable]` at 3.5, and R1 and R3 flag the same non-replication in their Weakness lists. The reviewers' point is sharper than "underpowered": because the primary-family effect is itself a routing-rate effect, a backbone that routes less (19.8% → 10.3%) will show less separation *whether or not it judges better*, so the second-family null is confounded with the mechanism rather than merely underpowered.
