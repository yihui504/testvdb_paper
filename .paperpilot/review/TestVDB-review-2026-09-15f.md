## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper reports an experience study of an LLM pipeline (TestVDB) that mines documentation–implementation inconsistencies in three production vector DBMSs (Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate). The pipeline extracts structured constraint records from vendor prose, turns them into executable probes bound to strategies registered before generation, executes them in a Docker-pinned sandbox, and confirms candidates in a two-part stage: an evidence builder assembles a five-section chain (documentation, execution, contract, trace, source grounding), and a chain auditor applies four mechanical checks and then reads the chain from four perspectives under a fixed aggregation rule that assigns Confirmed, False-Positive, or Human-Review. The study has three parts: a mining campaign whose submissions maintainers adjudicated; an audit of the (constraint, cited-page) pairs the confirmation stage reads; and a census of the confirmation stage's own judgments by the aggregation clause that closes each one.

For the re-adjudication study, twelve judge configurations — varying the four perspectives, the aggregation rule, implementation-source access, and the model backbone — each re-judge the same 81 frozen packages (51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 30 adjudicated false positives) three times, 243 judgments per configuration. The campaign yielded 51 of 81 submissions confirmed by maintainers and 23 merged fixes; the paper explicitly withholds a detection rate because the runs that would have measured one were voided for protocol violations. The package audit split 134 pairs into 18 supported as cited, 58 re-anchored from a source-file or landing-page anchor, and 58 with no support on the cited page, and exposed two leak channels that were repaired and the affected cases re-judged. The clause census locates the error mass in the one refuting clause the protocol does not guard: contract refutation closes 50 of the 243 judgments and 24 of them are real bugs (80% of the stage's incorrect closures to False-Positive), while the verbatim-guarded by-design clause closes 19 and is right 17 times. The organizational contrast is flat: the four-perspective decomposition changes which cases are decided but not how many (identical forced-verdict counts on both backbones, with an 11-bug loss on the second), and withholding the implementation source trades recall for released false positives, leaving net true-positive-minus-false-positive unchanged.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The evidence-package audit is a genuinely new measurement of a step the whole line of work performs but no one reports: how much of a distilled specification is supported by the pages it cites, with a blind second reader and two named failure mechanisms. — see 2.1, 3.1
- **S2:** The clause census localizes the error mass in the unguarded clause and is built to survive the explanations that would dissolve it (materials-vs-protocol cross-tab, invariance across both printed aggregation rules, maintainer-label anchoring, pre/post-repair counts). — see 2.2
- **S3:** Neutral and negative results are reported with the same prominence as the positive ones, and they are the transferable ones (perspectives fail to move counts; source access is a trade, not a win). — see 2.3, 3.1
- **S4:** Verifiability discipline: the artifact ships raw and cleaned generations, re-judged cases beside pre-repair states, verbatim dispatch texts, and scripts that recompute the printed numbers, with the paper stating exactly which quantities are printed but not scripted. — see 4.1, 4.2, 4.3
- **S5:** The related-work delta is verified against the competitors the paper rests on, and no mischaracterization was found. — see 3.2

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The census's comparative pattern is a primary-backbone result that reverses on the second backbone, so the paper's second headline lesson generalizes less than its positioning suggests; the reversal is entangled with a dispatch defect the authors found but did not repair. — see 2.4
- **W2:** Two post-repair "improvements" are convention- or replay-dependent, and the abstract states one of them without its qualifier. — see 2.5
- **W3:** The false-positive-side quantities rest on 30 negatives of which at least 23 carry a maintainer disposition (at most 7 are the authors' own calls, no inter-annotator study), and the yield carries no detection rate by construction. — see 1.3, 2.6
- **W4:** The prose is dense to the point of slowing evaluation, with undefined shorthand in one place and two abstract sentences that omit qualifiers the body supplies. — see 5.2, 5.3, 5.4

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Importance & Scope** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The paper defines its problem against the literature rather than asserting it. Section 2 builds the case from the VDBMS defect study and the community roadmap's statement that oracle definition is the field's open problem, then Table 1 walks six oracle families and names, per family, where its expectation is anchored and why that anchor misses system-level behavioral prose. I checked the competitor the paragraph rests on (Xie et al., *Toward Understanding Bugs in VDBMSs*, cached full text): functional failure is 57.3% against crash 15.1%, so "the dominant share … with crash-producing defects a minority class" is accurate; and VDBFuzz's own limitations section confirms its oracle is crash-only by construction, so "out of its reach by design" is fair. The target subset (untagged, system-level prose as the statement of intent) is a precise, defensible slice.
   - **1.2** Scope is stated with unusual precision for an experience paper: three production systems, 81 adjudicated cases, twelve configurations at three runs each, two backbones — and each claim is explicitly scoped where it needs to be (the census on the primary backbone, §4.5; no schema-repaired control on the second, §4.4; the unjudgeable case and who routes it, §4.1; the 32 screened-out candidates bounded without maintainer ground truth, §4.1).
   - **1.3 [minor, unfixable]** The campaign contribution is a record of submissions and adjudication, not a measurement of the pipeline's ability to find bugs. Section 4.2 reports that the 15-version detection experiment was voided in full on 2026-08-23 for dispatch-discipline violations, so the headline 51/81 is a conversion rate over already-submitted candidates, and the screening's effect is boundable only by the unsubmitted-stream anchor (19 of 32, no maintainer ground truth). The paper discloses this in the abstract, in contribution 1, in §4.2 and in Threats, and nothing in the paper depends on the voided runs — but it caps what the title's "mine" promises, and it cannot be repaired by revision since the runs are void.

2. **Insights & Evidence** — Excellent
   - **2.1** The audit of the confirmation stage's input (§4.3) is the paper's most original measurement: 134 (constraint, cited-page) pairs audited against the page each cites at the version the row claims, split 18 supported / 58 re-anchored (the anchor was a source-file path or an API landing page carrying no endpoint documentation) / 58 with no support on the cited page; two mechanisms are named (version drift, where one fixed version's contract supplied rows for another release, and "conceptual-only" documentation, where a bound exists as prose but not as a documented value); the rebuilt set is then checked for the failure this class of work invites — zero of 81 packages carry a sentence framed as an expectation, with the 19 phrasing hits inspected and confirmed as verbatim server responses. A blind second reader on a stratified sample of 36 of the 122 reachable pairs agrees 30/36 (κ = 0.75). I verified the novelty claim against Doc2OracLL (FSE 2025), the closest work measuring documentation's role in oracle generation: its object is documentation's *effect* on oracle quality, not per-citation support of the distilled claim. The rate is properly scoped to this distiller on these three vendors.
   - **2.2** The census (§4.5) is constructed to survive the explanations that would dissolve it. The obvious rival — that the 24 wrong contract refutations are the pair audit resurfacing (defective rows failing a containment check) rather than a protocol defect — is tested with a materials cross-tab: 20 of the 24 fall on cases whose rebuilt packages carry documented evidence (rate 0.11), 4 on the three weak-evidence packages, and none on the eighteen packages the audit left without documented evidence (rate 0.00), so the clause fires on material the audit judged supported. The load-bearing count is then checked for invariance across the two aggregation rules the dispatch prints (which disagree on the insufficient-evidence default; contract refutation assigns False-Positive under both), anchored on maintainer-confirmed labels only, and reported pre- and post-repair (47/20 → 50/19 for the two refuting clauses) with the disclosure that "by-design is the middle of the three" holds of the cleaned pool only. The 24-of-30 (80%) figure is the paper's most robust measurement, and the paper says why.
   - **2.3** The neutral and negative results are first-class findings. The four-perspective decomposition leaves the primary backbone at 39 vs 39 (discordant 4/4, p = 1.0) with an interval of ±0.11 that excludes a large effect while, correctly, not being called equivalence; on the second backbone it costs 11 true bugs (41 vs 30, 12/1, p = 0.0034). Forced-verdict counts are equal on both backbones (27 vs 27; 26 vs 26) while the decided sets overlap in only 22/27 and 23/26 — "equal, but not the same decisions" is exactly the right reading. Withholding the implementation source raises recall 39→48 and cuts suppression 21/30→12/30 while net true-positive-minus-false-positive is identical (30 either way; paired case-level bootstrap CI [−8, +8]) and F1's interval spans zero — the falsification anchor is priced as a trade, not a defeat.
   - **2.4 [minor, unfixable]** The census's comparative pattern is a primary-backbone result, and the paper knows it: on the second backbone, contract refutation closes 56 and the guarded by-design clause closes 50 and is wrong 22 — so "the protocol guards the clause that is already clean" does not hold there — and the unguarded clause supplies 24 of 48 incorrect closures (50%, not 80%) (§4.5). The scope is stated in the abstract, in §4.5 and in §5, and one contributing cause is identified (the dispatch's doubled perspective definitions leave the second backbone's D cells carrying two incompatible vocabularies, 217 source against 26 cognition). Two observations for the authors: (i) the *localization* does survive on both backbones — the unguarded clause is the single largest source of incorrect False-Positive closures on each (24 cells both times) — which the abstract's "not reproduced" under-states; and (ii) because the reversal is entangled with a dispatch defect that was reported but not repaired, a reader cannot separate backbone from instrument. Neither point is fixable by rewriting; it needs a re-run under a repaired dispatch.
   - **2.5 [minor, fixable]** Two quantified improvements travel with conditions the abstract and introduction do not always carry. The counterfactual "recovers seven true bugs and nine more false positives released" holds only under the deployment's convention, where a routed case counts as confirmed; under the forced reading the same replay changes nothing (27/51, 27/30, identical sets), which §4.5 states and the abstract does not. The hand-priced deltas (+3 for the rule-bearing control, +4 for the deployed stage, §4.4) rest on rulings whose agreement with an independent adjudicator is κ = −0.01/0.08/0.11 (§4.1); the paper correctly demotes the joint reading to a bound and reports the forced floor beside every contrast, but still prints "+3/+4" as the honest reading's bottom line. One inserted qualifier in each place fixes this.
   - **2.6 [minor, fixable]** The false-positive-side quantities (suppression at each level; "intercepts 21 of 30") rest on 30 negatives: the ledger's basis groups 11 of them, the vendors' by-design labels cover 12 more, and at most 7 are the authors' own calls, with no inter-annotator study (§4.1, Threats). The paper is explicit, and the load-bearing census count deliberately avoids the negatives — but a sensitivity analysis restricted to the 23 maintainer-dispositioned negatives (or a per-source breakdown of suppression) would bound the exposure cheaply, and the raw materials ship with the artifact.
   - **2.7 [minor, fixable]** The conclusion that a judge's internal organization does not determine its yield rests on bundles. Section 4.1 states that the deployed-change contrast edits two things (the schema line and the routing clause) and the four-perspective contrast five (the perspectives, the aggregation text, the red-line set, the declared verdict field, and cognition access). The paper decomposes one direction — the schema-line repair alone carries five of the bundle's nine bugs — but not the perspective bundle, so "organization changes which cases are decided, not how many" is asserted for a five-way change. The labeling is honest and the claim is still the best reading of the data; a per-element statement of what could and could not be isolated would sharpen it.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper reports an experience study of an LLM-based pipeline that mines documentation--implementation bugs (silent accepts, wrong results, uncontracted state transitions) in three production VDBMSs: Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate. The pipeline distils vendor documentation into cited constraint records, turns them into probes, executes them in a pinned sandbox, and then confirms candidates in a builder/auditor stage whose four perspectives (contract, objective constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition) feed a fixed aggregation rule with a three-valued verdict. The paper reports three things: a submission-and-adjudication campaign (51 of 81 adjudicated submissions maintainer-confirmed, 23 fixed by merged PRs, over 16 of the ledger's 19 versions, with the per-version detection runs explicitly voided and not used); an audit of the evidence packages the confirmation stage reads (134 (constraint, cited-page) pairs: 18 supported as cited, 58 re-anchored, 58 unsupported; two leak channels found and repaired, with every reported number recomputed on the cleaned pool); and a clause-level census of the deployed stage's 243 judgments on the primary backbone, which finds the protocol's unguarded refuting clause (contract refutation) closing 50 judgments with 24 on confirmed bugs, and the verbatim-guarded by-design clause closing 19 and being right 17 times.

The evaluation re-adjudicates all 81 frozen cases under twelve configurations (two backbones, three runs, majority rule) and reports paired exact McNemar at both the recall and confirmed-set levels, plus forced-verdict and hand-priced readings of the routing change, a package-strata cross-tab separating the census from the pair audit, a replay of the printed rule over the judge's own recorded values, and a run of the released VDBFuzz template set against Qdrant (0 anomalies). The census pattern does not reproduce on the second backbone, and the paper says so wherever the claim appears. All numbers from the paper cited below were checked against its own tables and text; competitor figures were checked against the sources named in 3.1.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The RQ2 input audit is a first-of-its-kind, quantified, independently re-checked measurement of how much of a documentation-derived oracle's material its own citations support, and it is scoped correctly (this distiller, these vendors). — see 2.1
- **S2:** The RQ3 census is built to survive the obvious rebuttals: the guarded clause's own closures are classified by evidence kind, the "defective rows" rival is separated by a package cross-tab, the load-bearing count is invariant across the two aggregation rules the dispatch prints, and it is computed on maintainer labels only. — see 2.2
- **S3:** The evaluation discipline is unusual: twelve frozen configurations, two backbones, three runs each, both McNemar levels for every contrast, unadjusted p-values printed together with both Holm family readings, Wald intervals, and hand-priced floors beside the convention numbers. — see 2.4, 2.5
- **S4:** Positioning against the closest competitors is accurate — every characterization I could check (VDBFuzz, Metamon with 0.722/0.480 verbatim, CASCADE's regenerated falsifier, Ma et al.'s bias-not-accuracy design, TRACE's documentation-plausible/implementation-drifted blind spot, mughal26's named-vs-measured gap) holds against the sources themselves, and scoped searches in my two areas surfaced no uncited highly-related work. — see 3.1

- **W1:** The mechanism behind the schema-line repair — which carries five of the headline nine bugs — is never given: it is not shown whether the three-valued field increased deferral or confirmations, so the causal story the conclusion tells is incomplete for its largest single component. — see 2.6

- **W3:** The material the RQ2 rates are computed on (the pre-rebuild packages) is not shipped and no configuration judged it; the Discussion's "the constraint-page pairs our stage read" blurs which generation was measured. — see 4.3, 5.3
- **W4:** The submission-screening paragraph compares a stage confirmation rate on an unlabeled 32-candidate pool with the deployed stage's confirmed-set rate on the labeled pool and concludes the two "are not one instrument's" — a claim a reader cannot reproduce from the text. — see 2.8

A paper that hides this would be simpler and weaker.
   - **2.4** The four-perspective result (§4.4) is reported with the decomposition the authors owe the reader: the contrast is labeled as changing five things against the rule-bearing flat judge, both the convention reading (39 vs 39 on the primary; −11 on the second, 12/1, p = 0.0034) and the forced reading (27 vs 27; 26 vs 26) are given, and the forced sets intersect in only 22 of 27 and 23 of 26 — which is what licenses the conclusion's "which cases are decided, not how many" (as scoped there to forced verdicts). The ±0.11 interval is quoted with the correct caution ("exclude an effect larger than about eleven points but do not establish equivalence").
   - **2.5** The counting convention is priced rather than buried: forced readings sit beside every convention contrast; the routing change is priced three ways (nine under the convention, three hand-adjudicated against the flat judge, four for the deployed stage); the independent adjudicator's agreement (4, 5, 6 of 20 common cases, κ = −0.01/0.08/0.11) is reported with "two of the three independent passes land at the forced floor"; and the Holm analysis is run under two family choices and reported both ways, which changes which of the paper's own contrasts survive. I re-derived both Holm readings from the printed p-values and they are exact (family of ten: four survive, stop at 0.0225; family of twenty: seven clear, stop at the first 0.0039, leaving 0.0034 standing under either family). I also re-derived the F1 pair (0.821 without source, 0.788 with) from the reported TP/FP counts; both match.
   - **2.6 [minor, fixable]** The schema-line repair's mechanism is missing. §4.4 and §5 report that changing the flat dispatch's output-schema line from a binary to a three-valued verdict field "moved five of the headline nine bugs" and lifted forced recall 25→29, but the paper never says what changed in the recorded verdicts. Since the conclusion's thesis is that the count is governed by how often the judge declines to decide, the natural question — did the field change produce *more deferral* (Human-Review, which the convention credits) or more confirmations — is answerable from the shipped per-run verdicts and belongs in the text; the "deferral" sentence currently rides on a component it does not explain.
   - **2.7 [minor, fixable]** The VDBFuzz baseline (§4.6) is a single run of the released template set against one Qdrant instance (205 templates, 50.8 minutes, 1,127 mutation stages, 11,629 status lines, no 5xx, 0 anomalies). The section is explicit that the run went "to natural completion" and bounds the zero correctly ("bounds the released configuration's reach into the silent majority, not the crash-oracle family"), and the disclosure of the 152 log lines that look like three-anomaly reports is exemplary. What is missing is the contrast with VDBFuzz's own run regimen: VDBFuzz's evaluation gives each tool a fixed time budget per system (two hours per VDBMS in its comparison protocol), whereas this run is bounded by the released template set, and §4.6 never states that contrast — so a reader cannot tell how far the zero is comparable to VDBFuzz's published results. One clause comparing the two regimens would close the gap.
   - **2.8 [minor, fixable]** The screening paragraph (§4.1) compares "the stage confirms 19 of 32 (0.594)" on the screened-out candidates (unlabeled, protocol minus cognition) with "the deployed stage … confirms 48 of 81 (0.593)", and concludes "the two rates are not one instrument's." A reader cannot recover what this establishes: the two rates are computed on differently configured instruments and one of them is a confirmed-set rate (39 bug + 9 false-positive cases) while the other is a raw stage-confirmation rate with no ground truth. If the intent is that the submission filter did not distort the stage's behaviour, that should be said in one plain sentence; as written it either exonerates the filter or shows the stage's rate is pool-stable, and it is unclear which.

3. **Perspective** — Excellent
   - **3.1** The paper's positioning against the competitors it names holds up under reading. I fetched and read VDBFuzz (ICSE 2026), Metamon (LLM4Code 2025), Doc2OracLL (FSE 2025), Ma et al. (EMNLP Findings 2025) and the VDBMS bug study (arXiv 2506.02617), and checked the cached summaries of CASCADE (FSE 2026), TRACE (arXiv), mughal26 (IEEE Access) and Molinelli et al. (ASE 2025), and the metadata record for Testora (ICSE 2026). Each characterization I could test is accurate: Metamon's published precision/recall is quoted verbatim; CASCADE's falsifier is indeed a code artifact regenerated from the same documentation; Ma et al. measure four biases by judgment consistency and find debate amplifies while a meta-judge resists; TRACE's blind spot is exactly the documentation-stays-plausible/implementation-drifts case (a 21–43 pp detection drop). No mischaracterization found, and scoped coverage searches in my two areas surfaced no uncited work that this paper needed to position against.
   - **3.2** The first lesson (§5, "audit what your oracle reads") is actionable and cheap, and the paper gives the recipe: sample pairs against their cited pages; cross the resulting rate against where the judge's errors land. Because both halves are provided here on a real pool, a reader building the next documentation-derived judge has a concrete procedure rather than a slogan.
   - **3.3** The lesson set for judge builders transfers beyond this pipeline: price the deferral channel (a routed case credited as confirmed makes recall and routing rate the same quantity), price the contrast rather than the level (the hand-adjudicated floor), check the dispatch before re-architecting (the schema line, the doubled aggregation rule, the C/D vocabulary), and replay your own rule over your own recorded values. Each is tied to a measurement in this paper, not asserted.
   - **3.4 [minor, fixable]** The census's most quotable claim-statement — §4.5's mid-section sentence "The protocol guards the clause that is already clean" — carries the protocol-level form. The scope (deployed stage, primary backbone) is stated at the top of §4.5 and re-stated later in the section ("What we claim is the primary-backbone result"), so the paper does bound the claim; §5's lesson paragraph bounds it too ("the rule is advice about where to look rather than a property of the protocol"). The residual problem is placement: that topic sentence sits after the clause legend and the census table, several paragraphs from the scope it depends on, and reads as a standalone summary, so it can be quoted in its general form. Adding the scope to that one sentence costs a clause.

4. **Verifiability** — Excellent
   - **4.1** The text alone is enough to recompute most of the paper's numbers. I re-derived the census table (243 judgments; 61+21+7+50+19+16+69; 24/30 = 80%), the pool arithmetic (51+30 = 81; per system, adjudicated = confirmed + false positives, so Milvus 29+14 = 43, Qdrant 14+14 = 28, Weaviate 8+2 = 10, with the 23 merged fixes counted inside the confirmations (11+9+3 = 23); 48/81 = 0.593; 19/32 = 0.594), the 53.1% vs 63.0% catch-all comparison as about one standard error, the F1 pair, and both Holm readings. Every one checks out. The paper's statement of the discordant-pair writing convention (a/b) and the two-level McNemar protocol is the kind of disclosure that makes the table walkable.
   - **4.2** The artifact declaration is specific and complete in the ways that matter for this paper's claims: the pool, the frozen per-run verdicts of all twelve configurations, the packages the study read, the dispatch texts with English renderings of the two judging prompts under rq2/prompts/, the pair-audit verdicts, the adjudication worksheet and blind passes, and five named analysis scripts — with an explicit coverage note naming what is printed but not yet scripted (the catch-all composition, the expectation-framing check, the C-row evidence classification). A declared artifact link is the bar in this category; I could not confirm the URL's reachability from my environment (the domain is blocked here), so I credit the declaration as made and do not treat the link as verified.

Since the pair-audit verdicts ship but the material they judge does not, a replicator can read the audit's outcomes but cannot re-audit the 134 pairs — the paper's cheapest-to-reproduce measurement is the one that cannot be reproduced from the package. Shipping the pre-rebuild generation (or a mapping from pair-audit rows to pack text) closes this. The fix-PR characterization (23 merged fixes, 15 with regression tests) is likewise outside the artifact; this one is disclosed in §4.2 and §6, so it is a lesser instance.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1 [minor, fixable]** The accounting sections require re-reading in several places because the referents of the printed pairs are not stated at the point of use: §4.4's four-perspective paragraph gives "39 vs. 39 on the primary backbone" and "51 vs. 48" / "51 vs. 35" on the confirmed set without saying which arm is printed first (the a/b convention is defined for discordant pairs, not for these counts), and "the two source-only configurations (0.706 and 0.725 recall …)" does not say which backbone each belongs to. §4.5's "Routing those 42 leaves the same confirmed set, because every case the clause touches closes in two or more runs and clears in one" compresses a majority-rule argument into undefined shorthand ("clears").

The abstract already carries the backbone scope for the census; it should carry this qualifier too, since it is the sentence a skimming reader will quote.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** Two sentences blur their objects: the abstract's "Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs) fail mostly without crashing, and the one dedicated VDBMS fuzzer is crash-oracle by construction, so the silent documentation--implementation bugs they admit are out of its reach by design" leaves "they" without a clear antecedent (the systems? the bugs?), and §5's "Thirteen per cent of the constraint-page pairs our stage read were supported by the page they cited" attributes to the stage material that §4.3 says the judges did not read (the audit measured the pre-rebuild generation). The second one matters beyond style because it is the sentence that carries the audit into the Discussion.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** The paper's four most decision-bearing contrasts (flat → schema → rule-bearing → full; source withheld; four perspectives; the census replays) are spread across §4.4–§4.5 as numerical prose.    - **5.5 [minor, fixable]** The conclusion's "the organization changes which cases are decided, not how many" is scoped by "under forced verdicts" in the same sentence, but the convention reading on the second backbone is a significant −11 true-bug change (41 vs 30, p = 0.0034) that survives both Holm families. ## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper reports TestVDB, an LLM-based mining pipeline for documentation--implementation inconsistencies in vector database management systems, and then audits two objects around the pipeline's final stage: the documentation-derived constraint material its judge reads, and the judgments the stage writes. The campaign ledger holds 81 adjudicated submissions across Milvus, Qdrant and Weaviate (16 of the ledger's 19 versions); maintainers confirmed 51 and merged fixes for 23, and the paper states repeatedly that this is a record of submissions and adjudication rather than a per-run detection rate, the detection-ability runs having been voided. A pair-level audit of the 134 (constraint, cited-page) pairs in the packages as produced splits them into 18 supported as cited, 58 re-anchored, and 58 unsupported on their cited page; the same audit exposed two leak channels, which were repaired with the affected cases re-judged, and every later rate is computed on the cleaned pool.

The empirical core is a set of twelve judge configurations that re-adjudicate the same frozen 81-case pool three times each on two model backbones, plus a census that classifies the deployed stage's 243 judgments by the aggregation clause that closes each one. The census reports the largest error mass in the unguarded contract-refutation clause (50 closures, 24 of them maintainer-confirmed bugs, 80% of the stage's incorrect false-positive closures), shows the verbatim-guarded by-design clause accurate 17 of 19 times, and replays a counterfactual in which routing the unguarded clause recovers seven true bugs and releases nine more false positives under the deployment's counting convention while changing nothing under the forced reading. Supporting experiments include a four-perspective contrast (null on the primary backbone, negative on the second), a source-withheld arm (recall 39→48 with net and $F_1$ ties), and a run of the released VDBFuzz template set against one Qdrant instance (0 oracle anomalies). The paper also reports four defects in its own dispatch texts.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The paper measures the step every "distil documentation, then judge against it" pipeline performs and none reports---how much of the distilled specification its own citations support---and validates the measurement with a blind second reader (30 of 36 agree, κ = 0.75) and two named failure mechanisms. — see 2.1
- **S2:** The clause census is built to survive the obvious attacks: the letters are identified by cell content rather than assumed, the materials rival is separated by a package-strata cross-tab, and the load-bearing count is invariant across the two printed aggregation rules and computed on maintainer labels rather than the authors' own negatives. — see 2.2
- **S3:** Negative and null results are reported at the same resolution as positive ones---the second backbone's reversal, the p = 1.0 four-perspective contrast, the net/$F_1$ ties, the independent adjudicator's κ ≈ 0, and the four dispatch defects the authors' own audit missed. — see 2.3, 3.2
- **S4:** The lessons in §5 are each tied to a specific measured finding and mostly transferable (audit the oracle's input; guard where the error mass is; price deferral and the contrast, not just the level; check the dispatch before re-architecting the judge). — see 3.1
- **S5:** The recomputation path is unusually concrete: frozen per-run verdicts for all twelve configurations plus five named analysis scripts covering the rates, both backbones' censuses, both replays, the net/$F_1$ intervals and the pair audit. — see 4.1

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** Presentation: the paper is dense to the point that several load-bearing sentences do not parse on first reading, and neither of its two notational conventions---arrow deltas, and the a/b discordant-pair reading stated once in §4.1---is restated at the point of use. — see 5.2, 5.3
- **W2:** Table 2 cannot identify its own rows: three configurations (flat judge, flat + schema, source-only) carry identical attribute entries on the same backbone although the paper reports different recalls for them, and the source-only arm is defined nowhere in the text. — see 5.1
- **W3:** The census---the contribution the paper itself designates as "the paper's claim"---is a single-backbone measurement whose second-backbone check reverses the pattern; the paper discloses this everywhere, but the contribution's generality is capped in a way no revision can widen. — see 2.4
- **W4:** Small reporting gaps in the headline claims: the abstract's counterfactual sentence drops the counting-convention qualifier; the counterfactual is not priced on the net axis the paper uses elsewhere (its own figures give a net loss of two); and the summary layers omit the evidence-access result, which is the same magnitude and survives correction. — see 2.5, 2.6, 2.7
- **W5:** Verifiability boundaries are lightly drawn: the pipeline's own stage code is not enumerated among the shipped materials, it is not stated whether the as-produced package generation that the pair-audit rates describe ships, and three computations entering reported numbers---the catch-all composition, the expectation-framing check, and the C row's evidence classification---are printed but unscripted. — see 4.2, 4.3

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Importance & Scope** — Excellent
   - **1.1** (§1, §2) The paper pins a real and specific target class rather than a generic framing: it argues that VDBMS defects are mostly non-crashing and that the one dedicated VDBMS fuzzer's oracle fires on a 5xx or a failed request, so a service returning HTTP 200 with wrong results is structurally out of its reach. (I take the fuzzer's characterization from the paper; I surveyed nothing, so this is provisional.) The class is then fixed by two maintainer-confirmed instances---Milvus \#49823's \texttt{nprobe} bound and Qdrant \#10369's dimension coupling that appears only after a state transition---and by an explicit consistency-vs-correctness separation that tells the reader what is and is not being measured.
   - **1.2** (§4.1–4.2, §6) Scope is declared and priced where it matters: 81 adjudicated cases from three systems and 16 of the ledger's 19 versions, twelve configurations × three runs, two backbones. The campaign is stated in the abstract, §4.2 and §6 to be a record of submissions and adjudication and not a per-run detection rate; the census is scoped to the primary backbone in §4.5 and again in §6; the one unjudgeable case and the submission screening are both disclosed and priced. For an experience paper this is the right instinct: scope is declared rather than left to inference, which matters because several of these numbers would otherwise be easy to over-read.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The paper reports the baseline's run (205 templates, 50.8 minutes, §4.6) but gives no cost for the pipeline or the campaign---no token or API spend, wall-clock, or the human hours behind the screening step (32 candidates filtered before submission, §4.1) and the hand-adjudications. A reader deciding whether to adopt the method can see the yield but not the price, and the human costs are exactly where this pipeline's effort concentrates.
   - **1.4** The priority claims---"the first quantitative pair-level measurement we know of" (§1, contribution 2) and the Table 1 sweep with its conclusion that no oracle family takes untagged, system-level behavioral prose as its statement of intent---are claims I cannot assess, since this review includes no literature task. I flag them provisional and read the assessment above as bearing on the problem's importance, which the paper does establish from its own record.

2. **Insights & Evidence** — Excellent
   - **2.1** (§4.3, Table 3) The input audit checks every cited pair in the packages as produced against the page it cites at the version the row claims: 18 supported as cited, 58 re-anchored (the anchor was a source file such as a \texttt{constant.go} path, or an API landing page), 58 unsupported on the cited page. The case level is a deliberately different object (12 headline assertions: five supported, three dropped, two over-strong, two weak-evidence), and the paper keeps the two apart. Two mechanisms are named---version drift from one fixed version's contract, and constraints that exist as prose but not as documented values (\texttt{nprobe} ∈ [1, \texttt{nlist}]; \texttt{M} ∈ [4, 64])---and the measurement is validated by a blind second reader (30 of 36 on a stratified sample, κ = 0.75, with all 12 SWAP pairs re-called). The rebuild is then checked for the failure this class invites: zero of 81 packages carry an expectation-framed sentence, the 19 phrasing hits being verbatim server responses. This quantifies a step that is normally invisible and checks itself rather than asserting.
   - **2.2** (§4.5, Table 4) The census is defended on construction rather than on reach. The letters are identified by cell content on the primary backbone (no cell outside C records a weak refutation; only D carries the source vocabulary), the materials rival is separated by a cross-tab showing that the 24 wrong contract-refutation closures fall on packages the audit judged supported (20 of the 24 on the 59 documented-evidence packages, rate 0.11; none on the 18 without), and the load-bearing count is checked for invariance: identical under both aggregation rules the dispatch prints, computed on maintainer-confirmed labels, and post-repair, with the pre-repair 47/20 split disclosed. The rival that cannot be settled---that contract refutation fires first on a larger, harder set---is stated, and the prescription is deliberately rested on error mass rather than on the accuracy comparison. This is the most carefully constructed measurement in the paper, and the honesty about what it does not settle is part of why it can be trusted.
   - **2.3** (§4.4, §4.5, §4.6) Negative results get the same resolution as positive ones: the second backbone's census (contract refutation closes 56, by-design 50 with 22 wrong, the unguarded clause supplying 24 of 48 incorrect false-positive closures---50% rather than 80%); the four-perspective contrast at p = 1.0 on the primary (4/4, interval ±0.11, so the data exclude an effect above about eleven points) and −11 true bugs on the second; the source-withheld arm's net and $F_1$ ties; the independent adjudicator's κ = −0.01/0.08/0.11, which turns the joint reading into a bound; and the baseline's zero, bounded by the released mutation vocabulary rather than by the oracle family. An experience paper is judged by what a reader can carry away, and these are the parts that carry.
   - **2.4 [minor, unfixable]** (§4.4, §4.5, abstract) The paper designates the census as its claim ("Section 4.5 carries the paper's claim"), and the census is the one contribution whose own second-backbone check reverses it. The paper is scrupulous---abstract, §4.5, §5 and §6 all carry the scope---so the claim as stated is supported; but the generality is capped by data no revision can widen, and the prescription reduces to "advice about where to look", which is a smaller object than a clause-level error census initially sounds. This bounds the weight the third contribution can carry; it does not undermine the claims as scoped, and the rating above reflects the evidence for the claims as stated.
   - **2.5 [minor, fixable]** (Abstract, vs §1 and §4.5) The abstract's closing sentence---"routing it instead of closing on it recovers seven true bugs for nine more false positives released"---drops the qualifier the rest of the paper is careful about: §4.5 shows the forced reading changes nothing, and §5 says the price is one "the convention sets rather than the stage's decisions". Since the paper itself insists the convention is not free, the abstract should carry the condition.
   - **2.6 [minor, fixable]** (§4.5 vs §4.4) The counterfactual is priced on recall and suppression but not on the net axis the paper uses elsewhere, where net true positives minus leaked false positives is 30 either way. On the same axis the counterfactual's own figures give 46 − 18 = 28 against the deployed 39 − 9 = 30: the prescription is an error-mass argument, not a net-gain argument, and printing the net would be consistent with §4.4 and pre-empt the objection.
   - **2.7 [minor, fixable]** (§8 vs §4.4) The conclusion's answer---"what moves the outcome is how often the judge declines to decide"---is supported by the routing bundle (forced 25→27; convention 30→39), but the summary layers (abstract, §8, the §5 lessons) omit the paper's other large recall movement: withholding the implementation source raises recall 39→48, a contrast that also clears the Holm correction at the recall level, with a forced component of +4 (27→31). The paper reports that finding as a net tie and claims nothing more; the issue is only that the summarized answer is narrower than §4.4's evidence.

3. **Perspective** — Excellent
   - **3.1** (§5) Five lessons, each tied to a named finding and each transferable beyond this deployment: audit what the oracle reads, not only what it concludes (motivated by the 13%/43%/43% split and by the cross-tab showing the error mass is not the material's fault); put the evidence guard where the error mass is (42 of the 50 closures cite neither intent evidence nor a maintainer quote); price the deferral channel and the contrast, not just the level (the routing change is worth nine under the convention, three to four once the routed queue is hand-adjudicated, two under forced verdicts); check the dispatch before re-architecting the judge (a one-line schema repair moved five of the headline nine bugs); and self-audit the stage against its own printed rule (22 forward departures, 10 of the 17 forced-to-false-positive ones on real bugs). "Price the deferral channel" is the most useful thing here for anyone comparing LLM judges: a recall difference between two judges may be a difference in how often they abstain and in how the deployment counts abstention. The paper is careful to say which lessons do not travel (its audit rates are one distiller's; the guard rule is advice about where to look).
   - **3.2** (§1, §5, §6 "Instrument") The paper publishes four defects in its own dispatches---a binary \texttt{verdict} field declared against a three-valued vocabulary in eight of twelve arms; two aggregation rules with opposite defaults printed in one prompt; two perspective definitions with C and D exchanged; and an output schema declaring the source vocabulary for the criterion the text calls cognition, which 98 of 243 cells use---and states that its own audit found none of them. For a paper whose methodological claim is that this pipeline is auditable, that is the right evidence to show, and it is a practice lesson in itself.

4. **Verifiability** — Excellent
   - **4.1** (§4.1 "Statistics"; Data Availability) The text is specific about how the numbers were produced and what recomputes them: each control is a traceable edit of the arm it extends; all dispatch texts ship verbatim, with renderings of the two judging prompts under \texttt{rq2/prompts/}; the frozen per-run verdicts of all twelve configurations, the packages the study read, the pair-audit verdicts, the adjudication worksheet and the blind passes ship; and five named scripts recompute the rates, both backbones' censuses (including the letter-identification test), both replays, the net/$F_1$ intervals and the pair audit. I judged this from the text and did not fetch the link (per instructions); read as a declared and described artifact, the recomputation path is unusually complete.
   - **4.2 [minor, fixable]** (Data Availability, vs §3 and §4.2) The statement enumerates what ships but leaves the boundary partly implicit: the pipeline's own stage code (extractors, attack agents, executor, gates) is not among the listed materials, so the campaign of §4.2 is not reproducible even in principle from the package; and it is not stated whether the as-produced (pre-rebuild) generation that §4.3's rates describe ships---only that "the earlier rebuilt generations are archived separately". The paper's claims do not depend on re-running the generation stages, but stating the boundary would save a reader the question.
   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** (§4.1 "Statistics", §4.3, §4.5) Three computations entering reported numbers are printed but not scripted, and §4.1 is explicit about which they are and that they differ from the recomputable set: the catch-all composition backs the 37/32 split of the 69 routed judgments and the 17-of-32 enrichment reading (§4.5); the expectation-framing check backs the "zero of 81 packages" statement (§4.3); and the C row's evidence classification backs the breakdown of the nineteen C=Refuted closures (15 comment or docstring, 2 name-validation rule, 2 code structure) and the two cases where the verbatim requirement was not applied (§4.5). These are separate from the quantities §4.1 does list as recomputable, which include the guard price---read off the fifty contract-refutation (A=Refuted) closures, a different row from the C=Refuted nineteen---and both census replays; so the gap is narrower than it first looks, but those three quantities can only be hand-checked.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1 [minor, fixable]** (Table 2) The configuration table's three attribute columns (perspectives, rule, source) cannot identify its own rows: the flat judge (\#2), flat + schema fix (\#3) and source-only (\#6) all read "---, ---, •, GLM-5.3-Flash", yet the paper reports different recalls for them (30, 35 and 36 of 51 in §4.4). \#2 vs \#3 differ by the schema line, which §4.1 explains in prose; \#2 vs \#6 is explained nowhere, and "source-only" is never defined. Since §4.4 asks the reader to follow contrasts across these arms, the table should carry the distinguishing attribute (e.g., the dispatch variant or the evidence scope) and the arm should be defined.
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** (§4.4 and throughout) The prose carries very high numeric density in a compressed notation: "The schema-line repair alone moves recall 30→35 (recall level 0/5, p=0.0625; confirmed-set level 34→39, 1/6, p=0.1250) and forced recall 25→29; adding the routing rule on top moves recall 35→39 at the recall level (2/6, p=0.2891; confirmed-set 39→51, 2/14, p=0.0042) and, forced, the other way (29→27, 3/1)." A reader must hold thirteen numbers and two conventions at once, and the a/b convention (a = the arm printed first) is stated once in §4.1 and not restated afterwards. Splitting per contrast and printing the convention beside each contrast would cost little and repay a lot.
