## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary
The paper targets *documentation-implementation bugs* in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): inputs silently accepted or behaviors silently produced that contradict the vendor's natural-language API documentation. Its argument is that this bug class is invisible to the existing dedicated VDBMS fuzzer (crash-oracle by design) and equally out of reach of every deterministic oracle candidate, because those anchor their expectations in crash signals, another implementation, output-output relations, machine-checkable properties, or structured schemas — never in system-level prose; reading the prose is left to an LLM. The system, TestVDB, is a four-stage LLM-agent pipeline: documentation crawling and specification extraction, strategy-pre-bound test generation, sandboxed execution, and a confirmation stage that splits an evidence builder from a chain auditor which cross-examines a five-section evidence chain from contract, physical, maintainer-cognition, and source perspectives.

The evaluation reports an operational, maintainer-adjudicated ledger (81 adjudicated submissions across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate: 51 confirmed real bugs, 23 fixed by merged PRs), a controlled three-run re-adjudication of the same 81-candidate pool on frozen packages under blinding that measures both sides of the confirmation stage's tradeoff (per-run false-positive suppression 0.867-0.900 for the contract core at recall 0.196-0.333; the full stage at suppression 1.000, majority-vote recall 0.373), an independent source-only arm over the same packages (recall 0.333, precision 0.944), and a bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz on Qdrant that finds the two oracles reach complementary bug classes.

### Core Strengths
- **S1:** Real, externally checkable maintenance impact, with the ledger's internal validity probed rather than asserted (fix-nature classification validated on a disproved case) — see 1.2, 3.1.
- **S2:** Unusually disciplined measurement design for an LLM-oracle paper: blinding on frozen packages, three independent runs, a genuinely independent single-perspective arm, an explicit refusal to count a re-aggregation as an arm, and reported statistics whose arithmetic I verified — see 3.2, 3.3.
- **S3:** The oracle-exclusion analysis (Table 1) holds against the named competitors I checked, and the paper states its own delta against the closest one (MASTOR) honestly, conceding the overlapping components — see 2.1, 2.2.
- **S4:** The method's boundary is characterized, not hidden: three named recall-loss mechanisms with per-system concentration, a stratification that rules out the easy explanation for the aggregate, and disclosure of an earlier, better-looking unblinded protocol — see 3.4.

### Core Weaknesses
- **W1:** The recall figures are the confirmation stage's recall over the paper's own submitted pool, but the abstract states them without that conditioning, and one sentence about the "lower bound" is ambiguous/inverted — see 3.5, 5.5.
- **W2:** The extraction stage is never measured, yet four of the thirty false-positive leaks are attributed to it and excluded from the confirmation stage's error budget — see 3.6.
- **W3:** A factual claim about the VDBFuzz baseline's released templates is incorrect as written ("none probes `wait=false`"); three released Qdrant templates do — see 3.7.
- **W4:** No artifact is reachable at review time: the prompts, frozen packs, judgment records, and RQ3 run configurations are absent from the text and the promised anonymized URL does not exist yet — see 4.1.
- **W5:** The four-perspective architecture is only partly attributed: source (D) is isolated as an arm, cognition (C) cannot be, and the aggregation rule is uncalibrated and unswept — see 3.8.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The problem is established with the field's own empirical anchors rather than asserted: Section 1 and Section 2.2 build on the VDBMS bug study (most defects are non-crashing functional failures) and on the testing roadmap, which names oracle definition as the open challenge for exactly this residual. The concrete harm story — a silently wrong result entering a RAG prompt with no crash and no error code — is specific to the systems at issue and easy to check on the API surface.
   - **1.2** The RQ1 result (Section 4.2, Table 2) is real maintenance impact: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs (50 carrying the projects' own bug label), 23 fixed via merged PRs, with a fix-nature classifier showing every one of the 23 modifies implementation code and none is documentation-only — and the classifier is validated on the one case the authors later disproved (Qdrant #9149's test-only PR), which is a rare self-check.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The significance claim mixes a human-machine deployment with the automated method: submission was author-decided with no deterministic rule and the pool is submission-filtered (Section 4.2, "The 30 adjudicated false positives…"), so the reader cannot quantify how much of the impact the automated stage would carry alone. The paper discloses the direction of bias but reports no number for it (e.g., how many pipeline-confirmed candidates were dropped pre-submission, or what fraction of the run's funnel the 81 represent).

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** I checked the claimed delta against the closest competitor, MASTOR (Deng et al., arXiv 2606.10465): MASTOR grounds its oracles in the implementation source (per-field status oracles plus behavioural-consistency oracles over operation sequences, with a ChallengerAgent reviewing generated oracles), and its own stated limitation is that it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code". The paper's characterization is accurate, it concedes the two overlapping ingredients (Section 6, "Oracles derived from structured sources"), and the claimed delta — prose as ground, implementation as falsifier, the "anchor inversion" — therefore holds as stated.
   - **2.2** The Table 1 exclusion claims survived verification against the named works I checked this session: VDBFuzz (Wang et al., ICSE 2026) is crash-only by its own statement; SATORI (Alonso et al., ASE 2025) generates per-response-field oracles from OpenAPI field names and descriptions; Metamon (Lee, An, Yoo, LLM4Code 2025) judges captured method-level regression-test oracles against Javadoc with an LLM (reported numbers verified: precision 0.722 / recall 0.480 at its permissive threshold, 0.967 at recall 0.099); Toradocu (Goffi et al., ISSTA 2016) is tagged-`@throws`, method-level, classical-NLP; AGORA+ is trace-anchored; CASCADE's abstract (Kiecker et al., FSE 2026, DOI 10.1145/3808175 — metadata only, full text unreachable this session) confirms its two-condition cross-check (existing code fails a test while documentation-generated code passes the same test). I found no counterexample to the claim that none of them derives its expectation from system-level, untagged prose.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** Missing related work (provisional — surfaced by a scoped coverage search this session, abstract/snippet level only): LogicHunter (arXiv 2607.06195) is an agentic oracle that uses source-code inspection plus executable validation to decide which failing tests are real bugs, evaluated on a developer-labelled corpus of 28 genuine bugs against 972 non-bugs (precision ≈0.91, recall ≈0.72 as reported) — the closest uncited work to the confirmation stage's function, with a negative pool roughly 32× the 30 false positives here. Also uncited and relevant to the extraction premise: "Documentation vs. Code Patterns" (arXiv 2608.00884), which reports that removing Javadoc clauses moves TOG-family oracle prediction by under one percentage point, i.e. doc-derived oracle accuracy can be driven by shortcut cues rather than documentation semantics. Section 6 should position the paper against these; no new evidence is required.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The ground-truth protocol (Section 4.2) is well built: an explicit adjudication rule (confirmed / false positive / duplicate-tracked as an orthogonal axis), re-verification of every submission's state as of September 2026, exclusion of the 49 unadjudicated submissions from every rate, and revisable adjudication demonstrated by the #9149 reclassification and its classifier cross-check. The confirmed counts are checkable against public trackers (specific issue numbers are named).
   - **3.2** The RQ2 protocol is the paper's strongest methodological component (Section 4.3 and the paragraph "A re-aggregation is not an arm"): frozen packages (observed transcript + documented contract), identical packages across three independent runs, blinding from adjudication labels and issue history, per-case judgments recorded as run output with an 81/81 denominator, a conservative binary rule resolving uncertainty toward False-Positive, a distinct independent source-only arm, and an explicit refusal to treat a re-decision of recorded fields as a measurement of that configuration. Rejecting the tempting shortcut is exactly right.
   - **3.3** The statistics are honest and internally consistent. I recomputed the reported exact McNemar p-values (16-vs-7 → 0.093; 17-vs-5 → 0.017; 11-vs-8 → 0.648) and the arithmetic behind every rate and ratio (e.g. 0.216→0.333→0.373, the 0.117-vs-0.040 step comparison, TP+FN = 51 and leaks+intercepted = 30 in every row of Tables 4 and 5); all check out. Wilson intervals accompany the headline rates, and per-run ranges are given for the arms.
   - **3.4** The recall-loss diagnosis (Section 4.3, "Recall loss is systematic, not noise" and "Where the boundary sits") is diagnostic rather than defensive: three named mechanisms — semantics not expressible as an assertion, approximate behavior compatible with an undocumented accuracy contract, and cognition-dependent defectiveness — with per-system concentration (Weaviate in (i), Qdrant in (ii)), plus the stratification (Table 6) showing that packs with a pinned contract row recall no better than those without, which removes the obvious extraction-gap explanation for the aggregate. The disclosure that an earlier unblinded protocol had reported recall 0.841 is candor the paper did not have to show.
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** The recall denominator is under-stated in the front matter and one sentence runs backwards (Section 4.3): "the recall figures below are a lower bound on what the automated confirmation stage misses on its own" reads as a floor on the miss rate, whereas the surrounding argument treats 0.373 as a floor on recall (the pool is harder than the raw stream: only candidates that survived author-side review were submitted). Since the abstract's "recalls only 0.196–0.333 of the maintainer-confirmed bugs" is denominator-ambiguous to a reader who has not reached Section 4.3, add the conditioning ("on the re-adjudicated submission pool") in the abstract and fix the sentence's direction.
   - **3.6 [major, fixable]** The extraction stage (Section 3.2) is never evaluated, yet it supplies the frozen packs and absorbs the blame for residual error. Section 4.3: "Because the frozen packs are products of the extraction stage, these four leaks are extraction-side errors that sit outside the confirmation-stage error budget reported here" — the four are counted in the reported 26/30 suppression, but their attribution rests only on the observation that the packs "contain contract rows that read as supporting the violation". No constraint-level audit of extraction is reported (how accurately constraints match the cited documentation pages; how many packs contain misleading rows), even though stage (i) is the pipeline's largest semantic-interpretation step and its quality bounds the end-to-end reading of both headline rates. The material for an audit exists (vendor doc versions plus frozen packs); report it, or state explicitly that end-to-end precision is unmeasured.
   - **3.7 [minor, fixable]** The RQ3 justification for #9045's escape is factually wrong about the baseline's released artifact (Section 4.4): "every points-upsert template uses `wait=true` (the path that correctly rejects) and none probes `wait=false`". Checking the released set this session (`security-pride/VDBFuzz`, `templates/qdrant/`: 205 `.py` templates plus `generation_summary.json`, matching the paper's own count), three templates issue `PUT /collections/client_test/points?wait=false` — `test_qdrant_client_test_point_upload_test.py`, `test_qdrant_client_test_records_upload_test.py`, and `test_qdrant_client_test_multiple_vectors_test.py` (the last contains no `wait=true` at all). What survives, and is presumably what the authors meant, is narrower: the empty-vector template uses the synchronous path, so the zero-length-vector scenario is not exercised with `wait=false`. The complementarity conclusion is unaffected (it rests on the silent-accept/crash asymmetry and the full-coverage run), but the sentence should be corrected.
   - **3.8 [minor, fixable]** The confirmation contribution is only partly attributed, and the measured configuration is not the shipped one. (a) The independent arm isolates D; C "cannot be isolated into a blinding-clean arm without circularity" and is reported "as suggestive only", so "cross-examined from four perspectives" is measured as C+D bundled versus D alone. (b) RQ2 forces the binary projection ("uncertainty always resolves toward False-Positive"; "The full stage's Neutral route… lies outside this measurement"), so the perfect-precision/0.373-recall pair describes a forced-binary variant rather than the deployed stage that can route a case to a human. (c) The priority rule is admitted to be "one uncalibrated choice among several defensible aggregations" and was not swept. Each is disclosed; together they bound how far the mechanism statement can be pushed.
   - **3.9 [minor, fixable]** RQ3 bundles two protocols and two versions into one comparison (Section 4.4): the "0 of 7" result is a 26,000-request sampled run on v1.18.2 with a budget the authors set, while the "full search space" result (205 templates, 50.8 minutes, zero crashes) is on v1.18.0. The claim "complementary by construction---now measured over VDBFuzz's full search space" is only version-locally supported; state the version attached to each half and give the commit hash/digest of the "commit-matched" instance.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

*Specialty areas: (a) LLM-derived test oracles / oracle generation for REST APIs; (b) empirical evaluation methodology for bug-finding tools. Within those areas I verified the paper's own named competitors against cached full texts — VDBFuzz, SATORI, MASTOR, Metamon (full reads); CASCADE (abstract level only — no reachable open-access copy this session); RBCTest (not retrievable — not yet indexed by the sources reachable here). Outside these areas I assess from the paper's own claims and internal evidence.*

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets *documentation-implementation bugs* in vector database management systems: behaviors that violate a VDBMS's own API documentation without crashing — an out-of-range search parameter accepted with HTTP 200, a cross-collection dimension coupling silently bypassed, a documented bound enforced where no schema states it. It argues, by walking the standard oracle candidates (crash signals, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property-based testing, and REST oracles derived from OpenAPI, traces, or source), that each anchors its expectation somewhere other than system-level natural-language prose, so an LLM reading the documentation is the practical oracle for this residual — and that this imports the LLM's false-positive problem, since the same model family writes and judges the oracle.

TestVDB is a four-stage pipeline: documentation crawled into a per-endpoint knowledge base and distilled into typed, evidence-leveled constraints; test scripts generated by three attack agents under deterministically pre-bound strategies; execution in a Docker-pinned sandbox with raw HTTP logging; and a confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly from a chain-auditing judge applying four mechanical checks and four perspectives (contract, physical, cognition, source), with the implementation source as the falsification anchor.

The evaluation reports three things. An operational ledger across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate: 81 adjudicated submissions, 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 fixed via merged PRs, with 49 submissions still unadjudicated and excluded. A controlled three-run re-adjudication of the same 81-candidate pool under frozen packages and blinding: the contract core suppresses 87–90% of false positives with 88.9% case-level agreement while recalling 0.196–0.333 of the confirmed bugs; the full stage reaches 0.373 at perfect suppression; and an independent source-only arm over the same packages recovers 0.333. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows complementary reach: on Qdrant v1.18.2, 26,000 mutated requests from the crash oracle reached none of seven live silent-accept true positives, while TestVDB reaches VDBFuzz's integer-overflow crash class on v1.4.0 by contract reasoning.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The structural account of *why* the deterministic-oracle candidates miss the documentation-implementation residual (Table 1, Section 2.4) is the paper's most reusable contribution, and its claims about two of the three REST-oracle tools it names (SATORI and MASTOR) survive verification against their own texts; AGORA+ was not verified in this review — see 1.1, 2.1.
- **S2:** An operational, maintainer-adjudicated result with the ledger discipline to match: an explicit confirmed/false-positive/duplicate-tracked taxonomy, a fix-nature classifier that is validated on a known case, and revisable adjudication — see 1.2, 3.2.
- **S3:** The RQ2 protocol is unusually well controlled for this literature: three independent runs per configuration, frozen packages, no case dropped, Wilson intervals, exact McNemar tests (which I recomputed and which are correct), a leakage audit of the maintainer-cognition corpus, and the disclosure that an earlier, unblinded protocol had reported recall 0.841 where the blinded protocol reports 0.373 — see 3.1.
- **S4:** The bidirectional VDBFuzz probe is fair, and its reading of VDBFuzz's protocol and Qdrant crash record is corroborated against VDBFuzz's published text; the repository-level template question is carried by 3.8, not settled here — see 1.3, 3.8.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The abstract's attribution claim — that the source-only arm "locat[es] source grounding as the mechanism's largest single contributor" — is not supported at conventional significance by the paper's own paired test (McNemar p = 0.093), which the body reports but the abstract does not hedge — see 3.3.
- **W2:** The recall denominator includes defects the paper itself places outside the assertion framework, and the three named recall-loss mechanisms are never counted, so the reader cannot see how much of the published recall loss is in-scope loss — see 3.4, 3.5.
- **W3:** No arm isolates the confirmation stage's scaffolding (five-section evidence chain, builder/auditor split) from a flat same-evidence LLM judge, so the machinery's own contribution over a Metamon-style single-judge design is unmeasured — see 3.6.
- **W4:** The novelty delta needs sharper positioning against the LLM documentation-oracle line, and the Pivoted Query Synthesis omission leaves the DBMS-logic-bug paragraph incomplete — see 2.2, 2.3, 2.4.
- **W5:** The replication package is not yet available and the 81-case pool is not enumerable from the text, so the adjudication-based ground truth cannot be checked at case level — see 4.2, 4.3.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Excellent
   - **1.1** Section 2.4 and Table 1 walk seven oracle candidates and give, per candidate, the structural reason it misses the residual — crash oracles fire only on crashes (the paper notes only 2 of its 51 confirmed bugs crash); differential testing has no cross-vendor reference because VDBMS semantics diverge by design; metamorphic relations check relations the tester already poses; property-based testing needs the prose claim itself as a machine-checkable property; structured-spec oracles bound fields rather than behavior. I checked two of the three REST-oracle rows against fetched competitor texts: SATORI's oracle catalog is indeed per-response-field and OAS-anchored, and MASTOR's own stated limitation — "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code" — is exactly the distance the paper claims; AGORA+ I did not verify, so its row stands on the paper's characterization alone. This is a well-argued delimitation of a real, under-tested problem class, and it is the part of the paper most likely to be reused by others.
   - **1.2** The yield case is concrete and actionable: Table 2 (81 adjudicated / 51 confirmed / 23 fixed / 30 false positives across three production systems), with 50 of 51 carrying an official bug label, plus the fix-nature classification showing all 23 fix PRs touch implementation code and none is documentation-only (Section 4.2). Merged fixes in production systems are the strongest available signal that a bug-finding paper's finds are real, and the paper reports both sides of the ledger rather than the find count alone.
   - **1.3** RQ3's bidirectional design is the right way to compare oracle classes: the systematic direction (seven live silent-accept true positives on Qdrant v1.18.2, none reached by 26,000 VDBFuzz-mutated requests) is explained structurally rather than statistically — a crash oracle cannot fire on a non-crashing accept — and the reverse direction (TestVDB reaching VDBFuzz's integer-overflow crash on v1.4.0 by contract reasoning) shows the reach is not one-way. I verified against the cached VDBFuzz full text that its protocol is "two hours (120 minutes) on each VDBMS", that its Qdrant crash table lists "OOM, FPE, DoS", and that the string "wait" never occurs in its paper — consistent with the paper's hedge that the released-template observation "is not about VDBFuzz's paper".
   - **1.4 [minor, fixable]** The cost profile in Section 4.1 (≈67 active hours over five calendar days, ≈860 sandboxed executions, ≈70M input / 4.9M output tokens for the orchestrating session alone, a lower bound) is reported but never weighed against the yield it produced: the same run (Section 4.2) registered five new defect numbers and 60 defect-confirming candidates of which most were re-confirmations or unsubmitted. For a paper whose pitch is a practical oracle for a production system class, a marginal-cost-per-new-defect figure — and the human screening time, which is part of the operational protocol — would let a reader judge whether the pipeline is worth deploying rather than merely worth studying.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** The strongest novel element is the *anchor inversion* plus the independence of the falsifier, and it holds against the closest prior work when checked directly. MASTOR (fetched) derives oracles from implementation source, reads only source-verified OpenAPI fields, and demotes unsupported claims — so its authority is the implementation; TestVDB takes the prose as ground and uses the source only to falsify, which is the complement, and MASTOR's own limitations section concedes the gap it cannot close. Metamon (fetched) asks an LLM whether captured behavior agrees with the documented specification, stabilized by metamorphic prompt pairs and self-consistency — and its own text confirms "no independent (non-LLM) evidence source is consulted", which is precisely the self-reference TestVDB's evidence chain is built to break; the paper's quoted Metamon numbers (0.722/0.480 at the permissive threshold on 9,482 pairs; precision 0.967–1.000 only below recall 0.1) are accurate. The paper also concedes, correctly, that a second LLM agent reviewing generated oracles is not new (MASTOR's ChallengerAgent) and that cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to it.
   - **2.2 [minor, fixable]** The expectation-derivation half of the pipeline is a recognizable extension of the LLM documentation-oracle line rather than a new mechanism: SATORI already has an LLM read natural-language OpenAPI field descriptions to produce assertions, Metamon already tests behavior against a *documented specification*, and RBCTest — the authors themselves note this in Section 6 — mines constraints including natural-language parameter descriptions and covers request parameters as well as responses. The delta is real but it is a delta of *surface* (endpoint-level accept/reject and stateful HTTP prose vs. per-field/structured/method-level anchors), not of kind. The paper should state that axis explicitly — "untagged, system-level, state-coupling prose" as the new expectation surface, with the four near-neighbours named and disposed of one sentence each — instead of leaving the boundary to emerge from Table 1's row structure, where it currently reads as a cleaner separation than the sources support.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** Table 1's row 6 groups RBCTest with method-level Javadoc tools (@tComment, JDoctor, DocTer, Toradocu) under "method-level constraints and specifications from tagged or structured prose", while Section 6 concedes that RBCTest's constraint surface "is closer to our accept/reject surface than a response-field framing suggests". A table should not assert a distance the prose disclaims; move RBCTest into the REST-oracle row (5) with the same qualifier the prose uses. (I could not retrieve RBCTest's own text this session — see 2.5 — so I flag the internal inconsistency between the paper's own two characterizations, not a mischaracterization of RBCTest.)
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** Related-work completeness in the DBMS-logic-bug paragraph of Section 6 ("VDBMS testing"): the sentence enumerating the intra-system differential-oracle line (NoREC, TLP, DQE, DDLCheck, and BUZZBEE as generic fuzzing) omits Pivoted Query Synthesis (Rigger & Su, OSDI 2020), whose full bibliographic entry I verified in the cached VDBFuzz reference list fetched this session; it is the sibling oracle to NoREC the paper already cites and the one most often associated with non-crashing DBMS logic bugs. In the same paragraph's spirit, the LLM-to-formal-specification step could be acknowledged with one clause to Endres et al. (PACMSE/FSE 2024, "Can LLMs Transform Natural Language Intent into Formal Method Postconditions?"; existence verified at metadata level only), which addresses the same prose-to-assertion transformation in a different artifact. Both are completeness items; neither threatens the paper's claims.
   - **2.5** *Verification status (not a fault of the paper):* CASCADE's design characterization ("generates code from the documentation to cross-check the generated tests") matches its abstract, which I retrieved; the quoted sentence about its two residual false positives could not be verified — no open-access copy was reachable (Unpaywall points to arXiv 2604.19400, unreachable from this environment), so I treat that quotation as provisional and do not count it either way. RBCTest is cited as an ICSE 2026 paper and is not yet indexed by the sources reachable here; its characterization likewise stands as the authors' claim.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The RQ2 measurement is the paper's methodological high point, and it survived my checks. Every table row sums correctly (17/34 + 4/26 = 51/30 per core run; 19/32 + 0/30 for the full-stage majority), per-run precisions (0.750–0.810, majority 0.733) and recalls match the quoted counts, and the quoted exact McNemar p-values are arithmetically correct (16-vs-7 → 0.093; 17-vs-5 → 0.017; 11-vs-8 → 0.648 — I recomputed all three). The protocol removes the two judgment-side threats the paper itself names (expectation framing, observation leakage) by construction, audits the maintainer-cognition corpus for contamination from the authors' own submissions and reports that removing the six affected entries changed no verdict, and — most credibly — the paper reports the price of that rigor by disclosing that an earlier unblinded protocol had produced recall 0.841 where the blinded protocol produces 0.373 (Section 4.3). Reporting a five-fold-lower number for one's own mechanism is the kind of disclosure that raises rather than lowers my confidence in the rest.
   - **3.2** Section 4.2's ground-truth discipline is careful in the three places it matters: the confirmed category is defined operationally and the duplicate status is handled as orthogonal to the confirmed/false-positive axis rather than folded into it; the fix-nature classifier is validated by being applied to the disproved #9149, which it correctly identifies as a test-only PR; and adjudication is treated as revisable after the authors' own seven-version re-probe reclassified a candidate (Section 5, construct validity). Rates for the 49 unadjudicated submissions are excluded rather than assumed.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The abstract's final attribution — "an independent source-only arm over the same packages recovers 0.333, locating source grounding as the mechanism's largest single contributor" — restates as established what Section 4.3 itself qualifies: the paired comparison between the contract core and the source-only arm is 16 wins to 7 losses, exact McNemar p = 0.093, "not individually significant", and the full-stage-vs-core comparison that is significant (p = 0.017) compares a bundle, not source grounding alone. The ordering of the three point estimates (0.216 < 0.333 < 0.373) is consistent with the claim, but with 81 cases the interval around a +0.117 difference comfortably includes zero, and "nearly three times the step" is a ratio of two differences with no interval at all. Either report a paired confidence interval or bootstrap for the two differences and let significance decide, or keep the abstract's phrasing to the ordering ("source grounding accounts for the larger share of the gain") — as written, the strongest causal statement in the paper is the one place where the statistical apparatus is not carried through.
   - **3.4 [minor, fixable]** The recall denominators and the framework's stated scope do not line up. Section 4.2 states that the five Result-Incorrectness bugs "sit outside the assertion framework by construction---no documented accuracy contract bounds approximate behavior", and Section 4.3 lists exactly that class as recall-loss mechanism (ii); yet all recall figures in Tables 4 and 5 are computed over all 51 true positives with no eligible-subset figure reported. The correction runs in the paper's favor (a core recall of 11/46 rather than 11/51), and the boundary claim needs both numbers to be read correctly: the current presentation makes the mechanism look weaker than it is on its own domain, while making the domain's limits look like mechanism limits. Report recall over all 51 and over the assertion-eligible subset side by side, and say which one the abstract quotes.
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** The three recall-loss mechanisms in Section 4.3 (inexpressible joint semantics; approximate-behavior defects; defects whose defectiveness depends on maintainer cognition) are described qualitatively, with only a per-system concentration ("most Weaviate misses fall in mechanism (i) and most Qdrant misses in mechanism (ii)") and no counts over the 40 majority-missed cases. Since this decomposition is the paper's central explanation of the boundary — and the basis for the claim that misses are "systematic, not noise" — a count table (per mechanism, per run, with the inter-run stability of the labels) is the evidence the claim needs.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** The ablation arms isolate *perspective sets* (contract core; +C+D; D-only) but never isolate the confirmation stage's *structure*. A flat baseline in which the same frozen evidence — transcript plus contract, plus the source clone in the D-only arm — is given to a single-prompt LLM judge, without the five-section chain, the four mechanical checks, or the builder/auditor split, would tell a reader whether the machinery earns its complexity or whether the result is carried by putting the source into the judge's context. The paper itself names Metamon as the prior art for this division of labour, which makes the missing comparison the most natural one in the area. If a fresh run is not possible, the recorded D fields and a re-run of a single-prompt judge over the same 81 packages would settle it.
   - **3.7 [minor, fixable]** The maintainer-cognition perspective (C) is mined from the same trackers that resolved the pool's candidates, and the paper's contamination control — re-mining one tracker's file to drop six candidate-anchored entries and re-adjudicating the eleven affected cases with no verdict change — is reported for entries anchored on the authors' own submissions, but the snapshot boundary is never stated: 23 of the 51 true positives have merged upstream fix PRs, and a cognition corpus that includes those PRs has access to the label for a substantial slice of the true-positive side. The risk is bounded by the D-only arm (which uses no cognition and reaches 0.333 of the full stage's 0.373, so at most ~0.04 of recall could be cognition-mediated), but a sentence fixing the snapshot time relative to the submissions would close the question outright.
   - **3.8 [minor, fixable]** Section 4.4's reverse-direction paragraph carries two claims that outrun their support. First, "none of the three reproduced on our v1.18.2 instance, so the run cleanly separates the two oracles' reach on this version" — non-reproduction of VDBFuzz's own crashes on a later version is evidence of version drift, not of oracle reach; the reach separation is established by the 0-of-7 result and the v1.4.0 reverse direction, both of which are strong on their own, and the sentence should say only that. Second, the released-template observation in the revision I reviewed ("every points-upsert template uses \texttt{wait=true} … none probes \texttt{wait=false}") is a repository-level universal whose only citation is the template count in \texttt{templates/qdrant/generation\_summary.json}; it is reported to have been withdrawn in revision — a check of the released Qdrant templates found three files that do issue \texttt{wait=false} upserts (\texttt{test\_qdrant\_client\_test\_point\_upload\_test.py}, \texttt{test\_qdrant\_client\_test\_records\_upload\_test.py}, \texttt{test\_qdrant\_client\_test\_multiple\_vectors\_test.py}; the check is reported to me, not reproduced in this environment) — and if any form of the universal survives, it should be dropped or restated file-by-file so the claim is checkable.

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate
   - **4.1** The text describes how the evidence was produced with more precision than is common in this area: each stage's artifacts are named and characterized (`knowledge.json` with its coverage and version-alignment gates; `specifications.json` records with `constraint_id`, assertion, type, evidence tier, source-verification flag, and level; `evidence_chain.json` with its five sections; `.executor.env`, `output_$B.log` and atomic `.done` markers with per-batch count reconciliation; `developer_cognition.json`), the generation gates are enumerated, and the RQ2 protocol (frozen packages, conservative binary rule, majority vote, blinding) is stated well enough to follow and to check against the tables — which I did, and the statistics hold.
   - **4.2 [minor, fixable]** The Data Availability section promises a replication package at "an anonymized URL" that does not yet exist ("the link will be added here once the archive is published"). For this review round the text is therefore the bar, and the text withholds the materials a replicator most needs: the agent role prompts, the strategy registry with its trigger predicates, the evidence-chain schema and a worked example, and the exact frozen packages. Declaring the link at submission time — even to an anonymized archive with the ledger inside — would move this criterion to Excellent.
   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** The 81-case pool is not enumerable from the paper. Twelve distinct issue numbers appear in the text — the eight representatives in Table 3, the remaining members of the duplicate trio (#52310, #52312), plus #9524 and #9149 — leaving the rest of the adjudicated pool, its versions, and the fix-PR cross-references unlisted. Since the ground truth is maintainer adjudication, a per-case appendix table (system, version, issue/PR link, adjudication status, duplicate flag) is the minimum artifact for the RQ1 and RQ2 numbers to be checkable by a reader who cannot run the pipeline.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1 [minor, fixable]** The abstract runs to roughly 310 words and carries the paper's entire quantitative payload — submission counts, fix counts, three recall figures, two suppression figures, the verdict-agreement rate — inside a few sentences. Most readers will take the two headline results from it; it should be cut to essentials, with the ranges moved to the introduction.
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** Several sentences in Section 4.3 stack so much qualification into one clause chain that the point is hard to hold: the priority-rule sentence ("The priority rule itself is one uncalibrated choice among several defensible aggregations (a cognition veto, a contract-dominant rule, and a source-dominant rule are all available from the same recorded judgments); we report the shipped rule…") and the opening of "The result" ("…correcting all four majority misconfirmations of the contract core---each vetoed by in-source by-design or cognition evidence---while adding none; every case-level disagreement…") each run past 60 words with a parenthetical or dash-delimited aside inside a semicolon-joined pair. The argument is sound but a copy-edit pass would materially improve readability.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** The paper distinguishes the stage's verdict `\textsc{False-Positive}` from a "false-positive candidate" only by capitalization after introducing the convention once in Section 4.3. Given how much of the evaluation turns on that distinction, use a visually distinct name for the verdict (and the same for `\textsc{Confirmed}` vs "confirmed bug").

## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Reject

### Summary

The paper targets a class of defects it calls documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems: cases where the system silently accepts an input or produces behavior that violates its natural-language API documentation (e.g., accepting `nprobe=0` when the documentation states a range of $[1, 16384]$). It argues that every deterministic-oracle candidate anchors its expectation somewhere other than system-level prose, leaving an LLM-derived oracle as the practical option, and that this imports a false-positive problem which the paper's design addresses by grounding confirmation outside the LLM.

TestVDB instantiates this as a four-stage pipeline: two-step behavioral specification extraction (crawl-and-distill, with typing, evidence leveling, and source verification), test-script generation by three attack agents under deterministically pre-bound strategies, sandboxed execution against a Docker-pinned target, and a bug-confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly (evidence builder, including source grounding on a version-pinned clone) from cross-examination (chain auditor with four mechanical checks and four perspectives: contract, physical, cognition, source). The paper reports an operational ledger of 81 maintainer-adjudicated submissions across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate (51 confirmed, 23 fixed), a controlled three-run re-adjudication of the same 81-candidate pool under frozen packages (contract-core false-positive suppression 0.867–0.900, recall 0.196–0.333; full stage suppression 1.000, recall 0.373; an independent source-only arm recall 0.333), and a bidirectional reach comparison against VDBFuzz on Qdrant.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The oracle-exclusion analysis (Table 1) and the anchor-inversion argument against MASTOR give the paper a crisp, falsifiable positioning: it is the only candidate deriving expectations from system-level untagged prose. — see 2.1, 2.2
- **S2:** The evaluation is unusually candid about its own weaknesses for the genre: it reports the low recall beside the suppression, retracts an earlier unblinded 0.841, exposes a cognition-file label-leakage near-miss and re-tests it, and labels the re-aggregation as "not an arm." — see 3.2, 3.4
- **S3:** A controlled three-run ablation with a blinded, frozen-package protocol and an independent fourth (source-only) arm — plus exact McNemar tests on the paired judgments — is the right shape of evidence for the paper's central mechanism claim. — see 3.1, 3.3

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The three result sets the paper asks to be read together (RQ1 ledger, RQ2 controlled rates) have incompatible provenance; the paper states the ledgers are not reducible to each other but does not state a rule for what the automated rates imply about the shipped pipeline. — see 3.5, 4.5
- **W2:** The RQ3 headline "reach 0 of 7" compares TestVDB evaluated on its own hand-picked true positives against VDBFuzz evaluated under a different search regime, with no neutral ground truth; that both author-run VDBFuzz experiments returned all-zero crash counts compounds the concern. — see 3.6, 3.7
- **W3:** The introduction's additive framing ("recovers 0.333 of that 0.373"), echoed in the abstract, sits above a body that states the source-only and full-stage arms are statistically indistinguishable; a reader who stops at that framing would draw a stronger compositional conclusion than the data supports. — see 5.1, 3.3
- **W4:** For a paper whose thesis is that evidence must not rest on a single LLM reader, the backbone is disclosed only at family granularity with no snapshot identifier, so no reported run is exactly reproducible. — see 3.9, 4.4

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** Section 2.3 separates consistency (behavior conforms to documentation) from correctness (result accuracy), and Section 3.5's verdict vocabulary plus the Limitations escalation of unassertable candidates (Section 5, internal-validity paragraph) show a careful boundary of what the oracle can decide. A well-drawn, honest scope boundary rather than over-reach.
   - **1.2 [minor, unfixable]** The headline evidence is operational rather than deployable: the RQ1 ledger ran with a human submission filter (Section 4.2 states this explicitly) and the closed, automated configuration that Section 4.3 re-measures loses half to four fifths of the main ground truth (recall 0.373 at best, with two defects visible to neither the assertion framework nor any blinded automated arm). A practitioner adopting the method would get an oracle whose measured behavior is the low-recall, human-reviewed configuration. The authors do not claim otherwise, but the paper never states plainly who should deploy this and what they should expect — a mismatch between the practical framing of the introduction and the demonstrated operating profile.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The engine settings the pipeline reasons about (e.g., `nprobe`, `lookup_from` dimension coupling) are classic query-engine settings, so my provisional generalist read is that the addressed bug class is real and impact-bearing for a specialized audience, while impact outside retrieval infrastructure is bounded. A revision cannot change this — it is a scope fact — but stating the audience directly would help.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate (rating provisional: no literature survey was performed for this review)
   - **2.1** Table 1 walks seven oracle candidates and gives a structural reason each misses the residual, including the exact in-scope carve-outs (SATORI's OpenAPI field anchoring, MASTOR's source anchoring, @tComment/JDoctor/DocTer/Toradocu's method-level tagged prose). This is by far the clearest articulation of the delta anywhere in the paper.
   - **2.2** Section 6's MASTOR paragraph is genuinely strong positioning: "cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to TestVDB" and "a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is not new here" concede the components and locate novelty in the anchor inversion (prose as ground, source as falsifier). This is the right way to claim a delta without hiding behind the pipeline's four-stage name.
   - **2.3 [major, fixable]** The novelty claim is anchored in what competitors are *not*, not in a demonstrated difference. There is no ablation of the two-step extraction versus single-step, no comparison against a same-backbone unstructured baseline (read the documentation, generate tests, judge results) — so the observed false-positive suppression (87–90% for the contract core, 1.000 for the full stage) cannot be attributed to the designed mechanisms (pre-binding, evidence chain, four perspectives) versus generic LLM behavior. A reader cannot tell what would be lost by discarding most of the pipeline; a revision could add exactly such a baseline, which would move this criterion only if it demonstrates a real mechanism effect.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** The pipeline borrows an established pattern from LLM code generation (generate, test against an independent execution, repair) without a citation, and the relation is not discussed, so the "break the self-reference" framing can read as a repackaging of a known loop. Naming the lineage and saying what is different would sharpen rather than weaken the claim. (Provisional: no survey performed.)

3. **Soundness** — Weak
   - **3.1** The controlled design itself is appropriate: identical 81 frozen packages, blinding to labels and issue history, three runs per configuration, 81/81 cases judged per run, a conservative binary rule, an independent fourth arm for the paper's most important claim (which perspective carries the decision), and exact McNemar tests with p-values on the paired comparisons (Section 4.3). The design shows awareness that a single run cannot settle a stochastic pipeline, and it reports the mechanism ordering (core → source-only +0.117 vs. source-only → full +0.040) with the caveat that the first leg is not individually significant.
   - **3.2** The false-positive family analysis (Section 4.3: the four leaked cases are a systematic, pack-induced contract-over-interpretation family rather than noise) and the leakage audit of the cognition files (six absorbed entries re-mined and re-adjudicated with no verdict change) are the kind of self-checking that is rare in this genre. The disproved Qdrant #9149 is retracted with its root cause (the "fix" PR added a test, not a fix), and the internal pointer is consistent.
   - **3.3** The source-only arm is a genuine and largely well-controlled test of the mechanism claim. It is also appropriately bounded: the paper reports that source-only and the full stage are statistically indistinguishable ($p=0.648$) while the full stage beats the core at $p=0.017$ and the source-only-over-core step is not individually significant ($p=0.093$), refrains from claiming the full stage dominates, and explicitly refuses to accept a re-aggregation of recorded fields as equivalent to the independent arm.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The actual outcome of the mechanism claim is that the evaluation is inconclusive on its own terms: the independent source-only arm (0.333) and the full stage (0.373) are statistically indistinguishable, and several internal comparisons point the other way — the cognition-only re-aggregation confirms 13/51 at precision 1.000 (Section 4.3), and the re-aggregation of D fields under the shipped rule yields 0.392, a full 13 cases above the independent arm, diverging on the Milvus type-coercion family where the full-stage judges and the independent judges disagreed on `validation_absent` versus `by_design_in_source` (Section 4.3). A two-to-four-case boundary delta on one family of cases is thin evidence for a claimed mechanism. The revision needs a defensible way to adjudicate these recorded-field divergences, or the central claim must be narrowed.
   - **3.5 [major, unfixable]** RQ1's headline (51/81 confirmed, 63.0%) is the operational pipeline's cumulative output: accumulated across mining rounds since early 2026, run unblinded with both judgment-side threats live, and filtered by a human submission decision as the paper itself discloses (Section 4.2). The introduction uses it as the motivating result — "maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs and fixed 23." The pool is therefore a submission-filtered subset that manual review had already (by the unblinded pipeline) judged the strongest, and the reportable-bug count depends on human judgments in the loop in a way that cannot be reconstructed from the text. The controls that RQ2 adds cannot, in any revision, make the ledger a controlled measurement of the system.
   - **3.6 [major, fixable]** The RQ3 comparison protocol is asymmetric. The 26,000-request VDBFuzz run was performed (Section 4.4) on a version chosen by the authors — one where all seven of the paper's own true positives are live — under a budget the authors set, and the ground truth for "reach" is TestVDB's own prior findings rather than a neutral benchmark. A reviewer cannot exclude that a different sampled-budget probe, a different target version, or a different definition of crash-class defects would move this number, and the paper explicitly labels the run "a bounded sampled probe whose budget we set." The requested clarification "reach under matched budgets" is itself the fix.
   - **3.7 [major, fixable]** In the same subsection, the head-to-head comparison's own summary is all-zero — "VDBFuzz reports zero crashes, zero failures, and zero errors" over its complete template space on qdrant v1.18.0 — and the earlier sampled run on a different version (v1.18.2) reproduced none of VDBFuzz's three published Qdrant crashes either. Two all-zero author-run VDBFuzz outcomes across two versions, reported only through summary counts, are at least as consistent with a harness that never exercises VDBFuzz's crash path (a misconfigured container, a build or invocation mismatch, or silently skipped templates) as with a true zero. The paper leans on this result for "complementary by construction," so the negative run needs instrumentation beyond a summary count: the crash-catching path should be validated by an injected crash in the same harness, and the observed request distribution, crash-class coverage, and container logs should be reported, before "zero" can be read as a property of VDBFuzz rather than of the setup.
   - **3.8 [major, fixable]** The pool-level rates of Section 4.3 are internal to a filtered pool, which the paper acknowledges, but the induced bias affects the recall figure as well as the precision figure, and the paper is silent on that direction. Its own stated argument — that candidates already judged obviously weak were not submitted — runs the other way for recall: if the pool excludes weak *true* positives it should also exclude weak *false* positives, and the exclusion operates on true- and false-positive-ledger entries asymmetrically (submission decisions are made after novelty screening, and a candidate later adjudicated as a false positive was submitted for a distinct reason — the authors' initial confirmation — than one adjudicated a true bug). The recall 0.373 is then a lower bound relative to the pool composition rather than necessarily a lower bound relative to what the automated stage would do on the unfiltered stream. The direction of the adjustment needs to be stated explicitly and, where possible, estimated (e.g., by re-adjudicating the unadjudicated 49 submissions or the 60 defect-confirming candidates of the run ledger, both of which are available to the authors).
   - **3.9 [minor, fixable]** The entire evaluation runs on one model family under default sampling with no pinned snapshot (Section 5, external-validity paragraph). Section 4.3 additionally shows that judgments "written as each run's output" still had to be post-processed (the re-mining of six cognition entries "changed no verdict in any of the three runs") — the leakage check and the recorded fields are entangled. Since the core claims concern the behavior of this judge, not of the pipeline as an engineered system, single-family calibration bounds the external validity of every rate reported.
   - **3.10 [minor, fixable]** The ledger's accounting cannot be reconciled across subsections. Section 4.2 states that the 49 unadjudicated submissions are excluded from every rate, but not their composition (how many await a maintainer verdict versus were withdrawn), and no table reconciles which of the 81 adjudicated candidates overlap the process ledger of one run (119 chain verdicts → 96 distinct candidates → 60 defect-confirming) or the RQ2 pool; the text asserts the pool and the run "overlap only through that submitted-back-fixed slice" without quantifying it. This is checkable in-house and cheap to report; as written, the reader cannot verify the funnel arithmetic across subsections.

4. **Verifiability** — Weak
   - **4.1** Every evaluation quantity I and the paper jointly depend on is stated in the text with a definition: the denominator (81/81 per run), the majority rule (≥2/3), the per-run and majority rows (Table 4), the Wilson intervals per run, the per-case agreement computations, the McNemar counts, and the stratified Table 6. The reader can follow the arithmetic of RQ2 without reading any other document — the key quantities are internally consistent (I checked the 51+30=81 pool and 24+27=51 stratification, the 60=19+11+5+25 partition, the 51=23+18+7+3 status split, and 37/51 ≈ 72.5%, all consistent with the prose as printed).
   - **4.2 [major, fixable]** No artifact is reachable. The unnumbered Data Availability section commits to releasing agent role definitions, per-version knowledge bases and specifications, generated scripts with raw logs, evidence chains, and the submission ledger, but only "at an anonymized URL" to be added later. The rubric's bar is a declared *and reachable* link or a self-contained text; this source is neither. Since the paper's own disclosures place the pipeline and several data files (knowledge.json, specifications.json, evidence_chain.json, developer_cognition.json, the signature fingerprint table, strategy registry entries) at the center of the claims, the absence of any accompanying material makes the empirical layer unverifiable for a reviewer in this cycle. A camera-ready-grade commitment with a working anonymous link would move this criterion — and the paper explicitly says "will be released."
   - **4.3 [major, fixable]** The parts of the method the paper promises as reproducible do not tie to the artifacts: strategies are "pre-bound deterministically" by a registry of (strategy, trigger predicate) tuples, but the registry itself is never shown even in excerpt, and the paper says the mapping is "a pure function of the constraint record" (Section 3.3) — a ten-line table of trigger predicates would let a reader reproduce the (constraint, strategy) binding on the published specifications and check the "removes a degree of freedom" claim. Similarly, the five generation gates are named but only three have concrete criteria, and the bug-pattern taxonomy's eight patterns (Section 4.2) have no construction rule, so a third party cannot independently label candidate bugs into the taxonomy.
   - **4.4 [major, unfixable]** The experimental record is unrecoverable by design in a way no revision can repair. Both VDBFuzz runs are reported through summary counts only: no per-request logs, no crash-class breakdown, no comparison of start/end state. The adjudication labels are not published. The judges' raw outputs from the three RQ2 runs and the independent arm are not available; the divergences the paper reports (16 vs. 7 for source-only vs. the core; 17 vs. 5 for the full stage vs. the core; 11 vs. 8 for source-only vs. the full stage) are stated as counts, and the two runs' outputs that would adjudicate item 3.4's open question are inside the authors' archive rather than in the paper.
   - **4.5** Section 4.2's provenance caveats are unusually transparent: the disclosure that the pool is submission-filtered, that the ledger "is cumulative across mining rounds…and predates this run," and that the two ledgers "measure different layers." This is not enough to make the layer-relation rigorous (see 3.5 and 3.8) but it does tell the reader exactly what is and is not being measured.
   - **4.6 [minor, fixable]** The confirmatory evidence for the ledger's correctness ("every one of the 23 modifies implementation code," "none is documentation-only") rests on a change-file classifier whose rule is not stated, and the validation against #9149 is a single case. The classifier's criteria and a confusion count over a sample of fix PRs would make this checkable.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1 [major, fixable]** The abstract's mechanism sentence compresses the paper's most contested comparison into an additive visual: "an independent source-only arm over the same packages recovers 0.333, locating source grounding as the mechanism's largest single contributor." In the body, the same facts read as neither additive nor a settled attribution: the source-only arm (0.333) and the full stage (0.373) are "statistically indistinguishable (11 vs. 8, $p=0.648$)," the core → source-only step is "$p=0.093$, not individually significant," and the re-aggregation questions the comparison entirely. An abstract that a reader can quote against the body's own numbers is a rewriting priority.
   - **5.2 [major, fixable]** The Results/Evaluation section is overloaded in a way that obstructs evaluation. RQ2 carries seven separate result threads — the core numbers, the recall-mechanism taxonomy, the full-stage ablation, the pack-completeness stratification, the source-only arm, the re-aggregation, and the cognition-only note — with new numbers appearing mid-paragraph (e.g., the "earlier full-stage run…had reported recall 0.841" sentence is embedded in the paragraph on pack completeness). The density forces the reader to cross-check four tables and roughly a dozen figures of merit against prose claims, and the key contradiction in 3.4 is easy to miss because it is not signaled.
   - **5.3** The running example is genuinely well executed: the same Qdrant #10369 defect is carried through all four stages with concrete artifacts at each step (Section 3.2's constraint `qdrant_state_recommend_001`, Section 3.3's scenario script, Section 3.4's log pair, Section 3.5's five-section chain and verdict), and it is a real defect the upstream maintainer accepted. It makes the pipeline concrete in a way the prose alone would not; a second worked example, on the false-positive side, would strengthen the reader's model of interception.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** The abstract introduces terminology ("an operational human–machine deployment with author-side screening and submission," "recalled only 0.196–0.333," "the contract core") that the body then uses with much narrower definitions (Section 4.3's "false-positive candidate" versus \textsc{False-Positive}). A reader who reads the abstract in isolation misreads "recall 0.196" as a detection rate. A one-sentence framing in the abstract of the frozen-package setup would remove the ambiguity.
   - **5.5 [minor, fixable]** Numbering and labeling the paper's own results inconsistently is a low-cost fix with real reading cost: the paper has six numbered tables (1 oracle candidates, 2 submission ledger, 3 bug patterns, 4 the RQ2 ablation matrix, 5 the source-only arm, 6 stratified recall), while the prose refers to "the two headline results," "three recurrent mechanisms," and "four majority misconfirmations" without stable anchors; the process ledger of one run (Section 4.2) never states a run date; and Table 6 below the paragraph "Stratifying recall by pack completeness" is placed after the paragraph that discusses it rather than before. The single consensus Weak is worth naming precisely, because it is the one place all three reviewers' evidence converged from different directions: **Verifiability**. The three reviews diverge most on **Soundness** (Excellent's neighbour Adequate twice, Weak once) — and the divergence is substantive rather than stylistic. Reviewer 3 reads the mechanism claim as inconclusive on the paper's own terms: the independent source-only arm (0.333) and the full stage (0.373) are "statistically indistinguishable" at p = 0.648, while a re-aggregation of the same recorded fields sits 13 cases higher at 0.392 and diverges on the Milvus type-coercion family — "a two-to-four-case boundary delta on one family of cases is thin evidence for a claimed mechanism." Reviewers 1 and 2 read the same numbers as an honest, well-controlled ordering (0.216 < 0.333 < 0.373) whose caveats the paper itself discloses. Reviewer 2's formulation is the one the consensus can act on — "keep the abstract's phrasing to the ordering … rather than as a settled attribution" — and that is precisely what Priority Revision 1 asks for.
