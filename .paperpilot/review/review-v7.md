## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

TestVDB targets documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs), where a system silently accepts an input that violates its API documentation. Because the boundary is natural-language prose, deterministic oracles (crash, differential, metamorphic, property-based, and existing REST-API tools) cannot adjudicate these accept/reject decisions, leaving an LLM as the practical oracle. The authors instantiate a four-stage pipeline (claim extraction, test generation, execution, confirmation) using LLMs to read documentation, generate tests, and adjudicate responses. A multi-perspective judging baseline (four specialized judge agents with voting) raises precision but collapses recall to ~15%. To address two false-positive failure modes (hallucination in extraction and self-preference bias in judgment), the authors introduce a dev-reviewer agent that acts as a source-grounded falsifier: reproducing candidates, cross-checking against implementation source, and attempting disproof. On a controlled 48-candidate retrospective, the dev-reviewer (GLM-5.2 backbone, 3-run any-confirmed ensemble) reaches 67% precision and 74% recall against a 37% recall single-LLM baseline. TestVDB surfaced 107 candidate issues across three VDBMSs; maintainers acknowledged 50 as true-positive defects (15 fixed via merged PR). A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows complementary coverage: TestVDB reaches a crash-class defect by contract reasoning, while VDBFuzz misses a TestVDB silent-accept defect under its current templates. A full cross-model re-run reveals family-specific verdicts (Cohen's κ vs. GLM: 0.32 DeepSeek, 0.20 LongCat, 0.18 Qwen), so cross-family generalization remains open. Since Round 6, the cross-family voting probe (GLM ∪ DeepSeek) has been escalated from a §7 side-demo to a §4.2 "recommended deployment default," reaching 81% recall at 73% precision.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Well-motivated problem setting — documentation-implementation defects are a prevalent, crash-escaping class that VDBFuzz cannot reach, with 50 maintainer-acknowledged true positives across three production VDBMSs (15 fixed). — see 1.1, 6.1
- **S2:** Systematic oracle exclusion argument (Table 2) — the paper walks through crash, differential, metamorphic, property-based, and existing REST-API oracles (AGORA+, SATORI, MASTOR), showing why each misses the documentation-implementation residual. This cleanly motivates LLM as the practical oracle for the gap. — see 2, 2.1
- **S3:** Source-grounded falsifier design — the dev-reviewer's three-check falsification (independent reproducibility, evidence sufficiency, falsifiability) breaks self-preference bias by using implementation source as ground truth rather than the same family's prior claim. Ablations confirm source grounding is the dominant contributor (75% FP suppression alone, 91% with threat-model anchor). — see 5, 6.2
- **S4:** Rigorous empirical evaluation — 107 submitted issues, 50 maintainer-adjudicated true positives (15 merged-PR-fixed), controlled 48-candidate retrospective with precision/recall metrics, family-specific generalization analysis, and a bidirectional VDBFuzz probe that isolates complementary coverage on controlled versions. — see 6
- **S5:** Honest limitation disclosure — the paper explicitly states cross-family generalization is open (κ all in slight/fair band), reports single-backbone (GLM-5.2) as a construct validity threat, and acknowledges the implementation-as-correct assumption bounds the approach (but notes no observed false negatives from 31 fixed or open PRs). — see 6.4

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** Cross-family escalation from side-demo to recommended deployment default is under-justified — the paper elevates cross-family voting (GLM ∪ DeepSeek) to "recommended deployment default" in the RQ2 paragraph within Section 5 (Evaluation) based on a single operating point (81% recall / 73% precision) without sufficient empirical grounding. The cross-model re-run reveals family-specific verdicts (GLM κ vs. DeepSeek = 0.39, indicating "fair" agreement, not complementary families), and the 81% recall figure rests on a post-hoc operating point selection (3-run union) with selection bias concerns. — see 6.2
- **W2:** Construct validity threat from family-specific verdicts is under-acknowledged — the full cross-model re-run shows κ = 0.32 DeepSeek, 0.20 LongCat, 0.18 Qwen (all "slight" to "fair"), meaning verdicts are family-specific, not robust across backbones. The paper frames this as "cross-family generalization remains open," but this is more fundamental: if the oracle itself is family-specific, the *detection* of documentation-implementation defects may be backbone-dependent, undermining the claim that TestVDB reliably finds a stable defect class. — see 6.2
- **W3:** Threats to validity undercount the post-hoc selection burden — the 3-run union operating point is selected from four configurations (single, 3-run, 5-run, majority) and justified as "post-hoc, exploratory." The Wilson CIs do not account for this selection, and while a bootstrap validation is provided, it operates on the same 48-candidate set. The operating point selection and cross-family escalation both introduce optimizer's-uncertainty that is not fully reflected in the stated uncertainty bounds. — see 6.2

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1:** The problem is real and prevalent: 50 maintainer-acknowledged true positives across three production VDBMSs, with 15 merged-PR fixes. Documentation-implementation defects are a crash-escaping class that existing VDBMS fuzzers (VDBFuzz) miss. The impact is clear: these defects corrupt query semantics (disabled filters, wrong thresholds) and propagate silently into LLM context.
   - **1.2 [minor, fixable]:** The setting is narrower than claimed: the paper generalizes to "REST APIs without OpenAPI, configuration validation, policy-as-code," but evaluation is VDBMS-only. Two porting case studies (CouchDB, Elasticsearch) are brief and do not establish generalizability. The broader transferability claim should be softened or deferred to future work. — see 7, 7.1
   - **1.3:** Overall impact is meaningful but bounded: TestVDB targets a specific, well-defined defect class in a critical infrastructure layer (VDBMSs for LLM applications). The 50 confirmed defects show practical utility, though the single-backbone dependency (see 3.1) limits immediate deployment confidence.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1:** The delta over SATORI is genuine: SATORI targets "response-field or runtime properties from OpenAPI fields, traces, or source" and explicitly cannot adjudicate "input-acceptance decisions on system-level prose." TestVDB's positioning of documentation (not source) as ground truth, then falsifying implementation against it, is a clear conceptual contribution. I verified this against the SATORI paper; the characterization holds.
   - **2.2:** The delta over MASTOR is also genuine: MASTOR "reads source to generate oracles that encode implemented behavior and so cannot detect a gap between documentation and code." TestVDB's source-grounded falsifier reads source to *disprove* documentation-derived claims, inverting MASTOR's direction. The dev-reviewer's three-check design (reproduce, cross-check, try-disprove) is novel relative to MASTOR's source-verification-only approach.
   - **2.3 [major, fixable]:** Cross-family voting as a primary contribution is under-developed relative to its new prominence. The escalation from §7 side-demo (Round 6) to "recommended deployment default" in the RQ2 paragraph (Round 7) is a significant reframe that should be accompanied by: (a) a more principled exploration of why voting works (is it error correction? ensemble diversity? or just more LLM calls?), (b) analysis of when cross-family availability correlates with better recall (e.g., are certain defect types family-agnostic?), and (c) discussion of the practical cost of deploying multiple backbones. Currently, the RQ2 paragraph presents it as a fact rather than a contribution to be evaluated. — see 6.2

3. **Soundness** — Weak
   - **3.1 [major, unfixable]:** Family-specific verdicts undermine the robustness of the claimed defect class. The cross-model re-run (DeepSeek, Qwen-3.8-Max, LongCat-2.0, each running the complete SOP with independent live probes and source exploration) shows Cohen's κ vs. GLM's 3-run union: 0.32 DeepSeek, 0.20 LongCat, 0.18 Qwen. These are in the "slight" to "fair" band (0.21–0.40), not even "moderate" (0.41–0.60). The paper acknowledges this as "cross-family generalization remains open," but this understates the threat: if the oracle itself is backbone-dependent, then the *definition* of a documentation-implementation defect may be backbone-specific. A defect GLM-5.2 CONFIRMS but DeepSeek REJECTS is either (a) a true positive that one backbone misses, or (b) a false positive that both hallucinate. The paper cannot rule out (b) without an external ground truth, which does not exist for VDBMSs. This is an inherent limitation of LLM-as-oracle in this setting; it should be fronted in the abstract and conclusion as a core caveat, not buried in §6.4 as one of three construct validity threats.
   - **3.2 [major, fixable]:** The cross-family escalation is not empirically justified as a "recommended default." The headline operating point (GLM ∪ DeepSeek, 81% recall / 73% precision) is selected from four options (single, 3-run union, 5-run union, majority) without a principled selection criterion. The bootstrap validation on the 48-candidate set supports that the selected point is not an artifact of that sample, but it does not address selection bias across the four configurations. More critically, the κ between GLM and DeepSeek on CONFIRMED-vs-not is 0.39 ("fair"), which suggests *moderate* agreement, not strong complementarity. The paper claims "families complementary" in Section 5 (Evaluation), but the data show moderate alignment. This weakens the case that voting is fundamentally better than a single backbone; it may just be more LLM calls. — see 6.2
   - **3.3:** The implementation-as-correct assumption is bounded but reasonably justified: 31 of the 50 true positives have merged or open fix-PRs (implementation confirmed buggy by maintainers), and no false positive was traced to an implementation bug (all FPs are maintainer-adjudicated by-design). The paper acknowledges the risk but notes no observed false negatives in the retrospective. Given the maintainer adjudication process, this is a pragmatic assumption with empirical backing.
   - **3.4:** The evaluation methodology is sound: maintainer adjudication as ground truth, controlled retrospective on 48 candidates, family-specific generalization analysis, and bidirectional VDBFuzz probe. The exclusion of Weaviate from the headline cross-family numbers (4 TPs only, insufficient for statistical power) is honest.

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate
   - **4.1:** The artifact link is declared and reachable (https://github.com/yihui504/testvdb-anon). The paper states it contains prompts (22 agent role definitions under `agents/`), ground truth (`test_questions/`), reproduction scripts (`reproduction/full52/`), target versions, per-token accounting, and the 48-candidate ground truth. This is sufficient for verification.
   - **4.2:** The text describes the pipeline in sufficient detail to follow or replicate: Section 3 outlines the four-stage pipeline, Section 4 describes the false-positive modes, Section 5 (Dev-Reviewer: Source-Grounded Falsifier) describes the dev-reviewer's three checks, and the LLM-automation paragraph in Section 3 describes the multi-agent orchestration. Tables 2–4 and Figure 1 provide architectural overviews. The appendix excerpts key agent prompts (contract-formalizer, dev-reviewer). A researcher could reimplement the core architecture from this text alone.
   - **4.3:** The data reporting is thorough: yield tables by vendor, per-run recall variance, operating-point trade-offs, cross-family κ scores, ablation results, and cost analysis. The Wilson CIs, Bonferroni corrections, and bootstrap validation show statistical care.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1 [minor, fixable]:** The cross-family escalation to "recommended deployment default" appears abruptly in the RQ2 paragraph of Section 5 (Evaluation). Readers comparing Round 6 to Round 7 will notice this promotion, but the rationale is not explicitly contrasted. A single sentence acknowledging the change (e.g., "Unlike Round 6, where cross-family voting appeared as a side-demo, we now recommend it as the default deployment mode when a second family is available") would improve transparency.
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]:** The family-specific verdicts (κ all ≤ 0.32) are buried in Section 6 (Evaluation), specifically in the RQ2 paragraph. These should be mentioned earlier (e.g., in the abstract or introduction) as a core caveat, alongside the single-backbone limitation. Currently, the abstract states "cross-family generalization is an open question," which understates the threat.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]:** Figure 2 (pipeline overview) is clear, but the figure could explicitly show the source-grounded anchor path (implementation source clone) as distinct from the documentation path. This would visually reinforce the dev-reviewer's key design.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]:** Some language is slightly overbroad: "REST APIs without OpenAPI coverage, configuration validation, and policy-as-code checks" in the abstract and §7 (Discussion and Limitations). Soften this to "structurally similar documentation regimes" and note that CouchDB/Elasticsearch are preliminary porting probes, not established generalizations.

### Questions for Authors

- **Q1:** On the cross-family escalation — Can you provide a more principled justification for selecting the 3-run union over the 5-run union (85% recall / 62% precision) or majority voting (26% recall)? The paper states "we select the 3-run union as the headline because it sits at the knee of the precision-recall trade-off," but this is post-hoc. If the goal is maximum recall at acceptable precision, why not 5-run? If the goal is stability, why not majority? Intended effect: clarify whether the recommendation is cost-sensitive, performance-sensitive, or validated on held-out data. — see 6.2

- **Q2:** On family-specific verdicts — Given κ (GLM vs. DeepSeek) = 0.39 on CONFIRMED-vs-not, which falls in the "fair" band (not "moderate" or "substantial"), can you characterize the *nature* of the disagreements? Are there systematic defect types that one family confirms and the other rejects? If disagreements are concentrated on ambiguous documentation prose, then cross-family voting is an error-correction mechanism; if they are random, then it is just more LLM calls. Intended effect: if disagreements are concentrated, strengthen the voting argument; if random, downgrade to "heuristic ensemble." — see 6.2

- **Q3:** On construct validity — The paper acknowledges that "no public ground-truth defect catalog for VDBMSs exists," so you "do not estimate recall" and instead use maintainer adjudication. However, maintainer adjudication is itself subject to bias (e.g., closing issues as wont-fix to avoid work). Did you track the adjudication time-to-resolution or any patterns in rejections? Intended effect: if most rejections were swift and unexplained, this may underestimate false negatives; if rejections included detailed counter-explanations, this strengthens the ground truth assumption. — see 6.1

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary
TestVDB targets **documentation-implementation defects** in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs) — cases where a VDBMS silently accepts inputs that violate its natural-language API documentation (e.g., `nprobe=0` when docs declare range `[1, 16384]`). Because the documented boundary is prose rather than a structured schema, deterministic oracles (crash detection, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property-based testing, OpenAPI-anchored tools) cannot adjudicate these accept/reject decisions, leaving an LLM as the practical oracle. The LLM introduces two false-positive modes (hallucination in claim extraction, self-preference bias in judgment) that multi-perspective judging cannot resolve. TestVDB introduces a **source-grounded dev-reviewer agent** that falsifies LLM-derived verdicts against implementation source (reproduce, cross-check evidence, check for by-design patterns) and suppresses false positives. In evaluation, TestVDB surfaced 107 candidate issues across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate; maintainers acknowledged 50 as true-positive defects (15 merged-PR-fixed). On a 48-candidate retrospective, the dev-reviewer (GLM-5.2 backbone, 3-run any-confirmed ensemble) reaches **67% precision / 74% recall** against a 56%/37% single-LLM baseline. A cross-family voting probe (GLM ∪ DeepSeek) reaches 81% recall @ 73% precision and is presented as the **recommended deployment default** when a second family is available. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows complementarity: VDBFuzz reached 0 of 14 silent-accept true positives on Qdrant v1.18.2; TestVDB reached a crash-class integer overflow via contract reasoning on v1.4.0.

## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary
This paper targets documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs), where a system silently accepts inputs that violate its API documentation. The authors argue that because documentation boundaries are expressed in natural-language prose, deterministic oracles (crash-based fuzzing, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property-based testing) cannot adjudicate these accept/reject decisions, leaving an LLM as the practical oracle. ### Core Strengths

- **S2:** Rigorous treatment of LLM reliability failure modes — see Section 5 (False-Positive Problem), which distinguishes hallucination from self-preference bias and shows why multi-perspective judging cannot resolve them (Table 2).
- **S3:** Substantive empirical validation across three dimensions — see Section 7 (Evaluation), which addresses RQ1 (detection capability: 50 TP across 3 systems), RQ2 (false-positive suppression: 67%/74% precision/recall with source grounding vs. 37% recall without), and RQ3 (complementarity with VDBFuzz via bidirectional probe).

- **S5:** Strong external artifact support — see Section 3.5 (LLM automation), which provides complete prompts (22 agent role definitions), ground truth, target versions, and a reproduction driver at a public GitHub repository.

### Core Weaknesses
- **W1:** Cross-family generalization limitation (acknowledged but remains unaddressed in main evaluation) — see Section 7, RQ2 paragraph, where a full independent cross-model re-run shows family-specific verdicts (κ vs. GLM's 3-run union = 0.32 DeepSeek, 0.20 LongCat, 0.18 Qwen), and the paper relies on a single LLM family (GLM-5.2) for all headline results. This bounds the practical deployment recommendation to environments where only one family is available.
- **W2:** Weaviate coverage is thin relative to Milvus/Qdrant — see Section 7, RQ2 paragraph, External Validity, where the Weaviate controlled subset is only 11 candidates (vs. 48 Milvus+Qdrant), precluding a per-vendor recall headline. The paper acknowledges this but it remains a coverage gap in the three-system claim.
- **W3:** Operating-point selection is post-hoc — see Section 7, RQ2 paragraph, where the 3-run union is selected after observing four operating points, and while the paper validates via bootstrap and cross-validation, the Wilson CIs do not account for selection. The authors flag this clearly, but it introduces some uncertainty around the stability of the headline precision/recall figures.

### Detailed Assessment

#### 1. Significance — Adequate

- **1.1** The problem addressed is real and practical: VDBMSs store embeddings for retrieval-augmented LLM applications, and more than half of VDBMS bugs manifest as functional failures rather than crashes (Section 1, citing bugstudy25). This is not a synthetic problem. The 15 merged-PR fixes across three production VDBMSs demonstrate practical impact — this is not a theoretical exercise.

- **1.2** The contribution is meaningfully bounded rather than foundational. The authors do not claim that TestVDB solves the general LLM-as-judge reliability problem; they target a specific regime (documentation-implementation consistency in VDBMSs) where structured sources (OpenAPI, traces) are unavailable. The claim to transferability beyond VDBMSs is explicit and qualified (Section 8: "only as a preliminary portability probe rather than a generalization result"). This is useful but scoped impact.

- **1.3** The significance is moderated by the single-family construct validity threat (Section 7, RQ2 paragraph, External Validity). All headline results use GLM-5.2, and cross-family runs show substantial variance (DeepSeek recall 56%, Qwen 19%, LongCat 22% vs. GLM 74%). The cross-family voting mitigation (GLM ∪ DeepSeek, 81% recall at 73% precision) is noted but not the main deployment default. This bounds the significance to environments where a single family is the only practical option, or where cross-family deployment is feasible.

#### 2. Novelty — Adequate

- **2.1** The LLM-as-judge framing is not new, but the specific application (documentation-implementation defects in VDBMSs) and the two-failure-mode decomposition (hallucination + self-preference) are a real, if incremental, delta over prior work. The paper distinguishes itself from REST-API oracle tools (AGORA+, SATORI, MASTOR) by the target (input-acceptance decisions on system-level prose vs. response-field properties under structural anchoring) and from documentation-derived oracles (Toradocu, AugmenTest, ChatAssert) by the falsifier design (source-grounded vs. runtime-behavior-only).

- **2.2 [minor, fixable]** The novelty positioning against prior LLM-as-judge reliability work could be more explicit. Section 6 (Related Work) cites self-preference bias (Panickssery24, Wataoka24) and intra-judge inconsistency (Haldar25), but does not explicitly state whether TestVDB's source-grounded falsifier is a known mitigation or a new contribution. Clarifying this in the Related Work section would sharpen the novelty claim.

- **2.3** The cross-family generalization limitation (Section 7, RQ2 paragraph) tempers the novelty claim. The results are clearly family-specific, and the paper does not claim cross-family robustness. This is honest (the authors flag it explicitly as an open question), but it means the contribution is proven for one LLM family rather than as a general LLM reliability solution.

#### 3. Soundness — Adequate

- **3.1** The main claims are defensible with notable gaps. The 50 maintainer-acknowledged true positives out of 107 submitted issues support the detection capability claim. The 67%/74% precision/recall on the 48-candidate retrospective supports the false-positive suppression claim. The bidirectional VDBFuzz probe supports the complementarity claim. All three claims have direct empirical backing.

- **3.2 [major, fixable]** The single-family construct validity threat is a notable gap. All headline results use GLM-5.2, and cross-family re-runs show substantial variance (Section 7, RQ2 paragraph: κ vs. GLM's 3-run union = 0.32 DeepSeek, 0.20 LongCat, 0.18 Qwen). The cross-family voting mitigation (GLM ∪ DeepSeek, 81% recall at 73% precision) is mentioned but not evaluated on the full 48-candidate set in the same way the GLM-only results are. The authors flag this explicitly as an open question, but it remains a gap in the main evaluation. The fix is to either (a) elevate cross-family voting to a co-headline evaluation, or (b) more explicitly bound the deployment recommendation to single-family environments.

- **3.3** The implementation-as-correct assumption is a stated limitation (Section 8), and the paper provides mitigating evidence: 31 of the 50 true positives have merged or open fix-PRs (implementation confirmed buggy by maintainers), and no false positive was traced to an implementation bug (all FPs are maintainer-adjudicated by-design). This is a reasonable defense of the assumption in the VDBMS setting, though it bounds generalization to settings where maintainers actively triage.

- **3.4** The Weaviate coverage gap is notable but clearly acknowledged. The 11-candidate controlled subset (vs. 48 for Milvus+Qdrant) precludes a per-vendor recall headline. The authors are transparent about this, calling it "a smaller controlled subset" in Section 7, RQ2 paragraph. This does not undermine the core claims, but it does mean the three-system framing is uneven.

- **3.5 [minor, fixable]** The operating-point selection is post-hoc. The 3-run union is selected after observing four operating points, and while the authors validate via bootstrap resampling and cross-validation, the Wilson CIs in Table 4 do not account for selection. The authors flag this clearly in Section 7, RQ2 paragraph, but the uncertainty remains. A simple fix is to add a Bonferroni-corrected CI to Table 4 (the authors mention the rough bounds in text: $[44, 84]$ for precision, $[51, 89]$ for recall) to explicitly account for the selection.

#### 4. Verifiability — Excellent

- **4.1** The paper provides sufficient detail to follow how the evidence was produced. Section 3.5 (LLM automation) specifies the runtime (Claude Code), the backbone (GLM-5.2 via BigModel Anthropic-compatible API under default sampling), the agent roles (22 role definitions), the per-target cost ($~10$), and the LLM-call distribution (50% dev-reviewer source-grounding, 25% extraction+generation, 25% judging+novelty gate).

- **4.2** The artifact is declared and reachable. Section 3.5 provides a GitHub URL (https://github.com/yihui504/testvdb-anon) with organized contents: `agents/` for the 22 prompts, `test_questions/` for the 48-candidate ground truth, and `reproduction/full52/` for a reproduction driver. The paper also specifies target versions (Milvus 2.6.19, Qdrant v1.18.2, Weaviate v1.38.2). This is strong artifact support.

- **4.3** The methodology is reproducible from the text alone. Section 3 (Approach) walks the four stages with sufficient detail that a reader could re-implement the pipeline: behavioral-claim extraction (contract-formalizer agent, JSON contract format), test-script generation (four attack agents), sandboxed execution (Docker-pinned versions), defect confirmation (judge quartet + dev-reviewer). Appendix A excerpts the two most critical agent prompts (contract-formalizer, dev-reviewer). A motivated reader could reconstruct the system from this.

- **4.4** The bidirectional VDBFuzz probe methodology is clearly specified. Section 7.3 (RQ3) describes the systematic direction (VDBFuzz on v1.18.2 with 26,000 mutated requests, 0 of 14 TestVDB TPs reached) and the controlled cases (v1.4.0 for TestVDB reaching VDBFuzz's integer-overflow crash; v1.18.0 for VDBFuzz missing TestVDB's empty-vector defect). This is sufficient to follow the probe design and its findings.

#### 5. Presentation — Adequate

- **5.1** The structure is sound and the paper is readable. The progression from problem (Section 2) to approach (Section 3) to false-positive problem (Section 5) to dev-reviewer solution (Section 6) to evaluation (Section 7) is logical. The figures support the text: Figure 1 (pipeline) sketches the architecture, Figure 2 (dev-reviewer three-check falsification) visualizes the suppression logic, and Figure 3 (per-run recall) shows variance. The tables are clear: Table 1 (exclusion argument) systematically rules out deterministic oracles, Table 2 (multi-perspective judging) lists the four judge agents, Table 5 (operating points) reports precision/recall with CIs, Table 7 (three configurations) compares baselines.

- **5.2 [minor, fixable]** Some notation is inconsistent across sections. Section 7, RQ2 paragraph refers to "3-run any-confirmed ensemble," "3-run union," and "any-confirmed (union)" interchangeably. Standardizing on one term would reduce reader friction. Similarly, Table 4 uses "3-run union (any-confirmed)" while the text sometimes uses "3-run any-confirmed." Minor consistency tweaks would improve polish.

- **5.3 [minor, fixable]** The Related Work section (Section 6) is dense and could benefit from a summary table. The authors walk through four related-work areas (VDBMS testing, REST-API oracle generation, LLM-as-judge reliability, documentation-derived oracles), and the distinction between each prior work and TestVDB is clear in prose, but a table summarizing the related work, their oracles, and why they miss the documentation-implementation residual would make the positioning more scanable.

- **5.4 [minor, fixable]** The abstract is long and dense. At 228 words, it packs the problem, approach, false-positive modes, dev-reviewer solution, and three RQ findings into a single paragraph. Splitting it into two paragraphs (problem/approach first, results second) would improve readability without losing content.

### Questions for Authors
- **Q1:** The cross-family re-run in Section 7, RQ2 paragraph shows substantial variance (κ vs. GLM's 3-run union = 0.32 DeepSeek, 0.20 LongCat, 0.18 Qwen). What would the headline RQ2 precision/recall be if the dev-reviewer used GLM ∪ DeepSeek cross-family voting (81% recall at 73% precision) as the primary configuration, and would this change the deployment recommendation?
- **Q2:** The Weaviate controlled subset is 11 candidates. What prevented a larger controlled subset for Weaviate, and is this a function of Weaviate's maintainer response rate, or of the sparsity of Weaviate's maintainer-fixed true positives (3 of 30)?
- **Q3:** The operating-point selection (3-run union) is post-hoc, and the Wilson CIs do not account for selection. Could you add Bonferroni-corrected CIs to Table 4 to explicitly bound the selection uncertainty, and would this change the qualitative claim about the 3-run union being at the knee of the precision-recall trade-off?
