## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets *documentation–implementation bugs* in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): silent misbehavior — an input accepted that the API prose forbids, a state transition that leaves a dangling coupling — that produces no crash signal and therefore escapes the one dedicated VDBMS fuzzer, VDBFuzz. Because the constraints at issue live in natural-language prose, the authors argue every deterministic-oracle family anchors its expectation elsewhere (crash, reference implementation, output relation, machine-checkable property, structured schema), leaving an LLM as the practical oracle, with its attendant false-positive problem. TestVDB answers with a four-stage pipeline: behavioral specification extraction from version-pinned documentation, strategy-pre-bound test generation, Docker-pinned sandboxed execution with raw HTTP logging, and a confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly from a four-perspective chain audit grounded in the implementation source.

Empirically, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate (23 fixed via merged PRs). The confirmation stage is then evaluated in an unusually careful re-adjudication: after an audit found 43.3% of the original evidence packs' cited (constraint, page) pairs unsupported, all packs were rebuilt, the pool of 51 true bugs + 30 false positives was re-judged three times per arm under five configurations (contract core, full stage, source-only, flat judge, second backbone). The contract core suppresses 96.7% of false positives but recalls 0.157; the full stage reaches 0.804 recall at 0.800 suppression under the deployment's counting convention, versus 0.588 for a flat single-prompt judge reading the same packs and sources — a difference that survives Holm correction (p=0.029) on the primary backbone but not on a second family (p=1.0). The decomposition attributes the full-vs-flat separation to the explicit Human-Review routing channel rather than forced-verdict accuracy, and the paper reports three scoring conventions (0.804 / 0.647 joint / 0.529 forced) side by side. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows complementary reach in both directions.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The confirmation measurement is a model of self-auditing evaluation: the authors audited their own evidence packs, found 43.3% of cited pairs unsupported, rebuilt every pack, aligned the protocol, and report all three layers plus three scoring conventions side by side (Table 6) instead of a single flattering number — see 3.1, 4.1.
- **S2:** Real, verifiable impact on production systems: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 fixed via merged implementation-code PRs (validated by a file-level PR classifier with a negative control), across three production VDBMSs — see 1.1, 3.1.
- **S3:** The novelty delta against the closest competitors is real and verified: MASTOR's own limitation statement concedes it cannot detect documentation-code gaps; CASCADE's and Metamon's falsifiers are LLM artifacts sharing the documentation's ambiguity — TestVDB's anchor inversion (prose as ground, implementation source as falsifier) is a genuine, well-positioned difference — see 2.1, 2.2.
- **S4:** RQ3's head-to-head is conducted with unusual care: the released runner's success/failure counters are shown to be uninformative (a no-server run reports 100% success), a per-template liveness oracle is substituted and its own blind spot (recovered panics) disclosed, the mutation-space bound (max 65,536 vs. the 2^62 needed) is measured from the released code, and the reverse direction is explicitly labeled a hand-executed probe — see 3.6.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The confirmatory McNemar tests pair the arms' *judged-positive sets* (true positives plus leaked false positives, 47 vs. 34 for the headline pair), conflating recall gain with precision loss; a true-positive-only test — the one matching the recall claim — is never reported, and my decomposition analysis shows its Holm-corrected outcome would depend on the (unrecorded) split of the 17/4 discordants. Recomputable from the shipped per-case outputs — see 3.2.
- **W2:** Appendix A states that the discipline clauses — including "no maintainer-cognition materials" — are *identical* for both judges, directly contradicting the arms description and the new asymmetry sentence in Section 4 (full stage reads cognition legitimately; flat judge is forbidden). One sentence must go, but as written the fairness basis of the central comparison is self-contradictory — see 3.3.
- **W3:** The central mechanism is single-backbone: the full-vs-flat separation does not replicate on the second family (p=1.0, routing rate collapses from 20.6% to 9.5%), and the full stage is statistically indistinguishable from its own source-forensics step alone (p=0.55). Both are disclosed, but the protocol's additive value over the cheapest structured component is unestablished at this pool size — see 3.4, 3.5.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The problem class is the dominant failure mode, not a niche: the cited bug study (57.3% functional failures vs. 15.1% crashes, verified against the cached full text) and the paper's own ledger (2 of 51 confirmed bugs crash) establish that the silent majority is real and unreached by the state of the practice. The delivered impact is concrete: 51 confirmed, 23 merged-fix bugs across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate (Table 2 ledger, bug-pattern taxonomy in Table 3), with fix PRs validated to modify implementation code (15 of 23 also adding regression tests) and a negative control on the classifier via the disproved #9149. For a testing-paper audience this is a strong, checkable yield.
   - **1.2** [minor, fixable] The cost profile (Section 4.1: ~67 active hours, ~860 executions, ~70M input tokens "for the orchestrating session alone") is disclosed as a lower bound and only for the Qdrant run; adopters cannot estimate per-target cost across the three systems from this. A per-target breakdown table would make the operational footprint assessable.
2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** Verified delta against the closest competitor. MASTOR (fetched, CoRR 2026) grounds oracles in the implementation source and its own limitations section states it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code" — the paper's characterization ("MASTOR's authority is the implementation") is accurate, not a strawman, and the claimed anchor inversion (Section 6, prose as ground / source as falsifier) is a real difference, not cosmetic.
   - **2.2** Verified deltas against the LLM-falsifier line. CASCADE (fetched, FSE 2026) cross-checks doc-derived tests against doc-regenerated code, and its own residual-FP analysis attributes both residual false positives to shared underspecification — the paper quotes this verbatim and correctly. Metamon (fetched, LLM4Code 2025) stabilizes an LLM judge with metamorphic prompts; its falsifier is the same LLM, and the quoted 0.722/0.480 profile matches. The positioning "a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is not new here; what is new is the anchor inversion" is honest and correct.
   - **2.3** [minor, fixable] Small related-work gaps: (i) the LLM-era documentation-code consistency checkers DocChecker and C4RLLaMA (surface in CASCADE's own comparisons) are absent from an otherwise exhaustive doc-derived-oracle line; (ii) RESTler, the canonical REST fuzzing baseline and VDBFuzz's own comparison partner, is uncited while its class is covered by LlamaRestTest/GentaREST; (iii) optionally, Bayesilisk (2026 preprint, uncited) independently argues for separating LLM proposal from non-LLM verification in E2E testing — adjacent support, not a competitor. None of these threatens the delta; each is a one-sentence citation fix.
3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The measurement architecture is the paper's best feature. The three-layer design (audit → rebuild → protocol alignment) isolates one variable per layer; five arms over identical rebuilt packs with three runs each; blinding with a measured leakage scan (zero expectation framing outside verbatim server responses); a discovered cognition-contamination channel treated by removing the section and re-adjudicating all affected arm-run combinations (thirteen runs) rather than arguing it away; the submission filter's direction anchored by a seeded 32-candidate re-adjudication (0.594, below the pool's 63.0%, as a screening filter predicts). The stratified analysis (evidence-present/weak/absent) then shows where recall lives and correctly decomposes the evidence-absent cell (0.909 convention but 0.273 forced) rather than letting the convention inflate the asymmetry claim.
   - **3.2** [major, fixable] The confirmatory statistics pair judged-positive sets, not true-positive sets. The paper states the basis in main text ("each test pairs the two arms' confirmed sets ... so a 'leak' counts as confirmed on both sides"), and my recomputation confirms all seven discordant nets equal the judged-positive-set differences (e.g., 47 vs. 34 for full-vs-flat). But the claim being tested is a *recall* claim ("recalls 0.804 where a flat judge reaches 0.588"), and a judged-positive set rewards confirming false positives as much as finding true ones: of the +13 net, +11 is true positives and +2 is *additional* leakage by the full stage. A true-positive-only McNemar is not derivable from the published discordants; working through the constraint equations, the TP-only discordants could range roughly from 11/0 (raw p≈0.001, survives Holm) to 15/4 (raw p≈0.019, Holm×4 → ≈0.077, does not survive). The headline significance claim is therefore decomposition-dependent. The fix is cheap and decisive: the artifact ships per-case judge outputs for every arm and run, so the TP-only (and FP-only) McNemar tests should be added to the confirmatory family and reported. If the TP-only test survives, 3.2 upgrades to a strength; if not, the claim must be reworded to "confirms more cases at slightly higher leakage."
   - **3.3** [major, fixable] Internal contradiction on what the judges could read. Appendix A asserts the discipline clauses — explicitly listing "no maintainer-cognition materials" — are *identical* across both prompts, while the arms description (Section 4, "Arms and adjudication") says the full stage receives "the same pack and pinned source clone plus the maintainer-cognition materials" and the flat judge has "cognition access likewise excluded," and the new asymmetry sentence in the decomposition paragraph rests on exactly this asymmetry ("the full stage legitimately reads the maintainer-cognition materials the flat judge is forbidden"). Perspective D of the full-stage prompt (Appendix A.1) reads `developer_cognition.json`. These cannot all be true. Given that this revision's precision wording was specifically about what the arms share, the appendix sentence must be corrected to state the actual access split; otherwise the fairness description of the central comparison is unverifiable. The fix is one sentence, but it is load-bearing.
   - **3.4** [minor, unfixable] The full-vs-flat separation is a single-backbone result: re-run three times on the second family it does not separate (discordant 7/7, p=1.0), and the routing rate — the located mechanism — collapses from 20.6% to 9.5%/7.8%. The paper discloses this prominently (abstract, contributions, Section 4's non-replication paragraph with its explicit "a property of that backbone under this protocol, not a law of the protocol" clause, and the Section 5 limitations disclosure), which is the right handling; the residual limitation — that the routing mechanism's operability across backbones is unknown — is inherent to the evidence and cannot be fixed by rewriting, only by further experiments on more families. The cross-family D-only-vs-flat significance (corrected p=0.029) is correctly flagged as backbone-confounded.
   - **3.5** [minor, fixable] The full stage is indistinguishable from its own source-forensics step alone (D-only: 0.706 recall / 0.733 suppression vs. full 0.804/0.800; p=0.55 on judged-positive sets). The paper reports the ordering cleanly ("core < flat < D-only ≈ full") and never claims full > D-only, but at n=81 this comparison is badly underpowered — the protocol's four-perspective machinery, chain audit, and rebuild loop add unproven value over just running the source-forensics mapping with the same red lines. One scoping sentence acknowledging this (and that D-only lacks the routing channel and leaks 2 more FPs) would prevent readers from over-crediting the full architecture.
   - **3.6** The new protocol–pool co-evolution paragraph is a genuine improvement: bounding the ef/nprobe carve-out (flips no verdict; four true bugs in the family confirmed on absence of sentinel semantics; no false positive involves those parameters) is exactly the right sensitivity analysis, and excluding the 16 objective-constraint-driven confirmations (recall drops to 0.490) quantifies the dependence. RQ3's methodology also merits note here: detecting that the released runner's counters cannot fire (verified by pointing it at a dead port → 100% success), substituting a per-template liveness oracle, measuring the mutation-space bound from the released constants, and disclosing that the recovered-panic class (HTTP 500 while GET / stays 200) is invisible to that oracle — this is a template for how to run a negative baseline credibly. The reverse direction is properly labeled a hand-executed probe informed by VDBFuzz's published case study.
   - **3.7** [minor, fixable] The co-evolution sentence "the flat judge admits no symmetric exclusion, since its single-prompt design records no perspective attribution" could mislead: the flat judge *shares* the objective-constraint red line (Appendix A.2), so the co-evolved rule inflates both arms' absolute rates and does not differentially favor the full stage in the headline comparison — which actually *strengthens* the full-vs-flat contrast. One clarifying clause would turn a potential misreading into a supporting point.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets *documentation-implementation bugs* in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): silent misbehavior that contradicts natural-language API documentation but produces no crash signal, and is therefore invisible to the one dedicated VDBMS fuzzer (VDBFuzz). Because the expectation for such checks lives in untagged behavioral prose, the authors argue every deterministic-oracle family is structurally anchored elsewhere, leaving an LLM as the practical oracle — which imports a false-positive problem. Their system, TestVDB, is a four-stage pipeline (specification extraction from documentation, strategy-bound test generation, sandboxed execution, and a confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly from a four-perspective cross-examination grounded in the implementation source).

The evidence is a human-machine deployment study plus a controlled re-adjudication study. Maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, with 23 fixed via merged PRs. Because an audit showed 43.3% of the citation pairs in the original evidence packages unsupported, the authors rebuilt all 81 packages against version-pinned documentation and re-adjudicated the pool under blinding in five judge arms, three runs each. The central comparison shows the structured confirmation stage at 0.804 recall / 0.800 suppression under the deployment counting convention versus 0.588 for a flat single-prompt judge on the same packs and sources (exact McNemar p=0.0072, Holm-corrected 0.029 across a pre-specified seven-test family); forced-verdict scoring collapses the two to parity, and hand-adjudicating the routed cases yields a joint judge-plus-human reading at 0.647 recall / 0.943 precision. A second model family replicates the source-only arm but not the full-vs-flat separation. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows complementary reach in both directions, measured over the baseline's full template space.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Real, verifiable practical impact: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs and 23 merged fix PRs across three production systems, with every fix-PR classified by changed files and a validated negative control (the disproved #9149 PR correctly classified test-only) — see 1.1, 3.1.
- **S2:** Measurement rigor well above the SE bar: an audit/rebuild/re-adjudication design that isolates pack quality from protocol, a disclosed contamination cleanup (10 packs re-run in 13 runs), and a pre-specified Holm family. I recomputed every reported interval, test, and decomposition and found zero discrepancies — see 3.2, 3.3, 4.1.
- **S3:** Honest multi-reading reporting: the three-valued verdict's three scorings (forced 0.529, joint 0.647, convention 0.804) are reported side by side in the abstract itself, and the decomposition attributes the separation to the routing channel rather than to forced accuracy — see 3.4.
- **S4:** Verified positioning against the closest prior work: I checked all five nearest competitors (VDBFuzz, Metamon, CASCADE, SATORI, MASTOR) and every characterization is accurate, including the paper's own self-limiting concessions — see 2.1–2.5.
- **S5:** The RQ3 probe substitutes an instrumented per-template oracle after demonstrating the baseline's released success/failure counters are uninformative, and draws only the conclusion the measurement supports — see 3.8.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The headline recall figure (0.804) is computed under a counting convention that credits Human-Review as confirmed, and the two arms route at very different rates (20.6% vs 8.6%), so the convention mechanically favors the higher-routing arm; under forced verdicts the judges are statistically indistinguishable. The paper discloses and decomposes this, but the abstract and contribution list still anchor on the convention-based number — see 3.4, Q3.
- **W2:** The central full-vs-flat separation is a single-backbone result: the three-run replication on a second model family does not separate (p=1.0). The disclosure is prominent and repeated, but it bounds the mechanism claim to one family — see 3.7.
- **W3:** The joint judge-plus-human reading is computed only for the full stage; the flat judge's routed cases receive no human pass, so the 0.647/0.943 joint figures have no equal-treatment comparator on the flat side, and the joint suppression arithmetic counts a returned case as intercepted — see 3.5, Q1.
- **W4:** Presentation density: the abstract runs to three dense paragraphs (~490 words) carrying dozens of numbers, and several RQ2 paragraphs exceed 300 words with multiply-nested clauses; the statistics are correct but effortful to extract — see 5.1–5.2.

## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where the system silently accepts an input or exhibits behavior that contradicts its natural-language API documentation, producing no crash and thus escaping crash-oracle fuzzers such as VDBFuzz. Because VDBMS documentation is untagged prose rather than a structured specification, the authors argue no deterministic oracle family can derive the expectation, leaving an LLM as the practical oracle — with its attendant false-positive problem. TestVDB is a four-stage pipeline: behavioral specification extraction from version-pinned documentation, test generation by attack agents under pre-bound strategies, sandboxed execution against a Docker-pinned target with raw HTTP logging, and a confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly (including source-code forensics) from a chain-auditor cross-examination under four perspectives, with a three-valued verdict that routes unresolvable cases to human review.

Empirically, across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs, 23 fixed via merged PRs. The paper then re-measures its own confirmation stage: an audit found 43.3% of cited (constraint, page) pairs in the frozen evidence packs unsupported, so all packs were rebuilt and the full 81-candidate pool re-adjudicated three times per judge configuration. The contract-only core recalls 8/51 (0.157) while suppressing 29/30 false positives; the full structured stage reaches 0.804 recall at 0.800 suppression against 0.588 for a flat single-prompt judge (exact McNemar p=0.0072, Holm-corrected 0.029), but the separation exists only under the counting convention that scores Human-Review routings as confirmed (forced verdicts: 0.529 vs 0.490, indistinguishable; the pair does not separate on a second model family), and the authors report all three scoring readings side by side. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz on Qdrant shows complementary reach: the crash oracle reaches none of the confirmed silent-accept defects across its full 205-template space, while a hand-guided TestVDB-style probe reaches VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Real-world yield with unusually strong external validation — 51/81 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 merged fix PRs, all fix PRs classified by changed files (none documentation-only), duplicate-tracked cases separated, and a per-case ledger shipping in the artifact — see 1.1, 3.1.
- **S2:** Measurement honesty that goes beyond disclosure to re-measurement: the authors audited their own evidence packs, rebuilt them, re-adjudicated everything, report three scoring conventions in one table, re-ran the flagship pair on a second model family and reported the null result, and withdrew an RQ3 explanation their own data disproved — see 3.1, 3.7.
- **S3:** Statistical hygiene that survives independent recomputation: seven exact McNemar tests with a declared Holm family, Wilson intervals, three runs with majority voting; every interval, p-value, multiplier assignment, and table row I recomputed agrees with the paper — see 4.1.
- **S4:** A clear problem formulation: Table 1's oracle-exclusion analysis (where each oracle candidate anchors its expectation) and a running example threaded through all four pipeline stages make the paper's position legible to a reader outside the niche — see 2.1, 5.1.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The headline comparison is convention-dependent and single-backbone: under forced verdicts the full stage and flat judge are indistinguishable (0.529 vs 0.490, p=0.51), and the full-vs-flat pair does not separate on the second model family (p=1.0). The abstract still leads with the convention-based 0.804 vs 0.588; the deployable joint system is 0.647 and not significantly better than the flat judge — see 3.2, 3.3.
- **W2:** Protocol–pool co-evolution: 16 of the full stage's 41 confirmations are driven by the seven objective-constraint classes codified from the same mining experience that produced the evaluated pool; excluding them lowers recall to 0.490 — see 3.4.
- **W3:** Every headline number sits downstream of author decisions: the pool is author-screened (no deterministic submission rule), and the 17 routed confirmations were hand-adjudicated non-blindly by the authors; 8 of those 17 failed that adjudication, underlining how much the 0.804 reading counts unadjudicated escalations — see 3.5, 3.8.
- **W4:** All evaluated candidates predate the reported pipeline generation (the GLM-5.3-Flash run contributed none of the 81), and the relationship between the described Section 3 pipeline and the pool-producing earlier rounds is not itemized in the text — see 3.6.
- **W5:** Isolated but real clarity and isolation flaws: the strata paragraph's overlap case (forced-confirmed majority AND routed via a minority-run Human-Review component) is easy to misread and the weak-evidence stratum's joint recall is never stated, and the flat-judge prompt enumerates only 4 of the 7 objective-constraint classes despite the appendix claiming shared rule content — see 4.2, 3.9.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** (strength) The deployment ledger (Section 4.2, Tables 2–3) is genuine practical impact: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs across three production systems, 23 fixed via merged PRs, with the fix-PR file classification validating actionability (all 23 modify implementation code, none documentation-only, and the classifier's one known failure mode — the test-only PR of the disproved #9149 — is itself documented). The bug-pattern taxonomy (72.5% silent-accept family) usefully characterizes the class. For a paper whose unit of value is confirmed upstream defects, this is a solid harvest.
   - **1.2 [minor, fixable]** The operational system is heavily human-in-the-loop, and the paper does not quantify the operator cost: author-side novelty screening and submission decisions sit in the loop for the 81-pool, and in the re-measurement 21 of 81 cases (17 routed confirmations + 4 routed false positives, Section 4.3) required hand adjudication — roughly one human decision per four candidates. A workload-per-confirmed-bug figure would let readers judge practical significance rather than infer it.
   - **1.3 [minor, unfixable]** Impact is bounded by construction to prose-constraint API regimes (Section 5, external validity: "we do not claim transfer to systems whose constraints live in machine-checkable schemas") and to three systems. This is honestly disclosed; it still caps the audience.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate (provisional; no literature survey performed)
   - **2.1** (strength, provisional) The paper's own positioning makes the delta legible even without field knowledge: it concedes that an LLM-generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is "not new here" (MASTOR) and that cross-request behavioral oracles are not unique to TestVDB, then locates its contribution in the *anchor inversion* — the prose is the ground truth and the implementation source is only the falsifier, whereas MASTOR anchors in the implementation, Metamon's falsifier is another LLM question, and CASCADE's falsifier is LLM-generated code sharing the documentation's ambiguity (its two residual false positives arose exactly from that shared assumption). As argued from the paper's text, this is a real, non-obvious delta.
   - **2.2 [minor, fixable]** (provisional) The delta's size is thin in places: the builder/auditor split, four perspectives, and fixed aggregation table are a disciplined rule system rather than a new inference mechanism, and the paper's freshest element — the audit-and-rebuild of one's own evaluation materials — is methodology rather than a technique others can reuse directly. Since Related Work concedes the nearest neighbors (MASTOR, Metamon, CASCADE, RBCTest) fairly, I rate Adequate on the paper's own argument, flagged provisional pending a specialist's check.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** The confirmation stage is never compared against any external confirmation/oracle technique on this pool — the flat judge is an internal ablation, and Metamon's profile is explicitly "not a head-to-head baseline." The Table 1 argument (no existing tool derives expectations from untagged system-level prose) partially excuses this, but even one adapted prose-constraint baseline would strengthen the mechanism claim.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** (strength) The measurement design is the paper's best feature. The two-correction attribution (Layer 0 shipped packs → Layer 1 rebuilt packs → Layer 2 protocol alignment) isolates one variable per layer; the seeded anchor (19/32 = 0.594 on never-submitted registrations) bounds the submission-filter direction; the three-scoring table exposes recall/suppression/precision under forced, joint, and convention readings; the four leak cases are re-measured on clean packs; the ten misses decompose into three named mechanisms; and the limitations section bounds each threat with a measurement rather than a disclaimer (e.g., the expectation-framing scan: 19 hits, all inside verbatim server responses).
   - **3.2 [major, fixable]** The central mechanism claim survives only under one reading and one backbone. Under forced verdicts the full stage and flat judge are statistically indistinguishable (0.529 vs 0.490, p=0.51), and the three-run re-run of the full-vs-flat pair on Qwen does not separate (0.588 vs 0.529, p=1.0, discordant 7/7). The paper is candid — "the routing advantage measured on the primary family is a property of that backbone under this protocol, not a law of the protocol itself" — and the abstract does state the non-replication. But the abstract and contributions still lead with 0.804 vs 0.588 under the convention that scores Human-Review routings as confirmed, and the joint system (0.647 recall, 0.943 precision) — the actual deployable reading — is not significantly better than the flat judge on this pool (confirmed sets 35 vs 34, p=1.0). Leading with the most favorable of three readings, in an abstract that must carry the nuance, overstates what is established. Fix: promote the joint reading (or a routing-rate comparison) to headline, demote 0.804 to the routing ablation it is, or extend replication to a third backbone.
   - **3.3 [minor, fixable]** The p=0.0072 McNemar pairs *confirmed sets*, pooling true-positive flips and false-positive leaks (the paper says so: "a 'leak' counts as confirmed on both sides"). Of the +13 net, +11 is recall and +2 is extra leakage. A recall-only McNemar over the 51 bugs is not reported; from the disclosed nets it would likely remain significant, but the reader cannot confirm the recall claim independent of the suppression trade.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** Protocol–pool co-evolution is disclosed and bounded but remains first-order: the seven objective-constraint classes were "codified from the same mining experience that produced the evaluated pool," and 16 of the full stage's 41 confirmations are driven by perspective B; the paper's own exclusion lowers recall to 0.490 — exactly the flat judge's forced-verdict level. The ef/nprobe carve-out check (flips no verdict; guards an absent pattern, conservative direction) is a good partial bound, but the class-level dependence means absolute recall conflates protocol quality with protocol fit. A revision should either evaluate on candidates from a post-hoc protocol generation or weaken the absolute-recall framing further.
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** The hand adjudication of the 17 routed confirmations and 4 routed false positives was performed by the authors, non-blind ("disclosed"), on pack materials only. It is honest, but 8 of 17 routed confirmations failing author adjudication (6 rejected, 2 material-insufficient) quantifies how far the convention's 0.804 sits above any human-validated system, and the adjudication was performed by the authors (adjudicator count undisclosed) with no inter-rater check.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** Provenance gap between method and data: all 81 evaluated candidates originate from earlier GLM-5.2 mining rounds, and the reported GLM-5.3-Flash run "contributed none of the 81" (Table 2 caption, Section 5). The Section 3 pipeline description therefore characterizes a system whose evaluated output is not in the pool; which generations produced the 81, and whether they included the pre-bound strategy registry and the confirmation stage as described, is not itemized in the text.

The paper treats adjudication as fallible and keeps two arbiter disagreements alive, which is the right posture; but with 49 unadjudicated submissions excluded, the 63.0% precision is an adjudicated-subset figure and the recall denominators inherit one arbiter's errors.
