## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets a class it names *documentation-implementation bugs* in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where a system silently accepts an input or produces behavior that contradicts its natural-language API documentation — no crash, no error code, wrong results (Section 1). It argues that no deterministic-oracle family anchors its expectation in system-level, untagged behavioral prose (Table 1), so an LLM-derived oracle is the practical option for this residual, which imports the LLM's false-positive problem. TestVDB is a four-stage pipeline: documentation is crawled and distilled into source-verified, typed constraints classified as endpoint- or system-level (Section 3.2); pre-registered strategies bound to three attack agents (boundary, state, semantic) turn constraints into executable probes under five generation gates (Section 3.3); probes run against a Docker-pinned target with raw HTTP logging (Section 3.4); and bug confirmation splits evidence assembly — a five-section evidence chain including implementation-source grounding and maintainer-cognition patterns — from cross-examination by four mechanical checks and four perspectives, with the implementation source as the falsification anchor (Section 3.5).

The evaluation reports an operational ledger over Milvus, Qdrant and Weaviate in which maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions, 23 fixed via merged PRs with the fix PRs classified as implementation-code changes (Section 4.2, Table 2); a blinded three-run re-adjudication of the same 81 candidates under five judge configurations, giving 87–90% false-positive suppression for the contract core at 0.216 majority recall, 0.373 at 1.000 precision for the full stage, 0.333 for a source-only arm, 0.392 for a flat single-prompt judge, and a second-backbone replication of the source-only arm (Section 4.3, Tables 4–5); and a bidirectional reach comparison on Qdrant against the crash-oracle fuzzer VDBFuzz, in which the fuzzer's full 205-template space reaches none of the silent-accept defects while TestVDB's boundary reasoning reaches VDBFuzz's integer-overflow crash class on v1.4.0, confirmed by hand (Section 4.4).

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** A real, verified-world yield: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs with 23 merged fixes across three production systems, backed by fix-nature classification and a hostile self-check (the classifier correctly flags the disproved #9149 fix PR as test-only) — see 1.1, 1.2.
- **S2:** An unusually disciplined and self-deflating evaluation: frozen-package blinding, three independent runs per arm, Wilson intervals, paired exact tests, a cognition-file leakage re-mining check that changed no verdict, and explicit reporting of a 0.841 → 0.373 recall drop as the cost of blinding — see 3.1, 4.1.
- **S3:** Positioning against the closest prior work is accurate and non-inflated; I fetched the four key competitors and every characterization I could check holds, including the paper's own admission that cross-request oracles and LLM-reviewed oracles are not new here — see 2.1, 2.2, 2.3.
- **S4:** The RQ3 comparison is done with real care for the baseline's side: the full template space is run with the time limit removed, the baseline's success counters are forensically examined (and shown uninformative by pointing the released runner at a dead port), and the conclusion is narrowed to the measured configuration rather than to "crash oracles" as a class — see 3.2.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The extraction stage — a core pipeline component, and the source of the four leaked false positives — is never measured; end-to-end precision is therefore unmeasured, and the headline suppression rates are conditional on the quality of artifacts that stage produced — see 3.3.
- **W2:** The RQ1 ledger's precision (63.0%) inherits an author-chosen submission filter and the two disclosed judgment-side biases that were live during that run, so the headline yield measures a human–machine process whose automated component re-measures at 0.373 recall under blinding; the abstract and contribution list should carry that qualifier explicitly — see 3.4.
- **W3:** The paper's own flat-judge arm removes the measured value of its central structural mechanism, and the novelty delta over the closest prior work (MASTOR, CASCADE, Metamon) is real but incremental; contribution 2's "discipline and auditability" claim is asserted, not evidenced — see 2.4, 2.5.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The paper solves a problem whose importance it does not have to argue from first principles: silent wrong-result failures in the storage layer of RAG systems. It shows this empirically rather than rhetorically — Table 1 reports that only 2 of the 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs produce a crash or panic, i.e. the class it targets is structurally outside the one dedicated VDBMS fuzzer's oracle (VDBFuzz, ICSE 2026, whose own text states it "currently only performs fuzzing for crash-related bugs"; verified in the cached full text). That is a clean, load-bearing motivating observation.
   - **1.2** The oracle-exclusion analysis (Table 1, Section 2.3) is the paper's most reusable conceptual contribution: it organizes prior oracle families by *where the expectation comes from* and demonstrates a residual — system-level untagged prose — that no structured-source family occupies. I checked the two rows that carry the most weight: SATORI's catalogue is unary response-field oracles derived from OpenAPI field metadata, and MASTOR's authority is the implementation (its own stated limitation is that it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code") — both characterizations in Table 1 hold. The analysis is a defensible research agenda statement even where one might argue about a row.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The run's cost profile is reported (≈67 active hours over five calendar days, ≈860 sandboxed executions, ≈70M input / 4.9M output tokens for the orchestrating session alone, Section 4.1) but never converted into a cost-effectiveness statement — no cost per confirmed bug, no comparison to the human alternative, and no statement of how many hours of human screening and submission the 81 submissions required. Since the significance claim rests on a deployed human–machine process, this accounting is the missing half of the impact argument.
   - **1.4 [minor, fixable]** The significance claim holds only under assumptions the paper states in the limitations but not in the contribution framing: prose-constraint APIs (not schema-governed systems) and a vendor-served model family. A third condition — that maintainer attention and a submission channel were available for dozens of candidates per target — is my own inference, not an assumption the paper states. Stating the paper-stated bounds next to the 51/23 headline (rather than only in Section 5) would make the impact claim more honest without weakening it.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** I fetched MASTOR (arXiv:2606.10465) and compared the paper's characterization of it against MASTOR's own text (the cached full text and its summary). Per MASTOR itself, it builds status oracles per execution path and field oracles on response bodies, generates behavioural-consistency oracles over cross-operation association types (its five: resource lifecycle, identifier flow, field-level data passing, nested resource dependency, state propagation), adds a ChallengerAgent review that drives one targeted regeneration, and grounds the oracles in implementation source. The paper under review describes that competitor in Section 6 and Table 1 row 5 as source-grounded, with "behavioural-consistency oracles over operation sequences" — a fair match to MASTOR's own account. The paper's conclusion that "a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is therefore not new here, and cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to TestVDB" is exactly right, and the remaining delta it claims — anchor inversion, prose as ground and implementation as falsifier — is corroborated by MASTOR's own stated limitation. Verdict: claim holds.
   - **2.2** I fetched CASCADE (DOI 10.1145/3808175) and verified both the paper's one-line description ("cross-checks documentation-derived tests against code generated from the same documentation") and its verbatim quotation of CASCADE's residual-false-positive analysis ("the generated code and tests made the same false assumption over something underspecified in the documentation"). Both match the cached full text. Verdict: holds, and the comparison is fair (CASCADE is method/Javadoc-level, which Table 1 row 6 already covers).
   - **2.3** I fetched Metamon (LLM4Code 2025) and SATORI (ASE 2025). Metamon's reported profile is quoted correctly (precision 0.722 / recall 0.480 on 9,482 pairs), as is its division of labour (EvoSuite regression tests capturing actual behavior; the same LLM judging agreement with the Javadoc and stabilizing itself via metamorphic prompts); SATORI's oracle catalogue is indeed unary response-field properties read from OpenAPI metadata, so "response-field rather than input-acceptance/state" is accurate. Verdict: both hold. The reference set here is close to exhaustive for the line — no high-relevance omission in the doc-derived-oracle family surfaced in my scoped searches.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** The delta over the closest prior work is incremental rather than clear: prose-to-constraint inference (Toradocu, RESTInfer, ICON), doc-derived tests judged by an LLM (CASCADE, Metamon), and source-grounded multi-agent oracle generation (MASTOR) all pre-exist. What is genuinely new is the *role assignment* (prose as ground, source as falsifier), the VDBMS instantiation with system-level constraints, and the empirical study; the paper says this itself, but the introduction's contribution framing ("a four-stage, multi-agent pipeline whose confirmation stage grounds every verdict in evidence sources outside the LLM... to break the extraction-judgment self-reference") still reads as a mechanism claim that the evaluation then removes. Repositioning around "we show which anchor combination actually works, at scale, and what it costs" would be more accurate to the evidence.
   - **2.5 [major, fixable]** The structural mechanism is claimed, then measured away by the paper's own arm: the flat single-prompt judge with the same evidence access matches the full stage (0.392 vs 0.373 majority recall, p = 1.000) and the source-only arm recovers most of the gain over the contract core (+0.117 of +0.157). Contribution 2 pre-empts this by claiming the four perspectives for "discipline and auditability", but nothing in the evaluation measures discipline or auditability — no count of what the rebuild loop caught, no audit-trail study, no analysis of whether the evidence chain made disagreements resolvable. As it stands, the paper's second contribution has no evaluated value proposition; either supply that evidence (e.g. rebuild-loop statistics, per-perspective traceability of the four leaked false positives) or state the mechanism as design rationale only. — see also Q3.
   - **2.6 [minor, fixable]** One uncited work bears directly on the paper's extraction premise and reliability discussion: "Documentation vs. Code Patterns: What Drives LLM-Based Exception Oracle Generation?" (2026, DOI 10.1145/3832783.3837464), which reports that stripping structured `@throws` documentation changes exception-oracle accuracy by less than one percentage point, i.e. LLM oracle predictions can ride shortcut signals rather than the documented semantics. That is the same failure mode the extraction stage must avoid and the same question the paper's hallucination paragraph (Section 6) raises with a generic citation; positioning against it would sharpen the paper's account of what its source-verification step buys. (Abstract-level check only; provisional.)

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The evaluation protocol is the strongest part of the paper and is reported in unusual detail: the RQ2 pool is re-adjudicated on frozen packages under blinding, every case is judged in every run (denominator 81/81, no case dropped), per-run and majority-vote figures are both given, intervals are Wilson, paired comparisons are exact McNemar, and the multiple-comparison correction is stated explicitly (Section 4.3). The cognition-file leakage check is exemplary — one tracker's file had absorbed six candidate-anchored entries; re-mining and re-adjudicating the eleven affected candidates changed no verdict — and the paper volunteers the deflationary comparison with its own earlier unblinded run (0.841 → 0.373). This is the standard the rest of the field should be held to, and it is what makes the negative results credible.
   - **3.2** The RQ3 design is equally careful in the other direction: VDBFuzz is run to natural completion on the same Qdrant v1.18.0 instance with the time limit removed (205 templates, 50.8 minutes, 22,540 mutations, 23,258 logged responses across 96 endpoint paths), the baseline's success counters are shown uninformative by a control experiment (pointing the released runner at a port with no server yields exit code 0 and a reported 100% success rate), and the paper measures both bounds of its claim — the released mutation space cannot construct the crashing input class, and of the crash classes a liveness oracle could record, only service death is in reach. I verified against the cached VDBFuzz full text that its own evaluation reports three Qdrant crashes (OOM, FPE, DoS — the bug-table row, cached-text line 885) with the DoS as Case Study #2 (dim overflow in `ChunkedVectors<T>`) and a 120-minute per-tool protocol (cached-text line 714); the paper's characterizations of the baseline are accurate, and it does not rest its result on the three legacy crashes it could not reproduce.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The extraction stage is a core pipeline component whose artifacts are never audited: "We report no constraint-level audit of extraction accuracy against the cited documentation pages, so end-to-end precision — extraction and confirmation together — is unmeasured" (Section 4.3). This matters for the headline because the confirmation stage is measured *on packs as extracted*, and the paper's own four leaked false positives are "extraction-side errors" whose packs "contain contract rows that read as supporting the violation". So the reported 87–90% suppression (contract core) and 1.000 (full stage) are properties of a pipeline whose first stage could be wrong in either direction, and the five generation gates (Section 3.3) and the source-verification step are precisely the places where such errors would or would not be caught. A sampled audit (e.g. 20–30 constraints per target, checked against the cited pages, with the extraction-stage error rate reported alongside) is a bounded revision that would make the paper's central numbers interpretable. — see Q1.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The RQ1 ledger — the paper's headline yield of 51 confirmed and 23 fixed — was produced with both disclosed judgment-side threats live ("expectation-framing bias ... affects precision-side rates, not the recall headline"; "observation leakage ... can unblind a nominally blind evaluation"; Section 5), by a submission process with "no deterministic submission rule" in which "submission decisions were made per candidate by the authors after novelty screening and manual review", and with 49 submissions excluded "still awaiting a maintainer verdict, or withdrawn by us without one" (Section 4.2). The paper discloses all of this and re-measures the same judgment under blinding — which is the right response — but the 63.0% precision figure cannot be read as the method's precision, and the abstract/introduction present "maintainers confirmed 51 of 81" without the confounding channel. Selective withdrawal is the one channel the paper never quantifies; if withdrawals followed partial maintainer signals, the adjudicated-subset precision is inflated by an unknown amount. — see Q2.
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** Generalization is narrower than the headline suggests: four of the five judge configurations are single-backbone (GLM-5.3-Flash), the RQ3 comparison is one vendor and (for the reverse direction) one old version, and the whole study covers three systems. The second-backbone arm checks the source-only rule only, and the paper says so (Section 5), so this is a bounded-claim issue rather than a hidden one — but the abstract's "across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate" should not be read as three-system generalization of the *rate* results, which come from a Qdrant run for the process ledger and a mixed pool for RQ2.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** The recall denominator mixes a measured quantity with a classification decision: the 51 confirmed bugs include the four approximate-count results that the paper itself places outside the assertion framework by construction ("all four are missed by every arm in every run"), so the headline 0.216/0.333/0.373 is partly a statement about what the framework cannot express. The paper reports the eligible-subset view (0.234/0.362/0.404 on 47) exactly once and leads with the all-51 figures "throughout"; leading with 47 when discussing recall marginals, and using 51 only for correspondence with the ledger, would avoid a reader attributing the gap to the judges.

4. **Verifiability** — Excellent
   - **4.1** The Data Availability section declares an artifact and enumerates exactly what is in it: agent role definitions and the strategy-trigger registry, per-version knowledge bases and specifications, all generated scripts with raw HTTP logs, one evidence chain per candidate, the submission ledger, the frozen RQ2 packages with per-case judge outputs for every arm, the RQ3 baseline reports with per-template logs, and "the analysis scripts that recompute every rate, interval, and paired test reported here". I checked the link resolves (HTTP 302 to the repository's file API), which with the enumeration above clears the rubric's bar for declared and reachable artifacts; the paper text is also self-contained enough to follow the method without the artifact.
   - **4.2 [minor, fixable]** Two residual caveats: the anonymous link is ephemeral by construction (a durable mirror, e.g. a Zenodo DOI, would protect the artifact after the anonymity period), and the LLM configuration is reported as serving identifiers ("GLM-5.3-Flash (September 2026)") rather than immutable weights (both in Section 5), so re-running the *generation* side of the pipeline is bounded by model drift. The frozen per-case outputs mitigate this for checking the reported rates, which is why the criterion still reads Excellent rather than Adequate.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1 [minor, fixable]** The arm numbering is internally inconsistent. Section 4.3 states "Five judge configurations follow ... contract core (pack only), full stage ..., source-only ..., flat judge ..., and source-only on a second backbone", which makes the source-only arm the third and the flat judge the fourth; but the text calls the source-only rule "a genuine fourth arm" and the flat judge "a fifth arm". Contribution 3's "five-arm evaluation" list orders them as core, full, source-only, flat, second backbone. Because the arms are the paper's central evidence, the numbering should be made consistent (and a table row naming each arm once, with its inputs, would help readers).
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** One accounting sentence does not parse against the tables: "relative to the source-only majority the full stage vetoes the one leaked false positive at the cost of four confirmed true positives" (Section 4.3). The majority counts have the full stage at 19 TP and source-only at 17 TP, so the net is +2 true positives, not a four-TP cost; if the intended meaning is case-level vetoes offset by recoveries elsewhere, the sentence needs the numbers. — see Q4.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** Two statistical statements need tightening. The parenthetical "the remaining perspectives add a smaller increment (p = 0.017 uncorrected...)" attaches the core-vs-full p-value to the source-only→full step, whose own paired p is reported as 0.648 in the preceding sentence. And the abstract's "no ordering step survives Holm correction at this pool size" generalizes beyond the text, which only says the p = 0.017 result does not survive Holm "across our six paired tests" — under a six-test Holm family the flat-vs-core comparison (p = 0.008) would be the first step and would sit at the correction threshold, so the family and the ordering claims should be stated precisely in one place.

### Questions for Authors

- **Q1:** Would a sampled constraint-level extraction audit (say, 20–30 constraints per target checked against the cited documentation pages) leave the reported suppression and recall rates qualitatively unchanged, and what is the measured rate of constraints whose cited page does not support them? — effect: if the extraction error rate is non-trivial, item 3.3's rating moves down, because the headline suppression numbers would then be partly an artifact of pack construction rather than confirmation performance.
- **Q2:** Of the 49 unadjudicated submissions, how many were withdrawn by the authors, and how many of those withdrawals followed a partial maintainer signal (a comment or an intent-to-close) rather than an explicit verdict? — effect: a clean answer would move item 3.4's rating up, since selective withdrawal is the one confounding channel the paper currently leaves unquantified.
- **Q3:** What evidence exists that the four-perspective chain and the builder/auditor split buy discipline or auditability — for example, how many verdicts did the rebuild loop change, and did any of the four extraction-induced false positives get flagged by a mechanical check before leaking? — effect: concrete numbers here would move item 2.5 from major toward minor, since the mechanism's claimed value would no longer rest on assertion alone.
- **Q4:** In the sentence comparing the full stage with the source-only majority, what exactly are the "four confirmed true positives" that are lost — case-level vetoes, or a net count — and how do they reconcile with the 19 vs. 17 TP majorities in Tables 4 and 5? — effect: clarifying this would resolve item 5.2 and prevent a reader from concluding the full stage's priority rule is net-harmful when the tables show it net-positive.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation–implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where a system silently accepts an input or returns behavior that violates its natural-language API documentation and therefore produces no crash signal. It argues structurally (Table 1) that every deterministic-oracle family anchors its expectation outside system-level untagged prose, and builds TestVDB, a four-stage pipeline that (i) crawls vendor documentation into typed, source-verified constraints with evidence tiers, (ii) compiles constraints into executable probes under a pre-bound strategy registry, (iii) runs them against Docker-pinned versions while logging raw HTTP, and (iv) confirms candidates through a five-section evidence chain cross-examined from contract, physical, cognition, and source perspectives, with the implementation source as the falsification anchor.

Across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, 81 adjudicated submissions yielded 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs and 23 merged fixes. In a controlled three-run re-adjudication of the same pool under frozen packages, the contract core suppresses 87–90% of false positives at 0.216 majority-vote recall; the full stage reaches 0.373 recall at 1.000 suppression; a source-only arm over the same packages recovers most of the gain while a flat single-prompt judge matches the structured stage; and no ordering step survives Holm correction at this pool size. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz on Qdrant reports complementary reach, including a defect in the released VDBFuzz runner's success/failure counters and two-sided bounds on what its released mutation space can construct.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The RQ2 measurement is unusually rigorous for LLM-oracle research: frozen packages, three independent runs per arm with per-case written verdicts, majority voting, Wilson intervals, paired McNemar tests, a cognition-file leak re-mining audit, and a flat-judge control the authors report against their own scaffolding. — see 3.1, 3.2, 3.3
- **S2:** The positioning against the closest prior work is verified and accurate; I checked the five works the paper's claim rests on (cached full texts) and found no mischaracterization — the "anchor inversion" delta holds. — see 2.1
- **S3:** The real-world yield is substantial and validated: 51 maintainer-confirmed defects, 23 merged fixes that all modify implementation code, a per-case ledger covering all 130 submissions, and a bug taxonomy dominated by the silent-acceptance family (37/51). — see 1.1
- **S4:** Epistemic integrity in reporting: the authors disclose the unblinded-to-blinded recall drop (0.841→0.373), withdraw an earlier explanation of VDBFuzz's silence, reclassify their own #9149 from true positive to not-a-bug after a seven-version re-probe, and demote the four-perspective chain to "discipline and auditability" once the flat judge matches it. — see 3.2, 3.3, 2.2

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The paper asserts that RQ2's frozen-package protocol is exposed to neither disclosed judgment-side bias channel, but no pack-side sanitization is shown; the packs are rebuilt from the run's own evidence chains — the artifacts that carry those channels in the operational pipeline. — see 3.5
- **W2:** Extraction is unaudited (the paper says so), so end-to-end precision is unmeasured and the paper's own motivating failure mode — an LLM over-reading prose into an over-strong constraint — is unquantified, even though the four leaked false positives are exactly that failure. — see 3.6
- **W3:** Under the paper's own flat-judge control, the system-level novelty reduces to "prose as ground, implementation source as falsifier"; the multi-agent scaffolding is not a measured differentiator, and the residual mechanism delta to MASTOR/CASCADE is thin. — see 2.2

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The problem is real and the yield is verified where it can be: Table 2 reports 81 adjudicated submissions, 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 fixed, with the fix-nature check (all 23 merged fix PRs modify implementation code, none documentation-only) and the #9149 classifier sanity check. Table 3's taxonomy shows the dominant family is silent acceptance (Silent-Accept 25 + Type-Coercion 8 + Silent-Ignore 4), which is exactly the class the paper argues crash-oracle fuzzers structurally miss. For the VDBMS/database-testing community this is a concrete, actionable result, and it is validated by an external signal beyond the authors' own judgment (merged upstream fixes).
   - **1.2 [minor, fixable]** The significance claim is bounded in a way that deserves a plainer statement. The Evaluation section says the 81-pool is "a submission-filtered subset of the pipeline's confirmed output rather than a random sample of it" (submission decisions were made per candidate by the authors after novelty screening and manual review), and 49 further submissions are excluded as unadjudicated. The authors anchor the filter's direction with the 32-candidate blinded re-adjudication (19/32, 0.594), which is good practice — but the automated stage's standalone discovery yield on a fresh system is still unquantified, so a reader may over-read the 63.0% as the pipeline's precision. A short explicit statement of the deployment model (the pipeline is a triage/evidence machine with a human submission gate; its measured strength is suppression, its measured weakness is recall) would keep significance claims aligned with what is measured. (Assessed from the paper's own evidence; tracker-level verification is outside my checked scope.)

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** I checked the paper's characterizations against the cached full texts of the works its delta rests on, each with a verdict: **MASTOR** (stem `mastor a multi agent approach to semantic test oracle generation for restful apis (corr 2026)`) — the paper says its authority is the implementation, and MASTOR's own stated limitation confirms it ("MASTOR infers semantics from implementation behavior. It cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code"); the source-grounded multi-agent design, cross-operation behavioural-consistency oracles, and ChallengerAgent review are all as described. Holds. **CASCADE** (stem `cascade detecting inconsistencies between code and documentation with automatic test generation (acm on software engineering 2026)`) — the paper's quote about "the generated code and tests made the same false assumption over something underspecified in the documentation" is verbatim in the cached text; the phase-2 falsifier is regenerated code from the same documentation. Holds. **Metamon** (stem `metamon finding inconsistencies between program documentation and behavior using metamorphic llm queries (ieee acm international workshop on large language models for code llm4code 2025)`) — "precision 0.722 at recall 0.480 on 9,482 pairs" matches verbatim; its falsifier is the same LLM judge it stabilises. Holds. **SATORI** (stem `satori static test oracle generation for rest apis (40th ieee acm international conference on automated software engineering ase 2025)`) — response-field oracles from OAS fields and their natural-language descriptions, not system-level prose. Holds. **VDBFuzz** (stem `vdbfuzz understanding and detecting crash bugs in vector database management systems (ieee acm international conference on software engineering icse 2026)`) — crash-oracle by construction; the Qdrant bug table lists three crashes typed OOM/FPE/DoS; the 120-minute protocol and the 205-template released Qdrant set (artifact check from the cached summary) are as described. Holds. The related-work framing ("a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is therefore not new here, and cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to TestVDB; what is new is the anchor inversion") is therefore an accurate statement of a genuine, non-obvious delta.
   - **2.2 [minor, fixable]** The residual delta is thinner than the contribution list suggests, on the paper's own evidence. The fifth arm (flat single-prompt judge) matches the structured stage (statistically indistinguishable from source-only, p=0.678, and from the full stage, p=1.000; beats the contract core, p=0.008), so the four-perspective chain is not a measured mechanism. What survives is (a) prose-as-ground with the implementation as falsifier, (b) the VDBMS instantiation, and (c) the empirical decomposition — while (a) sits close to MASTOR on the source-grounded side and to CASCADE/Metamon on the doc-derived-test side. The paper largely concedes this ("claimed for discipline and auditability"), but the title and contribution bullets still headline the pipeline. Recommend restating the novelty claim explicitly as the anchor inversion plus the evidence-access finding, with the pipeline described as an instantiation rather than as a mechanism-level contribution, so the claim matches the control experiment.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** Related-work coverage in my specialty is dense and accurate, with one gap worth closing (provisional — checked at abstract level). **DocPrism** (arXiv:2511.00215; DOI 10.1145/3832248) is an uncited LLM tool for code–documentation inconsistency detection whose stated motivation is the same over-flagging problem the paper's introduction raises ("over 90% of functions are flagged as inconsistent"), and which filters to incorrectness inconsistencies. It is method-level and offers no independent falsifier, so it does not challenge the delta — but the LLM-derived-oracle paragraph would benefit from positioning against it, since it is the newest instance of the "LLM reads prose and misreads it" problem. Two older ancestors of the doc-oracle line, "Using test oracles generated from program documentation" (IEEE TSE 1998) and "Inferring Resource Specifications from Natural Language API Documentation" (ASE 2009), are also uncited (metadata-level check only; provisional) — historical rather than decisive, but they predate Toradocu/RESTInfer as the origin of expectation-from-prose and would strengthen the line's completeness.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The RQ2 protocol is the paper's strongest methodological asset and is described well enough to be followed: identical frozen packages across five judge configurations, three independent runs each, no access to labels or issue history, every case judged in every run (81/81 per run, no case dropped), per-case verdicts written as run output, majority vote (≥2/3), per-run ranges alongside majority figures, Wilson intervals on all headline rates, and paired exact McNemar tests over the 81 cases. Between-configuration claims are also handled with unusual care: "a re-aggregation is not an arm" correctly refuses to re-decide recorded D fields as if they were the single-perspective arm. The test statistics check out against the tables (16 beats / 7 loses against a 9-case correctness difference; 17/5 against 12; 11/8 against 3 — all consistent). Among the closest comparators I surveyed (Metamon, CASCADE, MASTOR), each reports a single evaluation configuration; this three-run frozen-package design is a methodological step beyond them. (LLM-oracle evaluation methodology is one of my two specialty areas.)
   - **3.2** The ground-truth discipline is stronger than typical for LLM bug-finding: maintainer adjudication with an explicit duplicate axis kept orthogonal to the confirmed/false-positive axis, a per-submission ledger included in the artifact for all 81 adjudicated plus 49 unadjudicated submissions, fix-nature classification of all 23 merged PRs (none documentation-only), the classifier validated against the one case where the re-probe and the classifier agree that the "fix" was test-only, and the #9149 reclassification documented rather than buried. The cognition-leak audit (six candidate-anchored entries found and re-mined; eleven affected candidates re-adjudicated; no verdict changed in any run) is exactly the kind of check this literature needs.
   - **3.3** The RQ3 comparison is handled with matching discipline: the released runner's success counter is shown to count process-exit-zero as success and its failure markers never fire (a port with no server reports 100% success), so the authors substitute a liveness oracle and then bound what a liveness oracle can see, at the level of an individual claim. They also withdraw an earlier, wrong explanation of VDBFuzz's silence ("three released templates do issue wait=false upserts, so that explanation does not hold and we withdraw it"), and they state the reverse-direction confirmation is a hand-executed probe, not an automated run. This is the honest form of a comparison paper.
   - **3.4 [minor, fixable]** The measured half of RQ3 uses a baseline oracle that is strictly weaker than VDBFuzz's published one: the substituted per-template liveness check ("GET / must keep returning 200") cannot fire on recovered panics or 4xx/5xx responses, which VDBFuzz's crash oracle does count. The paper's own footnote confirms the consequence for the reverse direction — on v1.4.0 the integer-overflow bug it reproduces is "a recovered panic, not a service death---HTTP 500 with a divide-by-zero panic ... while GET / keeps returning 200, so a liveness oracle would not record it either". So the measured zero ("we applied 22,540 mutations ... and recorded none") is evidence about service death only, while the paper's headline comparison sentence ("its full template set reaches none of them") is structurally true for silent accepts regardless. This is disclosed and bounded, but the abstract reader will over-credit the measured run. The run already logs "14,022 2xx, 7,520 400, 1,512 404, 204 among 409/422/429" — reporting the per-template anomaly candidates over those response classes would let a reader see the substitution's blind spot quantified rather than argued.
   - **3.5 [major, fixable]** The RQ2 bias-freedom claim is asserted without a shown mechanism. The Limitations section says the two judgment-side threats (expectation-framing text "phrased from the reporter's perspective", and "probe description text enters judge observations without sanitization") are "addressed by the frozen-package RQ2 protocol", and that "the rates reported from that protocol are exposed to neither channel". But the RQ2 subsection says the packs were "rebuilt from the run's own evidence chains" — the very artifacts that carry reporter-side framing and unsanitized probe text in the operational pipeline — and no sanitization rule is stated for those two channels when the packs are assembled. The paper defines the pack's composition ("one candidate's frozen evidence bundle"; "the observed execution transcript plus the documented contract"), but that description does not say whether reporter-framed expectation text or probe descriptions were removed from the transcript or contract it names. The four leaked false positives are attributed to "contract rows that read as supporting the violation" (an extraction-side effect), which shows the pack text does move verdicts, making the sanitization question material rather than pedantic. One paragraph (or an artifact diff) specifying the sanitization rules applied when packs are assembled — and, if the probe text was not sanitized, the affected rates — is needed before the RQ2 numbers can be read as bias-free.
   - **3.6 [major, fixable]** The extraction stage is explicitly unaudited: "We report no constraint-level audit of extraction accuracy against the cited documentation pages, so end-to-end precision---extraction and confirmation together---is unmeasured." This is the half of the pipeline that produces the expectations, and the paper's own motivation identifies extraction hallucination as the first of the three threats an LLM oracle imports. The `source_verified` flag and per-constraint `source_url` (described in the extraction subsection) already provide the hook for a sampled audit — e.g., N randomly drawn constraints re-checked by a human/independent read against the cited page, reporting the rate of over-strong or unsupported constraints. Without it, the paper cannot say whether the 87–90% suppression measures the confirmation stage or the (unknown-quality) packs it consumes, and the four extraction-induced leaks are the only visible tip of an unmeasured population.
   - **3.7 [minor, fixable]** Perspective C (maintainer cognition) is partly circular as a confirmation signal: the cognition file is mined from the same issue/PR trackers that supply the ground-truth labels, so "confirms 13 of 51 at precision 1.000" partly measures agreement with the label-generating process rather than independent evidence. The paper already downgrades C to "suggestive only" and shows the re-mining audit, which is the right response; stating the circularity explicitly in the limitations (rather than only the leakage caveat) would make the bound on C's value clear to a reader.

4. **Verifiability** — Excellent
   - **4.1** The Data Availability section declares a replication package whose inventory is unusually complete for this kind of paper: agent role definitions and the strategy-trigger registry, per-version knowledge bases and specifications, all scripts with raw HTTP logs, one evidence chain per candidate, the submission ledger, the frozen RQ2 packages with per-case judge outputs for every arm, the RQ3 baseline reports with per-template logs, and analysis scripts that recompute every rate, interval, and paired test. The text itself is largely self-contained: versions and doc/image/source pins, the five gates, the level definition, the priority rule, the judge configurations, and the aggregation are all specified. If the package matches the description, an independent team can recompute the headline claims.
   - **4.2 [minor, fixable]** I attempted a reachability check of the declared link (as permitted: declared and reachable, no clone/run). The host redirected the viewer path to its API endpoint, which answered `401 {"error":"not_connected"}` — identically for a control nonexistent repository, so my probe carries no signal either way, and I did not attempt to clone. Since the artifact is anonymous-hosted and the review depends on it, I recommend the authors (a) verify the link resolves in a normal browser session before camera-ready, and (b) include a manifest with per-file hashes so that any later packaging changes are detectable. This is a process note, not evidence of a dead link.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1** The structure is sound and the authors invest in disambiguation where it matters: the two-layers framing that opens RQ2 ("The two headline results are earned under different configurations ... neither is the complement of the other") is exactly what a reader needs, and the false-positive terminology block (false-positive candidate vs \textsc{False-Positive} verdict vs "leak" vs pack vs case) prevents the most likely misreading. Tables 4–5 are compact and their captions carry the majority-vote caveats. Figure 1 (pipeline) exists in the source tree (both a PDF and PNG under `figures/pipeline-v9`), and the running #10369 example is threaded through all four stages coherently.
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** A numeric inconsistency in the extraction summary: "emitted 90 typed constraints, 75 endpoint-level checkable assertions, and 14 cross-request contracts (7 behavioral, 7 state-invariant)" — 75 + 14 = 89, not 90, and the next clause says "all 90 passed the schema-and-tier validation gate." Either one constraint has no checkable assertion (worth saying) or one of the counts is wrong; reconcile and, if the former, name the category.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** The abstract is three very long sentences carrying roughly a dozen quantitative results (suppression ranges, recall values, arm comparisons, Holm status, bidirectional probe results). It is accurate but not readable; one short plain-language sentence stating the tradeoff ("the system buys high precision at the cost of low recall, and the source-grounded judge is where the precision comes from") before the numbers would make the contribution legible to a first reader.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** Several sentences have ambiguous antecedents or stack too many clauses — e.g., in the External-corroboration paragraph, "The gate's own caveat: two of its by-design matches were word-level false positives" glosses "its" as the novelty gate only after a re-read; and the "Which perspective carries the decision" paragraph runs a full page without structural breaks. A light copy-edit pass (split, name the referent) would fix this class of issue; there are no language errors that obstruct evaluation.

### Questions for Authors

- **Q1:** What sanitization was applied to the two disclosed bias channels (reporter-framed expectation text; probe descriptions inside judge observations) when the packs were rebuilt from the run's own evidence chains — i.e., does the "observed execution transcript" contain raw HTTP only, or does it carry probe text? — intended effect: a concrete sanitization statement would let item 3.5's "exposed to neither channel" claim stand on evidence, moving Soundness up; absent that, 3.5's rating reflects an unsupported assertion at the center of the RQ2 measurement.

## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets a class of vector-database bugs it calls *documentation-implementation bugs*: inputs a VDBMS silently accepts, or behaviors it produces, that contradict its API documentation — for example a search API that accepts `nprobe=0` with HTTP 200 although the documentation declares the range `[1, 16384]`. Its central argument is that no deterministic oracle family gets its expectation from system-level, untagged prose: a table of oracle candidates (crash oracles, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property-based testing, REST spec-derived oracles, documentation-derived oracles) walks each family and locates its anchor elsewhere, leaving an LLM-derived oracle as the practical option and importing an associated false-positive problem. The proposed pipeline, TestVDB, has four stages: (i) extraction of structured, source-verified behavioral specifications from vendor documentation, with typed constraints, evidence tiers, and a version-alignment gate; (ii) generation of executable probes by three attack agents (boundary, state, semantic) under a registry of pre-bound strategies; (iii) sandboxed execution against a Docker-pinned version with raw HTTP logging; and (iv) confirmation, split into an evidence builder that assembles a five-section evidence chain including source grounding in the implementation, and a chain auditor that applies four mechanical checks and four perspectives (contract, physical, cognition, source) before issuing Confirmed, Refuted, or Neutral.

The evaluation reports three studies. RQ1 is a ledger of 81 adjudicated upstream submissions across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate: 51 confirmed by maintainers as real bugs, 23 fixed via merged PRs, 30 adjudicated false positives, with 49 further submissions excluded as unadjudicated; it also groups the 51 into eight bug patterns and reports a process ledger for one complete Qdrant v1.18.0 run. RQ2 re-adjudicates the 81-candidate pool under frozen packages, three independent runs per arm across five arms — contract core (false-positive suppression 0.867–0.900, majority recall 0.216), full stage adding source forensics and maintainer cognition (suppression 1.000, majority recall 0.373), a source-only arm (recall 0.333 at precision 0.944), a flat single-prompt judge that matches the structured arms, and a source-only arm on a second model family — with paired McNemar tests and Holm correction across six tests. RQ3 probes reach bidirectionally against VDBFuzz on Qdrant: VDBFuzz's full released template set (205 templates, 22,540 mutations) records no failure against the v1.18.0 instance where the paper's silent-accept defects are live, while a hand-executed boundary probe reaches VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow on v1.4.0.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The practical yield is real and adjudicated: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs with 23 merged fix PRs across three production systems, plus a fix-nature classification showing the fixes repair implementation code rather than documentation or tests. — see 1.1
- **S2:** RQ2 is an unusually disciplined measurement of an LLM pipeline: frozen packages, blinding, three runs, a full 81/81 denominator, a stated conservative verdict rule, majority voting, Wilson intervals, exact McNemar tests, a leakage self-audit of the cognition files, and arm designs (source-only, flat judge, second backbone) that test the paper's own mechanism claim rather than assuming it. — see 3.1, 3.2, 3.3, 4.1, 4.2
- **S3:** The paper repeatedly turns its evidence against its own framing: it demotes the four-perspective chain to discipline and auditability once the flat judge matches it, withdraws an earlier mis-explanation in RQ3, discloses that a previously reported recall (0.841) was inflated by the unblinded protocol, and reports extraction-induced false-positive leaks outside its confirmation-stage error budget. — see 2.2, 3.3

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** Every RQ2 number is measured on frozen packages *as extracted*, and the paper's own four leaked false positives trace to pack construction rather than judgment; yet no constraint-level extraction audit is reported and the abstract states the suppression and recall figures without the pack-conditional qualifier. — see 3.4
- **W2:** The RQ1 headline (51 of 81 confirmed, 23 fixed) is a human-in-the-loop, multi-round cumulative number; the paper reports a one-run funnel behind it (60 defect-confirming candidates resolving to five new defect registrations), but no per-run automated yield comparable to the cumulative ledger, and no quantification of author-side screening attrition, so the contribution's practical size cannot be read off the headline. — see 1.3, 3.5
- **W3:** The account of the judgment-side threats does not line up internally: the Limitations section says expectation framing affects "precision-side rates, not the recall headline", while RQ2 attributes the recall drop from 0.841 to 0.373 to "the disclosed framing and leakage threats"; the causal attribution is also asserted rather than isolated. — see 3.6
- **W4:** Several small accounting, statistical-reporting, and claim-scoping statements need reconciliation before the numbers and reach claims can be quoted with confidence. — see 3.7, 3.8, 3.9

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate

   - **1.1** [strength] The submission ledger (Section 4.2, Table 2) reports 81 adjudicated submissions: Milvus 43 adjudicated / 29 confirmed / 11 fixed / 14 false positives, Qdrant 28 / 14 / 9 / 14, Weaviate 10 / 8 / 3 / 2. The paper further classifies every merged fix PR by changed files (all 23 touch implementation code, 15 add regression tests, none is documentation-only) and cross-checks the classifier on the one candidate it later disproved (Qdrant #9149), where the classifier correctly identifies the fix PR as test-only. Real, adjudicated, and independently corroborated upstream impact — this is far stronger than a synthetic-benchmark yield and is the paper's clearest asset.
   - **1.2** [strength] Reference-table 1 (the oracle-candidate table in Section 2.3) is organized by a single crisp criterion — where each candidate oracle gets its expectation — and derives from it the paper's central exclusion argument, with explicit limit cases (differential testing's divergent reference; schemas bounding fields but not behavior). If it holds, this analysis is reusable well beyond VDBMSs, which gives the work a conceptual contribution on top of the pipeline. (Assessment of the individual row claims is provisional; see 2.3.)
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The practical impact is bounded by an accounting the paper scatters rather than states up front. Section 4.2 says the ledger is "cumulative across mining rounds and versions since early 2026 and predates this run", and the process ledger of one complete run reports 60 defect-confirming candidates resolving to five newly registered defect numbers. A reader cannot therefore tell from the abstract or the contribution list what a single automated run of TestVDB produces. Recommend surfacing the per-run automated yield (and, if recorded, the author-screening attrition) next to the cumulative headline.
   - **1.4 [minor, unfixable]** The demonstrated impact is bounded to three systems, to the prose-documentation regime the paper explicitly tests, and — for four of the five arms — to one LLM family (Section 5). These bounds are disclosed, and they are inherent to the study rather than fixable in revision, but they cap how far the significance claim should be extended.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate *(provisional: I did not survey the field; this is assessed from the paper's own positioning and reasoning.)*

   - **2.1** [strength] The Related Work section engages the closest named systems directly and states checkable deltas rather than generic claims: for MASTOR ("the closest to this work") it concedes that a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is not new and that cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to TestVDB, and pins the delta to the "anchor inversion" — MASTOR's authority is the implementation, whereas TestVDB takes the prose as ground and uses the implementation only as the falsifier. For Metamon it argues the falsifier is another LLM question — "precisely the self-reference TestVDB breaks"; for CASCADE it notes the falsifier shares the documentation's ambiguity. These are specific and falsifiable positioning statements.
   - **2.2** [strength] The paper concedes what is not new proactively, including against itself: contribution 2 states the four-perspective chain "is claimed for discipline and auditability, since a flat single-prompt judge with the same evidence access matches it". Honest positioning of this kind narrows the novelty claim but protects it.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** *(provisional)* The exclusion argument and the novelty delta both rest on load-bearing characterizations of prior work that I cannot verify from the paper alone: SATORI yielding "response-field rather than input-acceptance/state properties", AGORA+ "cannot reach inputs the traffic did not exercise", property-based tooling facing endpoints with no schema, the doc-derived line being method-/parameter-level and often tagged, and the domain claims that VDBFuzz is "the one dedicated VDBMS fuzzer". If any named tool already derives input-acceptance or state expectations from system-level, untagged prose, both the exclusion table's residual row and the anchor-inversion delta narrow. The authors should either strengthen the citations for these rows or mark them as their survey's reading.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** After the authors' own demotion of the scaffolding, the contribution list's second item ("a four-stage, multi-agent pipeline") does not state what remains new in positive terms. The remaining novelty is the anchor inversion plus the evidence-chain artifact and the field deployment; saying so explicitly would give reviewers a sharper target than a pipeline whose measurable edge the paper itself confines to suppression (30/30 versus 29/30).

3. **Soundness** — Adequate

   - **3.1** [strength] The RQ2 protocol (Section 4.3) is specified tightly enough to be scrutinized: identical frozen packages, blinding, three independent runs, every case judged in every run (denominator 81/81), a conservative binary rule that resolves uncertainty toward False-Positive, majority voting at ≥2/3, and per-run judge outputs written at run time. The statistics are reported with Wilson intervals and paired exact tests. I recomputed the reported Wilson intervals (19/51 → [0.253, 0.510]; 11/51 → [0.125, 0.346]; 26/30 → [0.703, 0.947]; 17/51 → [0.220, 0.470]; 15/51 → [0.187, 0.430]), the McNemar p-values (0.093 for 16 vs 7; 0.017 for 17 vs 5; 0.648 for 11 vs 8), and the per-run arithmetic of Tables 4 and 5 (every row sums to 51 true bugs and 30 false positives); all reconcile, as do the reported agreement figures once the vote structure is reconstructed (never-confirmed true bugs are unanimous rejections, so they count as agreement).
   - **3.2** [strength] RQ3 (Section 4.4) is a real baseline measurement rather than a strawman: the authors discover that the released VDBFuzz runner's own success counters are uninformative (it reports 100% success against a port with no server), substitute a per-template liveness oracle, run the full released template space (205 templates, 22,540 mutations, 50.8 minutes), and bound the result to the released configuration using code references and the required overflow magnitude, with the crash reproduced by hand on v1.4.0. Claims are stated only as narrowly as the measurement supports.
   - **3.3** [strength] The leakage self-audit (Section 4.3) is exactly the check a skeptical reader would demand for a perfect-suppression result: the paper detected that one tracker's cognition file had absorbed six candidate-anchored entries, re-mined it, re-adjudicated the eleven affected candidates, and reports that no verdict changed in any of the three runs; the other two cognition files reference no pool candidate. Combined with the disclosed withdrawal of an earlier explanation in RQ3, this materially raises trust in the reported numbers.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The paper's headline measurement claims are stage-conditional in a way the abstract conceals. Section 4.3 states that the four leaked false positives are "extraction-side errors that sit outside the confirmation-stage error budget reported here" and that "no constraint-level audit of extraction accuracy against the cited documentation pages" is reported, so "end-to-end precision — extraction and confirmation together — is unmeasured"; yet the abstract presents "87–90% of false positives" and "0.373 with zero false positives confirmed" without that qualifier, and the extraction stage is the pipeline's largest LLM-exposed component. Even a modest constraint-level audit (a scored sample of extracted constraints against their cited pages) plus carrying the qualifier into the abstract would close the gap; as it stands, a reader can reasonably mistake stage-level numbers for pipeline-level performance.
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** The RQ1 ledger is described throughout as a human-machine result, but it has no automated-only counterpart the reader can compare against it: the one complete run produced 60 defect-confirming candidates and five new defect registrations, reported, as the paper puts it, "at its two measured points, not as a complete chain of subtractions", and no figure quantifies what author-side screening removed before submission. The paper should state, per run, what the automation confirms and what the screening drops, so the 51/23 headline is not read as the system's output.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** The two statements about the judgment-side channels are not consistent with each other. The Limitations section says expectation-framing bias "can push genuine false positives toward confirmation; this affects precision-side rates, not the recall headline", but a bias that pushes toward confirmation would raise recall as well, and Section 4.3 attributes the recall drop from 0.841 to 0.373 to "the disclosed framing and leakage threats". Either the framing channel is recall-relevant (and the Limitations wording is wrong) or it is not (and the RQ2 attribution needs to rest on leakage alone). The 0.841-to-0.373 comparison is also asserted to be a blinding effect without evidence that the two runs differed only in protocol; a controlled statement of what else changed between them would settle both points.
   - **3.7 [minor, fixable]** The abstract's "no ordering step survives Holm correction at this pool size" is easy to misread against the paper's own numbers. With the six paired tests whose p-values appear in Section 4.3 (0.008, 0.017, 0.093, 0.648, 0.678, 1.000), a standard Holm procedure at α = 0.05 compares the smallest p (0.008, the flat judge versus the contract core) against 0.05/6 = 0.0083, so that comparison passes the first threshold while 0.017 fails the second. If "ordering step" is meant only for the core < source-only < full chain, say so explicitly; otherwise the sentence should be rewritten.
   - **3.8 [minor, fixable]** Two small ledger/pattern accounting statements need reconciliation. First, Section 4.2 says "four of the five" Result-Incorrectness bugs are in the RQ2 pool, while Section 4.3 defines the pool as "the full pool of 81 candidates (51 real bugs + 30 false positives)" and the eligible-subset arithmetic as "47 of the 51 true bugs"; if all five confirmed Result-Incorrectness bugs sit inside the 51, none can be outside the pool, and if one is genuinely outside, the pool definition needs adjusting. Second, the status sentence "23 have been fixed...; 18 remain open...; 7 were closed without a fix...; and 3 ... duplicate-tracked" sums to exactly 51 and therefore presents duplicate-tracked as a fourth exclusive status, whereas Section 4.2 earlier calls duplicate-tracking "orthogonal to the confirmed/false-positive axis" — clarify whether the three duplicates are inside or outside the fixed/open/closed buckets.
   - **3.9 [minor, fixable]** The reverse direction of RQ3 is disclosed as "a hand-executed probe of that boundary-value class, not an end-to-end automated run against that version", but the paper does not say whether the probe value was chosen with knowledge of the overflow mechanism. As a demonstration that the defect class is documented-valid and reachable by boundary reasoning, this is legitimate; as support for the abstract's "TestVDB's boundary reasoning reaches VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow", it would be stronger (or more modestly worded) if the authors stated whether the automated boundary strategy would generate a value in the failing range unaided.

4. **Verifiability** — Excellent

   - **4.1** [strength] The Data Availability statement declares a replication package with an unusually complete inventory: agent role definitions and the strategy-trigger registry, per-version knowledge bases and specifications, all generated test scripts with raw HTTP logs, one evidence chain per candidate, the submission ledger (including the 49 unadjudicated submissions), the frozen RQ2 packages with per-case judge outputs for every arm, the RQ3 baseline reports with per-template logs, and analysis scripts that recompute every rate, interval, and paired test reported. Per the review protocol I did not clone or run anything; I also could not confirm reachability because the artifact host is blocked from my environment — the tier assumes the declared link resolves for readers, and a dead link at review time would move this criterion down sharply.
   - **4.2** [strength] Even without the artifact, the text carries the procedural detail needed to follow the evidence for the key claims: pool composition and exclusion rules, blinding, denominators, the conservative decision rule, the majority threshold, the explicit statement that judge outputs were produced at run time rather than reconstructed, the named statistical procedures, and the statement that Neutral verdicts lie outside every automated rate. My independent recomputation of the reported intervals, paired-test p-values, and table sums (see 3.1) all checked out, which is the practical test of whether the described procedure was the one executed.
   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** Some procedures are named but not specified in the text: how the frozen packages for candidates found in earlier mining rounds were assembled (the text describes rebuild "from the run's own evidence chains" only for the 32-candidate seeded sample), how that sample was drawn (the seed and frame are not given), and how pack completeness was coded for the stratification in Section 4.3. These are plausibly covered by the artifact; the text should point there explicitly.
   - **4.4 [minor, fixable]** Model identity is a serving identifier (GLM-5.3-Flash for the reported run and the re-adjudication; GLM-5.2 for earlier mining rounds; Qwen3.8-Flash for the second-backbone arm) under "vendor default sampling", with no sampling parameters reported. This is disclosed as a limitation and is a general problem for LLM-pipeline papers, but a replicator needs to know what, if anything, the artifact pins.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate

   - **5.1** [strength] The structure is sound and the running example (#10369) is threaded effectively through all four stages (Sections 3.2–3.5), which makes an abstract pipeline concrete. Table captions are informative (Table 4's caption defines "FP leaked" and explains the majority-row semantics), and the oracle-exclusion table is a good expository device.
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** Density is the paper's main presentation risk. The Section 4.3 paragraph "Which perspective carries the decision" packs the source-only arm, its outcome decomposition, the McNemar battery, the re-aggregation disclaimer, the cognition caveat, and the second-backbone replication into one block; splitting it into labeled sub-paragraphs would change no content and much readability. The abstract similarly carries roughly ten distinct quantitative results.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** The terminology distinction between a "false-positive candidate" (a maintainer-adjudicated non-bug) and `False-Positive` (the verdict, in small caps) is easy to miss and reads like a typographical error at speed; the definition also arrives mid-Section 4.3. Distinct tokens (for example "non-bug candidate" versus "FP verdict") would remove the trap.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** The process-ledger paragraph in Section 4.2 is hard to parse: "119 chain verdicts (per-evidence-path), 70 defect-confirming and none unresolved, resolving to 96 distinct candidates of which 60 confirm defects" never explains what the non-confirming verdicts were or what "none unresolved" means, and the subsequent gate (19 novel + 11 covered + 5 by-design + 25 pending → five registrations) does not visibly subtract. A short list with the two measured points marked would fix this.
   - **5.5 [minor, fixable]** Mixed spelling conventions ("behavioral" throughout the system description versus "behavioural-consistency" and "stabilises" in Related Work) and a contribution-list inconsistency: contribution 3 advertises "a controlled five-arm evaluation ... plus independent source-only and flat-judge arms", although the source-only and flat-judge arms are two of the five arms.
   - **5.6 [minor, fixable]** The RQ3 response breakdown phrase "204 among 409/422/429" is ambiguous about whether 204 is a total across the three codes or a subset of one.
