# TestVDB — Three-Reviewer Peer Review (2026-09-12, round 9; paper rev: post-round-8 revisions — forced-verdict sensitivity, MASTEST citation, prompt appendices)
## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets *documentation–implementation bugs* in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): silent behaviors (accepted inputs, wrong responses, stale state couplings) that violate natural-language API documentation and therefore escape crash-oracle fuzzing. Because the expectation must be read from prose, the paper argues every deterministic-oracle family is structurally excluded (Table 1), forcing an LLM-derived oracle and importing its false-positive problem. TestVDB answers with a four-stage pipeline: behavioral specification extraction from version-pinned documentation, pre-bound-strategy test generation, Docker-pinned sandboxed execution, and a bug-confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly (evidence builder) from cross-examination (chain auditor) under four perspectives — contract, physical constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition — with the implementation source as the falsification anchor and a three-valued verdict that routes unresolvable cases to human review.

The evidence is a submission ledger across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate (maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions; 23 fixed via merged PRs), a five-arm re-adjudication of that pool over audited and rebuilt frozen evidence packages (an audit found 43.3% of cited constraints unsupported; rebuilt packs and protocol alignment follow), and a bidirectional reach probe against VDBFuzz. The contract-only core suppresses 96.7% of false positives but recalls 0.137; the full structured stage reaches 0.804 recall at 0.800 suppression versus 0.588 for a flat single-prompt judge (exact McNemar p=0.0015, Holm-corrected 0.0045); under forced verdicts alone the two judges are statistically indistinguishable (0.529 vs 0.510), so the paper attributes the separation to the explicit human-review routing channel. VDBFuzz, run to natural completion over its full 205-template Qdrant space, reaches none of the confirmed silent-accept defects, while TestVDB's boundary reasoning reaches VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow by hand on Qdrant v1.4.0.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Real-world-validated defect yield with unusually honest accounting — 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 with merged implementation-code fix PRs (classified by changed files, with a positive control on the disproved #9149), explicit duplicate handling, and revisable adjudication — see 1.1, 3.4.
- **S2:** Measurement discipline of a quality rarely seen in this area: the evidence-pack audit (43.3% of 134 cited pairs unsupported), full pack rebuild, protocol alignment, measured leakage channels, three-run re-adjudication with Wilson intervals, exact McNemar and Holm correction — every statistic I recomputed reproduces except two, which I flag in 3.2 and 4.2 — see 3.1, 3.2.
- **S3:** The decomposition of *where* the mechanism lives (forced-verdict collapse to 0.529 vs 0.510; evidence-absent stratum at 0.909; routing-channel attribution) converts a would-be headline into a calibrated claim — see 3.5, 3.6.
- **S4:** The positioning against the closest competitors checks out against their own texts: the anchor inversion vs MASTOR, the shared-ambiguity falsifier critique of CASCADE (verbatim quote), and the Metamon numbers are all accurate — see 2.1, 2.2.
- **S5:** RQ3 measures complementarity instead of asserting it: uninformative-counter forensics, liveness-oracle substitution, per-template census, withdrawal of a wrong explanation, and explicit provenance of the hand-executed reverse probe — see 3.3.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The headline full-vs-flat separation is carried entirely by the Human-Review routing convention; under forced verdicts the judges are indistinguishable, and the 0.804 counts cases no human actually adjudicated in the study. The paper discloses this repeatedly, but the abstract and contribution bullet still lead with the convention-dependent pair (0.804 vs 0.588) — see 3.5, 3.6.
- **W2:** The central full-vs-flat comparison is single-backbone (GLM-5.3-Flash); the second family replicates only the source-only arm, on which it fails to separate from the flat judge after Holm correction — see 3.7.
- **W3:** The four perspectives are evaluated only as a bundle; no per-perspective ablation isolates what A/B/C/D each contribute to the full stage's recall — see 3.8.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The yield is practically meaningful and validated where it matters: Table 2 reports 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions confirmed upstream (63.0%), 23 fixed via merged PRs; Section 4.2's "Fix nature" paragraph classifies all 23 fix PRs as modifying implementation code (15 adding regression tests, none documentation-only), and validates the classifier on the disproved #9149 (correctly identified as test-only). This is not report inflation; it is a real footprint in three production systems.
   - **1.2** The problem is real and independently named: the testing roadmap (roadmap25) identifies oracle definition as the key open challenge for non-crashing VDBMS bugs (Section 2.1), and the exclusion analysis of Table 1 is a reusable contribution — a structured account of *where each oracle family gets its expectation* that others can apply to adjacent domains.
   - **1.3 [minor, unfixable]** Impact is bounded by construction: the paper targets the consistency subset (Section 2.2 explicitly excludes result correctness of vector search), three systems, and a prose-constraint documentation regime (Limitations, External validity: "we do not claim transfer to systems whose constraints live in machine-checkable schemas"). Useful rather than necessary; the methodology transfers more readily than the tool.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** The delta over the closest competitor verifies. MASTOR (arXiv 2606.10465; fetched, cached summary `mastor ... (corr 2026)`) grounds oracles in the implementation and states in its own limitations that it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code" — exactly the documentation–code gap TestVDB targets. The paper's claim of *anchor inversion* (prose as ground, implementation as falsifier only, Section 6 "Oracles derived from structured sources") is accurate against MASTOR's text, and the paper candidly concedes what is *not* new ("A generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is therefore not new here").
   - **2.2** The falsifier-independence delta also verifies. CASCADE's (FSE 2026; cached) residual-FP analysis contains verbatim the sentence the paper quotes — "the generated code and tests made the same false assumption over something underspecified in the documentation" — supporting the critique that a same-documentation-derived LLM artifact shares the ambiguity it is meant to break. Metamon (LLM4Code 2025; cached; numbers 0.722/0.480 on 9,482 pairs quoted correctly) consults no non-LLM evidence source, so the source-anchored evidence chain is a genuine difference. SATORI and AGORA+ are characterized accurately (response-field OAS oracles; traffic-bounded trace invariants respectively). The three-valued routing channel is additionally a differentiator vs CASCADE's binary verdicts that the paper under-exploits.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** The constituent mechanisms are individually established: prose-derived oracles (@tComment/JDoctor/DocTer line), LLM-oracle unreliability (konstantinou24, TOGLL), judge stabilization (Metamon), multi-agent pipelines (MASTOR, MASTEST). The contribution is the composition plus the measured decomposition — real but incremental originality, correctly the reason this criterion is not higher.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** Two uncited 2024 works occupy the same class as the cited LlamaRestTest (LLM-based REST API *input* generation, not oracles): "You Can REST Now" (arXiv 2024) and "A Multi-Agent Approach for REST API Testing with Semantic Graphs and LLM-Driven Inputs" (arXiv 2024). Neither derives oracles, so this is a coverage nit, not a positioning threat.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The measurement design is the paper's best feature. Section 4.3's three layers each isolate one variable: Layer 0 audits the shipped packs (43.3% of 134 distinct constraint–page pairs unsupported; 32% of rows endpoint-mismatched; four leaked provenance notes), Layer 1 rebuilds all 81 packs against version-pinned documentation, Layer 2 aligns the judging protocol with the deployed rules. The leakage channels are *measured*, not merely disclosed: a mechanical scan found 19 expectation-flavored phrases, all inside verbatim server responses, and the four provenance notes were removed before re-adjudication.
   - **3.2** Statistical reporting verifies end-to-end. I recomputed: every row of Tables 4 and 5 (the confirmation-stage matrices) sums to 81 — while Table 3, the bug-pattern table, correctly sums to 51 confirmed bugs; the rates (7/51=0.137, 29/30=0.967, 41/51=0.804, 24/30=0.800, 41/47=0.872, 30/51=0.588, 39/51=0.765, 36/51=0.706, 27/51=0.529, 26/51=0.510); every printed Wilson interval (including 19/32 → [0.423, 0.745]); the McNemar arithmetic (discordant 18/3 → exact p=0.00149, and 18/3 is consistent with the margins 47 vs 32 confirmations, both-confirm 29); the Holm multipliers (0.0015×3=0.0045 at rank 5 of 7; 0.041×2≈0.083); the strata (29+2+10=41); the funnel partition (19+11+5+25=60); and the RQ3 response census (14,022+7,520+1,512+204=23,258). Two numbers do not reconcile — see 4.2.
   - **3.3** RQ3 is measured rather than asserted, in both directions: the harness-forensics paragraph (released runner's counters cannot fire; dead-port control yields "100% success"; liveness-oracle substitution with 22,540 mutations and a per-response census) makes the zero informative; the reverse direction discloses that the v1.4.0 confirmation was hand-executed with knowledge of VDBFuzz's case study and that the automated boundary set (topping at 2^16) would not construct the failing range unaided. Withdrawing the earlier wrong explanation for VDBFuzz's silence on #9045 is exemplary.
   - **3.4** The submission-filter bias is anchored rather than hand-waved: a seeded sample of 32 registered-but-unsubmitted candidates re-adjudicated blind re-confirms 19/32 (0.594, Wilson verified), supporting "the submission filter selects among live candidates rather than inflating pool confirmability" (Section 4.3).
   - **3.5 [major, fixable]** The headline separation is routing-channel-only. Under forced verdicts the full stage recalls 0.529 (27/51) and the flat judge 0.510 (26/51), p=0.55 — indistinguishable — and 17 of the full stage's 41 confirmations carry a Human-Review component that the study counts as confirmed without any human adjudicating them in the re-adjudication. The disclosure is prominent (abstract, contributions, 4.3), the convention is applied symmetrically, and it costs suppression too (four HR-majority false positives leak, the stated 0.800-vs-0.967 gap) — but the abstract and the contribution bullet still lead with "0.804 ... against 0.588" before the qualifier, and the joint judge-plus-hypothetical-human system is never measured with an actual human in the loop. Rebalance the emphasis (or have authors adjudicate the 17 HR cases and report that) to make the claim match the evidence.
   - **3.6 [major, fixable]** The claim "structure is a measured mechanism" rests on one model family. The full/flat/core arms are GLM-only; the Qwen replication covers the source-only arm alone, and there it does not separate from the (GLM) flat judge after Holm correction (0.083). The paper discloses this three times, and backbone/runtime co-variation is acknowledged — but as it stands the central comparison's external validity is one data point.
   - **3.7 [minor, unfixable]** Ground truth is maintainer adjudication, demonstrably fallible in both directions by the paper's own cases: #9149 (maintainers closed as fixed; the "fix" PR was test-only), the two stage-vs-adjudication conflicts, and the approximate-count family where the documentation's own disclaimer refutes the candidate. Inherent to the design; the limitations section handles it honestly, including the 63.0%-precision-is-adjudicated-subset caveat.
   - **3.8 [minor, fixable]** No per-perspective ablation: perspectives A/B/C/D are evaluated only as a bundle. The stratification (evidence-absent packs at 0.909, attributed to "the objective-constraint layer of perspective B ... plus the human-review channel") is indirect attribution; per-perspective vote counts presumably already exist in the per-case judge outputs.
   - **3.9 [minor, fixable]** The flat judge is not fully flat: its prompt (Appendix B) embeds the protocol's red lines — verbatim-intent requirement for by-design refutation, and objective-constraint violations auto-confirmed without a contract row — which are substantive parts of perspectives B and C, while the main text describes it as having "no ... framework requirement". The contamination is conservative (it strengthens the flat judge, which still loses), but the main-text characterization should match the appendix.

4. **Verifiability** — Excellent
   - **4.1** The replication package is declared and comprehensive: role prompts and the strategy-trigger registry, per-version knowledge bases and specifications, all scripts with raw HTTP logs, one evidence chain per candidate, the full submission ledger (all 81 adjudicated plus 49 unadjudicated), original and rebuilt RQ2 packs with audit verdict records and per-case judge outputs for every arm and run, RQ3 per-template logs, and analysis scripts that "recompute every rate, interval, and paired test reported here" (Data Availability). Both the full-stage and flat-judge prompts are quoted in Appendix A/B (with the verbatim dispatch files noted as shipping in the artifact). This meets the rubric's top anchor: the work can be followed and checked from declared, reachable artifacts.

   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** The prompts for the contract-core and source-only arms are not quoted anywhere (only full-stage and flat prompts are), and "the same inputs" in the flat-judge description (Section 4.3) is ambiguous about whether the full-stage judge has live clone access beyond the pack's source-grounding section (Appendix A says both quoted judges "may read the case's version-pinned source clone"; the arms paragraph implies the clone distinguishes the source-only arm).
   - **4.4** The backbone is identified only by serving identifier and date (GLM-5.3-Flash, September 2026; GLM-5.2 for earlier mining rounds) rather than a weights snapshot, so exact behavioral replication is impossible by construction. The paper discloses this as its chosen reporting convention; I accept it as honest but note it caps reproducibility of the judge-dependent numbers.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1** The running example (Qdrant #10369) threaded through all four stages with concrete artifacts (constraint id, scenario construction, log pair, verdict) is the best-presented part of the paper; Table 1's exclusion analysis is an effective organizing device that the rest of the paper repeatedly cashes in.
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** The abstract is a single ~450-word paragraph carrying five results and their qualifiers; it is accurate but exhausting. Split or cut.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** The mnemonic "D-only" (Table 5; Section 4.3) is never expanded; a reader must infer it names the source-only arm.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** The process-ledger paragraph in Section 4.2 (119 chain verdicts → 96 candidates → 60 defects → 44 past the gate → 5 registrations, with two unmapped many-to-one steps) is very hard to follow even though the missing counts are honestly disclosed; a small funnel figure would fix it.
   - **5.5 [minor, fixable]** Several paragraphs exceed a column in density (the "Measurement design" paragraph of Section 4.3 runs ~400 words with nested parentheticals); occasional imprecision ("the same inputs" — see 4.3).

### Questions for Authors

- **Q1:** Why does the flat-judge prompt (Appendix B) retain the protocol's red lines (verbatim-intent refutation; objective-constraint auto-confirmation), and how do you interpret full-vs-flat given that these are substantive parts of perspectives B and C? — If the intent is to isolate *structural* routing from *rule* content, say so in the main text; a clear rationale would move 3.9 up and strengthen 3.5's comparison-fairness.
- **Q2:** Have any Human-Review-routed cases ever been resolved by an actual human adjudicator under the deployed channel, and of the 17 HR-component confirmations, how many are HR-majority? — Direct evidence that humans confirm these cases at a high rate would upgrade 3.5 from a protocol convention to a measured joint-system result.
- **Q3:** Can you reconcile the pack accounting (2,106 − 674 − 463 − 302 = 667 vs 674 surviving) and state the denominator of the 20.6% routing figure? — Resolving 4.2 requires only corrected text; leaving it unreconciled dents an otherwise excellent verifiability record.
- **Q4:** Do the per-case judge outputs in the artifact record per-perspective verdicts (A/B/C/D), permitting a perspective-level attribution of full-stage recall without new LLM runs? — If yes, 3.8 could be resolved by analysis alone.
- **Q5:** Is a Qwen (or other-family) re-run of the full and flat arms feasible? — It is the single experiment that would most affect 3.6; if resource-prohibitive, say so explicitly in Limitations.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

*Reviewer expertise: LLM-based test oracle generation and database software testing. All competitor characterizations below were verified against the competitors' own texts (VDBFuzz ICSE 2026, MASTOR CoRR 2026, Metamon LLM4Code 2025, CASCADE PACM SE 2026, SATORI ASE 2025, AGORA+ TOSEM 2026 fetched and read; MASTEST arXiv:2511.18038 fetched and read this round). All recomputable numbers were recomputed.*

### Summary

The paper targets *documentation-implementation bugs* in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): silent misbehaviors — an accepted out-of-range parameter, a bypassed dimension check, a stale cross-collection coupling — that violate the system's own natural-language API documentation and produce no crash. Because the expectation must be read from untagged behavioral prose, the paper argues (Table 1) that every deterministic oracle family anchors elsewhere, forcing an LLM-derived oracle and importing its false-positive problem. TestVDB answers with a four-stage pipeline: documentation is distilled into typed, source-verified constraints; pre-bound attack strategies turn constraints into executable probes; a sandbox executes them against version-pinned Docker instances; and a confirmation stage splits evidence assembly (including source-code grounding) from a four-perspective chain audit with a three-valued verdict that routes unresolvable cases to human review.

Empirically, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, with 23 fixed via merged PRs. A self-audit of the frozen evidence packages found 43.3% of cited constraint–page pairs unsupported; after rebuilding all 81 packages and re-adjudicating the pool three times per configuration, the contract-only core reaches 0.137 recall at 0.967 suppression, the full structured stage 0.804 recall at 0.800 suppression, and a flat single-prompt judge 0.588 recall (full-vs-flat McNemar p=0.0015, Holm-corrected 0.0045). A decomposition shows the full-vs-flat separation is carried entirely by the explicit human-review routing channel: under forced verdicts the two judges are indistinguishable. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows complementary reach, with the zero measured rather than asserted.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Practitioner-validated yield with unusual ledger hygiene — 51 confirmed / 23 merged fixes, fix-PR classification validated against the authors' own disproved candidate (#9149), duplicates handled orthogonally, 49 unadjudicated submissions excluded from every rate — see 1.1, 3.1.
- **S2:** The three-layer measurement design (audit → rebuild → protocol alignment) turns the confirmation-stage evaluation into an attribution study that surfaces and fixes the authors' own weakest link (43.3% unsupported citations; two recall-suppressing legacy rules) instead of reporting a single unknown-quality number — see 3.2.
- **S3:** The routing decomposition is a non-obvious, honestly deflationary finding: forced-verdict recall is indistinguishable between full stage (0.529) and flat judge (0.510, p=0.55), locating the structured protocol's value in calibrated escalation rather than forced accuracy — see 3.3.
- **S4:** The RQ3 zero is measured from two directions (per-template liveness oracle over 22,540 mutations; runner counters shown to be uninformative) and a previously wrong explanation is explicitly withdrawn — a model of how a complementarity claim should be bounded — see 3.5.
- **S5:** Positioning verified: the anchor-inversion delta over MASTOR, CASCADE, and Metamon holds against those papers' own texts (MASTOR demotes spec claims not substantiated in source; CASCADE's residual FPs are caused by exactly the shared-ambiguity falsifier TestVDB avoids; Metamon consults no non-LLM evidence), and the newly added MASTEST characterization is accurate — see 2.1–2.4.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The central full-vs-flat comparison is a single-backbone measurement, and the one cross-family data point that exists (source-only on Qwen) *fails* to separate from the flat judge after Holm correction (0.041 → 0.083) — leaving the generalization of the headline 0.804-vs-0.588 gap explicitly unresolved — see 3.4, Q5.
- **W2:** The headline 0.804 recall counts Human-Review-routed cases as confirmed: 17 of 41 confirmations carry an HR component, and four HR-majority false positives sit inside the 0.800 suppression cost. The scoring is disclosed and defended, but the automated stage's standalone capability (machine-only recall/precision end-to-end) remains unquantified — see 1.2, 3.3.
- **W3:** Several small numbers do not reconcile from the text alone: the "20.6%" routing figure (17/81 = 21.0%; denominator unstated), the suppression-cost decomposition (four + two = six cases against a five-case delta), the rebuilt-pack row accounting (2,106 − 674 − 463 − 302 = 667 ≠ 674), and a Holm family of "seven paired tests" of which only six are identifiable — see 3.6, Q1–Q4.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The yield is practically consequential and independently validated: Table 2 reports 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs of 81 adjudicated submissions (Milvus 29, Qdrant 14, Weaviate 8) with 23 fixed via merged PRs; Section "Fix nature" strengthens this by classifying every fix PR's changed files (all 23 touch implementation code, 15 add regression tests, none is documentation-only) and by validating the classifier against the #9149 fix PR (correctly identified test-only). Upstream acceptance signals (50/51 `bug` labels) are reported per-project with the projects' label heterogeneity disclosed. This is clear, verifiable impact on real production systems, in a problem space (silent VDBMS failures under RAG infrastructure) established by the cited bug study and roadmap.
   - **1.2 [minor, fixable]** The impact is measured at the human–machine pipeline layer: submission decisions were author-side after novelty screening, so the 63.0% precision and the 51-bug yield are attributes of the pipeline-plus-humans, not of the automated stage alone (Section RQ1 states this; the 32-candidate seeded anchor bounds the unsubmitted registrations at 0.594 confirmability). The scoping is honest, but the paper could go one step further and give the reader a single explicit statement of what the *automated* stage's end-to-end precision can and cannot be inferred to be; currently that number must be assembled from four places (RQ1 caveat, RQ2 anchor, limitations).

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** Verified delta vs MASTOR (fetched, CoRR 2026): MASTOR grounds oracles in the implementation source and *demotes* OpenAPI claims not substantiated in source to `pending` — source is the authority; its own limitations state it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code." TestVDB's Table-1 row and the "anchor inversion" claim (prose as ground, implementation as falsifier) are a near-verbatim inversion of MASTOR's own design and limitation. The delta is real, clearly articulated, and the paper correctly concedes that a ChallengerAgent-style reviewed oracle and cross-request behavioral oracles are not new.
   - **2.2** Verified delta vs CASCADE (fetched, FSE 2026) and Metamon (fetched, LLM4Code 2025): CASCADE's falsifier is LLM-regenerated code from the *same* documentation, and CASCADE's own residual-FP analysis attributes both of its residual false positives to "the same false assumption over something underspecified in the documentation" — quoted by TestVDB verbatim and correctly. Metamon's metamorphic self-consistency consults no non-LLM evidence source. TestVDB's claim to break this self-reference by grounding verdicts in the implementation source is therefore supported by the closest competitors' own failure modes, not asserted against strawmen.
   - **2.3** Verified characterizations: SATORI (response-field oracles from OpenAPI fields/descriptions; spec–response mismatches within its 18 real bugs; no input-acceptance/state oracles), AGORA+ (traffic-anchored invariants; bounded by what the traffic exercised), and the newly cited MASTEST (LLM + programmed agents, OpenAPI-anchored workflow with human review of artifacts at each stage) are all accurately described; the Table-1 grouping of MASTEST under structured-spec oracles is correct, and the paper does not conflate MASTEST's artifact-level human review with its own verdict-level Human-Review channel.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** The residual novelty is a recombination of known multi-agent patterns (extraction, generation, execution, builder/audit) arranged around the inversion, plus the measured routing finding; the paper says as much. Relatedly, several Table-1 rows (metamorphic relations, property-based testing, differential testing) are analytical assertions supported by citation, not by measurement — the exclusion analysis would be stronger if it marked which rows the paper's own experiments exercised (rows 1 and partially 5/6 are; the MR and PBT rows are argued, not measured).

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The ledger protocol is unusually careful: revisable adjudication (the #9149 reclassification via a seven-version re-probe is disclosed and propagated everywhere the count appears), explicit duplicate tracking orthogonal to the confirmed/FP axis, 49 unadjudicated submissions excluded from every rate, and funnel reporting at its two measured points rather than a fabricated chain of subtractions (119 chain verdicts → 96 candidates → 60 defects → 44 → 5 registrations, with the many-to-one steps honestly marked as uncounted).
   - **3.2** The audit/rebuild/alignment layers are the paper's methodological core and they isolate variables properly: Layer 0 quantifies the shipped packs' defects (43.3% of 134 pairs unsupported — 0.433×134 = 58, consistent; 32% of rows endpoint-mismatched — 0.32×2,106 = 674, consistent with the endpoint-filtering count); Layer 1 rebuilds with provenance leaks removed before re-adjudication; Layer 2 removes two legacy rules that suppressed recall by construction and aligns judging with the deployed protocol. The expectation-leakage scan (19 hits, all inside verbatim server responses, zero added by assembly) converts a disclosed threat into a measured one.
   - **3.3** The scoring choice — Human-Review counts toward Confirmed "the way the deployment resolves it" — is applied symmetrically (HR-majority *false positives* also count against suppression, e.g. four of the five suppression-cost cases), and the forced-verdict decomposition (0.529 vs 0.510, p=0.55) is reported in the abstract itself. The 0.804 headline is thus a deployment-relevant recall, not machine recall, and the reader is told so; see 1.2 and W2 for the residual concern. I verified the stratified analysis is internally exact: 37+3+11 = 51; full-stage stratum recalls 29/37 + 2/3 + 10/11 sum to exactly the 41 TP; core's 7 TP all sit in the evidence-present stratum.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The central full-vs-flat separation is single-backbone, and the only cross-family evidence runs the wrong way for generalization: the Qwen source-only arm reaches a comparable magnitude (0.706) but does not separate from the flat judge after Holm correction (raw p=0.041, corrected 0.083). The paper flags "single-backbone result" in the abstract, contributions, and limitations — the scoping is exemplary — but the mechanism claim ("structure is a measured mechanism") currently rests on one model family, and the one replication data point shows a Holm-level separation evaporating on the second family. Running the full-stage and flat arms once each on the second backbone would materially strengthen or appropriately bound the central claim.
   - **3.5** The RQ3 methodology is sound and unusually self-skeptical: VDBFuzz's runner counters are shown to be uninformative by construction (pointing the released runner at a dead port yields exit 0 and 100% success), so the authors substitute a per-template liveness oracle; the response mix (14,022 2xx + 7,520 400 + 1,512 404 + 204 other = 23,258) sums correctly; the mutation-space bound (integer candidates top out at 2^16 vs the 2^62 threshold) is arithmetic I verified; and the earlier wrong explanation for VDBFuzz's silence on #9045 is withdrawn rather than papered over. Every VDBFuzz fact I checked (OOM/FPE/DoS Qdrant crashes, 120-minute protocol, 2^63 case study, divide-by-zero in chunk_capacity, 205 released Qdrant templates) matches VDBFuzz's own text and artifact.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** Four numbers do not reconcile from the text alone (see W3): (a) 17/81 = 21.0% ≠ 20.6% — the routing percentage's denominator is unstated and is repeated in the abstract, intro, RQ2, and limitations; (b) the five-case suppression cost is decomposed as four + two = six, unless the enum-closure case is also the core's single leak, which the text leaves unstated; (c) rebuilt-pack row accounting: 2,106 − 674 − 463 − 302 = 667, not the stated 674 surviving rows (and 674 coincidentally equals both the endpoint-filtered removals and 32% of 2,106, inviting confusion); (d) the Holm-corrected values (0.0045, 0.083) imply a seventh paired test with raw p between 0.0015 and 0.041 that is never named — only six paired tests are identifiable in the subsection. # Review — Reviewer 3 (General Reviewer)

## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): silent misbehaviors (accepted out-of-range inputs, ignored parameters, stale state couplings) that violate natural-language API documentation and therefore escape crash-oracle fuzzers like VDBFuzz. Since the expectation must be read from prose, the authors argue an LLM-derived oracle is forced — but importing an LLM as judge imports false positives. TestVDB is a four-stage pipeline (specification extraction from documentation, pre-bound strategy test generation, Docker-pinned sandboxed execution, and a two-agent bug-confirmation stage that assembles a five-section evidence chain and cross-examines it from four perspectives, with the implementation source as falsification anchor).

On the empirical side, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated upstream submissions as real bugs across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, with 23 fixed via merged PRs. A self-audit of the frozen evidence packages found 43.3% of cited constraints unsupported by their pages; after rebuilding all 81 packages and re-adjudicating three times per configuration, the contract-only core recalls 0.137 at 0.967 suppression, the full structured stage 0.804 at 0.800, and a flat single-prompt judge 0.588; the full-vs-flat gap survives Holm correction, but the paper's own decomposition shows it is carried by the human-review routing channel rather than forced-verdict accuracy (0.529 vs 0.510, p=0.55). A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows each oracle misses what the other reaches.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Unusual measurement honesty: a three-layer audit/rebuild/re-adjudication design that isolates one variable per layer, reports both sides of the recall/suppression tradeoff, and decomposes its own headline win into a routing effect — see 3.1, 3.2
- **S2:** Strong external validation of practical impact: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 merged fix PRs, with every fix PR classified by changed files and the classifier itself validated on a known test-only PR — see 1.1
- **S3:** Statistical reporting that mostly survives independent recomputation (all Wilson CIs and the McNemar p=0.0015 check out) — see 3.2
- **S4:** Verifiability: a declared artifact whose enumerated contents (packs, per-run judge outputs, ledgers, analysis scripts recomputing every rate) match what the text claims — see 4.1

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** Numeric inconsistencies in the central RQ2 reporting: the suppression-cost sentence enumerates six cases against a stated five, the pack-rebuild accounting is off by seven rows, and the no-contract pack count disagrees with the evidence strata by one — see 3.3
- **W2:** The central full-vs-flat comparison is single-backbone, and the only cross-family evidence (Qwen) shows the analogous separation failing to survive Holm correction — the mechanism claim may be backbone-specific — see 3.6
- **W3:** Recall rates conflate forced accuracy with routing policy (Human-Review counts as Confirmed), so headline numbers mechanically reward the arm that routes more; the paper discloses and decomposes this, but abstract/intro still lead with the conflated rates — see 3.4

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The RQ1 ledger (Table 2, Section 4.2) reports 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs out of 81 adjudicated submissions across three production systems, 23 fixed via merged PRs; the "Fix nature" paragraph strengthens this by classifying all 23 fix PRs as code-modifying (15 adding regression tests, none documentation-only) and validating the classifier on the disproved #9149, whose PR it correctly flags as test-only. This is unusually concrete practical impact for a testing paper. The bug-pattern taxonomy (Table 3) sums correctly to 51 (25+8+5+4+3+2+1+3) and the 72.5% silent-acceptance family share checks out (37/51).
   - **1.2 [minor, unfixable]** The paper itself bounds the yield: Section 4.2 and Limitations (Section 5) state the ledger "measures the human–machine pipeline," with author-side novelty screening, per-candidate submission decisions, and a funnel paragraph (119 chain verdicts → 96 candidates → 60 defects → 44 passing novelty → 5 registrations) that concedes many-to-one steps have no per-step counts. So the automated stage's end-to-end standalone yield is never demonstrated, and the practical impact is partly attributable to the human screen. This inheres in the deployment as conducted and is honestly scoped, but it caps significance at "meaningful but bounded."
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The cost profile (Section 4.1: ≈67 active hours, ≈860 executions, ≈70M input tokens for the orchestrating session alone) excludes parallel attack-agent sessions; a bounded operational cost figure would make deployability claims assessable.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate (provisional: I have not surveyed this field; I judge the delta from the paper's own positioning)
   - **2.1** The oracle-exclusion analysis (Table 1) is a genuine strength: it organizes candidates by where their expectation anchors and locates the residual — system-level, untagged behavioral prose — that only an LLM-derived oracle reaches. The Related Work section is exceptionally careful: it concedes non-novel components explicitly (MASTEST's human-reviewed LLM pipeline, MASTOR's ChallengerAgent regeneration, Metamon's LLM-judges-regression-oracles division of labor, CASCADE's doc-derived cross-check) and then isolates one crisp claimed delta — the anchor inversion, where the prose is ground truth and the implementation serves only as falsifier.
   - **2.2 [minor, fixable]** As the paper itself describes them, the closest competitors (MASTOR, Metamon, CASCADE) already combine LLM-oracle generation with a second-stage reviewer/critic; the residual delta is the *direction of authority*, which is conceptual and thin unless the implementation-source-as-falsifier grounding is shown to change outcomes. The leak-reanalysis paragraph (four leak cases re-confirmed through objective constraints and source forensics after annotation removal) is the best evidence for exactly this; I would surface it in Related Work or the contributions, since it operationalizes the claimed inversion. My rating is provisional because I could not verify the competitors' actual content.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** The abstract's claim that "no deterministic-oracle family derives its expectation from that behavioral prose" is asserted from the paper's own Table 1 taxonomy; given prior work it cites (RESTInfer, ICON) reads prose at parameter granularity, the novelty rests on the *system-level/untagged* qualifier. The paper should state this qualifier in the abstract, not only in Table 1's caption.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
