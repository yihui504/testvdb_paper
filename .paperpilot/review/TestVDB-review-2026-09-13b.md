# Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation–implementation bugs in vector DBMSs: silent misbehavior (an input accepted, a constraint ignored, a state transition leaving stale behavior) that violates a system's own API prose and produces no crash, so crash-oracle fuzzing is structurally blind to it. The paper's oracle analysis argues that every deterministic-oracle family anchors its expectation in a crash signal, another implementation, an output relation, a machine-checkable property, or a structured spec — none in system-level natural-language prose — which forces an LLM-derived oracle and imports its false-positive problem. TestVDB is a four-stage pipeline (specification extraction from documentation, strategy-bound probe generation, sandboxed execution, and a confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly from cross-examination over a five-section evidence chain, with the implementation source as the falsification anchor).

Evidence is reported at three layers. Operationally, across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs and fixed 23 via merged PRs. In a controlled re-adjudication of the same pool under rebuilt, audited evidence packages, the contract-only core suppresses 96.7% of the 30 adjudicated false positives at 0.157 recall; the structured full stage reaches 0.804 recall at 0.800 suppression against 0.588 for a flat single-prompt judge with identical evidence access (exact McNemar p=0.0072, Holm-corrected p=0.029 across seven paired tests) — but forced-verdict scoring collapses the two judges (0.529 vs 0.490), a hand-adjudicated judge-plus-human reading yields 0.647/0.943, and the full-vs-flat pair re-run on a second model family does not separate (p=1.0). A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz on the same Qdrant instance shows the crash oracle reaching none of the confirmed defects across its full 205-template space while the paper confirms VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow by hand on an older version.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The anchor-inversion delta over the closest prior work is real and verified: MASTOR grounds oracles in source and demotes spec claims absent from code; Metamon and CASCADE falsify with other LLM artifacts sharing the documentation's ambiguity; TestVDB takes the prose as ground and the implementation only as falsifier — see 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 2.4.
- **S2:** Measurement rigor is exceptional and independently recomputable: exact McNemar with a declared Holm family, Wilson intervals, three scoring conventions reported side by side, and a three-layer audit/rebuild of the stage's own evidence packs — see 3.1, 3.2.
- **S3:** Real-world impact with validated authenticity: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 merged fix PRs of which all modify implementation code, and the authors' own disproof of one of their own accepted bugs — see 1.1.
- **S4:** The VDBFuzz comparison is unusually fair and forensically bounded: full template space, time limit removed, harness-counter forensics with a no-server control, and a per-template liveness oracle substituted for demonstrably uninformative counters — see 3.3.
- **S5:** The three-scoring-convention reporting (forced / joint / deployment convention) converts what could have been a misleading headline into a measured decomposition of where the "recall" lives — see 1.2, Table 6.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The central mechanism — the structured protocol's routing advantage — is established on one model family; the mandated re-run on a second family does not separate (p=1.0) and routes far less, so generality is unestablished — see 3.4.
- **W2:** The ablation does not resolve what the protocol adds over source-informed unstructured judging: full vs source-only is indistinguishable (p=0.55), and the text never defines what materials distinguish the source-only ("D-only") arm from the flat judge — see 3.5, 4.3.
- **W3:** The 0.804 headline counts unperformed human work: 16 of 41 confirmations have no forced-driving perspective, and only 4 of 8 hand-adjudicated cases in the highest-recall stratum survive — the measured judge+human system is 0.647 — see 3.6.
- **W4:** Ground truth is fallible maintainer adjudication on an author-screened, submission-filtered pool with 49 of 130 submissions unadjudicated; the paper discloses all of this, but the yield's precision inherits human selectivity — see 3.8, 3.9.
- **W5:** The reverse direction of the VDBFuzz probe is hand-executed with knowledge of VDBFuzz's own case study (TestVDB's released boundary generator tops out at 2^16), and the structural "cannot fire" argument for the panic-phenotype bug #9045 is under-argued — see 3.7.

### Questions for Authors

- **Q1:** What exactly distinguishes the source-only ("D-only") dispatch from the flat-judge dispatch in materials and output format, and what is D-only's routing rate under forced/joint/convention scoring? — If the D-only arm's extra materials (e.g., cognition access) explain its 36/51 vs the flat judge's 30/51, item 3.5's attribution problem deepens; if the protocol's increment over D-only is genuinely within noise, tempering the mechanism claim would move 3.5 and 1.3 up.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

*Specialty declaration: I assessed this paper primarily as (a) an LLM-based test-oracle / automated-testing contribution and (b) an empirical-measurement contribution (paired binary outcomes, confidence intervals, multiplicity control). Within (a) I verified the paper's characterizations of MASTOR, SATORI, Metamon, CASCADE, VDBFuzz (full texts/summaries fetched), plus Konstantinou et al. 2024, Molinelli et al. (ASE 2025), MASTEST, and AGORA+ at summary level; outside these areas I assessed from the paper alone.*

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): silent misbehavior (typically a 2xx response violating a documented constraint) that crash-oracle fuzzing cannot observe. Because the relevant constraints live in natural-language prose rather than in schemas, the authors argue that an LLM must read the documentation, which imports the LLM false-positive problem; their system, TestVDB, answers with a four-stage pipeline (specification extraction, pre-bound strategy test generation, sandboxed execution, and a two-agent confirmation stage) in which an evidence builder assembles a five-section chain and a chain auditor cross-examines it from four perspectives, with the implementation source as the falsification anchor. Empirically, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, with 23 fixed via merged PRs. A controlled five-arm re-adjudication of the same 81-candidate pool over audited and rebuilt evidence packages measures the confirmation stage: a contract-only core recalls 8/51 (0.157) at 0.967 suppression; the full structured stage recalls 41/51 (0.804) at 0.800 under the deployment counting convention; a flat single-prompt judge with the same evidence access reaches 0.588, and the full-vs-flat separation survives Holm correction (raw exact McNemar p=0.0072) on the primary backbone while a three-run re-run of that pair on a second model family does not separate (p=1.0). A decomposition attributes the separation to the protocol's human-review routing channel rather than forced-verdict accuracy, and the paper reports forced-verdict (0.529) and joint judge-plus-human (0.647) readings side by side. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows the crash oracle reaches none of the confirmed defects across its full 205-template space while TestVDB's boundary reasoning reaches VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow by hand on an older version.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Measurement transparency and statistical hygiene of a standard rarely seen in tool papers: a three-layer audit/rebuild/protocol-alignment attribution, three scoring conventions reported side by side, and a negative second-backbone replication reported as prominently as the positive result — see 3.1, 3.2, 4.2.
- **S2:** Real, externally validated defect yield: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs with 23 merged fix PRs, with the fix classifier itself validated against a known-disproved case — see 1.1.
- **S3:** Competitor positioning that survives verification: every oracle-line characterization I checked against the fetched competitor texts (MASTOR, SATORI, Metamon, CASCADE, Konstantinou, Molinelli, MASTEST, AGORA+) matches what those systems actually say — see 2.1, 2.2.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The confirmation protocol (notably perspective B's seven "objective" violation classes and the ef/nprobe negative-sentinel carve-out) co-evolved in contact with the very candidate population it is evaluated on, and this circularity is nowhere named as a validity threat — see 3.3.
- **W2:** The paper's central measured mechanism (the full-vs-flat separation carried by routing) is backbone-specific: it does not replicate within the second model family, so the practical scope of the headline claim is one backbone under one protocol — see 1.3.
- **W3:** Missing related work: "Testing for Implicit Inconsistencies in Documentation and Implementation" (ICST 2019) addresses the paper's exact problem class by title and is uncited in an otherwise thorough doc-inconsistency lineage — see 2.4.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The RQ1 ledger (Table 2) and the fix-nature analysis are a substantive practical contribution: 51/81 adjudicated submissions confirmed by maintainers, 23 with merged fix PRs, every fix PR classified by changed files (all 23 touch implementation code, 15 add regression tests, none documentation-only), and the classifier validated on the known-disproved #9149 fix PR (correctly identified as test-only). This is exactly the kind of secondary validation most bug-finding papers omit, and the maintainer-adjudication protocol (Section 4.2, "Collection protocol") with duplicate-tracked handling is careful.
   - **1.2 [minor, fixable]** The yield measures the human-machine pipeline, not the automated stage: submission decisions were author-made with "no deterministic submission rule" (Section 4.2), 49 unadjudicated submissions are excluded, and the one complete-run funnel (Section 4.2, "Process ledger") reports many-to-one steps "without a maintained per-step count." The paper discloses all of this, and the 32-candidate non-submitted anchor (0.594 confirmability) is a good-faith attempt to bound the direction — but the field-level significance of the *automated* discovery capability remains unestablished; what is established is that the pipeline-plus-humans produces real bugs.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The central mechanism's significance is bounded to one backbone: the full-vs-flat separation does not replicate on the second family (p=1.0, Section 4.3, "Backbone replication"), and the paper correctly re-scopes ("a property of that backbone under this protocol, not a law of the protocol itself"). For practitioners choosing a backbone, this is genuinely useful; for the general claim that structure buys honest escalation, the evidence covers one family.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** Checked the delta against MASTOR (fetched): the paper's anchor-inversion claim is verifiable in the competitor's own words — MASTOR self-declares it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code," which is precisely the gap TestVDB targets. The Table 1 exclusion analysis (where each oracle family anchors its expectation) is a clean conceptual contribution, and the paper honestly marks rows 2–6 as "argued from each family's anchoring mechanism" rather than tested.
   - **2.2** Checked the delta against Metamon and CASCADE (both fetched): Metamon's falsifier is the same LLM under metamorphic prompting (verified: no independent non-LLM evidence source), and CASCADE's falsifier is code generated from the same documentation (verified: both of its residual FPs came from "the same false assumption over something underspecified"). The paper's differentiation — falsifying a prose-derived claim against the implementation source — is a real and clearly articulated delta against both nearest neighbors, and the paper correctly declines a head-to-head with Metamon given incomparable pools.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** The individual components are each established: LLM oracle generation from documentation (AugmenTest, ChatAssert, Doc2OracLL, TOGLL — cited), LLM-reviewed oracles (MASTOR's ChallengerAgent, conceded in Section 6), LLM-judge stabilization (Metamon), multi-agent REST pipelines (MASTEST — cited), and doc-code inconsistency detection (iComment through DocPrism lineage — cited). The originality is compositional plus the empirical mechanism finding (routing, not forced accuracy, carries the structured judge's advantage). Real but incremental; the exclusion table and the routing decomposition are the two genuinely new pieces.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** Missing related work: "Testing for Implicit Inconsistencies in Documentation and Implementation" (ICST 2019, DOI 10.1109/ICST.2019.00059) is uncited; its title names the paper's exact problem class. It belongs in the doc-derived lineage of Section 6 or Table 1 row 7 (its scope could not be verified beyond title/DOI level — no abstract was retrievable; the flag is provisional at title level).
   - **2.5** Positioning accuracy is a strength: of the eight oracle-line competitors whose characterizations I verified against fetched texts (MASTOR, SATORI, Metamon, CASCADE, Konstantinou et al. 2024, Molinelli et al. 3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The statistical treatment is correct and fully recomputable — I recomputed everything: all 8 reported Wilson intervals (7 distinct; the flat recall interval [0.452, 0.712] appears under both backbones) match to rounding (e.g., 41/51 → [0.675, 0.890]; 8/51 → [0.082, 0.280]); all nine exact McNemar p-values reproduce from the stated discordant counts (17/4 → 0.00720; 15/3 → 0.00754; 15/5 → 0.0414; 7/4 → 0.549; 7/6 and 7/7 → 1.0); the Holm adjustment over the seven-test family is correctly computed, including the step-down monotonicity that forces the fifth-ranked test (raw 0.0075, multiplier 3) to the same adjusted 0.029 as the fourth, and the sixth to 0.083 ("does not survive correction" — correct); the stated Holm multipliers match the ranks; and every discordant count is arithmetically consistent with the confirmed-set differences implied by Tables 4–5 under the footnote's pairing basis (including the subtle joint-vs-flat case, where the paired sets net +1 — 35 vs 34 — even though the true-positive counts differ by 3). All table row sums, the three-scoring table's precision/suppression denominators, the pack-rebuild accounting (2106 − 674 − 463 = 969 = 667 + 302; 667 + 7 = 674), the audit rate (58/134 = 43.3%), and the RQ3 response-status totals (23,258) check out. This is exemplary reporting discipline.
   - **3.2** The three-layer measurement design isolates its variables properly: the pack audit quantifies the evidence-quality defect (43.3% unsupported cited pairs, with the four provenance leaks identified and removed before re-adjudication), the rebuild separates evidence quality from protocol, the protocol-alignment layer names the two pre-deployment rules that suppressed recall by construction, and the contamination cleanup (ten packs, thirteen re-runs, pre-cleanup verdicts shipped) treats contamination as invalidating regardless of direction. The scoring-convention table (Table 6) and the routing decomposition are the right way to handle a three-valued verdict in a deployment that routes, and reporting the second-backbone null result prominently is the single most credibility-enhancing choice in the paper.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** Confirmation-protocol co-evolution with the evaluated population is unnamed as a threat. The evaluation pool (51 + 30) descends from mining rounds operated under the same chain-auditor rules that the re-adjudication adopts ("the deployed protocol"), and those rules bear fingerprints of this population: perspective B's ef/nprobe negative-sentinel carve-out exists precisely because the paper's motivating parameter family (nprobe, the Milvus #49823 example) has a documented by-design sentinel, and Layer 2 records that the protocol was *changed* when the authors "found our first re-adjudication protocol still carried two pre-deployment rules that... suppressed recall by construction." The rules were tuned while looking at these defect families; absolute recall (0.804) and the stratum analysis therefore cannot separate protocol quality from protocol fit. Mitigations exist in the paper — the flat judge shares the load-bearing red lines (verbatim-intent requirement, four of the seven objective classes), so the full-vs-flat *delta* is less exposed than the absolute numbers, and the per-perspective attribution (16/41 confirmations driven by objective constraints) quantifies B's contribution — but the paper never names the circularity, and no protocol-frozen or post-freeze-held-out evaluation exists. ### Summary

The paper targets *documentation-implementation bugs* in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where the system silently accepts an input or produces behavior that contradicts its own API documentation (e.g., Milvus accepting `nprobe=0` when the documentation declares [1, 16384]). Because such constraints live in natural-language prose, the authors argue that no deterministic test oracle can derive expectations for them, forcing an LLM-derived oracle — which imports an LLM false-positive problem. The proposed system, TestVDB, is a four-stage pipeline: (i) distilling documentation into structured, source-verified behavioral constraints; (ii) generating probe scripts via three attack agents bound to pre-registered strategies; (iii) sandboxed execution against Docker-pinned versions with raw HTTP logging; and (iv) bug confirmation, split into an evidence builder that assembles a five-section evidence chain (grounded in the implementation source as a falsification anchor) and a chain auditor that cross-examines the chain from four perspectives with a three-valued verdict (Confirmed / False-Positive / Human-Review).

Empirically, across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submitted issues as real bugs, 23 fixed via merged PRs. The confirmation stage is then evaluated in a controlled re-adjudication of the same 81-candidate pool (51 true bugs, 30 maintainer-adjudicated false positives) using frozen evidence packages that were first audited (43.3% of cited constraint-page pairs unsupported), rebuilt against version-pinned documentation, and re-adjudicated in three runs per configuration. Under the deployment counting convention (Human-Review counts as confirmed), the contract-only core recalls 8/51 (0.157) at 0.967 suppression; the full structured stage recalls 41/51 (0.804) at 0.800 suppression; a flat single-prompt judge with (stated as) the same evidence access reaches 0.588. The full-vs-flat separation survives a Holm-corrected family of seven exact McNemar tests (raw p = 0.0072, corrected 0.029), but a decomposition shows the separation is carried by the human-review routing channel (forced verdicts only: 0.529 vs 0.490, indistinguishable), and the pair does not separate when re-run three times on a second model family (p = 1.0). A bidirectional probe against the crash-oracle fuzzer VDBFuzz on Qdrant shows the crash oracle reaches none of the silent-accept defects, while TestVDB's confirmation of VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow is a hand-executed probe, disclosed as such.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Real, deployment-validated impact: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs with 23 merged fix PRs across three production systems, with the fix nature validated (all 23 fix PRs touch implementation code, none documentation-only) and a sanity check of the fix classifier against a known test-only PR — see 1.1, 1.2.
- **S2:** Statistical rigor that survives independent recomputation: exact McNemar with a defined pairing basis, Wilson intervals, and a correctly stepped-down Holm family of seven; every headline number I recomputed matched — see 3.1, 3.2.
- **S3:** Unusual honesty and self-audit: the paper audits its own evidence packs, finds and fixes their defects, reports the confirmation stage under three scoring conventions side by side, decomposes its own headline result (routing, not forced accuracy), and reports its own non-replication on a second backbone — see 3.3, 3.4, 3.5.
- **S4:** Verifiability: a declared artifact with a comprehensive inventory, verbatim dispatch files, and analysis scripts that recompute every reported rate, interval, and test; the text alone was sufficient for me to verify essentially all quantitative claims — see 4.1.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The claim that the flat judge has "the same evidence access" as the full stage is contradicted by the paper's own appendix and limitations: the full stage legitimately reads maintainer-cognition materials (perspective D) that the core, flat, and source-only dispatches forbid — see 3.5.
- **W2:** The central full-vs-flat mechanism is a single-backbone result: it does not separate when re-run on the second family (p = 1.0), and the headline 0.804 exists only under the counting convention (forced scoring collapses it to 0.529 vs 0.490). The paper discloses and correctly calibrates all of this, but the abstract still leads with the convention-based numbers — see 3.6, 3.7.
- **W3:** Presentation density: the abstract is roughly a page of nested parentheticals, the confirmatory statistical family is defined inside a footnote, and the arm name "D-only" is never expanded — see 5.1–5.3.
