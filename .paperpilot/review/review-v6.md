## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

TestVDB targets documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs): cases where a VDBMS silently accepts an input or behavior that violates its API documentation (e.g., accepting `nprobe=0` when documentation declares range [1, 16384]). These defects corrupt query semantics without crashing, escaping crash-oracle fuzzers like VDBFuzz. The authors instantiate a four-stage pipeline that uses LLMs to extract behavioral claims from natural-language documentation, generate tests, execute them against sandboxed VDBMS instances, and confirm defects. Two LLM failure modes—hallucination in claim extraction and self-preference bias in judgment—produce false positives. Multi-perspective judging (four specialized judge agents) raises precision but collapses recall (15%). The authors introduce a **dev-reviewer agent** that acts as a source-grounded falsifier, reproducing candidates and cross-checking them against implementation source. On 107 submitted issues across three VDBMSs (Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate), maintainers acknowledged 50 true-positive defects (69% adjudicated yield; 15 fixed via merged PR). On a controlled 48-candidate retrospective, the dev-reviewer reaches 67% precision and 74% recall (3-run any-confirmed ensemble) versus 37% recall without source grounding. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz on Qdrant shows complementary coverage: TestVDB reaches a crash-class defect (integer overflow) by contract reasoning, while VDBFuzz misses a TestVDB silent-accept defect under current templates.

### Core Strengths

- **S1: Novel defect class** — TestVDB identifies a real, prevalent gap in VDBMS testing: documentation-implementation defects that silently corrupt query semantics without crashes. The problem setup is well-motivated by empirical bug studies and a roadmap that identifies oracle definition as a key challenge (see 1, 2.1).
- **S2: Systematic oracle exclusion** — Table 1 (exclusion argument) systematically walks through standard oracle candidates (crash, differential, metamorphic, property-based, REST-API tools) and explains why each misses the documentation-implementation residual, leaving LLM as the practical oracle. This clarifies the contribution's novelty (see 2.1).
- **S3: Source-grounded falsification** — The dev-reviewer is a sound response to the two failure modes. By grounding falsification in implementation source rather than documentation, TestVDB breaks self-preference bias and lifts recall from 37% to 74% (see item 4.2; Section 5, RQ2 paragraph).
- **S4: Real-world impact** — 50 maintainer-acknowledged true positives (15 fixed via merged PR) across three production VDBMSs demonstrate practical relevance (see 5.1).
- **S5: Complementarity to VDBFuzz** — The bidirectional reachability probe (Section 5.3) convincingly shows TestVDB and VDBFuzz reach different defect classes: TestVDB finds a crash via contract reasoning (size overflow) that VDBFuzz's templates happen to miss, while VDBFuzz cannot reach silent accepts by construction (see 5.3).

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1: Limited generalization evidence** — The evaluation is VDBMS-only (three systems). The portability claim to non-VDBMS documentation regimes (CouchDB, Elasticsearch) rests on two lightweight examples, not a systematic transfer study (see Section 7, Discussion).
- **W2: Single-backbone evaluation** — All dev-reviewer results use one LLM family (GLM-5.2). An independent cross-model re-run shows verdicts are family-specific (κ = 0.18–0.32 vs. GLM), with recall ranging 19–56% across families. Cross-family generalization remains an open question.
- **W3: Ground truth construction bias** — The 48-candidate retrospective is maintainer-adjudicated but non-random; the authors did not pre-register the candidate set. This threatens internal validity and raises questions about selection bias.
- **W4: Untuned operating point** — The 3-run any-confirmed ensemble is a post-hoc selection from four operating points. The Wilson CIs in Table 4 do not account for this selection, and the Bonferroni correction is exploratory rather than a pre-registered analysis.
- **W5: Limited comparison depth with REST-API tools** — Section 6 (Related Work), REST-API oracle generation paragraph compares TestVDB to AGORA+, SATORI, and MASTOR, but the comparison is conceptual (scope difference) rather than empirical. A head-to-head evaluation on shared targets would strengthen the novelty claim.

### Detailed Assessment

#### 1. Significance — Adequate

- **1.1** TestVDB addresses a real problem: VDBMS defects are costly (more than half are functional failures per bug study [1]), and existing fuzzers detect only crashes [2]. The problem setup is well-motivated by empirical evidence and a testing roadmap that identifies oracle definition as a key challenge (1, 2.1).
- **1.2 [minor, fixable]** The practical impact is meaningful but bounded. 50 acknowledged defects across three production VDBMSs (15 fixed via merged PR) show utility, but the 107-submission yield is biased by the tool's design toward documentation-implementation defects, so the proportion (46.7% worst-case precision) is not a population estimate (5.1). The authors acknowledge this but do not attempt capture-recapture or unbiased defect sampling, which would strengthen the significance claim. A brief discussion of how yield would generalize (e.g., extrapolating from the 22 by-design/rejected cases) would address this.
- **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The application scope is narrow: documentation-implementation defects in VDBMSs. The transferability claim to other documentation regimes (REST APIs without OpenAPI, configuration validation, policy-as-code) is asserted on structural grounds only and evaluated with two lightweight examples (CouchDB, Elasticsearch) rather than a systematic study (6.1). One additional non-VDBMS case study (e.g., a REST API with natural-language docs) would significantly strengthen generalizability.

#### 2. Novelty — Adequate

- **2.1** The core contribution is the **dev-reviewer**: a source-grounded falsifier that mitigates two LLM failure modes (hallucination, self-preference) by anchoring judgment in implementation source rather than documentation. This is a novel twist on LLM-as-judge frameworks, which typically leave the LLM as the final arbiter [4, 5].
- **2.2** TestVDB is novel relative to REST-API oracle tools (AGORA+ [6], SATORI [7], MASTOR [8]) in scope and grounding. Table 1 (exclusion argument) systematically shows these tools target response-field/runtime properties under per-field structural anchoring (OpenAPI, traces, source), whereas TestVDB targets input-acceptance decisions on system-level prose. This clarifies the delta and is a strength of the contribution (2.1).
- **2.3 [minor, fixable]** The comparison to documentation-derived oracles (Toradocu [9], Doc2OracLL [10], AugmenTest [11], ChatAssert [12], Testora [13]) in Section 6 (Related Work), documentation-derived oracles paragraph could be deeper. TestVDB's distinction is treating the LLM as a provisional oracle whose verdicts are falsified against source, whereas prior work treats the LLM as the final semantic arbiter verified through runtime behavior. This is a meaningful methodological advance, but a structured comparative table (oracle derivation, verification mechanism, false-positive handling) would make the delta sharper.
- **2.4** The novelty is incremental on LLM-as-judge reliability work. Self-preference bias [4] and intra-judge inconsistency [5] are established problems; TestVDB's contribution is applying source-grounded falsification to a test-oracle pipeline. The application context is new, but the underlying technique (use an independent signal to break same-family bias) is a straightforward adaptation.

#### 3. Soundness — Weak

- **3.1 [major, fixable]** The evaluation relies on a **single LLM backbone** (GLM-5.2) for all dev-reviewer results. An independent cross-model re-run (DeepSeek, Qwen-3.8-Max, LongCat-2.0) shows verdicts are family-specific: Cohen's κ against GLM's 3-run union ranges 0.18–0.32, and recall ranges 19–56% vs. GLM's 74% (Section 5, RQ2 paragraph). This is a **major limitation** for a method whose central claim is that source grounding breaks LLM bias. If the verdict is backbone-dependent, the dev-reviewer may simply be trading one bias (same-family preference) for another (model-specific calibration). The authors acknowledge this as an open question, but it undermines confidence that the technique generalizes beyond GLM-5.2.
- **3.2 [major, fixable]** The **ground truth construction is non-random**. The 48-candidate retrospective is maintainer-adjudicated but not pre-registered; the authors do not explain how candidates were selected or whether selection was biased toward easier/harder cases. This threatens internal validity because selection bias could inflate performance metrics. The authors should either (a) disclose the selection procedure, or (b) run the retrospective on a random sample of all 107 candidates (or a subset where maintainer adjudication exists) to demonstrate robustness.
- **3.3 [major, fixable]** The **operating point selection is post-hoc**. The 3-run any-confirmed ensemble is chosen from four operating points (single, 3-run union, 5-run union, 5-run majority) after observing the data. The Wilson CIs in Table 4 do not account for this selection, and the Bonferroni correction is presented as exploratory validation rather than a pre-registered analysis. This raises the risk of overfitting: the headline 67%/74% precision/recall may not replicate on new data. A held-out cross-validation (200 random 50/50 splits) is presented as validation, but splits are from the same 48-candidate pool, not independent data. The authors should either pre-register the operating point selection rule or clearly mark 3-run as exploratory.
- **3.4** The threat model for construct validity is well-articulated. The **implementation-as-correct assumption** (source falsification treats the implementation as ground truth) bounds the approach: an implementation bug could wrongly falsify a correct documentation claim. The authors report no observed false negatives of this type (all FPs are maintainer-adjudicated by-design), and 15 merged-PR fixes suggest the assumption holds often enough to be useful (6.1). This is an acceptable limitation given the context, but the false-negative rate is not quantified—which would require a ground-truth catalog of documentation errors that does not exist.
- **3.5 [minor, fixable]** The **threats to validity** section is thorough but one-sided. External validity threats (limited to VDBMS, non-random candidate set) are acknowledged, but the authors do not attempt to bound them. For example, a simple sensitivity analysis (e.g., how many additional by-design cases would be needed to push yield below 50%) would contextualize the observed yield.

#### 4. Verifiability — Adequate

- **4.1** The paper provides enough information to follow the work and replicate key results. The four-stage pipeline is well-described (Section 3), with example paths (Milvus #49823, Qdrant #9255, #9045) that make the workflow concrete. The artifact (https://github.com/yihui504/testvdb-anon) reportedly contains all agent prompts, target versions, per-token accounting, and a reproduction driver (3.1, Appendix A). This is excellent for reproducibility.
- **4.2 [minor, fixable]** Some procedural details are missing for full replication. The paper states that "roughly half of the calls are dev-reviewer source-grounding steps" and "roughly $10^4$ calls and roughly $\$10$ per target" (Table 2), but does not break down the remaining 50% between claim extraction, test generation, and judging. A more detailed cost breakdown (e.g., Table 2 extended to show absolute call counts per stage) would help readers budget their own experiments.
- **4.3 [minor, fixable]** The **target versions** are specified (Milvus 2.6.19, Qdrant v1.18.2, Weaviate v1.38.2), but the paper does not explain how these were chosen. Were they the latest stable at time of study? If not, what was the selection rationale? Version selection matters for reproducibility, especially given the bidirectional probe with VDBFuzz that relies on specific version liveness (5.3).
- **4.4 [minor, fixable]** The **agent role prompts** are excerpted in Appendix A, but only for two agents (contract-formalizer, dev-reviewer). The paper states "The full 22 agent definitions are in the artifact," but readers should not have to consult external files to understand core methodology. A supplementary table listing all 22 agents with brief descriptions would improve readability.

#### 5. Presentation — Adequate

- **5.1 [minor, fixable]** The paper is well-structured and readable overall, but some sections are dense. Section 5, RQ2 paragraph packs multiple operating points, ablation results, cross-model re-runs, and per-vendor breakdowns into a long narrative. Figure 3 (per-run recall) helps, but the text could be reorganized to separate the main operating point selection from the ablation and cross-model results. A separate subsection for "Operating point selection" and "Cross-model validation" would improve clarity.
- **5.2 [minor, fixable]** **Notation inconsistency:** The paper uses both `$n$` (Section 3.1) and `$N$` (Section 5, RQ2 paragraph) for sample size. Standardize on one notation throughout.
- **5.3 [minor, fixable]** The **Cohen's κ** reporting in Section 5, RQ2 paragraph is incomplete. The paper reports κ values vs. GLM's 3-run union but does not specify the confidence interval or whether it uses linear or quadratic weighting. Given the emphasis on reliability metrics, a brief note on how κ was computed (e.g., "Cohen's κ with 95% CI") would align with the rigorous treatment of other metrics.
- **5.4 [minor, fixable]** The **figures are effective** but could be sharper. Figure 1 (pipeline) clearly delineates LLM-driven vs. non-LLM stages, and Figure 2 (dev-reviewer three-check falsification) is a clean visual summary. However, Figure 3 (per-run recall) uses dots without confidence bands, which understates the variance. Adding error bars or a box plot would make the single-run variability more salient.
- **5.5 [minor, fixable]** **Typos and minor formatting issues:**  
  - Table 4 caption: "Wilson 95% CIs in brackets" should clarify that CIs are for precision and recall separately, not a joint CI.  
  - Section 5, RQ2 paragraph: "DeepSeek reaches 75% recall on Milvus (3-run union, inter-run κ = 1.0)"—this κ refers to consistency across DeepSeek's own runs, not agreement with GLM. Clarify.  
  - Section 6, documentation-derived oracles paragraph: "ChatAssert [12]" should cite the most relevant work; if ChatAssert is not directly comparable, consider Doc2OracLL or AugmenTest as closer analogs.

### Questions for Authors

- **Q1:** You report that cross-model re-runs show verdicts are family-specific (κ = 0.18–0.32, recall 19–56% vs. GLM's 74%). Do you have evidence that **source-grounded falsification** is the primary driver of this variance, rather than differences in claim extraction or test generation? Ablating the dev-reviewer's source access across models would isolate whether the bottleneck is in falsification or upstream stages. — see item 3.1.
- **Q2:** The 48-candidate retrospective is maintainer-adjudicated but non-random. Can you characterize the **selection procedure**? For example, were candidates chosen to represent a spread of difficulty levels, or were they the first 48 where maintainers responded? Knowing the selection criteria is essential for assessing whether performance metrics are biased. — see item 3.2.
- **Q3:** You select the 3-run any-confirmed ensemble as the headline operating point based on precision-recall trade-offs. Have you considered **pre-registering** a decision rule (e.g., "choose the operating point with highest recall at precision ≥60%")? If so, does the 3-run union consistently win under that rule? This would address the post-hoc selection concern. — see item 3.3.
- **Q4:** The portability claim to non-VDBMS systems rests on two examples (CouchDB, Elasticsearch). Have you considered a **more systematic transfer study**, even if limited to one additional domain (e.g., REST APIs with natural-language docs)? For example, running TestVDB on 5–10 such systems and reporting yield/precision would strengthen generalizability claims significantly. — see item 1.3.
- **Q5:** Table 1 (exclusion argument) systematically rules out standard oracles. Did you consider **mutation testing** on the input space (e.g., using schema validators or property-based testing tools like schemathesis) as an intermediate? Your argument focuses on why these miss the prose residual, but a brief empirical check (e.g., "we tried schemathesis on 20 documented constraints and it caught X") would make the exclusion concrete rather than conceptual. — see item 2.2.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary
TestVDB targets documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs): cases where a VDBMS silently accepts an input or produces a behavior that violates its natural-language API documentation. Because the documented boundary is prose rather than a structured specification (e.g., OpenAPI), the paper argues that classical oracles (crash detection, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property-based testing) cannot adjudicate these accept/reject decisions, leaving an LLM as the practical oracle. The LLM introduces two false-positive failure modes (hallucination in extraction and self-preference in judgment). TestVDB addresses these with a dev-reviewer agent that acts as a source-grounded falsifier, reproducing candidates, cross-checking against implementation source, and attempting to disprove each. On 107 submitted issues across three VDBMSs, maintainers acknowledged 50 true-positive defects (15 merged-PR-fixed). A 48-candidate retrospective shows the dev-reviewer (GLM-5.2 backbone, 3-run any-confirmed ensemble) reaches 67% precision and 74% recall, against 37% recall without source grounding. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz (Table 8) shows complementary coverage. Since Round 5, a cross-family voting probe was added: the union of GLM-5.2's 3-run verdicts with DeepSeek's lifts recall from 74% to 81% at 73% precision, with families showing complementarity (per-candidate κ = 0.39).

### Core Strengths
- **S1:** Concrete, actionable mitigation of cross-family brittleness — see 2.1, 2.2. The cross-family voting probe (commit 2deb03f) directly addresses the previous [major, unfixable] concern with a 7-point recall lift (74% → 81%) at controlled precision cost, demonstrating that families are complementary rather than redundant.
- **S2:** Source-grounded falsification as a generalizable reliability pattern for LLM-as-judge systems — see 1.1, 2.3. The dev-reviewer's three-check architecture (reproducible, evidence-sufficient, falsifiable) breaks both extraction hallucination and judgment self-preference by anchoring ground truth in implementation rather than documentation or same-family LLM outputs.
- **S3:** Rigorous false-positive diagnosis and ablation — see 3.3, 3.4. The 12-FP/4-TP control ablation (Table 6) and the source-grounding-disabled control (recall 74% → 19%) directly measure the contribution of each anchor, triangulating source grounding as the dominant factor.
- **S4:** Honest acknowledgment of post-hoc operating-point selection — see 4.2. The paper flags the 3-run union as a post-hoc choice justified by falsifier semantics, reports Wilson CIs without selection correction, and provides bootstrap and cross-validation validation. This transparency is critical for interpretability.

### Core Weaknesses
- **W1:** Post-hoc operating point selection inflates reported recall — see 4.2 [major, fixable]. The 3-run union is selected as the "headline" from four operating points (Table 6) after observing performance on the 48-candidate set. The Wilson CIs do not account for this selection, and the Bonferroni-corrected CIs across the four operating points (α = 0.05/4; roughly [44%, 84%] precision, [51%, 89%] recall for the 3-run) are substantially wider. The bootstrap validation (2000 resamples) helps but does not eliminate selection bias.
- **W2:** Cross-family generalization remains open despite the new probe — see 2.2, 4.3 [major, unfixable]. The cross-family voting mitigation shows promise (81% recall at 73% precision), but the underlying verdict is family-specific (κ = 0.32 DeepSeek, 0.20 LongCat, 0.18 Qwen vs. GLM). The paper does not demonstrate that the voting strategy transfers to new VDBMSs or new LLM families beyond the specific combination tested. Cross-family brittleness is partially mitigated but not fundamentally resolved.
- **W3:** Single-backbone evaluation for headline results — see 4.3 [minor, fixable]. All headline dev-reviewer results (67% precision / 74% recall) use GLM-5.2 only. The cross-model re-run (DeepSeek, Qwen, LongCat) shows wide variance (56%, 22%, 19% recall on the 3-run union). Without a clear rationale for GLM-5.2 as the default backbone, the generalizability of the headline numbers is unclear.

### Summary

They introduce TestVDB, a four-stage pipeline that uses LLMs to extract behavioral claims from documentation, generate tests, execute them, and confirm defects. A key contribution is a source-grounded falsifier ("dev-reviewer") that checks candidates against implementation source to suppress two LLM false-positive modes: extraction hallucination and self-preference bias.

On 107 submitted issues across three VDBMSs (Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate), maintainers acknowledged 50 true-positive defects (69% adjudicated yield; 15 fixed via merged PR). A 48-candidate retrospective shows the source-grounded dev-reviewer reaches 67% precision and 74% recall (3-run any-confirmed ensemble) against a 37% recall baseline without source anchoring. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz explores complementary coverage: TestVDB reaches a crash-class defect via contract reasoning, while VDBFuzz misses TestVDB's silent-accept defects under current templates. Since Round 5, the authors added a cross-family voting probe (Section 7, RQ2): the union of GLM-5.2's verdicts with DeepSeek's lifts recall from 70% to 81% at 73% precision, with families showing complementary coverage (DeepSeek recovers 3 TPs GLM misses; per-candidate κ=0.39).

### Core Strengths
- **S1:** Clear problem formulation — The paper cleanly separates documentation-implementation consistency from mathematical correctness and formalizes why deterministic oracles cannot reach the accept/reject residual — see 1.1, 2.1.
- **S2:** Source-grounded falsification as a reliability contribution — The dev-reviewer's three-check design addresses the LLM-as-judge reliability problem by anchoring ground truth in implementation source rather than LLM interpretation — see 3.1, 3.2, 3.3.
- **S3:** Empirical grounding with maintainer adjudication — The 50 maintainer-acknowledged true positives (15 with merged PRs) provide concrete evidence the defect class exists and TestVDB surfaces real issues — see 3.1.
- **S4:** Complementarity to existing work — The bidirectional VDBFuzz probe (Table 4, Section 7.3) cleanly isolates why crash oracles miss silent-accept defects and how TestVDB reaches crash-class defects via contract reasoning — see 3.3.

### Core Weaknesses
- **W1:** Post-hoc operating point selection — The 3-run any-confirmed ensemble is selected across four operating points after seeing the data (Table 3); Wilson CIs do not account for this selection, and while bootstrap validation and held-out cross-validation are provided, the headline operating point remains exploratory rather than pre-registered — see 2.2 [major, fixable].
- **W2:** Cross-family generalization remains open — The cross-family voting probe (Section 7, RQ2) shows backbone-dependence is vendor-specific (κ=0.32 DeepSeek, 0.20 LongCat, 0.18 Qwen vs. GLM) with a concentrated failure mode on Qdrant (0% recall for three families vs. GLM's 29%); union voting mitigates but does not resolve the generalization question — see 2.3 [major, unfixable].
- **W3:** External validation limited to two VDBMSs — The 48-candidate retrospective covers Milvus and Qdrant only; Weaviate evaluation is a small controlled subset (11 candidates) without a per-vendor recall headline, and no non-VDBMS case study has full evaluation despite the Discussion section claiming transferability on structural grounds — see 2.4 [major, fixable].

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The paper addresses a real problem: VDBMS defects that are costly (§1, paragraph 2: "Their defects are costly") and prevalent (107 surfaced, 50 maintainer-acknowledged), with concrete impact (15 merged-PR fixes). The defect class—silent accepts that corrupt query semantics—is well-motivated and distinct from crash bugs. The scope is bounded to documentation-implementation defects rather than all VDBMS bugs, which keeps the contribution focused but limits the reach. — Good: Real-world impact demonstrated.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** The paper's core novelty is treating documentation-implementation consistency as a testable property when documentation is natural-language prose, and using source-grounded falsification to address LLM-as-judge reliability. The oracle-exclusion argument (Table 2) cleanly positions this against deterministic oracles (crash, differential, metamorphic, property-based) and REST-API tools (AGORA+, SATORI, MASTOR). The related work section (§8) correctly distinguishes TestVDB from prior documentation-derived oracles (Toradocu, Doc2OracLL, AugmenTest) which treat the LLM as final arbiter whereas TestVDB falsifies against source. — Good: Clear positioning against existing work.

   - **2.2 [major, fixable]** The operating point selection is post-hoc. Section 7.2 (RQ2) reports four operating points (single run, 3-run union, 5-run union, 5-run majority) and selects 3-run union as the headline because it "sits at the knee of the precision-recall trade-off at modest reproducibility cost." The Wilson CIs in Table 3 do not account for this selection across four points. The authors provide partial mitigation: Bonferroni correction widens CIs to roughly [44,84]/[51,89] (precision/recall), bootstrap resampling gives [53,83]/[71,96], and held-out cross-validation confirms 3-run is not an overfit (test precision/recall 68%/71%). However, the fundamental issue is that the operating point was chosen after observing the data, not pre-registered. The paper correctly flags this as "post-hoc, exploratory" and explicitly states "the Wilson CIs above do not account for this selection," but this undermines confidence that the reported recall/precision would hold on new data. Fixability: A future cycle could pre-register the operating point selection rule or use a larger candidate sample to reduce selection pressure. — The post-hoc selection is acknowledged but not resolved, leaving the headline metrics as exploratory findings rather than confirmed effects.

   - **2.3 [major, unfixable]** Cross-family generalization remains open despite the new cross-family voting probe. Section 7.2 reports that three additional families (DeepSeek, Qwen-3.8-Max, LongCat-2.0) show verdict is family-specific (κ=0.32 DeepSeek, 0.20 LongCat, 0.18 Qwen vs. GLM), with a concentrated failure mode on Qdrant where all three families stay ≤14% recall vs. GLM's 29%. The union voting probe (GLM ∪ DeepSeek) lifts recall from 70% to 81% at 73% precision by leveraging complementary coverage (DeepSeek recovers 3 TPs GLM misses; per-candidate κ=0.39). However, this does not establish cross-family robustness—it shows that families are complementary but not that the method generalizes across families. The dependence on Qdrant (all three alternatives collapse) suggests the dev-reviewer's source-grounded falsification interacts with vendor-specific implementation patterns in ways not yet understood. The authors correctly state "Cross-family generalization remains open" and frame union voting as "a viable mitigation direction" rather than a solution. This is inherent to the contribution: the method's reliability depends on LLM backbone behavior, and the paper shows this dependence is vendor-specific. No revision can establish broad cross-family robustness without substantially more evaluation across vendors and families. — Backbone-dependence is a limitation the paper correctly identifies but cannot resolve within its current scope.

   - **2.4 [major, fixable]** External validation is limited to two VDBMSs for the controlled retrospective. The 48-candidate retrospective covers Milvus and Qdrant only (Table 3: "Milvus 32, Qdrant 16; Weaviate is covered by a smaller controlled subset"). The Weaviate subset is 11 candidates (Section 7.2, paragraph 4: "a Weaviate controlled subset of 11 candidates"), which precludes a per-vendor recall headline. The Discussion section (§9) claims transferability to non-VDBMS systems (REST APIs without OpenAPI, configuration validation, policy-as-code) and provides a preliminary probe on Apache CouchDB and Elasticsearch, but this is not integrated into the formal evaluation (no yield numbers, no maintainer adjudication, no controlled retrospective). Fixability: Adding one full non-VDBMS case study with maintainer adjudication would strengthen the external validity claim; alternatively, the paper could soften the transferability claim to "preliminary evidence only" rather than a structural assertion. — The limited Weaviate evaluation and lack of a full non-VDBMS case study constrain how broadly we can generalize the results.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The main claims are supported by appropriate methods. RQ1 (detection capability) uses maintainer adjudication as ground truth, which is the right validation approach for defect-detection work. The 50 true positives out of 107 submitted (69% adjudicated yield, 46.7% worst-case bound) are realistic given the defect class. RQ2 (false-positive suppression) uses a controlled 48-candidate retrospective with maintainer-adjudicated ground truth (27 TP, 21 FP), which is appropriate for a precision/recall study. The three-condition ablation (Table 4) and source-grounding-disabled control (Section 7.2, paragraph 5) are well-designed isolations. RQ3 (comparison with VDBFuzz) uses a bidirectional probe that cleanly separates the two oracles' reach: the systematic direction (VDBFuzz on v1.18.2, 26,000 requests, 0 of 14 silent-accept TPs reached) is the generalizable result, and the controlled cases on v1.4.0 and v1.18.0 isolate crash-class mechanisms (n=1 each). — Good: Methods match the research questions.

   - **3.2** Statistical rigor is adequate. The paper reports Wilson 95% CIs for the headline precision/recall (Table 3), which is appropriate for binomial proportions. It provides multiple validation approaches for the operating point selection: Bonferroni correction across the four operating points, bootstrap resampling (2000 resamples of the 48-candidate set), and held-out cross-validation (200 random 50/50 splits). The threats to validity section (Section 7.4) explicitly acknowledges the post-hoc operating point, non-random candidate set, and lack of pre-registration. Cohen's κ is used appropriately to measure inter-family agreement on the CONFIRMED-vs-not split. The bidirectional probe correctly identifies the n=1 limitation of the controlled cases while emphasizing the systematic direction as the generalizable result. — Good: Statistical methods are appropriate and limitations are acknowledged.

   - **3.3** The implementation-as-correct assumption (Section 7.4: "the implementation-as-correct assumption bounds the approach: an implementation bug can wrongly falsify a clause whose documentation is right") is a necessary simplification for a source-grounded falsifier and is adequately discussed. The paper provides evidence the assumption holds in practice: 31 of 50 TPs have merged or open fix-PRs (implementation confirmed buggy by maintainers), and no false positive was traced to an implementation bug ("all FPs are maintainer-adjudicated by-design"). The threats section correctly flags this as a limitation and notes that false negatives would most likely surface as maintainer-rejected confirmed TPs without source-level explanation, which the authors did not quantify (reasonably, given the lack of a ground-truth documentation-error catalog). — Good: The assumption is acknowledged and empirical evidence supports its practical validity.

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate
   - **4.1** The paper provides enough detail to follow how the results were produced. Section 5 (TestVDB Approach) describes the four-stage pipeline with sufficient detail to understand the flow: behavioral-claim extraction (contract-formalizer agent), test-script generation (four attack agents), sandboxed execution (Docker-pinned versions: Milvus 2.6.19, Qdrant v1.18.2, Weaviate v1.38.2), and defect confirmation (judge agents + dev-reviewer). Table 2 provides per-target LLM-call distribution (~50% dev-reviewer source-grounding, ~25% claim extraction + test generation, ~25% judging + novelty gate). Section 7.1 gives the yield breakdown by vendor (Table 1) and Wilson CIs. Section 7.2 provides the full operating-point table (Table 3) and per-run variance (Figure 3). The artifact link (https://github.com/yihui504/testvdb-anon) is declared and reachable, with prompts under `agents/`, ground truth under `test_questions/`, and reproduction under `reproduction/full52/`. — Good: Self-contained text plus declared artifacts.

   - **4.2 [minor, fixable]** The cross-family voting probe (Section 7.2, RQ2) is described but lacks full procedural detail. The paper states "a full independent cross-model re-run on all 48 candidates with three additional families (DeepSeek, Qwen-3.8-Max, and LongCat-2.0, each running the complete SOP with independent live probes and source exploration, three independent runs per family)" and reports recall/precision/κ per family, but does not specify the GLM operating point used for the union (assumed 3-run union based on context) or whether union voting required any parameter tuning. The per-candidate κ=0.39 for GLM ∪ DeepSeek union is reported without defining how CONFIRMED-vs-not is encoded for the union case (does any family CONFIRM trigger union CONFIRM?). Fixability: Add a sentence clarifying the union voting rule and the GLM operating point used. — Missing detail on the union voting mechanism.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1** The paper is well-structured and readable. Section 1 (Introduction) cleanly motivates the problem and distinguishes documentation-implementation consistency from correctness. Section 2 (Background) provides a clear oracle-exclusion argument (Table 2) that explains why an LLM is the practical oracle for the residual. Section 5 (TestVDB Approach) describes the pipeline with a helpful figure (Figure 1) and a concrete example (the #49823 path, Section 5, paragraph 5). Section 7 (Evaluation) is organized around three research questions with appropriate tables and figures. Section 8 (Related Work) positions TestVDB against VDBMS testing, REST-API oracle generation, LLM-as-judge reliability, and documentation-derived oracles. Section 9 (Discussion) explicitly acknowledges limitations (source requirement, implementation-as-correct assumption, yield bias). — Good: Clear structure and flow.

   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** Minor language and formatting issues. Table 3 caption says "Wilson 95% CIs in brackets (recall/precision values in percent)" but the table shows "67% [49, 81]" which visually pairs the CI with precision when the bracketed values are actually [recall, precision] based on the Bonferroni correction mention ("widens the 3-run CI to roughly [44,84]/[51,89]"). Fixability: Clarify the bracket order in the caption or swap to precision/recall order. Section 7.2, paragraph 3: "each running the complete SOP with independent live probes and source exploration, three independent runs per family" — "SOP" is not defined (assumed "standard operating procedure"). Section 9, paragraph 1: "all three gaps reproduced on 3.5.2" — contextually clear but could explicitly state "the same three input-validation gaps" for readability. — Minor presentation issues that do not obstruct understanding.

### Questions for Authors
- **Q1:** For the cross-family voting probe, what is the exact union voting rule? Does any family's CONFIRM verdict trigger union CONFIRM, or is it a majority vote? What GLM operating point (3-run union, 5-run union, single run) is used for the union baseline? — Intended effect: Clarifying item 4.2's missing detail would strengthen Verifiability.
- **Q2:** For the Weaviate controlled subset (11 candidates), why was a full retrospective not pursued? Was this due to maintainer-response availability, candidate-set construction challenges, or sparse TP density? Would expanding the Weaviate evaluation to 30+ candidates change the cross-family generalization findings, particularly on Qdrant where all three families collapsed? — Intended effect: Clarifying item 2.4's scope limitation would help assess External validity and whether cross-family weakness is Qdrant-specific or systemic.
- **Q3:** The held-out cross-validation selects 3-run on 27% of splits; when 5-run is selected (61% of splits), test recall reaches 85%. Given that 5-run's recall is substantially higher while precision stays ≥60%, why retain 3-run as the headline rather than 5-run? Is this driven by reproducibility cost (3 runs vs. 5 runs), or by the desire to present a more conservative estimate? — Intended effect: Clarifying the operating-point selection rationale would address item 2.2's post-hoc concern.
