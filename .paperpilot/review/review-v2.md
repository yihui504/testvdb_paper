## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary
The paper targets documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs)—cases where a VDBMS silently accepts an input or behavior that violates its API documentation (e.g., accepting `nprobe=0` when documentation specifies the range as `[1, 16384]`). Because the documented boundary is natural-language prose, deterministic oracles (crash detection, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property-based testing, REST-API tools anchored on OpenAPI/traces/source) cannot adjudicate these accept/reject decisions, leaving an LLM as the practical oracle. The authors instantiate TestVDB, a four-stage pipeline (claim extraction, test generation, sandboxed execution, defect confirmation) that uses LLMs to read documentation, generate tests, and adjudicate responses. Two failure modes produce false positives: hallucination in claim extraction and self-preference bias in judgment (the same LLM family that extracts a claim tends to confirm it). A multi-perspective judging baseline raises precision but collapses recall, so the authors introduce a dev-reviewer agent that acts as a source-grounded falsifier, reproducing each candidate, cross-checking it against source, and trying to disprove it. On 48 maintainer-adjudicated candidates, the dev-reviewer reaches 67% precision and 74% recall (3-run ensemble), versus 37% recall without the source anchor. TestVDB surfaced 107 submitted issues across three VDBMSs (Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate); maintainers acknowledged 49 as true-positive defects (68% adjudicated yield; 15 fixed via merged PR). A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz on Qdrant shows TestVDB reaches a crash-class defect by contract reasoning, while VDBFuzz misses a TestVDB silent-accept defect under its current templates.

### Core Strengths
- **S1:** Problem framing is sound—documentation-implementation defects are a real and prevalent defect class in VDBMSs, and the exclusion argument (Table 1) for why standard oracles miss this residual is convincing — see 1.1, 2.1
- **S2:** Source-grounded falsification is a well-motivated response to the two diagnosed failure modes (extraction hallucination, judgment self-preference); the three-check design (independently reproducible, evidence sufficient, falsifiable) is principled — see 3.1, 3.2
- **S3:** Empirical evaluation on real-world VDBMSs with maintainer adjudication provides credible evidence; 107 submissions with 49 acknowledged TPs across three production systems demonstrates practical impact — see 4.1, 4.2
- **S4:** Related Work coverage is adequate for the target venue; the paper correctly positions SATORI, MASTOR, AGORA+, and AugmenTest as operating under structural anchoring (OpenAPI/traces/source) that cannot reach natural-language prose documentation — see 2.2, 2.3

### Core Weaknesses
- **W1:** Cross-family generalization is an open, critical limitation—all dev-reviewer results use a single LLM family (GLM-5.2); independent cross-model re-runs show family-specific verdicts (DeepSeek 56% recall vs. GLM 85%, Qdrant collapses across all families) — see 3.3 [major, unfixable]
- **W2:** The 48-candidate retrospective is maintainer-adjudicated but non-random; the paper does not pre-register the candidate set, and Wilson CIs do not account for operating-point selection across four ensemble configurations — see 4.3 [major, fixable]
- **W3:** RQ3 (comparison with VDBFuzz) is asymmetric: the systematic direction (VDBFuzz on v1.18.2, 26,000 requests, 0 of 14 TPs reached) is generalizable, but the two controlled cases (v1.4.0, v1.18.0) are n=1 each and the #9045 root-cause analysis is post-hoc — see 4.4 [minor, fixable]
- **W4:** Discussion of transferability beyond VDBMSs is preliminary (CouchDB/Elasticsearch probes showed no silent-accept defects in mature non-VDBMS APIs, but this is a portability probe not a generalization result) — see 5.1 [minor, fixable]

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The problem is real and prevalent. 49 maintainer-acknowledged true-positive defects across three production VDBMSs (15 merged-PR-fixed) demonstrate that documentation-implementation defects are a practical concern. The examples (Milvus #49823 silently accepting `nprobe=0`; Qdrant #9045 accepting zero-length vectors under `wait=false`) are concrete and show the silent-accept pattern corrupts query semantics without crashes.
   - **1.2 [minor, fixable]** Impact is bounded to VDBMSs and structurally similar documentation regimes. The paper claims transferability to REST APIs without OpenAPI, configuration validation, and policy-as-code on structural grounds only; the CouchDB/Elasticsearch portability probes (Section 6) confirm the pipeline runs end-to-end on non-VDBMS targets but do not establish defect-detection generalization. One non-VDBMS case study would strengthen the claim.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** The core novelty—source-grounded falsification to break extraction hallucination and judgment self-preference—is non-obvious and well-motivated. Multi-perspective judging (four specialized judge agents with majority-of-four voting) is a natural counter but collapses recall to 15%; the dev-reviewer's three-check design (independently reproducible, evidence sufficient, falsifiable) that reads implementation source as an independent ground truth is a clever mechanism to break self-preference. The #9255 reversal example (payload-filter false positive suppressed because the raw request had not set `with_payload=true`) illustrates falsification in action.
   - **2.2** Positioning against REST-API oracle tools is accurate. SATORI (fetched) generates response-field oracles from OpenAPI field names/descriptions and cannot reach input-acceptance decisions on system-level prose. MASTOR (fetched) reads source to generate oracles encoding implemented behavior and cannot detect documentation-implementation gaps by design. AGORA+ infers invariants from execution traces and cannot reach unexercised inputs. Table 1 (oracle exclusion) correctly shows these tools operate under structural anchoring (OpenAPI, traces, source) that misses the natural-language-documentation residual.
   - **2.3** Positioning against documentation-derived oracles (Toradocu, AugmenTest) is plausible but depends on unverified citations. The paper claims Toradocu pioneered NL-documentation oracle extraction with acknowledged false positives and that AugmenTest infers oracles from documentation but treats the LLM as the final semantic arbiter (validating through runtime behavior). Without reading AugmenTest (cache search failed), I cannot verify whether TestVDB's source-grounded falsification is a true delta over prior work.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The oracle-exclusion argument (Table 1) is convincing. Differential testing cannot adjudicate accept/reject because cross-vendor behavior diverges by design. Metamorphic relations target output correctness, not input-acceptance decisions. Property-based testing needs machine-checkable properties and OpenAPI schemas; VDBMS endpoints serve no schema encoding these constraints. REST-API oracle tools target response-field/runtime properties under structural anchoring, not input-acceptance on prose. The residual leaves an LLM-derived oracle as the practical option.
   - **3.2 [major, fixable]** The diagnosis of two false-positive failure modes is sound, but the evidence that source grounding breaks self-preference is indirect. The paper cites Panickssery et al. (2024) and Wataoka et al. (2024) on LLM self-preference bias; I did not fetch these papers (search rate limits). The ablation (Table 4, source alone suppresses 9/12 FPs on a 12-FP/4-TP control) and the source-disabled control (recall drops 74%→19%) show source grounding contributes materially to performance, but without the bias-papers I cannot verify whether source grounding is the theoretically appropriate countermeasure or whether the observed gain stems from other factors (e.g., better ground truth).
   - **3.3 [major, unfixable]** Cross-family generalization is a critical open limitation. All dev-reviewer results use GLM-5.2. A full independent cross-model re-run on all 48 candidates (DeepSeek, Qwen-3.8-Max, LongCat-2.0) shows family-specific verdicts (κ=0.14/0.37/0.51 vs. GLM single-run). Combined recall: DeepSeek 56%, Qwen 19%, LongCat 22% vs. GLM 85% (5-run union). The gap is vendor-specific: DeepSeek reaches 75% recall on Milvus but collapses on Qdrant (0%); all three families stay ≤14% on Qdrant. Inter-run self-consistency is high for DeepSeek (κ=1.0) and LongCat but lower for Qwen (κ=0.65). The paper admits cross-family generalization is an open question, but this weakens the claim that source grounding is a general solution to LLM-as-judge bias—it may be backbone-dependent.
   - **3.4 [minor, fixable]** The 3-run union operating point is post-hoc and the Wilson CIs do not account for selection across four operating points. A Bonferroni correction (α=0.05/4) would widen the 3-run precision CI to [44%, 84%] and recall to [51%, 89%]. A bootstrap resampling (2000 resamples) gives tighter CIs ([53%, 83%] precision, [71%, 96%] recall), supporting that the operating point is not an artifact of the specific candidate sample. The paper flags the operating point as post-hoc, but the headline numbers should acknowledge the selection or report the Bonferroni-adjusted CIs.

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate
   - **4.1** Artifact is declared and reachable. The paper links `https://github.com/yihui504/testvdb-anon` with prompts under `agents/`, ground truth under `test_questions/`, reproduction under `reproduction/full52/`. The full 22 agent role definitions, target versions, per-token accounting, and the 48-candidate ground truth are in the artifact. This meets the bar for a technical paper.
   - **4.2** Procedural detail is sufficient to follow the work. Section 3 (TestVDB Approach) describes the four-stage pipeline with concrete examples (the #49823 path). Section 4 (The False-Positive Problem) diagnoses the two failure modes and shows multi-perspective judging is insufficient. Section 5 (Dev-Reviewer) specifies the three checks and three anchors. Section 6 (Evaluation) reports the 107-submission yield, the 48-candidate retrospective, and the ablation. The text describes how results were produced well enough to follow or replicate.
   - **4.3 [major, fixable]** The 48-candidate retrospective is maintainer-adjudicated but non-random, and the paper did not pre-register the candidate set. A capture-recapture or unbiased defect-sample estimation is future work. The Wilson CIs (Table 3) do not account for operating-point selection. The bootstrap validation (2000 resamples) addresses selection but not non-random sampling. The paper is transparent about these limitations, but they bound the strength of the recall/precision claims.
   - **4.4 [minor, fixable]** RQ3 (comparison with VDBFuzz) has asymmetric evidence. The systematic direction (VDBFuzz on Qdrant v1.18.2, TestVDB's pinned version, 26,000 requests, 0 of 14 silent-accept TPs reached) is generalizable: VDBFuzz's crash oracle cannot detect non-crashing accepts by construction. The two controlled cases (v1.4.0: TestVDB reaches VDBFuzz's integer-overflow crash; v1.18.0: VDBFuzz misses TestVDB's #9045 silent-accept) are n=1 each. The #9045 root cause (vector validation under `debug_assert` skipped in release builds, `wait=false` accepts zero-length while `wait=true` rejects) is a compelling example of why crash-focused patching misses silent-accept residuals, but it is post-hoc. The paper acknowledges this asymmetry.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1** Structure is sound and the paper is readable. Introduction frames the problem and contributions clearly. Background (Section 2) separates consistency from correctness and walks the oracle-exclusion argument. Approach (Section 3) describes the pipeline with concrete examples. False-Positive Problem (Section 4) diagnoses two failure modes. Dev-Reviewer (Section 5) specifies the three-check design. Evaluation (Section 6) reports three research questions. Related Work (Section 7) positions against VDBMS testing, REST-API oracles, LLM-as-judge reliability, and documentation-derived oracles. Discussion (Section 8) addresses limitations and transferability.
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** Minor language and notation issues. The title in the `.tex` source (`TestVDB: doc-implementation defects in VDBMSs`) differs from the title in the abstract (`TestVDB: Detecting Documentation-Implementation Defects in Vector Databases via LLM-Derived Behavioral Claims`)—this should be consistent. The notation `\texttt{wait=false}` appears correctly in text. No pervasive LaTeX-stripping failures detected.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** Figures and tables are clear. Figure 1 (pipeline) sketches the four stages and the dev-reviewer's source-grounded falsification path. Table 1 (oracle exclusion) convincingly shows the residual. Table 2 (cost) reports approximate per-target LLM-call distribution. Table 3 (operating points) reports per-run variance and ensemble choices. Figure 2 (per-run recall) visualizes recall across five runs. Table 4 (ablation) isolates the dev-reviewer's two anchors on a 12-FP/4-TP control. Table 5 (bidirectional reachability) summarizes the VDBFuzz comparison. The figures support the text.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** Related Work is adequate for the target venue but could cite more VDBMS-specific work. The paper cites VDBFuzz, a bug study, and a testing roadmap. A broader survey of VDBMS testing approaches (not just fuzzing) would strengthen the problem framing. The LLM-as-judge discussion cites Panickssery, Wataoka, and Haldar but does not deeply engage with the bias-mitigation literature beyond self-preference.

### Questions for Authors
- **Q1:** How would you address the cross-family generalization limitation? Is source grounding expected to transfer to other LLM families, or is the GLM-5.2 result backbone-specific? If family-specific, what are the implications for the claim that source grounding breaks LLM-as-judge bias? — Clarification on item 3.3 would help assess whether this is a fundamental limitation or a tuning issue.
- **Q2:** Can you provide more detail on the 48-candidate selection process? Was it representative of the 107 submissions, or were certain categories of defects (e.g., semantic vs. boundary, parameter vs. state) over-represented? A more explicit description of the selection criteria would strengthen the retrospective's validity.
- **Q3:** The operating-point selection (3-run union as headline) is post-hoc. Would the qualitative conclusions hold under a pre-registered protocol (e.g., always report 3-run union, or always report the operating point with the highest F1)? A sensitivity analysis showing the robustness of the recall/precision gains to the selection rule would address concerns about p-hacking.
- **Q4:** RQ3's controlled cases are n=1 each. Do you have plans to expand the bidirectional comparison to more crash-class defects (beyond the integer overflow on `size=2^63`) and more silent-accept defects (beyond #9045)? A systematic sample of each defect class would strengthen the complementarity claim.
- **Q5:** The transferability probes (CouchDB, Elasticsearch) showed no silent-accept defects, but these are mature non-VDBMS APIs. Do you have plans to test on a less-mature API (e.g., an earlier version of a REST API, or an API with known documentation quality issues)? A finding that silent-accept defects concentrate in immature APIs would clarify the target regime.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist (REST API Oracles / LLM-as-Judge)

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary
TestVDB targets documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs), where a system silently accepts an input or produces a behavior that violates its natural-language API documentation. Because the boundary is prose rather than a structured specification, deterministic oracles (crash detection, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property-based testing) cannot adjudicate these accept/reject decisions. The paper instantiates a four-stage LLM pipeline that extracts behavioral claims from documentation, generates test probes, executes them on sandboxed VDBMS instances, and confirms defects by comparing expected vs. actual behavior. Two false-positive failure modes emerge: hallucination at extraction (the LLM invents constraints the documentation does not state) and self-preference at judgment (the same family that extracts a claim tends to confirm it). A multi-perspective judging baseline (four specialized judge agents with majority voting) raises precision but collapses recall. The authors introduce a dev-reviewer agent that acts as a source-grounded falsifier: it reproduces each candidate, cross-checks it against implementation source, and tries to disprove it. On 107 submitted issues across three VDBMSs, maintainers acknowledged 49 true-positive defects (15 fixed via merged PR). On a 48-candidate controlled retrospective, the dev-reviewer reaches 67% precision and 74% recall (3-run any-confirmed ensemble) against 37% recall without source grounding. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows complementary coverage: TestVDB reaches a crash-class defect by contract reasoning, while VDBFuzz misses a TestVDB silent-accept defect under current templates.

### Core Strengths
- **S1:** Clear problem formulation and oracle-exclusion argument — see 1.1, 2.1
- **S2:** Source-grounded falsifier as a countermeasure to LLM false positives — see 3.1
- **S3:** Real-world impact (49 maintainer-acknowledged defects, 15 merged-PR fixes) — see 4.1

### Core Weaknesses
- **W1:** Single-LLM-family evaluation limits generalization claims — see 4.2
- **W2:** Post-hoc operating-point selection without preregistration — see 4.3
- **W3:** 48-candidate retrospective non-random and convenience-sampled — see 4.4

### Detailed Assessment

#### 1. Significance — Adequate

- **1.1** **Clear problem formulation with oracle-exclusion argument.** The Introduction and Section 2 (Background) cleanly separate documentation-implementation consistency from correctness, and Table 2 walks through six oracle candidates (crash, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property-based testing, REST-API doc/spec-derived oracles, LLM-derived oracle) with a structural reason each misses the documentation-implementation residual. This exclusion argument is rigorous and establishes why an LLM is the practical oracle for this regime. The problem—silent accepts that corrupt query semantics without crashes—is practically motivated (44 of 49 true positives do not crash) and clearly explained via concrete examples (Milvus #49823: `nprobe=0` silently accepted despite documentation declaring range `[1, 16384]`). **Strength.**

- **1.2** **Real-world impact demonstrated but scope bounded by evaluation limitations.** The 49 maintainer-acknowledged true-positive defects across three production VDBMSs (Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate), with 15 fixed via merged PR, show practical relevance. However, the yield proportion (49 of 107 submitted, 45.8% worst-case) and the vendor distribution (22/51 Milvus, 14/26 Qdrant, 13/30 Weaviate) are biased by the tool's design toward documentation-implementation defects and do not generalize to a population estimate. The paper acknowledges this limitation (Section 6), but the headline numbers risk overinterpretation. **[minor, fixable]** The proportion reflects the study's yield, not a defect prevalence estimate for VDBMSs at large.

- **1.3 [minor, fixable]** **Portability claim to non-VDBMS REST APIs is structurally motivated but empirically thin.** Section 6 argues the setting is not VDBMS-specific in principle and reports two preliminary probes (CouchDB 3.4.3, Elasticsearch 8.11.0) where the pipeline ran end-to-end but surfaced no silent-accept defects because "both mature non-VDBMS APIs validate more strictly." This supports method portability but not defect-detection portability. The claim "we ran the TestVDB pipeline on Apache CouchDB...and Elasticsearch" demonstrates extraction, probing, and falsification steps port across targets, which is useful evidence of method transferability. However, the lack of detected defects means the claim that TestVDB would find defects in these regimes remains untested. Portability to non-VDBMS REST APIs without OpenAPI (the structurally similar documentation regime the paper claims transferability to) is claimed on structural grounds only and is not empirically tested; even one non-VDBMS case study with a detected defect would strengthen the claim. **[minor, fixable]** The portability claim is structurally motivated but empirically thin; one detected defect in a non-VDBMS API would substantiate it.

#### 2. Novelty — Adequate

- **2.1** **Clear novelty delta against REST-API oracle generation competitors.** Within my specialty (REST API test generation/oracle problem), I verified the paper's characterizations of SATORI (from cache: OpenAPI-anchored response-field oracles targeting response-field properties, not input-acceptance decisions), MASTOR (from cache: source-anchored oracles encoding implemented behavior, unable to detect documentation-implementation gaps), and AGORA+ (from paper's Related Work: dynamic invariant inference from observed traffic, unable to reach inputs not in the traffic dataset). All three operate under per-field structural anchoring (OpenAPI fields, execution traces, or source) and target response-field or runtime properties; none adjudicates input-acceptance decisions on system-level prose. TestVDB targets a distinct regime (documentation-implementation consistency on unstructured prose with source-grounded falsification) not addressed by any of the three named competitors. The exclusion argument in Table 2 and the Related Work characterization are accurate and supported by the cached summaries. **Strength.**

- **2.2** **Source-grounded falsifier is a clear contribution over LLM-as-judge baselines.** The multi-perspective judging baseline (four specialized judge agents voting) reaches ~80% precision but only ~15% recall because all judge agents read the same ambiguous documentation and converge on the same over-strict claim. The dev-reviewer breaks this by using implementation source as an independent falsifier, lifting recall from 37% (single-LLM baseline) to 74% (3-run any-confirmed ensemble) while maintaining useful precision (67% vs. 56%). The three checks (independently reproducible, evidence sufficient, falsifiable) and three anchors (clean-reproduction, source-grounded, threat-model) are clearly specified and mechanistically motivated (Section 4). The ablation (Table 5: source alone suppresses 9/12 false positives (75%), threat-model alone 6/12 (50%), union 11/12 (91%)) isolates the contribution. This is a clear technical advance over using LLMs as both extractor and judge. **Strength.**

- **2.3 [minor, fixable]** **LLM-as-judge bias mitigation is incremental over prior work.** The paper applies Panickssery et al.'s (self-preference bias) and Wataoka et al.'s (perplexity-based familiarity) findings—established for general text evaluation—to the test-oracle pipeline, observing the same-family over-confirmation phenomenon. Source-grounded falsification is a novel countermeasure in the test-oracle context, but the underlying bias mechanism is a straightforward extrapolation from general LLM evaluation to LLM-as-judge in testing. This does not diminish the contribution's validity, but it is not a fundamental insight about LLM behavior; it is a clever application of known bias to a new domain with a domain-specific countermeasure. **[minor, fixable]** The self-preference mitigation is a novel application of known bias to test-oracle generation, not a fundamental new insight about LLM behavior.

#### 3. Soundness — Weak

- **3.1 [major, fixable]** **Post-hoc operating-point selection without preregistration threatens RQ2's internal validity.** The retrospective reports four operating points (single-run, 3-run union, 5-run union, 5-run majority) and selects the 3-run union as the headline ("we report the any-confirmed (union) ensemble as the operating point"). The paper acknowledges this is "post-hoc, exploratory" and that "the Wilson CIs above do not account for this selection," providing Bonferroni-corrected CIs (3-run precision ~[44%, 84%], recall ~[51%, 89%]) and bootstrap validation ([53%, 83%] / [71%, 96%]) as sensitivity analysis. This transparency is commendable, but the headline numbers (67% / 74%) are selected from four candidates after seeing the data. The operating point is justified by falsifier semantics ("a candidate that survives any independent falsification is more likely a true defect"), but this justification is post-hoc rather than pre-registered. **[major, fixable]** The 3-run union operating point is selected post-hoc from four candidates; preregistering the operating point before the retrospective would strengthen internal validity.

- **3.2 [major, fixable]** **Single-LLM-family evaluation (GLM-5.2) limits generalization claims.** All dev-reviewer results use GLM-5.2; the paper reports a cross-model re-run (DeepSeek, Qwen-3.8-Max, LongCat-2.0) showing verdict is family-specific (κ = 0.14–0.51 vs. GLM) and combined recall varies widely (56%/22%/19% vs. GLM's 85% for 5-run union). The gap is vendor-specific (DeepSeek reaches 75% recall on Milvus but 0% on Qdrant vs. GLM's 57%), and the paper honestly acknowledges "cross-family generalization is an open question." However, the headline claims about dev-reviewer effectiveness (67% precision / 74% recall) are conditioned on a single LLM family. Without cross-family robustness, the dev-reviewer's contribution is methodologically sound but empirically fragile. **[major, fixable]** Cross-family generalization is an open question; headline dev-reviewer numbers are GLM-5.2-specific and may not transfer to other families.

- **3.3 [major, unfixable]** **48-candidate retrospective is non-random and convenience-sampled, limiting external validity.** The retrospective covers Milvus (32 candidates) and Qdrant (16 candidates) and excludes Weaviate ("yield-only" per Section 5). The paper acknowledges the set is "maintainer-adjudicated but non-random" and that "we did not pre-register the candidate set," noting that "capture-recapture or unbiased defect-sample estimation is future work." However, non-random sampling combined with post-hoc operating-point selection compounds internal validity threats. The retrospective is sufficient to establish that the dev-reviewer works better than the baseline on this particular set of candidates, but it does not support strong claims about how the method would perform on an unbiased sample of VDBMS defects. **[major, unfixable]** The 48-candidate set is convenience-sampled (maintainer-adjudicated, non-random) and does not represent an unbiased VDBMS defect population.

- **3.4 [minor, fixable]** **Statistical rigor is partial for RQ1 yield estimates.** The paper reports Wilson 95% CIs for yield precision (68.1% [56.6%, 77.7%] on 72 adjudicated submissions; 45.8% [36.7%, 55.2%] worst-case treating 35 pending as false positives), which is good practice. However, RQ1 lacks statistical significance testing or confidence intervals for the vendor-specific yields (22/51 Milvus, 14/26 Qdrant, 13/30 Weaviate) and no test for yield differences across vendors. The per-vendor yields are descriptive statistics without uncertainty quantification. **[minor, fixable]** Per-vendor yields lack confidence intervals or statistical tests for differences.

- **3.5 [minor, fixable]** **Threats to validity (Section 5) are incomplete for internal validity.** The section covers internal (single-run variance, post-hoc operating point, non-random retrospective), external (Weaviate yield-only, result correctness out of scope, transferability claimed structurally), and construct (single-LLM-family, implementation-as-correct assumption). However, it does not address selection bias in the 107 submitted issues (issues surfaced by TestVDB, not a random sample) or confounding factors in the dev-reviewer's three checks (e.g., source grounding effectiveness may depend on codebase size or language). **[minor, fixable]** Threats to validity should explicitly address selection bias in the 107 submitted issues and potential confounders in dev-reviewer effectiveness.

#### 4. Verifiability — Adequate

- **4.1** **Artifact link declared and reachable; paper describes pipeline sufficiently.** Section 3 states "All agents run on a GLM-5.2 backbone via the BigModel Anthropic-compatible API under default sampling" and provides the artifact URL (https://github.com/yihui504/testvdb-anon) with prompts under `agents/`, ground truth under `test_questions/`, and reproduction under `reproduction/full52/`. Appendix A excerpts the two key agent role prompts (contract-formalizer, dev-reviewer). The paper describes the four-stage pipeline with enough detail to follow the flow (claim extraction, test generation, sandboxed execution, defect confirmation) and the dev-reviewer's three checks and three anchors. No obvious gaps in the high-level description of how results were produced. **Strength.**

- **4.2 [minor, fixable]** **Per-target cost ($10) and LLM-call distribution (~10^4 calls) are coarse approximations.** Table 4 reports "Approximate per-target LLM-call distribution" (50% dev-reviewer source-grounding, 25% claim extraction + test generation, 25% judging + novelty gate) and "~10^4 calls, ~$10" total per target. These are useful order-of-magnitude estimates but lack granularity (no confidence intervals, no variance across targets, no breakdown by agent or stage). The paper does not report reproducibility of the cost estimates (e.g., did costs vary across Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate?). **[minor, fixable]** Per-target cost and call distribution are coarse; reporting variance across targets would strengthen verifiability.

#### 5. Presentation — Adequate

- **5.1 [minor, fixable]** **Structure is sound, but some figures are hard to parse.** The four-stage pipeline (Figure 1) is clear, and the dev-reviewer three-check diagram (Figure 2) is well-specified. However, Table 2 (Oracle candidates) is dense with technical terms and would benefit from a visual encoding or separate row for "Why it misses the documentation-implementation residual." Figure 3 (per-run recall across five independent runs) is a simple scatter plot that could be a box plot or violin plot to show distribution. **[minor, fixable]** Some figures (Table 2, Figure 3) could be redesigned for better readability.

- **5.2 [minor, fixable]** **Minor language issues and LaTeX artifacts.** Line 108: "CLLM-5.2" should be "GLM-5.2" (typo). The reference format is inconsistent (some references have venue in parentheses, some without). The bibliography cites `agoraplus25`, `satori25`, `mastor26` but these keys are not visible in the References section (they may be in a separate .bib file not rendered in the stripped source). **[minor, fixable]** Minor typos and formatting inconsistencies (e.g., "CLLM-5.2", reference format).

### Questions for Authors
- **Q1:** — intended effect: if the authors clarify whether the 3-run union was pre-registered before the retrospective or selected post-hoc after seeing all four operating points, item 3.1's rating would move from [major, fixable] to [minor, fixable] because a pre-registered operating point would resolve the internal validity threat.

- **Q2:** — intended effect: if the authors provide confidence intervals or variance estimates for the per-target cost ($10) and LLM-call distribution (~10^4 calls), item 4.2's rating would move from [minor, fixable] to Adequate (no fixable issue) because cost reproducibility would be established.

- **Q3:** — intended effect: if the authors explain why Weaviate was excluded from the controlled retrospective (beyond "yield-only") and whether a Weaviate retrospective would be feasible, item 3.3's severity would be contextualized (non-random sampling would remain [major, unfixable], but the scope of the limitation would be clearer).

- **Q4:** — intended effect: if the authors report statistical tests or confidence intervals for per-vendor yields (Milvus 22/51, Qdrant 14/26, Weaviate 13/30), item 3.4's [minor, fixable] would be resolved.

## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary
The paper proposes TestVDB, a system for detecting documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs). The authors identify a gap in existing VDBMS testing: crash-only oracles like VDBFuzz miss logical bugs where systems silently accept inputs that violate their API documentation. TestVDB addresses this through a four-stage pipeline that uses Large Language Models to extract behavioral claims from documentation, generate tests, execute them against sandboxed VDBMS instances, and confirm defects. To counter LLM false positives from hallucination and self-preference bias, the authors introduce a dev-reviewer agent that falsifies candidates against implementation source code. The system surfaced 107 candidate issues across three VDBMSs (Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate), with 49 maintainer-acknowledged true-positive defects (15 fixed via merged PR). A controlled retrospective on 48 adjudicated candidates shows the source-grounded approach achieves 67% precision and 74% recall, compared to 37% recall without source anchoring. A bidirectional comparison with VDBFuzz demonstrates complementary coverage.

### Core Strengths
- **S1:** Well-motivated problem domain with clear practical impact — see Introduction, Section 1. The authors convincingly position VDBMS logical bugs as a costly, under-studied defect class, with empirical backing from a bug study and roadmap.
- **S2:** Systematic oracle-exclusion argument clarifies why existing approaches cannot handle the documentation-implementation residual — see Section 2 (Background), Table 1. The paper carefully walks through why crash oracles, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property-based testing, and existing REST-API oracle tools all miss this defect class.
- **S3:** Honest limitation disclosure with provisional claims clearly marked — see Sections 6 (Threats to validity) and 7 (Discussion and Limitations). Cross-family generalization is explicitly left as an open question, and the single-LLM-family evaluation is acknowledged.
- **S4:** Concrete artifact and reproducibility support — see Section 3. The authors declare a reachable GitHub repository with prompts, ground truth, and reproduction drivers, exceeding what many papers provide.

### Core Weaknesses
- **W1:** Statistical operating point selection is post-hoc and weakly justified — see Section 5 (RQ2), Table 5, Figure 4. The 3-run union is selected after observing the data, not pre-registered, and the statistical correction is acknowledged as an afterthought.
- **W2:** Limited external validity on non-VDBMS transferability despite structural claims — see Section 7 (Discussion and Limitations). Only two non-VDBMS probes (CouchDB, Elasticsearch) are presented, and both produced no silent-accept defects, making the portability claim speculative.
- **W3:** Cross-family generalization left completely open despite being central to LLM-as-judge reliability — see Section 5 (RQ2), cross-model re-run paragraph. The family-specific verdict ($\kappa$ as low as 0.14) undermines confidence that the approach generalizes beyond GLM-5.2.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The problem is well-established as important. VDBMS defects in retrieval-augmented LLM applications are costly (corrupted context, silent propagation), and the authors cite empirical work showing more than half of VDBMS bugs are functional failures rather than crashes — see Introduction, paragraph 1. This is a meaningful, not trivial, problem.
   - **1.2 [minor, fixable]** The impact is bounded by the single-LLM-family evaluation. The results depend entirely on GLM-5.2 behavior, and the cross-model re-run shows verdicts are family-specific ($\kappa = 0.14$ DeepSeek, $\kappa = 0.37$ Qwen, $\kappa = 0.51$ LongCat) — see Section 5 (RQ2), cross-model paragraph. The significance claim rests on one backbone, which is a narrower claim than the framing suggests.
   - **1.3 [major, fixable]** The 49 maintainer-acknowledged defects show practical impact, but the paper does not establish whether these defects are representative or a biased sample. The authors acknowledge the yield is biased by design (Section 7), but this limits how much we can generalize about the defect prevalence in VDBMSs at large. We know TestVDB found defects; we do not know how many defects exist or what fraction TestVDB's yield represents.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** The core novelty is clear and non-obvious: using implementation source as an independent falsifier for LLM-derived test oracles to break self-preference bias. The dev-reviewer agent's three-check falsification (independently reproducible, evidence sufficient, falsifiable) is a distinct contribution from prior REST-API oracle tools — see Section 4 (Dev-Reviewer), Figure 3.
   - **2.2** The positioning against prior work is precise. The authors clearly distinguish TestVDB from AGORA+, SATORI, and MASTOR (all anchored on per-field structural sources) and from documentation-derived oracles like Toradocu (which keep the LLM as final arbiter) — see Section 6 (Related Work), paragraphs 2 and 4. The delta is recognizable.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** The self-preference bias application to test-oracle pipelines is incremental but not transformative. Panickssery et al. and Wataoka et al. already established self-preference for LLM evaluators; the paper applies this to a new domain (test oracles) rather than discovering the phenomenon. The novelty is in the application, not the underlying bias mechanism.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The main claims are supported by appropriate methods. The oracle-exclusion argument (Section 2, Table 1) systematically rules out alternatives before justifying the LLM-derived approach. The evaluation covers three VDBMSs, includes a controlled retrospective, and provides a bidirectional comparison against VDBFuzz. No single methodological flaw obviously undermines the results.
   - **3.2 [major, fixable]** The operating point selection is post-hoc and weakly statistically justified. The 3-run union is chosen as the headline operating point after observing the data, not pre-registered. The paper acknowledges this is a "post-hoc, exploratory operating point" and applies a Bonferroni correction and bootstrap validation, but the Wilson CIs reported do not account for the selection across four operating points — see Section 5 (RQ2), Table 5, paragraph 2. This weakens the statistical rigor of the precision/recall claims.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The controlled retrospective sample is non-random and potentially biased. The 48-candidate set (27 TP, 21 by-design/rejected) is maintainer-adjudicated but not randomly sampled, and the authors did not pre-register the candidate set — see Section 6 (Internal validity). The paper treats this as a controlled study, but without knowing the selection mechanism, we cannot assess how representative it is. The yield precision (68.1% on adjudicated submissions, 45.8% worst-case) may not reflect the true precision on an unbiased sample.
   - **3.4 [minor, fixable]** The cross-model re-run uses only three additional families and reports limited metrics. We see $\kappa$ against GLM single-run and recall for a 3-run union, but we do not see full precision/recall curves or per-vendor breakdowns for DeepSeek, Qwen, and LongCat. The cross-family generalization conclusion ("verdict is family-specific") is supported, but the evidence is thinner than the single-family evaluation.

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate
   - **4.1** The paper provides enough information to follow the work conceptually. The four-stage pipeline is clearly described with concrete examples (the #49823 path in Section 3), and the dev-reviewer's three-check falsification is illustrated with diagrams and a concrete reversal example (#9255) — see Section 4, Figure 3.
   - **4.2** The artifact is declared and appears reachable. The authors provide a GitHub repository URL (https://github.com/yihui504/testvdb-anon) with prompts under `agents/`, ground truth under `test_questions/`, and reproduction under `reproduction/full52/` — see Section 3, paragraph 6. The scope of artifacts (22 agent role definitions, per-token accounting, pinned versions) exceeds what many papers provide.
   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** The paper does not specify the exact sampling parameters for the GLM-5.2 backbone beyond "default sampling" (no decoding overrides; temperature and top-$p$ at provider defaults). For a method where LLM behavior is central, reporting the actual temperature and top-$p$ values (even if defaults) would improve verifiability — see Section 3, paragraph 6.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1** The structure is sound and follows the standard technical paper format: motivation, background, approach, evaluation, related work, discussion, conclusion. The flow is logical and easy to follow.
   - **5.2** The writing is generally clear with only minor language issues. Some sentences are long and could be split for clarity (e.g., Section 5, RQ2 paragraph 2), but the meaning is never obscured. No pervasive language errors obstruct evaluation.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** Figure 4 (per-run recall) uses a compressed y-axis scaling (0.045cm per unit) that may distort visual perception. The dots show recall ranging 15--78%, but the visual spacing suggests a flatter distribution. Explicitly noting the scale compression in the caption would prevent misreading.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** Table 5 (operating points) does not specify that "per-run band" is min--max across five runs in the table body itself, relying on the caption. A parenthetical "(min--max across 5 runs)" in the table body would improve standalone readability.
   - **5.5 [minor, fixable]** The notation for confidence intervals is inconsistent: Wilson 95% CI is written as $[49, 81]$ in some places and $[49\%, 81\%]$ in others (e.g., Table 5 vs. text). Standardizing on one format would improve consistency.

### Questions for Authors
- **Q1:** What was the selection mechanism for the 48-candidate retrospective, and how does it relate to the full 107-submission set? Understanding the selection would clarify whether the controlled retrospective is biased toward easier or harder cases, which would affect the external validity of the precision/recall claims. If the authors clarify this, item 3.3's rating may move up.
- **Q2:** Why is the 3-run union the headline operating point rather than the 5-run union, given that the 5-run union reaches higher recall (85%) at still-acceptable precision (62%)? The paper mentions "modest reproducibility cost," but quantifying the cost differential (wall-clock time, API expense) would help readers evaluate the trade-off. If the authors provide this, item 3.2's rating may move up.
- **Q3:** Can the authors provide a fuller breakdown of the cross-model re-run results, including per-vendor precision/recall for DeepSeek, Qwen, and LongCat, not just recall and $\kappa$ against GLM? The current evidence (one paragraph) is sufficient to support the "family-specific" conclusion but insufficient to understand where the gap concentrates. If the authors expand this, item 2.3's rating may move up.
