## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets a class of bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs) that crash-oracle fuzzers cannot see: cases where the implementation silently accepts an input or behaves in a way that contradicts the vendor's natural-language API documentation (e.g., Milvus accepting `nprobe=0` when the prose declares `[1, 16384]`). It argues that every deterministic oracle family anchors its expectation somewhere other than system-level prose, leaving an LLM-derived oracle as the practical option, and that this imports an LLM false-positive problem which a multi-perspective panel does not fix because the ambiguity is shared. The proposed system, TestVDB, is a four-stage pipeline: documentation distilled into source-verified behavioral specifications, attack agents that turn constraints into executable probes under deterministically pre-bound strategies, Docker-pinned sandboxed execution with raw HTTP logging, and a bug-confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly from cross-examination, grounding every verdict in evidence outside the LLM — above all the implementation source — with a three-valued verdict that routes unresolvable cases to human review.

The evaluation has two halves. RQ1 reports an operational human–machine deployment across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate in which maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions (23 fixed via merged PRs), with a per-pattern taxonomy dominated by the acceptance family. RQ2 audits the frozen evidence packages that fed the confirmation stage (43.3% of cited constraint–page pairs unsupported), rebuilds all 81 packs against version-pinned documentation and the tested version's own OpenAPI artifact, re-adjudicates the full pool of 51 true bugs plus 30 false positives three times per arm, and reports the confirmation stage's verdicts under three scoring conventions side by side: the contract core at 0.157 recall / 0.967 suppression, the full structured stage at 0.804 / 0.800, and a flat single-prompt judge reading the same packs and sources at 0.588. The paper decomposes that separation — routed cases counted as confirmed carry it; under forced verdicts the two judges are indistinguishable (0.529 vs. 0.490), and hand-adjudicating the routed cases yields a joint judge-plus-human reading of 0.647. RQ3 probes complementarity against VDBFuzz bidirectionally on Qdrant.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The RQ1 ledger is a strong empirical result for this area — 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 merged fixes across three production systems, with the fix nature independently classified and the classifier sanity-checked — see 1.1, 1.2.
- **S2:** The RQ2 measurement design (audit → rebuild → protocol alignment) and the three-scoring decomposition are unusually rigorous; every headline number I recomputed closes exactly, and the paper locates its own mechanism against itself — see 3.1, 1.3.
- **S3:** The finding that the structured protocol's advantage is routing/escalation rather than forced accuracy is a transferable design lesson for the LLM-oracle line, and the paper states it against its own interest — see 1.3.
- **S4:** The novelty claim is a real inversion, and the positioning against MASTOR, Metamon, CASCADE, SATORI, and AGORA+ holds on the cached texts; the RQ3 baseline characterizations are verifiable against the released VDBFuzz artifact and are correct — see 2.1–2.4, 4.2.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The "advantage survives Holm correction (p=0.029)" claim is family-dependent: under a maximal family that includes the second-backbone replication test, the adjusted value is 0.0072 × 7 = 0.0504 — see 3.2.
- **W2:** Missing related work: Argus (arXiv:2510.06663), LLM-derived test oracles for DBMSs with a formal equivalence prover as the anti-hallucination anchor, is the closest DBMS-side analogue of the paper's central methodological move and is uncited — see 2.5.
- **W3:** The flagship comparison's "structure" is bundled; nothing isolates the mandatory routing rule from the four-perspective scaffolding, so "structure pays" cannot yet be narrowed to "routing pays" — see 3.7.
- **W4:** Presentation density, plus key terms ("suppression", the three senses of "confirmed") defined only well after first use, makes the three-scoring argument harder to follow than the underlying work warrants — see 5.2–5.4.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation (behavioral-conformance) bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where a system silently accepts an input or behaves in a way that violates its natural-language API documentation, so that no crash signal fires. It argues structurally (Table 1) that every deterministic-oracle family anchors its expectation elsewhere — crash signals, another implementation, output relations, schemas, or structured specs — leaving an LLM the practical reader of system-level, untagged prose. TestVDB instantiates this as a four-stage pipeline: documentation is distilled into typed, source-verified constraints; attack agents bound to a pre-registered strategy registry generate executable probes; a Docker-pinned sandbox executes them and logs raw HTTP; and a bug-confirmation stage splits evidence assembly (an evidence builder) from cross-examination (a chain auditor) across four perspectives, with the implementation source as the falsification anchor. Across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions were maintainer-confirmed as real bugs, and 23 were fixed via merged PRs.

The evaluation then re-audits the frozen evidence packs, rebuilds them against version-pinned documentation, and re-adjudicates the full 81-candidate pool three times per configuration across five judge configurations. The contract core recalls 0.157 at 0.967 suppression; the structured full stage reaches 0.804 at 0.800; a flat single-prompt judge with the same packs and source access reaches 0.588 at 0.867, and the full-vs-flat confirmed-set difference (47 vs 34) is significant (exact McNemar, raw p=0.0072; Holm-corrected p=0.029 in the paper's declared seven-test family). Under forced verdicts the two judges are indistinguishable (0.529 vs 0.490), a hand-adjudicated joint judge-plus-human reading sits at 0.647 recall / 0.943 precision, and a second model family replicates the source-only arm but not the full-vs-flat separation. RQ3 probes the crash-oracle baseline (VDBFuzz) bidirectionally on Qdrant.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** A real-world yield carried by an unusually disciplined ledger: maintainer-confirmed bugs with issue numbers, acceptance-signal accounting, fix-nature classification of all 23 merged PRs, and a self-initiated re-probe that reclassified one previously counted true positive (#9149) rather than leaving it standing — see 1.1, 1.2.
- **S2:** The audit → rebuild → protocol-alignment measurement makes the confirmation stage's value legible: every headline rate, interval, and paired test is recomputable from the paper and closes arithmetically (I verified all of them), and the known leakage channels were measured and cleaned with replacement runs — see 3.1, 4.1.
- **S3:** The three-scoring decomposition (convention 0.804 / joint 0.647 / forced 0.529) reports the mechanism honestly instead of hiding it: the paper itself states that the convention overstates the joint system and forced scoring understates it, and that routing rather than forced accuracy carries the separation — see 3.2, 3.6.
- **S4:** The novelty delta is asserted against works it actually holds against: I checked the paper's characterizations of MASTOR, SATORI, AGORA+, CASCADE, and Metamon against their cached texts, and the "anchor inversion" claim (prose as ground, implementation as falsifier) survives all five — see 2.1, 2.2.
- **S5:** The RQ3 comparison is bounded rather than triumphant: the paper detects that VDBFuzz's released success counters cannot fire, substitutes a liveness oracle, measures a live zero, and bounds that zero from the mutation-space and crash-class directions — see 3.3.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The flagship claim "survives Holm correction" depends on the seven-test family defined in a footnote; I recomputed that if the three tests the paper labels sensitivity analyses join the family, the full-vs-flat adjusted p becomes 0.0504. The exclusions are principled (re-scorings and a replication), but no pre-registration pointer is given — see 3.4, Q1.
- **W2:** The full arm's extra cognition channel has no stated corpus cutoff and no ablation; §4.3's "no access to adjudication labels or issue history" is inaccurate for that arm, and "cognition contributes to none of the separation beyond the batch-delete leak" rests on rationale inspection — see 3.5, Q2.
- **W3:** "Whatever separates the full stage from the flat judge is structure, not rules" is bounded by the flat judge's four-of-seven objective-class enumeration and by the routing instruction its prompt already carries; a flat+full-rules control is the clean isolation — see 3.7, Q3.
- **W4:** The deployment-relevant reading — the joint judge-plus-human system — rests on author-run, non-blind adjudication and has no equal-treatment comparator for the flat arm; its advantage over the flat judge is +3 executions (p=1.0) — see 3.6, 1.3, Q4.
- **W5:** Several headline-adjacent definitions and one contradiction remain: the contract core's verdict rules are unspecified, the weak-evidence stratum is undefined, and §3.5 says Human-Review cases "lie outside every automated rate" while §4.3 counts them as confirmed in every rate — see 3.10, 4.2, 4.3, Q5.

## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets silent, non-crashing logical bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where an implementation's observable behavior — what it accepts, rejects, returns, or retains across state transitions — contradicts what its natural-language API documentation prescribes. The authors argue that no crash signal, reference implementation, output relation, or machine-checkable schema anchors its expectation in that prose, so an LLM-derived oracle is the practical option, and they build TestVDB as a four-stage pipeline: (i) crawling vendor documentation and distilling it into a structured specification of typed constraints; (ii) generating executable Python probes through three attack agents whose strategies are pre-bound to constraints by a deterministic trigger registry; (iii) running every probe against a Docker-pinned instance while logging raw HTTP traffic; and (iv) a confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly from cross-examination — an evidence builder assembles a five-section chain with source-code grounding, and a chain auditor applies four mechanical checks and four perspectives (contract, physical constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition) to produce Confirmed / False-Positive / Human-Review verdicts.

The evaluation is in three parts. RQ1 reports an operational, author-screened submission campaign across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate: 81 maintainer-adjudicated submissions, 51 confirmed as real bugs, 30 adjudicated false positives, 23 fixed via merged PRs, grouped into eight bug patterns. RQ2 audits the frozen evidence packs that had fed the confirmation stage (finding 43.3% of cited (constraint, page) pairs unsupported by the pages they cite), rebuilds all 81 packs against version-pinned documentation, and re-adjudicates the 51-bug / 30-false-positive pool three times per configuration across five judge arms (contract core, full stage, source-only, a flat single-prompt judge, and source-only on a second backbone), reporting the stage under forced-verdict, joint judge-plus-human, and deployment-convention scorings. RQ3 probes the reach of the crash-oracle fuzzer VDBFuzz bidirectionally on Qdrant v1.18.0 and v1.4.0, including a full-template-run head-to-head on the same instance.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Under a single, fully disclosed protocol the paper reports three defensible readings of the confirmation stage's recall — forced 0.529, joint judge-plus-human 0.647, deployment convention 0.804 — and localizes the stage's measured advantage to its human-review routing channel rather than to forced-verdict accuracy. — see 3.1
- **S2:** The audit-and-rebuild of the confirmation stage's own evidence packs (43.3% of cited (constraint, page) pairs unsupported; assembler provenance notes leaking adjudication-adjacent annotations into four packs) is a rare act of self-audit, corrected in three separated layers and followed by re-adjudication of every affected case. — see 3.2
- **S3:** The RQ1 ledger is grounded in maintainer adjudication with unusually explicit integrity checks: a fix-nature classification of every merged PR, duplicate-tracked bugs excluded from the fix column, and one previously counted true positive reclassified after the authors' own seven-version re-probe disproved it. — see 1.1, 1.2
- **S4:** Negative and boundary results are reported prominently rather than buried: the full-vs-flat separation fails to replicate on a second model family (p = 1.0, stated in the abstract), and the RQ3 head-to-head checks that the baseline harness actually ran before reading anything into its zero (the released success counters are shown to be uninformative; a per-template liveness oracle and 23,258 logged responses substitute). — see 3.1
- **S5:** The paper and its declared artifact are inspectable at a level of detail unusual for an LLM-pipeline study: both judging prompts, the fixed aggregation rule, the strategy-trigger registry, the per-run judge outputs, the ledger keys, and the analysis scripts are all named and located. — see 4.1

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The flagship full-vs-flat separation (and its Holm-corrected p = 0.029) is measured under the counting convention that the paper's own hand-adjudication shows overstates the joint system by 8 true positives (0.804 vs. 0.647); under the fairest comparison the authors can currently make (joint full vs. convention flat) the difference is p = 1.0, and the flat arm has no joint counterpart because its routed cases were never hand-adjudicated. — see 3.4
- **W2:** The Holm family is defined as "the seven paired tests in this subsection" without documented pre-specification, and the family boundary is outcome-relevant: one cross-backbone pairing that replicates is inside the family while the second-family full-vs-flat test that fails to replicate is excluded as a "sensitivity analysis"; at a ten-test family size the flagship adjusted p would be ≈0.050. — see 3.5
- **W3:** What the headline RQ2 numbers estimate is bounded on three sides at once — a submission-filtered pool from earlier pipeline generations, a direction-only anchor measured on the pre-audit packs under a different protocol, and a primary backbone on which alone the decisive pair separates. Each bound is disclosed, but the abstract's "0.804 ... against 0.588" still reads as a deployment-level contrast. — see 1.3, 3.8
- **W4:** Two internal accountings do not visibly reconcile: the decomposition sentence "16 carry no forced-driving perspective" versus forced-verdict recall of 27/51 (which implies 14 routed-only confirmations), and §3.5's "such cases lie outside every automated rate" versus §4.3's "Human-Review cases count toward Confirmed in every rate below". — see 3.6, 5.2
- **W5:** The RQ3 reverse direction — TestVDB reaching VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow — is a hand-executed, mechanism-informed probe, not an automated run; the paper states that the released integer boundary set would not have generated a failing value unaided, so the complementarity claim is measured asymmetrically. — see 3.7

### Detailed Assessment

Note on scope: as the general reviewer I ran no literature search. Claims about prior systems (VDBFuzz, MASTOR, CASCADE, Metamon, SATORI, and others) are taken from the paper's own descriptions and my judgments about novelty/related-work coverage are accordingly **provisional**. I recomputed everything that is recomputable from the text: all eight reported Wilson intervals reproduce to the printed precision, the seven confirmatory exact McNemar p-values recompute exactly (0.0072, 0.0075, 0.0414, 0.549, and the three < 0.0001 pairs), as do the separately reported sensitivity pairs (0.51, 1.0, 1.0) and decomposition pairs (0.0074, 0.688), the Holm chain reproduces (raw 0.0072 × 4 = 0.0288 → 0.029; 0.0075 × 3 = 0.0225 monotonicity-raised to 0.0288; 0.0414 × 2 = 0.0828 → 0.083), every per-run table row sums to 51 bugs and 30 false positives, the three-scoring table is internally consistent (27/29 = 0.931; 33/35 = 0.943; 41/47 = 0.872; 28/30 = 0.933 and 24/30 = 0.800 as both suppression and forced-leak arithmetic), the pack-rebuild accounting closes (2,106 − 674 − 463 = 969; 969 − 302 = 667; 667 + 7 = 674; 22 + 652 = 674; 674/2,106 = 32.0%), the strata cells close (29/37, 2/3, 10/11 = 41 convention confirmations; 3 + 4 = 7 routed joins the outright 3 to give 6/11 only if one counts as I explain in 3.6), and the RQ3 status counts sum (14,022 + 7,520 + 1,512 + 204 = 23,258). The paper's quantitative reporting is, with the one exception in 3.6, arithmetically sound.

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The problem is well established from the paper's own material: VDBMSs sit under retrieval-augmented LLM applications, their dominant defect class is functional failure without a crash (Section 2.1), the one dedicated VDBMS fuzzer is crash-oracle by design (Section 2.1), and the authors' own ledger contains only 2 crash-or-panic cases among 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs (Section 1). The running example — Qdrant #10369, a dimension-validation bypass on the `lookup_from` path — makes the class concrete from documentation sentence to confirmed fix. This is a real, clearly motivated problem.
   - **1.2** The empirical yield is the paper's strongest impact evidence: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 30 adjudicated false positives and 23 merged fix PRs across three production systems, with the fix-nature classification ("every one of the 23 modifies implementation code, 15 also add regression tests, and none is documentation-only") and label signals (50/51 carry an official `bug` label) pre-empting the natural objection that "confirmed" buys report inflation. The eight-pattern taxonomy adds a usable characterization (the acceptance family — Silent-Accept, Type-Coercion, Silent-Ignore — is 37 of 51 bugs).
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The significance claim is bounded by the human-machine framing in a way the abstract compresses. The 51/81 (63.0%) is the deployment's cumulative ledger across earlier mining rounds, "re-verified as of September 2026", and it "carries author-side screening and the submission decision" (Section 1, Section 4.2); the reported GLM-5.3-Flash run contributed none of the 81, and the automated stage's own measured profile is the convention-dependent 0.804/0.647/0.529 of Section 4.3. The paper states all of this, but a one-clause restatement in the abstract of what the 63.0% does and does not estimate would prevent readers from attributing the yield to the automated stage.
   - **1.4 [minor, fixable]** External scope is narrow and disclosed: three VDBMSs over 15 version-pinned candidates, one LLM family for all primary measurements, and — critically — the decisive full-vs-flat pair does not separate when re-run on the second family (p = 1.0), while the source-only magnitude does replicate (0.725 vs. 0.706). The paper's own conclusion ("a property of that backbone under this protocol, not a law of the protocol itself") is the right one; the consequence is that the demonstrated impact is a validated method-plus-ledger, not a validated general mechanism.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate (provisional; assessed from the paper's own positioning, without a field survey)
   - **2.1** The "anchor inversion" is a crisp, checkable delta, and the paper articulates it against the nearest neighbors rather than burying it: for MASTOR the authority is the implementation and the oracle encodes implemented behavior; for CASCADE the falsifier is code generated from the same documentation and shares its ambiguity; for Metamon the falsifier is another LLM question (Section 6). TestVDB takes the prose as ground and the implementation as falsifier, which is a genuinely different placement of authority. The oracle-candidate exclusion table (Table 1) is a useful conceptual contribution in its own right — the "where does each candidate get its expectation" axis organizes the space cleanly.
   - **2.2** The domain delta is visible: the non-crashing, documentation-consistency residual in VDBMSs has, from the paper's account, only a crash-oracle fuzzer aimed at it, and the RQ3 probe operationalizes complementarity rather than asserting it.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** From the text alone the novelty reads as a well-executed integration plus domain shift rather than a new mechanism: the paper itself concedes "A generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is therefore not new here, and cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to TestVDB" (Section 6), and the confirmation stage's components (multi-perspective review, evidence chains, source grounding) are recognizable SE patterns. That is an honest framing; the sharpest remaining delta — prose-anchored expectation with source-only falsification, measured against a flat judge — is exactly what the fragile, single-backbone comparison in Section 4.3 supports. My rating could move up or down with a survey of how close the nearest LLM-oracle systems actually are, which I did not perform.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The design is unusually self-aware for an LLM-pipeline evaluation, and it reports its own failure modes at the resolution they occurred: three independent runs per arm with majority voting and every case judged in every run; exact paired tests with discordant nets given per test; a three-layer audit/rebuild/protocol-alignment account; and a three-reading report under which the authors state plainly that "the convention overstates the joint system, forced scoring understates it, and the measured truth sits in between" (Section 4.3). Negative results are surfaced rather than buried — the second-family non-replication appears in the abstract, and the RQ3 zero is validated before it is interpreted (the released success counters are shown to be uninformative and a per-template liveness oracle substituted, with 23,258 logged responses tabulated). The mechanism claim is correctly narrowed to routing ("routing, not forced accuracy, is where the structure pays"). This is the standard of reporting I would want for this kind of study.
   - **3.2** The pack audit is a first-class contribution and is carried through rigorously: 43.3% of 134 distinct (constraint, cited-page) pairs unsupported, the four provenance-leaking packs identified and cleaned, the ten packs with inherited cognition sections removed and their affected verdicts re-adjudicated "thirteen runs across both backbones", with pre-cleanup files shipped for comparison. The two leak cases re-confirming through legitimate routes on clean packs (type-tautology; source forensics on the id-reference path) is exactly the right way to show the leak was not load-bearing.
   - **3.3** I recomputed the reported statistics and they reproduce: Wilson intervals (with the standard formula, e.g. core recall 8/51 → [0.082, 0.280]; anchor 19/32 → [0.423, 0.745]), the seven confirmatory exact McNemar p-values (core-vs-flat, core-vs-D-only, core-vs-full all < 0.0001; full-vs-flat 17/4 → 0.0072; D-only-Qwen-vs-flat 15/3 → 0.0075; D-only-vs-flat 15/5 → 0.0414; full-vs-D-only 7/4 → 0.549) as well as the separately reported sensitivity pairs (forced 0.51; joint-vs-flat 1.0; second-family 1.0) and decomposition pairs (true-positive-only 13/2 → 0.0074; leak-only 4/2 → 0.688), the Holm chain at multiplier 4 (0.0288 ≈ 0.029) and the monotonicity raise for the fifth rank, all per-run row sums, the three-scoring table, and the pack-rebuild arithmetic. The paper's numbers can be checked against its own tables and survive; that is worth saying explicitly because most of its qualitative claims ride on them.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The paper's "central comparison" (full stage vs. flat judge, Section 4.3) is statistically significant only under the deployment convention under which `Human-Review` counts as confirmed, and the authors' own joint measurement shows the convention overstates the full stage by 8 true positives (41 vs. 33). The comparison that would settle the claim under the honest scoring — joint judge-plus-human for both arms — cannot be made: "only its routed cases were hand-adjudicated, so the flat arm has no equal-treatment joint counterpart". The closest available bridge, joint full (33 TP + 2 leaks = 35 confirmed) against convention flat (30 + 4 = 34), is reported by the authors as "a joint advantage that does not reach significance on this pool (discordant 7/6, p = 1.0; confirmed sets 35 vs. 34)". Meanwhile the flat judge routed 8.6% of its verdicts (21 of 243), so its routed cases exist and could in principle be adjudicated the same way. As written, the abstract's one-line contrast (0.804 convention vs. 0.588) is accurate but privileges the layer the paper itself demonstrates to be overstated; the flat arm's own convention number is 0.588 while its forced number is 0.490, so under any scoring that penalizes routing the two judges converge. A minor related asymmetry inside the joint scoring: a routed false positive "returned for a cleaner reproduction" still counts as intercepted (suppression closes at 28/30), while a routed true bug returned as material-insufficient counts as not recalled; the two choices both favor the stage. Fixable by hand-adjudicating the flat arm's routed cases (existing materials, same human role) or by leading with the routing-mechanism claim and demoting the convention contrast.
   - **3.5 [major, fixable]** The Holm correction is presented as the flagship's license to claim significance, but the family is defined by location ("the seven paired tests in this subsection") rather than by any stated pre-registration, and the family boundary is doing work: the confirmatory set includes one cross-backbone pairing that replicates (D-only-Qwen vs. flat, adjusted 0.029) while excluding, as "sensitivity analyses outside this confirmatory family", the second-family full-vs-flat pair (p = 1.0), the forced-verdict pair (p = 0.51), and the joint-vs-flat pair (p = 1.0) — three of which sit in the same subsection and two of which are alternative scorings of the same comparison. Excluding alternative scorings is defensible; excluding the failing second-family replication while retaining the passing cross-family pairing is harder to justify without a pre-specified rule. The stakes are concrete: the flagship raw p is 0.0072, so at family size 7 its adjusted value is 0.029, but at family size 10 it is 0.0504 — just over the conventional threshold. A revision should either document the family's pre-specification (or the stopping rule that produced it) or report the adjusted values as a function of family size.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** One decomposition sentence does not reconcile. Section 4.3 states that "of the full stage's 41 majority confirmations, 7 are driven by the contract perspective, 16 by the objective-constraint perspective, 2 by the maintainer-cognition perspective, and 16 carry no forced-driving perspective at all — they confirm only because the protocol routes them to Human-Review". The three-scoring table gives forced-only recall 27/51, so at most 41 − 27 = 14 confirmations can be routed-only, and the earlier sentence in the same paragraph says 17 confirmations carry at least one Human-Review component (of which 12 are majority-routed, 2 lose their majority under forced scoring, 3 are ordinary two-to-one votes). The three numbers (17 / 16 / 14) describe overlapping but distinct sets, and I could not derive 16 from any reading consistent with the 27. A small reconciliation table (per-case vote patterns → convention / forced / joint counts) would resolve it; the headline conclusions are unaffected.
   - **3.7 [minor, fixable]** The RQ3 complementarity is measured asymmetrically: VDBFuzz is run to natural completion under its own protocol over its full released template space (205 templates, 22,540 mutations, 50.8 minutes) with a substituted per-template liveness oracle — careful work, including the demonstration that the released success counters are uninformative (a port with no server reports 100% success) — while TestVDB's reach into the crash class is "a hand-executed probe of that boundary-value class, not an end-to-end automated run", with "the probe value ... chosen with knowledge of the overflow mechanism from VDBFuzz's own published case study", and with the paper itself noting that the released integer boundary set "tops out at 65,536 ... nearly fourteen orders of magnitude below the 2^62 overflow threshold". Both disclosures are present, but the abstract's "a hand-guided probe built on TestVDB's boundary reasoning reaches VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow" should not be read (and could be phrased so as not to be read) as evidence that the pipeline would find it unaided. Either direction alone is a fair bound; the asymmetry between a fully automated negative and a human-assisted positive is a claim-strength gap.
   - **3.8 [minor, fixable]** The measured rates are pool-internal and the paper says so, but three provenance facts compound: (a) the pool is a submission-filtered subset of the pipeline's confirmed output with "no deterministic submission rule"; (b) all 81 adjudicated submissions "originate from the earlier mining rounds (Section 5)", spanning "earlier pipeline generations whose per-generation configuration we do not itemize"; and (c) the direction anchor (19/32 = 0.594, Wilson [0.423, 0.745]) "was measured on the original packs under the pre-audit protocol, so its absolute value is not comparable to the rebuilt-package numbers". Each is disclosed and the conservative-direction argument for suppression is plausible; what is missing is any estimate of pipeline-generation heterogeneity inside the 81, which is the quantity a reader needs to know how much the rebuilt-package measurement and the RQ1 ledger are about the same system. (Questions Q5.)

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate
   - **4.1** The declared artifact is unusually well specified in the text: one repository with agent role definitions and the strategy-trigger registry, per-version knowledge bases and specifications, all generated scripts with raw HTTP logs, one evidence chain per candidate, the submission ledger (system, version, issue number, adjudication status, duplicate flag for all 130 submissions), original and rebuilt packs with the audit verdict records and per-case judge outputs for every arm and run, RQ3 baseline reports with per-template logs, and the analysis scripts "that recompute every rate, interval, and paired test reported here". Appendix A prints both the full-stage and the flat-judge prompt texts. That is enough to follow how every number was produced.
   - **4.2 [minor, fixable]** The prompts printed in Appendix A are "English renderings prepared by the authors of the dispatch files actually used in the re-adjudication; the verbatim Chinese files ship in the artifact and are authoritative". Since the full-vs-flat contrast is the paper's central claim and turns on prompt content (perspective vocabulary, aggregation rule, the enumeration of objective-constraint classes), the authoritative texts should be the ones in the paper, or the rendering should be certified as complete; the appendix's own analysis (the flat judge reaches 13 of the 16 objective-constraint-driven confirmations) depends on the reader trusting the rendering.
   - **4.3 [minor, unfixable]** Reproduction of agent behavior is explicitly not expected — serving identifiers only ("GLM-5.3-Flash", "Qwen3.8-Flash", September 2026), no weights snapshots, vendor default sampling — which is honest but means the runs are not repeatable in the strict sense; the artifact ships verdict files, so re-analysis is repeatable while re-judging is not. A related soft spot: the human role in the joint reading was played by the authors, non-blind, "against their pack materials only, without consulting the issue trackers" (Section 4.3), and the 21 hand-adjudications that produce the joint 0.647/0.943 are not independently checkable beyond the shipped materials. All disclosed; none of it is repair-able by revision, but it should bound how the joint reading is cited.
   - **4.4 [minor, fixable]** I could not verify the artifact link from this review environment (the fetch to the anonymous.4open.science URL was blocked), so I judge the link as declared but unverified rather than dead or reachable. If it is live and complete as the manifest describes, this criterion would read as Excellent; the honest fix on the authors' side is only to confirm persistence at camera-ready.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1** The structure is sound and the reading path is well designed for a dense paper: the running example (Qdrant #10369) is threaded through all four stages with a worked instance per stage; the eval subsections are RQ-aligned; the limitations section is organized by validity type and is genuinely load-bearing rather than boilerplate; and the per-run tables are given in full rather than only majorities. (I reviewed the stripped LaTeX source only and could not inspect the pipeline figure itself, so I do not assess its legibility.)
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** Two statements about `Human-Review` contradict each other on their face: Section 3.5 says routed cases "lie outside every automated rate in Section 4", while Section 4.3 says "Human-Review cases count toward Confirmed in every rate below". The intent — automated (forced) rates exclude them; deployment-convention rates include them — is recoverable only after re-deriving the scoring conventions, and the word "every" makes the second sentence false for the forced rows of the three-scoring table. Rewrite one of the two.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** The ablation and independent-arm tables report only TP / FP-leaked / FN / TN; every recall, suppression, precision, interval, and p-value in the text must be recomputed by the reader (I did). Adding suppression and precision columns (and the majority rate) costs little width. Two smaller in-table issues: the contract-core rows are identical across all three runs (9/1/42/29) with the explanation only in prose, which reads as a copy error on first pass; and the second-family full/flat arms are reported in prose with no table rows at all.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** Density is the main obstacle. The abstract is one paragraph carrying roughly twenty numbers and three named scorings; the RQ2 narrative introduces, within a few pages, layers 0/1/2, five arms, three run-level verdicts, three scoring conventions, three strata, and a seven-test family. The "Process ledger of one complete run" paragraph is especially hard to follow — 119 chain verdicts, 70 defect-confirming, 96 distinct candidates, 60 defect confirmations, 19/11/5/25 gate partition, 44, five registrations — and is acknowledged as "not a complete chain of subtractions", which makes the reader wonder why the intermediate counts are reported at all. A summary figure or a one-row-per-accounting reconciliation per subsection would pay for itself.
   - **5.5 [minor, fixable]** Corralled nits, each trivially fixable: (a) "Section~3" is hard-coded in the limitations while the rest of the paper uses `\ref`; (b) the same arm is called "source-only" and "D-only" interchangeably, and the arm list introduces "the 'D-only' rows in the tables" parenthetically; (c) the false-positive candidate / False-Positive verdict / leak / pack / case disambiguation paragraph appears after first use of several of the terms; (d) the submission-ledger caption says the ledger accumulates "the earlier GLM-5.2 mining rounds plus the reported GLM-5.3-Flash run, which contributed none of the 81", which is self-cancelling as written; (e) "the surviving count numerically coincides with the endpoint-filtered count that follows" invites the reader to suspect an error that is not there — say it plainly or drop it; (f) "Qwen3.8-Flash" as a serving identifier, while presumably accurate, will read as a typo of Qwen3-8B-style naming to many readers.
