## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary
The paper targets documentation–implementation bugs in vector database management systems: cases where a system silently accepts an input or behaves in a way its natural-language API documentation does not allow (the running example is `nprobe=0` accepted while the documentation declares `[1, 16384]`). Its argument is structural: every standard oracle family anchors its expectation in a crash signal, another implementation, an output relation, a machine-checkable property, or a structured spec, so for system-level untagged prose an LLM-derived oracle is the practical option — and that imports the LLM's false-positive problem, which is what the paper's confirmation stage is built to break. The pipeline has four stages (behavioral specification extraction, test-script generation under pre-bound strategies, sandboxed execution against a Docker-pinned version, bug confirmation); confirmation splits an evidence builder from a chain auditor that cross-examines a five-section evidence chain from four perspectives (contract, physical constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition), with the implementation source as the falsification anchor.

The evaluation reports a maintainer-adjudicated ledger across Milvus, Qdrant and Weaviate (51 of 81 adjudicated submissions confirmed as real bugs, 23 fixed via merged PRs); a three-layer audit-and-rebuild of the frozen evidence packs that fed the confirmation stage, followed by a controlled re-adjudication of all 81 candidates three times per configuration across five judging arms; and a bidirectional reach probe against the crash-oracle baseline, VDBFuzz, on Qdrant. Headline numbers: the contract core suppresses 96.7% of the 30 adjudicated false positives while recalling 0.157 of the 51 true bugs; the full structured stage reaches 0.765 recall at 0.700 suppression against 0.588 for a flat single-prompt judge reading the same packs and sources; forced-verdict scoring makes the two judges indistinguishable (0.529 vs. 0.490); hand adjudication of routed cases yields a joint judge-plus-human reading of 0.647 recall at 0.917 precision. The stage's advantage over the flat judge is attributed to its mandatory routing of unresolvable cases to human review, and a three-run re-run of the pair on a second model family does not separate (p=0.58).

### Core Strengths
- **S1:** Real-world validation rather than a benchmark: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs (23 with merged fix PRs, 50 of 51 carrying the project's own `bug` label) across three production VDBMSs, with a per-case ledger re-verified as of September 2026 and a fix-nature classification showing every merged fix modifies implementation code — see 1.1.
- **S2:** Uncommon measurement discipline: the paper's own shipped evidence packs were audited, found defective (43.3% of 134 cited (constraint, page) pairs unsupported), rebuilt against version-pinned documentation, and every affected rate re-measured; the design uses three-run majority voting, Wilson intervals, an explicitly defined Holm family plus a ten-test sensitivity, and four separately handled leakage channels — two of them (an embedded cognition section in ten packs; candidate-anchored entries in the cognition materials) removed and re-adjudicated across every affected arm and run with pre-cleanup verdict files archived, one measured to zero hits, one stripped before re-adjudication — see 3.1, 4.1.
- **S3:** Verified positioning against the closest prior work: the paper's characterizations of VDBFuzz, MASTOR, SATORI, Metamon and CASCADE match what those works actually say (including MASTOR's self-stated inability to detect "requirements that are not reflected in code" and the verbatim CASCADE residual-false-positive analysis), and the anchor-inversion delta — prose as ground, implementation only as falsifier — is real — see 2.1, 2.2.
- **S4:** The RQ3 comparison is executed and bounded adversarially rather than asserted: the crash-oracle baseline is run over its full released 205-template space on the same instance, the released success/failure counters are shown to be uninformative and replaced by an instrumented liveness oracle, the reverse direction is explicitly labeled hand-guided, and an earlier explanation the released templates do not support is withdrawn in print — see 3.2.

### Core Weaknesses
- **W1:** The flagship significance claim is carried by a paired test that counts a confirmed false positive as a win. Of the 16-vs-2 discordant net behind "the structured protocol's advantage survives Holm correction", 5 are cases where the flat judge was right (it intercepted a maintainer-adjudicated non-bug) and the full stage leaked; the recall-only test does not survive correction (Holm-adjusted 0.090) and the hand-adjudicated reading is not significant (p=0.11), yet the abstract, introduction and contribution list attach the p-value to the 0.765-vs-0.588 recall gap — see 3.3, 5.2.
- **W2:** The "only an LLM reading prose can do this" framing is in tension with the paper's own decomposition: 16 of the full stage's 39 confirmations come from perspective B's seven objective constraint classes, which are violations "without contract endorsement" and read no documentation at all — excluding them drops recall to 0.451 — see 3.4, 2.4.
- **W3:** The mechanism claim is narrower than the contribution list implies. Under forced verdicts the structured protocol is statistically indistinguishable from a flat single-prompt judge (0.529 vs. 0.490, p=0.34) and under the hand-adjudicated reading the same direction does not reach significance (p=0.11); what is measured is an escalation policy (route rather than force), not better judgment, and that policy does not replicate on the second backbone (p=0.58) — see 3.3, 3.5, 2.4.
- **W4:** The 51-bug ledger measures a human–machine pipeline family, not the system the paper describes: all 81 adjudicated candidates originate from earlier mining rounds and pipeline generations whose per-generation configuration is not itemized, the reported GLM-5.3-Flash run contributed none of them, and submission was a per-candidate author decision with no deterministic rule — see 1.2, 1.3.

### Detailed Assessment
1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** [strength] §4.2 reports the submission ledger (the table captioned "Submission ledger"): 81 maintainer-adjudicated submissions across Milvus (43/29 confirmed), Qdrant (28/14) and Weaviate (10/8), 23 fixes merged, 50 of 51 confirmed bugs carrying the project's official `bug` label, and a fix-nature classification showing all 23 merged fixes modify implementation code while the classifier itself is validated against the one disproved case (Qdrant #9149, correctly identified as test-only). This is the paper's strongest asset: unlike the two VDBMS empirical studies and the one fuzzer it builds on, this is a working deployment with external adjudication, and the findings are concentrated in patterns those studies flag as hard to detect (`Silent-Accept`/`Type-Coercion`/`Silent-Ignore` = 37 of 51 bugs). The problem is real and the paper demonstrates it is addressable.
   - **1.2 [major, fixable]** The yield is not the described system's yield, and the boundary appears only inside the evaluation and limitations sections — §4.3's opening paragraph states the pool "spans earlier pipeline generations whose per-generation configuration we do not itemize" and that "the reported GLM-5.3-Flash run postdates the pool and contributes to the ledger additively"; §5's external-validity paragraph adds that "all 81 adjudicated submissions in the ledger originate from those earlier GLM-5.2 rounds, and the reported run contributed none of them"; the ledger table's caption repeats "contributed none of the 81"; and §4.2 states that "submission decisions were made per candidate by the authors after novelty screening and manual review (no deterministic submission rule)". The abstract and §1 nonetheless present "maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions" as the paper's empirical contribution without that boundary. Either move the attribution boundary into the abstract/§1, or add a per-generation breakdown so the reader can see what the described system itself produced.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The cost profile in §4.1 is computed in machine terms only (~67 active hours, ~860 sandboxed executions, ~70M input / 4.9M output tokens for the orchestrating session, a lower bound). The method's real price includes the human labour its design requires: author-side screening of candidates, the submission decision, and the hand adjudication of routed cases — 21 full-stage and 11 flat-stage cases in §4.3. Since routing is the mechanism the paper credits (see 3.3), human hours are part of the contribution's cost and should appear next to the token counts.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** [strength] I checked the delta the paper claims in §6 ("Oracles derived from structured sources") and Table 1 row 5 against its declared closest competitor, MASTOR (stems in `background.md`): MASTOR's oracles are grounded in implementation source, its OAS is used only as an endpoint list, and its own limitations state it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code". The paper's "anchor inversion" claim — MASTOR's authority is the implementation, TestVDB's is the prose, with the implementation used only as falsifier — holds as stated, and the paper correctly concedes that a second-LLM review of generated oracles (MASTOR's ChallengerAgent) and cross-request behavioural oracles are not new here.
   - **2.2** [strength] The other central characterizations — Table 1 rows 1, 5, 6 and the corresponding paragraphs of §6 ("VDBMS testing", "Oracles derived from structured sources", "LLM-derived oracles and judge reliability") — are also accurate against the works themselves: SATORI is a static, per-response-field OAS oracle generator (its real bugs include response-side spec mismatches — exactly the "subset of our residual whose prose lives in schema descriptions" the paper claims); Metamon's judge is the LLM itself, stabilized by metamorphic assertion-negation queries (its quoted precision 0.722 / recall 0.480 on 9,482 pairs matches); CASCADE's falsifier is a second implementation regenerated from the same documentation, and the paper's quotation of CASCADE's residual-false-positive cause is verbatim; VDBFuzz is crash-oracle by construction and its Qdrant bug-table row reads "OOM, FPE, DoS" as the paper states. Nothing here is mischaracterized.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** Six scoped coverage searches (logged in `background.md`) surfaced no uncited work closer than the cited set; the VDBMS field beyond VDBFuzz/the bug study/the roadmap appears empty of dedicated defect-finding tools, which supports the paper's "the one dedicated VDBMS fuzzer" claim. Two adjacent families are one line short: the LLM-era code-comment-inconsistency detectors (DocChecker, EACL 2024 demo; RustC4++, TOSEM 2026) are absent while their older relatives (iComment, DocRef, DocPrism) are cited — they fall inside the row-6 delimitation, so the delta is unaffected — and the DBMS logic-bug line is cited through its techniques (NoREC, TLP, PQS, DQE) rather than its frameworks. Neither needs a rewrite; one sentence in §6 suffices.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** The novelty attached to the confirmation protocol should be resized to what is demonstrated. Appendix A's preamble shows the flat judge shares the protocol's red lines and four of the seven objective constraint classes, and reports that the flat judge "reaches majority confirmation on 13 of the 16 objective-constraint-driven confirmations the full stage makes" — so the experiment isolates the four-perspective organization and the aggregation rule, not the judgment content. The demonstrated novelty is therefore (i) the anchor inversion, (ii) the evidence-chain audit with source falsification, and (iii) mandatory escalation in place of forced closure. Claim (iii) is real and measured; claims (i) and (ii) are conceptual and verified above; but the paper should not let the reader infer that the structured protocol's judgments are more accurate — its own forced-verdict result (3.3) says otherwise.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** [strength] The measurement design is unusually rigorous, and the paper's willingness to audit its own instrument is the reason I rate this criterion Adequate rather than Weak. §4.3 documents a three-layer correction: an audit of the shipped packs (43.3% of 134 distinct (constraint, page) pairs unsupported; 32% of rows addressing an unrelated endpoint; four packs leaking adjudication-adjacent annotations), a rebuild from version-pinned documentation (674 surviving constraint rows, 22 of them `explicit`), and a protocol alignment that removed two pre-deployment rules "that suppressed recall by construction". The evaluation then runs five configurations, three runs each, over the full pool with majority voting; reports Wilson intervals; defines the Holm family explicitly ("the seven confirmation-arm pairs on the primary pool") and bounds the selection with a ten-test sensitivity (flagship adjusted 0.0092); and reports per-perspective attribution. §5 also enumerates four judgment-side leakage channels and treats them according to what each turned out to be: the expectation-framing channel was measured to zero hits ("zero expectation framing was added by assembly, so no sanitization was applied and none was needed"), the observation-leakage channel's four provenance notes were removed before re-adjudication, and the two remaining channels — an embedded maintainer-cognition section found in ten of the 81 rebuilt packs, and candidate-anchored entries inside the cognition materials themselves — were each removed and every affected case re-adjudicated in every affected arm and run on both backbones, with the pre-cleanup verdict files shipped for comparison. The discipline I am crediting is that the two channels requiring cleanup were cleaned and re-measured rather than merely disclosed; it does not extend uniformly to all four, since two needed no cleanup. I could not falsify any arithmetic in the tables — the ablation table ("Confirmation-stage ablation over the 81-candidate pool"), the independent-arms table, the three-scorings table and the two percentage statements (19.8% = 48/243 routed; 8.6% = 21/243) are mutually consistent.
   - **3.2** [strength] §4.4 handles the baseline comparison the way I would want: the systematic direction is a live run of VDBFuzz's full released Qdrant template set (205 templates, 22,540 mutations, 23,258 HTTP responses, 50.8 minutes) on the same v1.18.0 instance the paper's defects were found on, with the released success/failure counters demonstrated to be uninformative (the runner reports 100% success against a dead port) and an instrumented liveness oracle substituted; the mutation space's inability to construct the overflow is verified against released defaults (`65,536` integer boundary, `10,000` dimension candidates) and the crash is reproduced by hand with the recovered-panic semantics distinguished from service death. The reverse direction is labeled hand-guided, and the paper states plainly that it did not measure whether its automated boundary strategy would generate a value in the failing range unaided. This is adversarial self-checking, not baseline theatre.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The flagship comparison's test statistic does not measure the quantity the headline asserts. In §4.3 (the paragraph "Structure is a measured mechanism---and the decomposition locates it") the paired test "pairs the two arms' confirmed sets (Confirmed or Human-Review under the convention above, so a `leak` counts as confirmed on both sides)": the discordant net of 14 (48 vs. 34) is decomposed in the same paragraph into +9 true positives and +5 "additional leakage", and the paper itself notes that the true-positive-only exact McNemar (11/2, raw p=0.0225) does not survive correction (Holm-adjusted 0.090) and that "the flagship's Holm survival therefore rests on the confirmed-set basis — the unit the deployment resolves". Treating a confirmed non-bug as a win for the arm that produced it is not a valid superiority measure: in the deployment, an extra leak is a false report, and the arm with the worse precision (0.812 vs. 0.882) is rewarded for confirming more. The framing then travels upward: the Abstract states that the 0.765-vs-0.588 "separation survives Holm correction under the deployment convention", and §1's contribution 2 puts it most sharply — "it recalls 0.765 where a flat single-prompt judge reaches 0.588 ($p{=}0.0013$, Holm-corrected $0.0052$)" — attaching a p-value from a different statistic to a recall gap. Fix: wherever the flagship is stated, report the recall-only and leak-only components beside the p-value, and describe the surviving test as what it is (a confirmed-set difference that includes additional leakage).
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The oracle-forcing argument and the system's actual recall source pull in opposite directions, and the tension is never reconciled where the argument is made. §1 and the Abstract claim that the documentation-implementation residual leaves "an LLM as the practical oracle" because only row 7 of Table 1 anchors in system-level untagged prose; §2.3 repeats it. But §3.5 defines perspective B as seven objective constraint classes that "constitute violations without contract endorsement" — "a limit parameter accepting zero is an objective violation even if no contract row states a bound" — and §4.3 attributes 16 of the full stage's 39 majority confirmations to B, with §5 recording that excluding them lowers recall to 0.451. Those classes read no documentation; a deterministic implementation of the numeric-lower-bound, enum-closure, mutual-exclusion and type-tautology checks would reach them. The paper should state the marginal contribution of the prose-derived oracle explicitly (contract core 0.157; full-minus-B 0.451; the difference is what the LLM's prose reading buys) next to the Table 1 argument, rather than leaving the reader to compute it from two sections.
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** Stability is reported but not interpreted. §4.3 gives the full stage's three-run verdict agreement as 67.9% (55 of 81), against 96.3% for the pack-only core; the per-run rows of the ablation table ("Confirmation-stage ablation over the 81-candidate pool") show true positives of 40/38/35 and leaks of 8/9/11 across the full-stage runs, so a single run can sit at recall 0.686 with 11 leaks while the reported operating point is a majority vote. The flat arm's own three-run agreement is not reported anywhere, so the reader cannot tell whether the comparison of majorities compares equally noisy estimators. Add the flat arm's agreement and one sentence on what a user who runs the stage once should expect.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** The RQ2 pool mixes in-class and out-of-class bugs. §4.2 states that the five `Result-Incorrectness` bugs "sit outside the assertion framework by construction ... so they entered the ledger on maintainer adjudication alone", and §4.3 reports that four of the full stage's ten misses are exactly that family whose packs quote the documentation's own disclaimer. They are still five of the 51 in every recall denominator. Reporting recall both over all 51 and over the class the method targets would keep the headline recall from being partly a measure of behavior the documentation disclaims.

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate
   - **4.1** [strength] The Data Availability section declares a replication package with an unusually complete manifest: agent role definitions and the strategy-trigger registry, per-version knowledge bases and specifications, all generated scripts with raw HTTP logs, one evidence chain per candidate, the submission ledger, the original and rebuilt RQ2 packs with pack-audit verdict records and per-case judge outputs for every arm and run, the RQ3 baseline reports with per-template logs, and analysis scripts that recompute every rate, interval and paired test. Non-determinism is handled honestly ("we report these serving identifiers and run dates rather than an immutable weights snapshot, so exact reproduction of agent behavior is not expected").
   - **4.2 [minor, fixable]** Reachability of `https://anonymous.4open.science/r/TestVDB_artifact-EC36/` could not be confirmed from my review environment (the fetch was blocked by network policy, not refused by the host), so I judge this criterion from the declaration and the manifest. The authors should re-check the link immediately before submission and mirror the package.
   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** Three items that the text's argument depends on are not in the declared manifest: (i) the hand adjudications of routed cases that produce the joint reading (§4.3, 21 full-stage and 11 flat-stage cases) — these are the paper's closest approximation to the deployment's ground truth and are described only in prose; (ii) the maintainer-cognition files and their cleaned variants — §5 says the cleaned pool's numbers depend on removing candidate-anchored entries from them, and the artifact list does not mention them; (iii) the per-generation configuration of the mining rounds behind the ledger, which §4.3 explicitly says is not itemized. None of these is fatal, but each is a place where a reader cannot currently check the paper's own account.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1 [minor, fixable]** Figure 1 (the pipeline figure) contradicts the text on the paper's central mechanism: its legend reads "4 perspectives: A contract · B physical · C cognition · D source", while §3.5 and Appendix A define C as behavioral elegance and D as maintainer cognition, with source grounding as the falsification anchor rather than a perspective. The figure's verdict panel likewise mixes perspective-level values with the final verdict ("Refuted", "Neutral → require human review") where the paper's three-valued verdict is Confirmed / False-Positive / Human-Review, and it omits the aggregation rule. Since the four perspectives are what the contribution claims, the figure must be relabeled.

Splitting these and naming each statistic explicitly would also make 3.3 easier to fix.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** The "Submission ledger" table's caption reads "accumulated across the earlier GLM-5.2 mining rounds plus the reported GLM-5.3-Flash run, which contributed none of the 81" — the clause contradicts itself as written; the reported run should be excluded from the accumulation clause.

### Questions for Authors

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector DBMSs: inputs a system silently accepts, or behaviors it silently produces, that contradict its natural-language API documentation. It argues this residual is out of reach of every deterministic oracle family, because those anchor expectations in crash signals, reference implementations, output relations, machine-checkable properties, schemas, or method-/parameter-level prose, whereas the constraint at issue lives only in system-level untagged prose; an LLM-derived oracle is therefore the practical option, and it imports the LLM false-positive problem. TestVDB answers with a four-stage pipeline: documentation is crawled and distilled into typed, source-verified constraints; pre-bound strategies and three attack agents turn constraints into executable probes; a Docker-pinned sandbox runs every probe and logs raw HTTP; and a confirmation stage has an evidence builder assemble a five-section evidence chain per candidate and a chain auditor cross-examine it (four mechanical checks and four perspectives — contract, objective physical constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition), with the implementation source as the falsification anchor and a three-valued verdict that routes unresolvable cases to human review.

The evaluation has three parts. RQ1 reports a maintainer-adjudicated submission ledger on Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate: 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions confirmed as real bugs, 23 fixed via merged PRs, with a per-case ledger and a fix-nature classification. RQ2 re-adjudicates that pool (51 bugs, 30 maintainer-rejected false positives) over rebuilt, version-pinned evidence packs in a five-arm, three-run design — contract core, full stage, source-only, a flat single-prompt judge reading the same packs and sources, and a second-backbone source-only arm — reporting recall and suppression under three scoring conventions (forced verdicts, a joint judge-plus-human reading, and a deployment convention that counts human-review cases as confirmed). RQ3 probes the crash-oracle baseline VDBFuzz bidirectionally on Qdrant and finds complementary reach. The measured picture: the structured stage recalls 0.765 at 0.700 suppression under the deployment convention against 0.588 for the flat judge (Holm-corrected p = 0.0052); under forced verdicts the two are indistinguishable (0.529 vs. 0.490); and a three-run rerun of the pair on a second model family does not separate (p = 0.58).

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The novelty positioning was independently verified against the five closest named competitors — VDBFuzz, MASTOR, SATORI, Metamon, and CASCADE — and every characterization the paper makes of them matches what those works actually say; the claimed anchor-inversion delta holds. — see 2.1, 2.2
- **S2:** A self-audited measurement design: the authors found their own evidence packs defective, rebuilt all 81, removed three separate leakage channels, re-ran every affected verdict set, and report all three scoring readings plus the negative second-backbone result. — see 3.1, 3.2
- **S3:** A real, adjudicated yield: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs and 23 implementation fixes across three production systems, with duplicate-tracked entries handled as an orthogonal flag and a fix-nature classifier guarding against fix-count inflation. — see 1.1
- **S4:** The RQ3 comparison is measured, not asserted: the released harness counters are shown uninformative, a substituted liveness oracle is used, the zero is bounded from two directions, and an earlier explanation is explicitly withdrawn. — see 3.4
- **S5:** Verifiability: a declared, comprehensive artifact (rebuilt packs, per-case judge outputs for every arm and run, audit records, the submission ledger, RQ3 per-template logs, and scripts that recompute every rate) plus both judging prompts in the appendix. — see 4.1, 4.2

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The paper's central comparison (full stage vs. flat judge) is convention-bound and backbone-bound: the separation exists only where human-review routing counts as confirmation, is carried entirely by that routing channel rather than by forced accuracy, and does not replicate on the second model family. All of this is disclosed, but the abstract, intro, and contribution bullet lead with the convention-based number. — see 3.5
- **W2:** The human-review labor and the judge-side compute that the routing channel and the structured protocol require are not quantified, so the deployment impact claim lacks its cost half. — see 1.3, 3.8
- **W3:** An internal count inconsistency in the RQ2 miss analysis: "The ten misses" decomposes 10 false negatives, while Table 4 reports 12 for the same arm. — see 3.6
- **W4:** The submission-filter anchor compares the stage's own confirmation rate (0.594) against maintainers' adjudication rate (63.0%); the symmetric stage-side comparison is never reported. — see 3.7

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** [strength] The problem is sized with evidence rather than asserted, and the yield is the strongest kind of impact evidence for a bug-finding paper. §2.1 (`subsec:bugs`) grounds the non-crashing majority in the cited empirical studies — I checked Xie et al.'s VDBMS bug study in the shared cache and the paper's characterization is accurate (functional failures 57.3% vs. crashes 15.1%) — and the class is anchored in a concrete instance (Milvus documents `nprobe` in [1, 16384] yet the search API accepts `nprobe=0` with HTTP 200, issue #49823). Table 2 (`tab:yield`) then reports 51/81 maintainer-confirmed submissions with 23 merged fixes, and the fix-nature paragraph in §4.2 shows every one of the 23 fix PRs modifies implementation code with none documentation-only. A 51-bug, 23-fix campaign across three production systems is a clear real-world result.
   - **1.2** [strength] The paper repeatedly scopes its claims to what the evidence supports and withdraws readings it cannot back. §5 (`sec:limitations`, "Internal validity: judgment-side threats") states the headline yield "measures the human--machine pipeline, not the automated stage's end-to-end precision or recall"; the three scoring readings are reported side by side (Table 6, `tab:scorings`) rather than choosing the flattering one; and §4.4 (`subsec:rq3`) withdraws an earlier explanation of VDBFuzz's silence ("three released templates do issue `wait=false` upserts, so that explanation does not hold and we withdraw it") while noting it did not measure which template paths reach a zero-length vector. This kind of self-correction raises the credibility of the claims that remain.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The deployment assumptions that carry the impact claim are not costed. The 51-bug yield depends on author-side novelty screening and the submission decision (§4.2: "submission decisions were made per candidate by the authors after novelty screening and manual review (no deterministic submission rule)"), and the recall advantage that the paper claims for the structured protocol depends on the human-review channel — 19.8% of full-stage verdicts (§4.3), whose designated human role the authors played themselves. §4.1 (`subsec:setup`) gives the mining run's cost profile (~67 active hours, ~860 executions, ~70M input tokens) but no cost for the human channel or for the judging arms. Without even a coarse labor accounting, a reader cannot tell whether the deployment's recall advantage over a flat judge survives its cost; state the missing half or narrow the impact claim.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** [strength] I fetched and read the five closest competitors the paper names, and its characterization of each matches the source; no mischaracterization found. VDBFuzz (ICSE 2026, `vdbfuzz26`): crash-oracle by construction, and it self-states that it "only performs fuzzing for crash-related bugs" — so Table 1 (`tab:exclusion`, row 1) and the RQ3 framing are correct, and the complementarity claim (silent accepts cannot fire a crash oracle) follows. ## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets silent logic bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where the system silently accepts an input or produces a behavior that violates its natural-language API documentation, such as Milvus accepting `nprobe=0` when the documentation declares the range [1, 16384]. Its argument is that every deterministic-oracle candidate anchors its expectation somewhere other than system-level prose (Table 1), which leaves an LLM-derived oracle as the practical mechanism for this residual and imports the false-positive problem that comes with it. TestVDB is presented as a four-stage pipeline: documentation is crawled and distilled into source-verified behavioral specifications (§3.2); pre-bound strategies drive three attack agents that turn constraints into executable probes (§3.3); a Docker-pinned sandbox executes them and logs raw HTTP exchanges (§3.4); and a confirmation stage splits an evidence builder — documentation, execution output, implementation source — from a chain auditor that applies four mechanical checks and four perspectives (contract, objective physical constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition) under a fixed aggregation producing Confirmed / False-Positive / Human-Review (§3.5). A running example (Qdrant #10369) threads through all four stages.

The evaluation has three parts. RQ1 (§4.2) reports a maintainer-adjudicated submission ledger across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate: of 81 adjudicated submissions, 51 were confirmed as real bugs, 23 fixed via merged PRs, and 30 adjudicated as false positives (Table 2), with eight named bug patterns partitioning the 51 (Table 3). RQ2 (§4.3) audits the frozen evidence packs that fed the confirmation stage (43.3% of cited constraint–page pairs unsupported), rebuilds them against version-pinned documentation, and re-adjudicates the full 81-case pool three times per configuration across five arms: contract core, full stage, source-only, a flat single-prompt judge, and a source-only arm on a second model family. Under the deployment convention (Human-Review counts as confirmed) the contract core reaches 0.157 recall at 0.967 suppression, the full stage 0.765 at 0.700, and the flat judge 0.588 at 0.867 (Tables 4–6); the full-vs-flat difference survives Holm correction on the primary backbone but a three-run re-run on the second family does not separate (p=0.58), and a decomposition reports that under forced verdicts the two judges are indistinguishable (0.529 vs. 0.490) while a hand-adjudicated joint reading sits in between (0.647 recall, 0.917 precision). RQ3 (§4.4) runs VDBFuzz on the same Qdrant v1.18.0 instance (205 templates, 22,540 mutations) with zero recorded anomalies and none of the confirmed defects reached, and shows in the reverse direction a hand-guided TestVDB-style boundary probe reaching VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow on Qdrant v1.4.0.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Deployed, externally validated bug yield — 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs and 23 merged fixes across three production systems, with the fix count itself audited (every fix PR modifies implementation code; the classifier is sanity-checked on the disproved #9149). — see 1.1
- **S2:** The oracle-anchoring criterion (where each candidate family gets its expectation) turns "why does this need an LLM?" into a structural argument rather than an assertion, and RQ3 makes the complementarity claim concrete on a real instance. — see 1.2
- **S3:** An evaluation that audits its own inputs: pack audit, rebuild from version-pinned docs, protocol alignment, and four leakage channels surfaced and assessed, the last two cleaned with re-adjudication and pre-cleanup verdict files archived. — see 3.1, 4.1, 4.2
- **S4:** The internal accounting is reproducible: I re-derived every Wilson interval, exact McNemar p-value, Holm multiplier, confirmed-set difference and stratum sum I could reach from the tables, and only two places failed the check (3.7, 3.8). — see 3.2
- **S5:** Unusual candour in the claims themselves — three scoring readings reported side by side, the second-family non-replication stated in the abstract, and the RQ3 footnote conceding that the released baseline cannot construct the crash it reports. — see 2.1, 3.9

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The flagship "separation survives Holm correction" is computed on confirmed sets that count five additional confirmed false positives as part of the advantage; the recall-only comparison does not survive correction (Holm 0.090). — see 3.3
- **W2:** The full-vs-flat separation fails on the second backbone (p=0.58) and routing rates halve with it (19.8% → 10.3%), leaving the central comparative claim standing on one model family. — see 3.4
- **W3:** The full protocol is statistically indistinguishable from its own source-forensics component (39/51 vs. 36/51, p=0.39), and the paper does not draw the implication for contribution 2. — see 3.5
- **W4:** Two accounting checks fail: the "ten misses" decomposition (4+2+4) against Table 4's FN = 12, and the flat arm's joint row (29/51 at 29/30) against its 11 routed cases and 6/4/1 outcomes. — see 3.7, 3.8
- **W5:** The joint reading — one of the three headline numbers — depends on non-blind author adjudication and uses an asymmetric rule for returned cases (returned false positive counts as intercepted; returned true positive is dropped from recall). — see 3.6

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The RQ1 ledger is the paper's strongest asset. §4.2 and Table 2 (`tab:yield`) report 81 adjudicated submissions (Milvus 43, Qdrant 28, Weaviate 10) yielding 51 confirmed (29/14/8), 23 fixed, and 30 false positives, with the 51 further split into 23 fixed / 18 open with acknowledgment / 7 closed without fix / 3 duplicate-tracked, and with per-project acceptance signals (50 of 51 carry the project's `bug` label; 25 of 29 Milvus bugs carry `triage/accepted`). The fix count is then validated rather than assumed: every merged fix PR is classified by changed files, all 23 modify implementation code, 15 add regression tests, none is documentation-only, and the classifier is sanity-checked against the independently disproved #9149 (it flags that PR as test-only). Bugs confirmed and fixed by the projects themselves are the clearest external validation a testing paper can offer, and this is done with more care than is typical.
   - **1.2** Table 1 (`tab:exclusion`) contributes a reusable analytical frame: instead of asserting that existing oracles cannot reach this bug class, it organizes each candidate family (crash, differential, metamorphic, property/schema, structured-spec-derived, doc-derived) by where its expectation is anchored, and §3.5 supplies the constructive counterpart — the implementation source as a falsifier that is independent of the documentation the specification was read from. RQ3 (§4.4) then makes the complementarity concrete on one instance (VDBFuzz's full template set against the Qdrant v1.18.0 instance where the silent-accept defects are live) rather than asserting it from a sampled probe.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The yield's attribution is bounded in ways the paper discloses but does not consolidate. The ledger "measures the human–machine pipeline rather than the automated stage alone" (abstract, §4.2); it is "cumulative across mining rounds and versions since early 2026" whose per-generation configuration "we do not itemize" (§4.3), with the reported run contributing none of the 81; and the 81-candidate pool is a per-candidate author decision with "no deterministic submission rule" (§4.2, §5). A reader cannot therefore attribute the 51/23 yield to the reported pipeline configuration. Fix: one sentence in §4.2 stating what the reported run contributed and what earlier rounds contributed, or a restatement of the contribution as the program's yield rather than the pipeline's.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate (provisional: no literature survey was performed for this review; assessed from the paper's own positioning and general knowledge, and a surveyed reviewer should check the exclusivity claim against the cited neighbours)
   - **2.1** The anchor inversion is a legible delta, and §6 positions it precisely: prior documentation-derived oracles keep the derived oracle as the final arbiter (with Konstantinou et al. cited for the resulting actual-versus-expected gap), while TestVDB takes the prose as ground and the implementation only as the falsifier. The related-work section names its nearest neighbours and concedes what is *not* new — MASTOR's second-LLM oracle review and cross-request behavioural oracles, CASCADE's code-vs-tests cross-check, Metamon's LLM-judged regression oracles, SATORI's response-field oracles — which is exactly what a novelty claim should look like. (I did not fetch or verify any of these works; my evidence here is the paper's characterization of them.)
   - **2.2** The confirmation construction — a five-section evidence chain, four mechanical chain checks, four perspectives including objective-constraint classes that confirm without contract endorsement, and a fixed aggregation with an explicit routing channel — is recognizably new as an assembly even though every part has antecedents, and the three-valued verdict with routing is the component the measurements actually support (§3.5, Appendix A).
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** The exclusivity claim is analytic rather than experimental. The abstract and §1 state that "no deterministic-oracle family derives its expectation from that behavioral prose," while Table 1's own caption concedes that rows 2–6 are "argued from each family's anchoring mechanism" and only rows 1 and 7 are exercised anywhere in the paper. The argument is plausible, but it rests on the paper's taxonomy of what each family can express. Fix: hedge the framing to "in our analysis each family anchors elsewhere," or add one worked attempt to build a competing oracle for a specific case — the Doc-Spec-Gap bug (Table 3, Qdrant #9942, where the schema declares a minimum but omits the maximum that the bug violates) is a natural candidate for asking whether a schema-based oracle could in fact reach it.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** What survives correction as "the mechanism" is routing: a protocol rule that mandates escalation, measured on one backbone, with forced-verdict accuracy statistically indistinguishable between the structured and flat judges by the paper's own decomposition (§4.3). The delta over prior work is therefore real but modest at the level of the measured mechanism. A repositioning that foregrounds the anchor inversion, the assertion-framework floor (0.157/0.967) and the audit methodology — rather than the full-vs-flat pair — would present the contribution more accurately.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The correction discipline in §4.3 is unusual and deserves credit. The authors audited the originally assembled packs (43.3% of 134 distinct (constraint, page) pairs unsupported; a further two over-strong; 32% of rows addressing an unrelated endpoint; four packs carrying assembler provenance annotations), traced every defect to the assembler rather than the confirmation stage, rebuilt all 81 packs from version-pinned documentation and the tested version's own OpenAPI artifact (674 rows surviving from 2,106; 21 packs left with no contract row for their endpoint), and then discovered and fixed a second problem: the first re-adjudication protocol still carried two pre-deployment rules that "suppressed recall by construction." Four leakage channels are surfaced and assessed rather than merely disclosed, and each is handled differently: expectation framing was measured and found not to be operating (a mechanical scan found zero expectation-framed sentences added by assembly, so no sanitization was applied and none was needed); the four assembler provenance notes were removed before any re-adjudication, so the reported rates are exposed to neither channel (the operational ledger ran with both live); and the two channels that did contaminate the re-adjudication — embedded cognition sections in ten packs, and candidate-anchored cognition entries referencing seven pool cases — were cleaned with the affected cases re-adjudicated in every affected arm and run, the affected verdicts replaced, and the pre-cleanup verdict files archived for comparison. For a bug-finding paper, self-auditing to this depth is rare.
   - **3.2** The arithmetic holds up under checking, which materially raises my confidence in the results. I re-derived: all Wilson intervals (8/51 → [0.082, 0.280]; 29/30 → [0.833, 0.994]; 39/51 → [0.632, 0.860]; 21/30 → [0.521, 0.833]; 30/51 → [0.452, 0.712]; 37/51 → [0.591, 0.829]; 19/32 → [0.423, 0.745]); all reported exact McNemar p-values (0/25, 1/40, 2/37 < 0.0001; 16/2 → 0.0013; 11/2 → 0.0225; 15/3 → 0.0075; 15/5 → 0.041; 8/4 → 0.39; 7/3 → 0.34; 8/2 → 0.11; 8/5 → 0.58); the Holm multipliers (fourth-smallest ×4 = 0.0052; fifth ×3 = 0.023; the ten-test maximal family ×7 = 0.0092); the confirmed-set and discordant-pair consistency (48 vs. 34 = 16−2; TP-only 11/2; leak-only 5/0; joint 36 vs. 30 = 8−2); the strata sums (29+2+8 = 39; 24+0+3 = 27; 26+1+6 = 33; core 8+0+0 = 8); and the ledger splits (51 = 23+18+7+3; 30 = 27+3; 25+8+5+4+3+2+1+3 = 51). Only 3.7 and 3.8 below fail this check — everything else I tried reproduces exactly.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The flagship significance test counts confirmed false positives as part of the "separation." §4.3 states that the full-vs-flat comparison pairs the two arms' confirmed sets, "so a 'leak' counts as confirmed on both sides": full 48 (39 TP + 9 leaks) vs. flat 34 (30 + 4), discordant 16/2, p=0.0013, Holm 0.0052. The authors decompose the net themselves — "+9 of that net is true positives and +5 is additional leakage" — and state that survival "rests on the confirmed-set basis … rather than on the recall-only comparison alone," where the recall-only test is 11/2, raw p=0.0225, Holm-adjusted 0.090 (does not survive). So five of the sixteen "wins" are cases where the full stage confirmed a false positive that the flat judge correctly rejected, and a judge can inflate this unit by confirming more cases regardless of correctness — in tension with the paper's own purpose of intercepting false positives. The abstract's "That separation survives Holm correction" will be read as a claim about the recall advantage (0.765 vs. 0.588), which is precisely what does not survive. Fix: make the recall-only (or a precision-penalized) comparison the primary claim and report 0.090 plainly, or state in the abstract that the surviving separation is on the confirmed-set unit and explicitly includes five additional false-positive confirmations.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The flagship separation is a single-backbone result whose replication attempt fails, and it varies with a quantity the paper reports: §4.3 shows the second-family full stage at 0.588 recall / 0.833 suppression versus the flat judge's 0.529 / 0.833 (discordant 8/5, p=0.58; forced 0.510 vs. 0.451, p=0.75), with routing rates dropping from 19.8% to 10.3% (full) and 7.8% (flat). The paper is explicit that the routing advantage is "a property of that backbone under this protocol, not a law of the protocol itself," and reports the null prominently in the abstract — that is the right treatment. It nevertheless leaves the central comparative claim on one family. Fixable either by a third family, or better, by a routing-rate-conditioned analysis across both backbones: if the separation tracks the routing rate, the anomaly becomes evidence *for* the mechanism rather than against the protocol.
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** The decomposition shows the structured protocol is statistically indistinguishable from its own source-forensics component, and the paper does not draw the implication for contribution 2. §4.3 concludes "the ordering resolves cleanly: core < flat < D-only ≈ full," with full-vs-D-only at p=0.39; the source-only arm reaches 36/51 (0.706) at 0.733 suppression without the four perspectives, without the cognition materials, and without the aggregation rule. Moreover the arm is defined as "its dispatch's four source verdicts mapped mechanically to final verdicts," and the text never says whether it can emit Human-Review — if it cannot, its forced and convention scores coincide (0.706/0.733), which would put the full protocol's forced score (0.529) *below* its own source component. Either way, contribution 2 as written claims more for the four-perspective protocol than the numbers show. Fix: one explicit paragraph on what the perspectives and aggregation add over source forensics alone, plus the verdict-mapping table in the artifact.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** The joint reading — one of the three headline numbers in the abstract — depends on non-blind author adjudication and uses an asymmetric rule for returned cases. §4.3 states the authors adjudicated both arms' routed cases "without consulting the issue trackers, playing the channel's designated human role (non-blind; disclosed)," and that "a returned case is never adjudicated a false positive, so it still counts as intercepted in the joint system and the joint suppression closes at 27/30." On the recall side the same logic is not applied: the two returned true positives are dropped (39 − 4 rejected − 2 returned = 33/51). The three statements cannot all hold.
