# TestVDB — Three-Reviewer Peer Review (2026-09-13, round 10; paper rev: post-cognition-cleanup clean pool — all RQ2 numbers re-derived, contamination handling rewritten as strip-and-rejudge)

## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): silent behaviors — accepted out-of-range parameters, ignored fields, stale state couplings — that violate the system's own natural-language API documentation and produce no crash, hence escape the crash-oracle fuzzer VDBFuzz. The authors argue (Table 1) that every deterministic oracle family anchors its expectation in a signal or structured source other than system-level prose, forcing an LLM-derived oracle and importing its false-positive problem. Their pipeline, TestVDB, extracts behavioral specifications from documentation, generates probes under pre-bound strategies, executes them against Docker-pinned instances, and splits bug confirmation into an evidence builder and a chain auditor cross-examined from four perspectives, with the implementation source as the falsification anchor. On Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs and fixed 23 via merged PRs. A controlled five-arm re-adjudication of the 81-candidate pool over audited and rebuilt evidence packages finds the contract-only core at 0.157 recall / 0.967 suppression, the structured full stage at 0.804 / 0.800, and a flat single-prompt judge at 0.588 / 0.867; the full-vs-flat separation survives Holm correction (p=0.029), and a decomposition attributes the separation to the structured protocol's human-review routing channel rather than to forced-verdict accuracy, which the two judges share (0.529 vs 0.490, p=0.51). A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz on Qdrant v1.18.0 shows complementary reach.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Real-world validation is unusually strong: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs with 23 merged fix PRs across three production systems, with the fix classifier itself validated against the #9149 disproof — see 1.1, 3.5.
- **S2:** The measurement turned its own weakness into evidence: a three-layer audit/rebuild/re-adjudication design, plus removal and re-judging of ten contaminated packs across thirteen runs, with pre-cleanup files shipped — see 3.2, 3.3.
- **S3:** The novelty delta against the closest competitors is real and the paper's characterizations of them are accurate where I verified them against source (MASTOR, Metamon, CASCADE, SATORI, AGORA+, MASTEST, VDBFuzz) — see 2.1, 2.2, 2.3.
- **S4:** Statistical rigor and negative-result hygiene: exact McNemar with Holm correction across a declared seven-test family (every raw p, ordering, and multiplier reconciles under my recomputation), Wilson intervals throughout, and an RQ3 that validates the baseline's zero rather than trusting it — see 3.4, 3.5.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The central mechanism claim — the structured protocol beats a flat judge with the same evidence — is measured on one backbone, its separation is carried by the routing convention rather than forced accuracy, and a single-run re-run of the pair on a second family does not separate (p=0.81); the mechanism's generality is the load-bearing part of the contribution — see 3.1, 3.6.
- **W2:** The headline comparison (0.804 vs 0.588) holds only under the Human-Review-counts-as-Confirmed convention that the paper's own adjudication shows overstates the joint system (0.647 recall); the decision-relevant joint-vs-flat comparison (0.647 vs 0.588, discordant 7/6, p=1.0) is not significant and is buried in body text — see 3.1, 3.6.
- **W3:** Ground truth is fallible maintainer adjudication over a submission-filtered, partially unadjudicated pool (49 excluded), and 4 of the full stage's 10 misses are ground-truth-adjudication disagreements the paper cannot itself resolve — inherent, disclosed, but it bounds how hard the precision/recall numbers can be read — see 3.7.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The submission ledger (Table 2, Section RQ1) reports 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions confirmed by maintainers (63.0%), 23 fixed via merged PRs, with a changed-file classification showing all 23 fixes touch implementation code (15 add regression tests, none documentation-only). The classifier was itself validated on the disproved #9149, whose fix PR it correctly flags test-only. This is genuine, checkable impact on three production systems that back RAG applications.
   - **1.2 [minor, unfixable]** Impact is bounded by scope the paper itself acknowledges (Section Limitations, External validity): one LLM family for the operational pipeline, three systems in the prose-constraint documentation regime, ~67 active hours for the full Qdrant run (across five calendar days, dominated by LLM calls), and an explicit disclaimer of transfer to schema-constrained systems. Useful rather than necessary.
   - **1.3** The bug-pattern taxonomy (Table 3) concentrates 37/51 (72.5%) in acceptance-family patterns and localizes per-vendor clusters (Type-Coercion only in Milvus; approximate-count under-counting in Qdrant). This gives the community a reusable characterization of what documentation-implementation bugs look like in VDBMSs, independent of the tool.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** The anchor inversion against the closest structural competitor holds: MASTOR (cached full text, arXiv:2606.10465) grounds oracles in the implementation and demotes OAS-declared items not substantiated in source to `pending`; its own limitation section states it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code." TestVDB's inversion — prose as ground, implementation as falsifier — is therefore a clear, source-verified delta, not a repackaging.
   - **2.2** The falsifier-independence delta vs the LLM-judgment line also holds: Metamon's judge is the LLM itself (no non-LLM evidence source), and CASCADE's falsifier is LLM code generated from the same documentation — CASCADE's own residual-FP analysis attributes both residual false positives to "the same false assumption over something underspecified in the documentation." TestVDB's source-anchored evidence chain addresses exactly this documented failure mode; the paper quotes CASCADE accurately.
   - **2.3** Table 1's exclusion analysis is a useful formalization of where each oracle family's expectation anchors. It is honest about its own limits (rows 2–6 "argued from each family's anchoring mechanism," not measured), and its characterizations of SATORI (response-field oracles from OAS fields; spec–response mismatch bugs reported) and AGORA+ (trace-bounded invariants) match those papers' own texts.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** Related-work coverage has one small gap: GentaREST ("A Multi-Agent Approach for REST API Testing with Semantic Graphs and LLM-Driven Inputs," ICSE 2025) is uncited; it belongs beside LlamaRestTest in the input-generation line of the last Related-Work paragraph. Also, MASTEST's own design (human review of test artifacts, not of per-candidate verdicts) would sharpen the paper's distinction of its verdict-level review channel.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1 [major, fixable]** The central mechanism claim rests on one backbone. The full-vs-flat separation (41/51 vs 30/51 majority) is real on GLM with the routing convention, but (i) under forced verdicts the two judges are indistinguishable (27/51 vs 25/51, p=0.51), (ii) the second-family full-vs-flat re-run does not separate (0.588 vs 0.529, p=0.81, single run), and (iii) the cross-family source-only-vs-flat separation carries a disclosed backbone confound. The paper scopes its conclusion correctly ("a property of that backbone under this protocol, not a law of the protocol itself"), and the abstract discloses the non-replication — but Contribution 2 still sells the structured protocol as "a measured mechanism, not scaffolding" on the strength of a single-family, routing-carried result. A multi-run second-family replication of the pair is the fix; without it, the mechanism's generality remains open.
   - **3.2** The audit/rebuild/protocol-alignment layers are well designed: the pack audit (43.3% of 134 cited constraint-page pairs unsupported; 32% of rows addressing wrong endpoints — both consistent with the 674 rows removed by endpoint filtering out of 2,106), the rebuilt-pack accounting (2,106 − 674 − 463 = 969; 969 − 302 = 667; 667 + 7 = 674; 22 explicit + 652 inferred), and the protocol alignment that removed two recall-suppressing pre-deployment rules each isolate one variable. Reporting all three layers rather than only the corrected one is methodologically exemplary.
   - **3.3** The contamination handling is correct in kind: ten packs with embedded maintainer-cognition materials (forbidden to three of the four arms) were cleaned, all affected arm-run combinations re-adjudicated across both backbones, and pre-cleanup verdicts shipped. Because the direction of the contamination is not stated per-arm, replacing affected verdicts rather than dropping the runs is the right call.
   - **3.4** I recomputed all inferential statistics. All seven exact McNemar p-values reconcile from the reported discordant counts (0/25 → <0.0001; 2/37 → 1.0e-8; 1/39 → 1.5e-10; 17/4 → 0.0072; 15/3 → 0.0075; 15/5 → 0.041; 7/4 → 0.55), and the Holm step-down is correctly executed: full-vs-flat is the fourth-smallest with multiplier 4 (0.0072×4 = 0.029), D-only-Qwen-vs-flat's multiplier 3 gives 0.023 but the monotonicity floor from the previous step raises it to the reported 0.029, and D-only-vs-flat at 0.041×2 = 0.083 correctly fails to survive. All Wilson intervals I checked (8/51, 29/30, 41/51, 24/30, 30/51, 37/51, 19/32) match to the stated precision. All confusion-matrix rows sum to 81. The seven-test family excludes the forced-only and joint comparisons (p=0.51, p=1.0, p=1.0), which are reported descriptively — a defensible scoping choice, but worth stating explicitly.
   - **3.5** RQ3's negative result is handled with rare care: the authors discovered the baseline runner's success/failure counters cannot fire (verified against a no-server port), substituted a per-template liveness oracle, and measured the zero over the full template space (22,540 mutations; 23,258 responses whose status counts sum exactly: 14,022 + 7,520 + 1,512 + 204). They also correctly narrowed the crash-class conclusion to the released configuration (mutation space cannot construct 2^62-scale `size`), and withdrew an earlier wrong explanation for the empty-vector silence when their own inspection contradicted it.
   - **3.6 [major, fixable]** The headline framing leads with the convention-favorable comparison. The abstract's "0.804 recall at 0.800 suppression—against 0.588" is computed by counting Human-Review as Confirmed; the paper's own human adjudication of the 17 routed confirmations shows the convention overstates the joint system (0.647), and the joint-vs-flat comparison the deployment actually implements (33/51 vs 30/51; suppression 0.933 vs 0.867; discordant 7/6, p=1.0) is not significant. All of this is reported in the body, and the abstract does state the forced-verdict collapse — but the ordering of claims inverts the paper's own finding: the structure's measured value is honest escalation, yet the headline numbers present the convention. Reframe the abstract and Contribution 2 around the routing decomposition (or give the joint system equal billing).
   - **3.7 [minor, unfixable]** Ground truth and pool construction limit how the rates can be read: maintainer adjudication is fallible (self-demonstrated by #9149), the 81-pool is a submission-filtered subset with author-side screening in the loop, 49 submissions remain unadjudicated, and the approximate-count family (4 of the full stage's 10 misses) is a ground-truth disagreement the documentation's own disclaimer makes genuinely ambiguous. All disclosed; inherent to the data.
   - **3.8 [minor, fixable]** Small internal inconsistencies and ambiguous juxtapositions: (i) the Qwen single-run sentence juxtaposes the full stage's recall (0.588, i.e., 30 of the 51 true bugs) with the flat judge's 0.529 (27 of 51) and then reports discordant pairs 10/8 — but those discordants are computed over the confirmed sets (true positives plus leaked false positives), not over the recall numerators; a reader who takes 30/27 as the McNemar marginals derives an apparent inconsistency (overlap 30 − 10 = 20 would give the flat judge 28, not 27), so one clarifying clause stating the denominators would prevent the misreading — a presentation nit, not an arithmetic error; (ii) the core is described as "three runs return identical verdicts on the re-measured pool" while case-level agreement is 96.3% (78/81) — at best ambiguous; (iii) the decomposition says the flat judge "has no such verdict," but Appendix A.2 gives the flat judge the same three-valued verdict space — what it lacks is the aggregation rule that mandates routing, and the flat judge's primary-family routing rate is never reported.

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets *documentation–implementation bugs* in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): silent misbehavior (accepts, wrong successes, state couplings) that violates natural-language API documentation and therefore escapes crash-oracle fuzzing. Its oracle analysis argues that every deterministic-oracle family anchors its expectation somewhere other than system-level untagged prose, forcing an LLM-derived oracle and importing the LLM's false-positive problem. The proposed system, TestVDB, is a four-stage pipeline (specification extraction from documentation, strategy-bound test generation, sandboxed execution, and a two-agent bug-confirmation stage) whose confirmation stage grounds every verdict in an evidence chain anchored in the implementation source rather than in another LLM judgment, with a three-valued verdict that routes unresolvable cases to human review.

Empirically, across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs (23 fixed via merged PRs). The confirmation stage is then measured in a five-arm re-adjudication of the same 81-candidate pool over rebuilt, audited evidence packages: the contract-only core recalls 0.157 at 0.967 suppression, the full structured stage 0.804 at 0.800, versus 0.588 for a flat single-prompt judge, with the full-vs-flat separation reported as Holm-corrected significant; a decomposition attributes the separation to the human-review routing channel (forced-verdict recall 0.529 vs 0.490, indistinguishable; joint judge-plus-human 0.647 recall / 0.943 precision). A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows complementary reach: the fuzzer's full 205-template Qdrant space reaches none of the confirmed silent-accept defects, while TestVDB's boundary reasoning reaches VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow by hand.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Measurement honesty of an unusual degree: a three-layer attribution (audit of the shipped packs, rebuild against version-pinned documentation, protocol alignment) isolates the assembler from the confirmation stage, and a later contamination discovery (cognition sections in ten packs) triggered re-adjudication of every affected arm with pre-cleanup verdict files retained — see 3.1, 3.2, 3.4.
- **S2:** The novelty delta against the closest competitors is real and verifiable: MASTOR's own limitation statement concedes source-anchored oracles "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code" — precisely the gap TestVDB fills by inverting the anchor — and CASCADE's falsifier is a doc-derived LLM artifact whose shared-ambiguity residual false positives the paper quotes accurately — see 2.1, 2.2, 2.3.
- **S3:** The mechanism decomposition (forced 0.529 / convention 0.804 / joint 0.647) locates the structured protocol's advantage in the routing channel rather than asserting it, and reports the unflattering sides: the convention overstates the joint system, and the full-vs-flat separation fails to replicate on a second model family — see 3.5, 3.8.
- **S4:** Demonstrated operational impact with verified bookkeeping: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 merged fix PRs, all 23 classified as implementation-code changes by a classifier validated against the known test-only PR of the disproved case — see 1.1, 3.2.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The headline comparison's statistical pairing is carried by an undisclosed-at-point-of-use convention: the McNemar discordant counts reconcile with each arm's *confirmed set* (TP plus leaked, Human-Review counted as confirmed), not with the recall numerators a reader naturally derives, so the central significance claim looks inconsistent on a first read and depends on the routing convention that the paper's own decomposition shows is doing the separative work — compounded by single-backbone evidence for the full-vs-flat separation — see 3.6, 3.8.
- **W2:** One quantity is reported inconsistently twice: the contract-core majority appears as 8 TP / 43 FN in Table 4 but 7 TP / 44 FN in Table 5's reference row, plausibly a stale pre-cleanup value; combined with the missing flat-judge per-run rows, the paper's central tables cannot be fully recomputed from the paper itself — see 3.7, 4.3.
- **W3:** The central mechanism claim rests on a single LLM family: the full-vs-flat pair does not separate on the second family (p = 0.81) and the cross-family source-only separation carries a backbone confound; the paper scopes this honestly in Sections 4.3 and 5, but the contribution list still phrases the protocol advantage as a mechanism-level finding — see 3.8.
- **W4:** The headline yield measures the human–machine pipeline (author-side screening and submission decisions in the loop), not the automated stage; the seeded 32-candidate anchor mitigates this but was itself measured under the pre-audit protocol — see 1.2, 3.3.

### Detailed Assessment

Third-party maintainers merging 23 fixes is clear practical impact on real systems.
   - **1.2 [minor, fixable]** The automated stage's standalone value is not established end-to-end: the 81-pool is a submission-filtered subset (no deterministic submission rule, disclosed in Section 4.2), and the only window into the non-submitted stream is the seeded 32-candidate anchor (19/32 = 0.594, Wilson interval recomputes exactly), which the authors themselves flag as measured under the pre-audit protocol and hence not comparable to the rebuilt-package numbers. ## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets *documentation-implementation bugs* in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where the system silently accepts an input or behaves in a way that contradicts its natural-language API documentation. Because every deterministic test-oracle candidate anchors its expectation in a crash signal, a reference implementation, an output relation, a property, or a structured spec — none in untagged behavioral prose (Table 1) — the authors argue an LLM-derived oracle is the practical option, which imports a false-positive problem. TestVDB is a four-stage pipeline: specification extraction from documentation, test generation under pre-bound strategies, Docker-pinned sandboxed execution, and a bug-confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly from a four-perspective chain audit grounded in the implementation source. Across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs, 23 fixed via merged PRs.

A second evaluation audits the frozen evidence packs feeding the confirmation stage (43.3% of cited constraint pairs unsupported by their pages), rebuilds all 81 packs against version-pinned documentation, and re-adjudicates the pool in five judge arms across three runs each: the contract-only core recalls 0.157 at 0.967 suppression; the structured full stage reaches 0.804 recall at 0.800 suppression versus 0.588 for a flat single-prompt judge with identical evidence access (exact McNemar p=0.0072, Holm-corrected 0.029). A decomposition attributes the full-vs-flat separation to the explicit Human-Review routing channel rather than forced-verdict accuracy, and a single-run re-run of the pair on a second model family does not separate. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows the crash oracle reaches none of the silent-accept defects, while TestVDB's boundary reasoning reaches VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow by hand.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Upstream-validated yield with an audited fix ledger — 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 merged fix PRs, all 23 verified to modify implementation code — see 1.1
- **S2:** Statistical reporting that overwhelmingly reconciles under recomputation: nearly all confusion-matrix arithmetic (one cross-table mismatch flagged in 3.2), all recomputed Wilson intervals, exact McNemar p-values, and the Holm step-down reproduce exactly — see 3.1
- **S3:** Unusually candid self-decomposition: the headline separation is located in the routing convention, the second-backbone non-replication is reported, and a wrong earlier claim is withdrawn in-text — see 3.3, 3.7
- **S4:** Verifiability: declared artifact with per-case judge outputs and recomputation scripts; both judge prompts quoted verbatim in the appendix — see 4.1

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** Cross-table contradiction in the reported contract-core result (Table 5 reference row 7/1/44/29 vs Table 4 and the text 8/1/43/29) — see 3.2
- **W2:** The headline 0.804 recall counts Human-Review as Confirmed by convention; the paper's own adjudication of the routed cases measures the joint judge-plus-human system at 0.647, so the abstract leads with a convention the paper itself shows overstates the system — see 3.3
- **W3:** Key downstream numbers rest on fallible or non-blind adjudication: maintainer labels as ground truth (one TP disproved in-house), and the authors themselves playing the routed-cases human role — see 3.4, 3.5
- **W4:** The central full-vs-flat separation is single-backbone (does not replicate on the second family), and presentation density in Section 4.3 obscures an otherwise strong argument — see 3.6, 5.2–5.4

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** Table 2 (Section 4.2) reports 51 of 81 adjudicated upstream submissions confirmed by maintainers and 23 fixed via merged PRs, across three production systems; the fix-nature audit (Section 4.2) finds all 23 fix PRs modify implementation code, 15 add regression tests, none is documentation-only, and the classifier correctly flags the disproved #9149 PR as test-only. This is unusually strong, externally validated evidence of practical impact for a testing technique.
   - **1.2 [minor, fixable]** The motivating prevalence claim — "most VDBMS bugs are functional failures" (Sections 1, 2.1) — rests entirely on the paper's own cited bug study and roadmap; the studies' actual counts are never given, so a reader cannot gauge how large the silent majority really is.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The demonstrated deployment is heavily human-in-the-loop: author-side submission screening (Section 4.2), plus 20.6% of full-stage verdicts routed to human review (Section 4.3). Significance as an *automated* technique is correspondingly bounded, and the cost profile (~67 active hours, ~70M input tokens, Section 4.1) is disclosed but never compared against a manual-testing baseline.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate (provisional: I have not surveyed the field; assessed from the paper's own positioning)
   - **2.1** The "anchor inversion" is a crisply articulated delta (Section 6, Table 1): prior documentation-derived oracles keep the derived oracle as final arbiter, validated only at runtime, whereas TestVDB takes the prose as ground and the implementation source as falsifier. As the paper describes the prior line, this is a recognizable, non-trivial reorientation rather than a repackaging.
   - **2.2 [minor, fixable]** The paper itself concedes the nearest components exist in works it cites: an LLM oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent (MASTOR's ChallengerAgent), cross-request behavioral oracles (MASTOR, MASTEST), a documentation-vs-code cross-check (CASCADE), and an LLM judge stabilized against documentation (Metamon, Section 6). What remains as the delta is the independent (non-LLM-artifact) falsifier, the fixed aggregation rule, and the measured routing channel — real but incremental.    - **2.3** The audit-rebuild-re-adjudicate methodology (Section 4.3, Layers 0-2) — turning "one unknown-quality number" into a three-layer attribution and quantifying its own contamination channels — is, provisionally, an unusually rigorous evaluation pattern that the cited LLM-oracle literature (e.g., the contamination warning of Molinelli et al., Section 6) only calls for.
