## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where the system silently accepts an input or returns a result that contradicts its natural-language API documentation without crashing. It argues structurally (Section 2.3, Table 1) that the standard oracle families all anchor their expectations elsewhere---crash signals, reference implementations, output relations, machine-checkable properties, structured specs---so an LLM-derived oracle is the practical option for this residual, which imports the LLM's false-positive problem. TestVDB is a four-stage pipeline (Section 3): natural-language documentation is distilled into typed, source-verified constraints (Section 3.2); pre-bound strategies and attack agents turn them into executable probes (Section 3.3); a Docker-pinned sandbox runs the probes and logs raw HTTP traffic (Section 3.4); and a confirmation stage splits an evidence builder, which assembles a five-section chain including source grounding in a pinned clone, from a chain auditor that applies four mechanical checks and four perspectives (contract, objective constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition) under a fixed aggregation into Confirmed / False-Positive / Human-Review (Section 3.5).

The evaluation reports three things. First, a ledger of 51 maintainer-confirmed real bugs (23 fixed by merged PRs) out of 81 adjudicated submissions across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, with an eight-pattern taxonomy of the confirmed bugs (Section 4.2, Tables 2--3). Second, a controlled re-adjudication of the same 81 candidates over audited and rebuilt evidence packs: a contract-only core reaches 0.157 recall at 0.967 suppression, a structured full stage reaches 0.765 recall at 0.700 suppression (counting human-review routing as confirmed), and a flat single-prompt judge reaches 0.588, with the full-vs-flat separation surviving Holm correction on the confirmed-set unit on the primary backbone but not replicating on a second backbone (p=0.58) (Section 4.3, Tables 4--6). Third, a bidirectional reach probe against the crash-oracle fuzzer VDBFuzz on Qdrant: under its released configuration VDBFuzz reaches none of the confirmed defects, while a hand-guided probe built on the paper's boundary reasoning reaches VDBFuzz's integer-overflow crash on v1.4.0 (Section 4.4).

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** A deployment-scale empirical result that is maintainer-validated rather than self-reported: 51 confirmed bugs and 23 merged implementation fixes across three production VDBMSs, with the fix-PR nature independently classified and the classifier itself validated against a disproved case --- see 1.1, 1.2.
- **S2:** The oracle-exclusion account (Section 2.3, Table 1) is a genuine framing contribution, and every competitor characterization I checked against the papers themselves (VDBFuzz, MASTOR, CASCADE, Metamon, LogicHunter) holds --- see 2.1, 2.3, 2.4, 2.6.
- **S3:** Exceptional self-auditing of the paper's own evidence: the pack audit (43.3% of cited (constraint, page) pairs unsupported), the rebuild against version-pinned documentation, the protocol alignment that removed two recall-suppressing rules, and two further leak channels found and cleaned after re-adjudication --- see 3.1.
- **S4:** The confirmation-stage evaluation is a properly controlled ablation with the failing statistic reported next to the surviving one (recall-only Holm-adjusted 0.090 vs flagship 0.0052), the second-backbone non-replication in the abstract, and the three scoring readings disclosed side by side --- see 3.2, 3.4.
- **S5:** The RQ3 comparison is measured, not asserted: the zero from VDBFuzz's released runner is validated (its counters are shown uninformative; a per-template liveness oracle is substituted), and the reverse direction is explicitly marked as a hand-guided probe with the released mutation space's bounds reported --- see 3.8.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The claim that the implementation source is the falsification anchor that breaks the extraction-judgment self-reference (Abstract; Section 3.1; Figure 1) is not isolated by any experimental arm: source access is confounded with the rules, perspectives, and routing in the core-vs-full comparison, and the flat judge also has source access --- see 3.3.
- **W2:** The flagship full-vs-flat separation is fragile: it survives Holm correction only on the confirmed-set unit, five of its fourteen net cases are additional false-positive confirmations, the recall-only comparison does not survive (0.090), and the pair does not separate on a second backbone (p=0.58) --- see 3.4.
- **W3:** The deployment-faithful joint reading (0.647 recall at 0.917 precision) is produced by the authors adjudicating their own routed cases non-blind; the human half of the mechanism is not independently measured --- see 3.5.
- **W4:** The RQ1 precision and RQ2 recall are both pool-internal (the 81 candidates are the pipeline's own submission-filtered output), and the direction anchor for the submission filter compares a stage re-confirmation rate with a maintainer-confirmation rate --- see 3.6, 1.3.
- **W5:** Novelty positioning: the mechanism inventory (multi-agent extraction/generation, a reviewing agent over generated oracles, LLM judgment, escalation) is recognizable from prior work the paper itself cites; the verified delta is the anchor inversion plus the VDBMS class and the deployment evidence, and the paper should say so more sharply --- see 2.2, 2.5.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The problem is established from two independent directions: the paper's own structural account (Sections 2.1--2.2) that most VDBMS bugs are functional failures that keep the service up, and the deployment evidence itself --- of the 51 confirmed bugs only one produces a crash or panic (Qdrant #9045, Section 1, Table 3 "Other" row), while 37 of 51 fall in the acceptance family (Silent-Accept, Type-Coercion, Silent-Ignore; Section 4.2, Table 3). The demonstrated yield (51 maintainer-confirmed, 23 merged fixes, Table 2) is exactly the kind of impact that matters for infrastructure on which RAG applications depend, and it is validated by external actors, not by the authors' judgment. I verified against VDBFuzz's full text that its oracle is crash-only by construction and that its API-sequence stage targets "logical inconsistencies or invalid state transitions", so the class the paper targets is genuinely outside the only dedicated VDBMS fuzzer's reach (Section 2.1, Table 1 row 1).
   - **1.2** The "Fix nature" analysis (Section 4.2) closes the usual loophole in bug-count claims: all 23 merged fix PRs are classified by changed files, every one modifies implementation code, 15 also add regression tests, and none is documentation-only; the classifier is itself checked against the reclassified Qdrant #9149, whose "fix" PR it correctly identifies as test-only. The "Upstream acceptance" paragraph (50/51 carry a bug label; 25/29 Milvus triage/accepted) and the explicit accounting of the 49 unadjudicated submissions are the right reporting choices.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The impact statement is bounded by attribution: the ledger "measures the human--machine pipeline rather than the automated stage alone" (Abstract; also Section 4.2 and Section 5), so neither the 63.0% precision nor the 51-bug yield can be read as the pipeline's own output. The paper should report the aggregate automation split from its own artifact --- how many pipeline-registered candidates the author screening withheld, and how the Qdrant run's funnel (96 distinct candidates, 60 defect-confirming, five registered; Section 4.2) maps onto the 81 submitted --- so readers can price the machine's contribution against the human's.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** Checked the delta against VDBFuzz (fetched): the crash-oracle baseline reaches only crash-class bugs, and the paper's use of it is fair --- it is cited for the class it can reach, exercised on its own protocol with the time limit removed, and never claimed to be a general baseline (Section 4.4). The complementarity claim is bidirectional and each direction's limit is stated.
   - **2.2** Checked the delta against MASTOR (fetched; the paper calls it "the closest to this work", Section 6): the anchor inversion holds. MASTOR's oracles are generated from source --- its own stated limitation is that it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code" --- whereas TestVDB takes the prose as ground and admits the source only as falsifier, and MASTOR's doc-versus-source discrepancy signal stays in `pending` fields rather than becoming an oracle. This is a real, non-trivial repositioning. However, the mechanism overlap is substantial and the paper concedes it: multi-agent oracle generation, a ChallengerAgent-style second-LLM review, and cross-request behavioural oracles all exist in MASTOR. **[minor, fixable]** The contribution statement should own the delta as "anchor inversion + a VDBMS documentation-implementation class + deployment evidence" rather than letting the pipeline read as the novelty.
   - **2.3** Checked the delta against CASCADE (fetched): the paper's characterization is accurate to the letter --- CASCADE's falsifier is a second implementation generated from the same documentation, and its own two residual false positives are explained by the generated code and tests "ma[king] the same false assumption over something underspecified in the documentation" (Section 6). Using the implementation as the falsifier rather than a second doc-derived artifact is a genuine distinction.
   - **2.4** Checked the delta against Metamon (fetched): the "same division of labour" framing is correct --- generated regression-test oracles judged by an LLM against the documented specification, stabilized by metamorphic prompt variants; no non-LLM evidence source is consulted. The reported profile (precision 0.722 at recall 0.480 on 9,482 pairs) matches Metamon's own numbers verbatim, and the paper's choice not to treat it as a head-to-head baseline is right.
   - **2.5 [minor, fixable]** Checked the delta against LogicHunter (fetched): the inversion claim holds, but the one-line reduction of its oracle's authority to "the documentation it retrieves, with the implementation as a further input" understates it --- LogicHunter's agent has reflection-based `code_search` and sandboxed `run_code` tools and actively inspects runtime state, and its motivating ambiguity argument is primarily that ordinary exceptions are ambiguous in pure Python, not only that crash oracles miss silent failures. Tighten the sentence so the delta is stated against what LogicHunter actually does.
   - **2.6** Related-work coverage is strong across four separate lines (VDBMS testing, structured-source oracles, doc-derived oracles and judge reliability, doc-code inconsistency detection), and the paper positions itself against each named competitor with specific mechanisms. My scoped coverage searches (VDBMS testing; empirical VDBMS bug studies; doc-implementation inconsistency detection; LLM REST-API oracle generation; LLM oracles for database testing) surfaced no high-relevance uncited work after de-duplication against the 63-entry bibliography. The use of the Mughal and Bilal authority taxonomy (Section 6) is apt: I verified that the review's organizing axis is indeed the source of an oracle's authority, which is the axis the anchor inversion turns on.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The measurement corrections (Section 4.3, "Measurement design") are the most complete self-audit of this kind I have seen in this literature. The audit found 43.3% of 134 distinct (constraint, cited-page) pairs unsupported, two over-strong pairs, and 32% of constraint rows addressing an unrelated endpoint; the rebuild is reported with full arithmetic (674 surviving rows of 2,106; 22 explicit and 652 inferred-from-behavior; 667 + 7 = 674); and the protocol alignment removed two rules that suppressed recall by construction (bare structural inference accepted as a by-design refutation; "validation present" as non-defect evidence). The four provenance notes belong to that Layer-0 audit --- they were found among the originally assembled packs and removed before any re-adjudication (Section 4.3); Section 5 then reports the two further channels that surfaced after the re-adjudication --- ten rebuilt packs carrying an embedded maintainer-cognition section, and seven candidate-anchored entries in the runtime cognition files --- with the re-adjudication counts for those. The paper's own equivalence of disclosure here is a real methodological contribution.
   - **3.2** The statistics are sound and internally consistent. I re-derived the Wilson intervals (8/51 [0.082, 0.280]; 29/30 [0.833, 0.994]; 39/51 [0.632, 0.860]; 37/51 [0.591, 0.829]; 19/32 [0.423, 0.745]), the exact McNemar values (16/2 -> 0.0013; 11/2 -> 0.0225; 5/0 -> 0.0625), the Holm multipliers (4 -> 0.0052; 3 -> 0.023; the ten-test sensitivity set -> 0.0092), and the accuracy metrics (F1 0.788 vs. 0.706 and 0.698 vs. 0.651; Youden J 0.465 vs. 0.455 and 0.422 vs. 0.363) --- all check out. The family is defined by construction (footnote to Section 4.3), the recall-only test is reported as failing correction, and the F1/Youden triangulation is the right response to the confirmed-set unit's ambiguity. The three-run core accounting (96.3% case agreement, three flipping bugs each confirmed in exactly one run) is exactly consistent with the per-run and majority rows of Table 4.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The falsification-anchor claim is architectural rather than measured. The Abstract, Section 3.1 ("the falsification power of stage (iv) comes from grounding the falsification in the implementation source"), and Figure 1 all present the source anchor as the mechanism; but no arm isolates source access --- the core-vs-full comparison changes source access, the rule set, the perspectives, and the routing together, and the flat judge (Section 4.3, Table 5) already has source access. What the arms do show is consistent with the anchor being the workhorse (source-only majority recall 0.706, essentially equal to the full stage's 0.765 convention rate, p=0.39) but also with the extra perspectives being inert; meanwhile the paper's own decomposition attributes the headline full-vs-flat separation to the routing channel, not to the source (Section 4.3). A revision can resolve this either by adding the missing arm (four perspectives and aggregation with the source clone withheld) or by re-framing the contribution around what is measured and reporting how often source evidence flipped a would-be confirmation.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The headline comparison is fragile in three disclosed but consequential ways: the flagship McNemar survives on the confirmed-set unit, in which five of the fourteen net cases are additional false-positive confirmations (Section 4.3); the recall-only test does not survive correction (Holm-adjusted 0.090); and the same pair re-run three times on a second family does not separate (p=0.58). The authors are transparent about all three, and they add a per-family F1/net-TP triangulation, but the net effect is that the paper's central quantitative claim --- "The structured confirmation protocol is a measured mechanism on the primary backbone, not a law of the protocol" (Section 1) --- is supported by one backbone and by a unit that prices a confirmed false positive the same as a confirmed true bug. The revision should narrow the claim to exactly that measurement, and, if possible, add a third backbone or more runs so the within-family replication is not a single negative.
   - **3.5 [major, fixable]** The routing channel is the measured mechanism, and the human in it is the authors. Section 4.3 states that they "adjudicated the routed cases of both arms against their pack materials only, without consulting the issue trackers, playing the channel's designated human role (non-blind; disclosed)". The design is right in one important respect --- the equal-treatment control (the flat arm's 11 routed cases adjudicated under the same rule, 0.569 vs. 0.647, p=0.11) is a genuine methodological strength --- but the 0.647/0.917 joint numbers, which the paper calls the deployment-faithful reading, come from non-blind insiders. An independent adjudicator on the same 32 routed cases (21 full + 11 flat) would close this.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** The direction anchor for the submission filter is stated in mismatched units. Section 4.2 compares "never-submitted registrations confirm at 0.594" with "the adjudicated pool's 63.0%" --- but 0.594 is the blinded stage's re-confirmation rate on 32 seeds measured under the pre-audit protocol on original packs (Section 4.3), whereas 63.0% is the maintainers' confirmation rate on submissions. The paper notes the anchor is not comparable to rebuilt-package numbers, but not that the two rates come from different arbiters; the sentence's inference ("a filter that mildly selects stronger candidates") needs either a unit-matched measurement or an explicit directional-only caveat.
   - **3.7 [minor, fixable]** Protocol--pool co-evolution is disclosed and bounded but not eliminated: Section 5 states that the objective-constraint classes were codified from the same mining experience that produced the evaluated pool, that 16 of the full stage's 39 confirmations are driven by them, and that excluding them lowers recall to 0.451. The appendix's check that the flat judge reaches 13 of those 16 confirmations is the right control, and it shows the comparison is not confounded; what remains unmeasured is how much of the absolute 0.765 is protocol fit rather than protocol quality. Applying the sealed classes to a fresh target (a fourth system, or a version never mined) would bound it.
   - **3.8** RQ3 (Section 4.4) is the most carefully bounded comparison in the paper. The zero from VDBFuzz's released runner is not taken at face value: the authors show the success counter fires on process exit and that both failure markers are unreachable (a control run against a dead port reports exit code 0 and 100% success), substitute a per-template liveness oracle, and then report the bound from two directions --- the released integer boundary set tops out at 65,536 and the dimension candidates at 10,000, while the crash needs roughly 2^62; the 2^63 proof-of-concept is not among the 205 swept templates; and the hand-reproduced crash is a recovered panic (HTTP 500) that a liveness oracle would not record either. The reverse direction's probe is explicitly marked as hand-guided with knowledge of VDBFuzz's case study, and the released-template space (205 templates, 50.8 minutes, 22,540 mutations, status histogram) is fully reported.

Distinct terms for the two senses of leak would prevent real confusion in Section 4.3.

Moving the short definitional paragraph to the start of Section 4.3 would fix the backward references.

### Questions for Authors

- **Q1:** Can you report the contribution of source grounding *within* the structured protocol --- e.g., how many verdicts changed once the source clone was consulted, or an arm that keeps the four perspectives and aggregation but withholds source access? --- intended effect: if source access is shown to carry the separation rather than routing alone, item 3.3's rating would move up (and the Abstract's anchor claim would be supported as written); as it stands the decomposition points at routing.
- **Q2:** The reported rates use a three-run majority, but the deployment resolves each case once, and full-stage case agreement is 67.9%. What single-run recall and suppression should a reader expect from one deployment pass (the per-run rows of Table 4 span 35--40/51 true positives)? --- intended effect: if the single-run expectation is materially below 0.765, the headline number's interpretation changes and item 3.4's rating would move.
- **Q3:** The routed cases were adjudicated by the authors, non-blind, under the pack-materials rule (Section 4.3). Have you considered an independent (blind) adjudicator on the same cases to test the 0.647/0.917 joint figures? --- intended effect: independent adjudication would raise item 3.5's rating by removing the insider-adjudication caveat from the deployment-faithful reading.
- **Q4:** Is the 0.594 anchor (Section 4.3, blinded stage re-confirmation on pre-audit packs) measured in the same unit as the 63.0% maintainer-confirmation rate it is compared with in Section 4.2? If not, what supports the sentence's directional inference about the submission filter? --- intended effect: a unit-matched anchor would move item 3.6 up; otherwise the sentence should be restated as directional only.
- **Q5:** Of the pipeline's confirmed and registered candidates, how many were withheld from submission by the author screening, and for how many of the 51 was the final issue text or reproduction authored by the human rather than the pipeline? --- intended effect: this would let readers attribute the RQ1 yield between the machine and its operators, moving item 1.3 (and the significance assessment) up.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets a class it calls *documentation-implementation bugs* in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): inputs a system silently accepts, or behaviors it exposes, that violate its natural-language API documentation—observable neither by crash oracles nor by the structured-source oracles the paper surveys. It first argues structurally (Table 1, "oracle candidates by the bug class they reach") that each standard oracle family anchors its expectation somewhere other than system-level untagged prose, leaving an LLM-derived oracle as the practical option for this residual. It then presents TestVDB, a four-stage pipeline: documentation is distilled into version-pinned, source-verified constraints (§3.2); three attack agents bound to pre-registered strategies turn constraints into executable probes with inline oracle lines (§3.3); a sandbox runs every probe against a Docker-pinned version while logging raw HTTP traffic (§3.4); and a confirmation stage splits an evidence builder from a chain auditor that cross-examines a five-section evidence chain under four mechanical checks and four perspectives—contract, physical/objective constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition—with a three-valued verdict that includes an explicit human-review channel (§3.5).

The evaluation has three parts. RQ1 (§4.2, Table 2) reports an operational ledger of 81 adjudicated upstream submissions, 51 of them maintainer-confirmed as real bugs and 23 fixed via merged PRs across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, accumulated over earlier mining rounds. RQ2 (§4.3, Tables 4–6) audits the frozen evidence packs that fed the confirmation stage (43.3% of cited constraint–page pairs unsupported), rebuilds them against version-pinned documentation, and re-adjudicates the resulting 81-candidate pool five ways—contract core, full stage, source-only, flat single-prompt judge, and source-only on a second backbone—three runs each, under three scoring conventions; the headline is 0.765 recall at 0.700 suppression for the full stage against 0.588 for the flat judge, with the paper's own decomposition attributing that separation to the human-review routing channel rather than to forced-verdict accuracy. RQ3 (§4.4) probes VDBFuzz bidirectionally on Qdrant: its released template set run to natural completion reaches none of the silent-accept defects, while a hand-guided boundary probe built on the paper's reasoning reaches VDBFuzz's integer-overflow crash.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The RQ2 measurement chain—audit the shipped packs, rebuild them from version-pinned sources, then align the judging protocol—is executed with the discipline of a meta-analysis: every candidate leak channel is audited and the result acted on—assembler provenance notes and cognition-anchored entries were removed and the affected cases re-adjudicated in every affected arm and run, while expectation framing was measured and found not to be operating (zero expectation-framed sentences outside verbatim server responses), so nothing there needed removal. — see 3.1, 4.1
- **S2:** The evaluation reports its own negative results at full resolution: forced-verdict ties (0.529 vs. 0.490), a non-significant joint comparison (p=0.11), a failed second-backbone replication of the flagship pair (p=0.58), and a per-perspective attribution showing 14 of 39 confirmations carry no forced-driving perspective. This is the rare paper whose decomposition is more informative than its headline. — see 3.2, 3.3
- **S3:** The novelty delta is real and I verified it against the closest competitors I could read: MASTOR's authority is genuinely the implementation (claims absent from source are demoted to `pending` fields and never become oracles); CASCADE's falsifier is genuinely a regenerated implementation from the same documentation; METAMON's is genuinely the same LLM judge under metamorphic prompting; SATORI and AGORA+ genuinely produce unary response-field oracles only. The paper's "anchor inversion" claim survives all four checks. — see 2.1, 2.2, 2.3
- **S4:** The RQ1 ledger comes with an unusual verification apparatus for a bug-finding paper: every merged fix PR classified by changed files (with the classifier validated against the one candidate the authors themselves disproved), duplicate-tracked bugs held off the fix count, submission states re-verified, and adjudication declared revisable. — see 1.2, 4.1
- **S5:** The running example (Qdrant #10369, `lookup_from` dimension bypass) is threaded through all four stages (§3.2–§3.5), which makes an otherwise abstract pipeline concrete and lets a reader check mechanism against outcome. — see 5.1

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The paper's headline numbers are earned under a counting convention that treats unresolved (Human-Review) cases as confirmed. Under the paper's own "deployment-faithful" joint reading the full-vs-flat gap is 0.647 vs. 0.569 and is *not* significant (p=0.11); under forced verdicts the two judges are indistinguishable (0.529 vs. 0.490, p=0.34); and the second backbone does not separate them at all (p=0.58). The abstract and introduction nevertheless lead with the 0.765/0.588 convention pair. — see 3.3
- **W2:** What the experiments separate is a *routing rule*, not the four-perspective structure the paper's contribution statement advertises. The flat-judge prompt already grants the identical materials and red lines, so the measured difference is that the full stage's aggregation rule *mandates* routing; no arm isolates the perspectives from that mandate. Worse for the framing, the source-forensics step alone—no perspectives, no routing—lands within four confirmed-set cases of the full stage (48 vs. 44, p=0.39) and exceeds the full stage's recall under forced scoring (0.706 vs. 0.529). — see 3.4
- **W3:** The 51-bug ledger is presented as TestVDB's yield, but Table 2's caption (§4.2), §4.3, and §5 state that all 81 adjudicated submissions come from earlier GLM-5.2 mining rounds and that the reported GLM-5.3-Flash run contributed none of them (it registered five new defects via the funnel of §4.2). The paper's strongest practical claim therefore measures a pipeline lineage, not the evaluated configuration. — see 3.7
- **W4:** RQ3's comparison instruments VDBFuzz with a substituted oracle (the released success/failure counters are shown uninformative) and omits its API-sequence-mutation stage; the reverse direction is a single hand-executed probe whose value was chosen with knowledge of VDBFuzz's own case study, and the paper does not measure whether the automated boundary strategy would generate a value in the failing range. Both facts are disclosed, but the abstract's phrasing ("a hand-guided probe built on TestVDB's boundary reasoning reaches VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow") transmits capability the experiment does not establish. — see 3.5
- **W5:** Two adjacent uncited works belong in the positioning: ACME (FSE 2026), which uses LLM-constructed clause mappings to enable differential testing across DBMSs and reports 59 previously unknown bugs, and Bodicoat et al. (AIware 2025), which measures whether LLM-generated oracles capture actual versus expected behavior—the same failure mode the paper's judge-reliability paragraph cites Konstantinou et al. and Molinelli et al. for. — see 2.4

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The problem is real, current, and sharply delimited. §1 and §2.2 define documentation-implementation *consistency* observationally (what the system accepts, errors on, returns, and how state evolves) and explicitly separate it from result *correctness* (ANN recall, ranking), which the paper declines to claim. The motivating prevalence claim rests on the bug study I checked (cache: `toward understanding bugs in vector database management systems (arxiv preprint 2025)`): functional failures at 57.3% of confirmed bugs against crashes at 15.1%—so the target class is the majority shape, not a corner.
   - **1.2** Practical impact is demonstrated rather than asserted. §4.2 and Table 2 report 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs of 81 adjudicated submissions, 23 fixed via merged PRs (each verified to modify implementation code; 15 also add regression tests), 50 of 51 carrying an official `bug` label, and 18 still open with maintainer acknowledgment. The fix-nature classifier is validated against the authors' own disproved candidate (#9149), which strengthens confidence in the "fixed" column.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The measured impact is bounded by a human channel whose cost is never priced. §4.3 reports that 19.8% of full-stage verdicts (48 of 243 pooled case judgments) route to review, and the deployment-faithful reading requires the authors to hand-adjudicate 32 cases under the pack-materials rule; the cost profile in §4.1 counts tokens, wall-clock, and sandbox runs but no human time per routed case or per confirmed bug. For an operational claim ("the deployment routes them to human adjudication rather than closing them"), the reader needs the human side of the ledger to judge whether routing "pays".

## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

This paper targets logical bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs) that leave the service running: cases where a system silently accepts an input or produces behavior that contradicts its own natural-language API documentation. It argues that the standard oracle families — crash signals, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property-based testing, structured-source oracles, and documentation-derived oracles operating at method or parameter granularity — all anchor their expectations somewhere other than system-level, untagged prose, and concludes that an LLM-derived oracle is the practical route to this residual, importing the LLM's false-positive problem. The proposed system, TestVDB, is a four-stage pipeline: documentation is distilled into source-verified, typed constraints; attack agents bound to pre-registered strategies turn constraints into executable probes; a sandbox runs each probe against a Docker-pinned version and logs raw HTTP traffic; and a confirmation stage splits evidence assembly (a five-section evidence chain including implementation-source grounding) from cross-examination by a chain auditor that applies four mechanical checks and four perspectives (contract, objective physical constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition) under a fixed aggregation rule with a three-valued verdict.

The evaluation has three parts. RQ1 reports a cumulative, maintainer-adjudicated submission ledger: 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions confirmed as real bugs (23 fixed via merged PRs) across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, grouped into eight bug patterns, plus a process ledger of one complete Qdrant v1.18.0 run. RQ2 audits the frozen evidence packs that fed the confirmation stage (43.3% of their cited constraint–page pairs turned out to be unsupported by the pages they cite), rebuilds every pack against version-pinned documentation, and re-adjudicates the same 81-candidate pool in three independent runs under five judge configurations, reporting recall and suppression under three scoring conventions — forced verdicts, a joint judge-plus-human reading, and the deployment's counting convention — together with paired exact tests and Holm correction. RQ3 probes the crash-oracle baseline VDBFuzz bidirectionally on Qdrant: its released template set run to completion on v1.18.0 reaches none of the reported defects, while a hand-executed boundary probe on v1.4.0 reaches a crash-class defect.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Real-world, per-case-checkable impact: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs and 23 merged fixes across three production VDBMSs, with acceptance labels and a validated fix-nature classifier, plus a taxonomy that names the dominant silent-acceptance shapes — see 1.1, 1.2
- **S2:** A measurement design that audits, rebuilds, and re-adjudicates its own materials and reports the adverse layers (forced-verdict collapse; second-family non-replication), turning a single unknown-quality number into a bracketed one — see 3.1, 3.3
- **S3:** The quantitative accounting is internally consistent: I re-derived the table decompositions, the rate calculations, the F1/Youden values, the stratum-weighted recalls, sampled Wilson intervals, and every exact McNemar p-value and Holm multiplier, and they all check out — see 3.2
- **S4:** The RQ3 probe both demonstrates complementarity and shows that the baseline's released runner cannot report its own failures (100% "success" against a dead port); the authors then bound their own zero from two directions — see 1.3
- **S5:** Verifiability is strong: a declared artifact whose inventory matches every claim, plus a text that names its intermediate artifacts, gates, prompts, and scoring conventions — see 4.1, 4.2, 4.3

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The paper is written at a density that hides its own contribution: long paragraphs that carry a dozen numbers per sentence, and an abstract whose result paragraphs read as a results dump — see 5.2
- **W2:** The flagship structure-vs-flat result is single-backbone, and the second-family null is over-read as backbone-specificity — see 3.4, 2.3
- **W3:** The "deployment-faithful" joint reading (0.647 recall / 0.917 precision) is produced by non-blind author adjudication of their own routed cases — see 3.5
- **W4:** Two bounding asymmetries remain: the objective-constraint sensitivity has no flat-arm counterpart, and Table 1's central exclusion claim is argued for five of its seven rows rather than exercised — see 3.6, 3.7

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The RQ1 ledger (Table 2, §4.2 "Collection protocol"/"Bug status") reports 51 maintainer-confirmed real bugs out of 81 adjudicated submissions, 23 fixed by merged PRs, and the paper validates the confirmation mechanism three ways: 50 of the 51 carry an official bug label (the exception, Qdrant #9524, is backed by its merged fix PR); 25 of 29 Milvus bugs carry `triage/accepted` and 5 of 14 Qdrant ones `accepted`; and the fix-nature classifier shows all 23 merged fix PRs modify implementation code (15 also add regression tests; none is documentation-only), with the classifier itself checked against the one candidate that was reclassified (Qdrant #9149, whose PR it correctly identifies as test-only). For a general reader this is the paper's clearest evidence of real-world value, and it is maintained rather than asserted: duplicates are tracked orthogonally, adjudication is treated as revisable, and every case ships in the artifact (`rq1/ledger`).
   - **1.2** The bug-pattern table (Table 3, §4.2 "Bug categories") groups the 51 bugs into eight named patterns, dominated by the acceptance family: Silent-Accept 25 + Type-Coercion 8 + Silent-Ignore 4 = 37 of 51 (72.5%). I verified the rows sum to 51. The text's per-system concentration claims are also arithmetically compatible with Table 2: if the five patterns whose representatives are Qdrant issues (Result-Incorrectness 5, Weak-Diagnostic 3, Stale-Coupling 2, Doc-Spec-Gap 1, Other 3) are entirely Qdrant's, they sum exactly to Qdrant's 14 confirmed bugs — a neat check the paper itself does not state and could. The taxonomy is the paper's most reusable empirical artifact: it names the shapes that documentation-derived oracles should be aimed at, and the paper is explicit that one cluster (Result-Incorrectness) sits outside its assertion framework by construction.
   - **1.3** RQ3 (§4.4) does more than a reach comparison. Running VDBFuzz's released Qdrant template set to completion on the same v1.18.0 instance (205 templates, 22,540 mutations, 23,258 HTTP responses over 96 endpoint paths, 50.8 minutes) reaches none of the confirmed defects, and the paper shows why the baseline's own zero is uninformative: in the released runner a template counts as a success whenever its process exits zero, and neither failure marker can fire (one is emitted by no code path; the other does not match the message the templates emit) — pointing the runner at a dead port reproduces "exit 0, 100% success". The authors substitute a per-template liveness oracle, and then bound their own result in both directions (the released mutation space cannot construct the `2^62`-scale input their own reproduction needs; their reproduced crash is a recovered panic that a liveness check would not record). The reverse direction (a hand-guided boundary probe reaching the v1.4.0 integer overflow) is explicitly labeled hand-guided and chosen with knowledge of the mechanism. This is a model of how to report a baseline comparison: the baseline's weakness is demonstrated, and the resulting claim is narrowed to the configuration actually measured.
   - **1.4 [minor, fixable]** §4.2 excludes the 49 unadjudicated submissions (Milvus 18, Qdrant 11, Weaviate 20) from every rate and states that they "admit no bias estimate". That is honest, but 49 of 130 submissions is 38% of the record; a one-sentence sensitivity (the ledger's rates if all 49 are counted as confirmations, and if all are counted as false positives) would tell the reader how far the snapshot could move.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate *(provisional: I did not survey the field; my assessment is from the paper's own positioning and general knowledge, as my persona requires)*
   - **2.1** The related-work section (§6) does the positioning work carefully and, unusually, concedes what is not new: "a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is therefore not new here, and cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to TestVDB". It names MASTOR (an arXiv preprint at this writing) as the closest prior work and states the claimed delta as an *anchor inversion* — MASTOR's authority is the implementation, whereas TestVDB holds the prose as ground and admits the implementation only as falsifier. It distinguishes Metamon (an LLM-question falsifier), CASCADE (an LLM-artifact cross-check that shares the documentation's ambiguity), TRACE (the doc-plausible/implementation-drift blind spot), and LogicHunter (documentation-retrieval agentic oracle). A general reader can see exactly what is claimed against what.
   - **2.2** Two elements look genuinely new relative to the paper's own account: (a) the audit-and-rebuild of the stage's *own* evidence packs as a measured correction (43.3% of cited constraint–page pairs unsupported; 674 constraint rows surviving out of 2,106 assembled), and (b) the decision to report three scoring conventions side by side with a decomposition of where the measured gap comes from (routing, not forced-accuracy). Practice contributions of this kind are scarcer in this literature than pipelines.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** (provisional) The paper's mechanisms are recombinations of known parts — a builder/judge split, multi-perspective LLM review, source grounding — and the one measured advantage of the integration is scoped to a single model family (§4.3 "Backbone replication"). Whether the anchor-inversion framing plus the measurement methodology clears the novelty bar in a field with contemporaneous works the paper itself cites (MASTOR, LogicHunter, CASCADE) is a judgment I cannot make without the survey; a domain expert should read §6 and decide. If the delta is judged thin there, this is where the paper's verdict would move.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The RQ2 design (§4.3 "Measurement design: audit, rebuild, protocol alignment") is unusually self-correcting: three layers, each isolating one variable — the audit of the shipped packs, the rebuild from version-pinned documentation and the tested version's own OpenAPI artifact, and the alignment of the judging protocol with the deployed chain-auditor rules after discovering that the first re-adjudication protocol carried two pre-deployment rules that suppressed recall by construction. Five configurations × three independent runs over the full 81-candidate pool, every case judged in every run, per-case outputs shipped, and the scoring convention stated before the numbers ("Unless a row or a sentence says otherwise, every rate below is computed on the rebuilt, cleaned packs, with each configuration's three runs reduced by majority vote and Human-Review counted as confirmed").
   - **3.2** The arithmetic closes. Checking from the tables and text alone: every row of Tables 4 and 5 satisfies TP+FN = 51 and FP+TN = 30; the recall, suppression, and precision figures recompute (8/51 = 0.157, 29/30 = 0.967, 39/48 = 0.812, 33/36 = 0.917, 30/34 = 0.882, 25/26 = 0.962, 37/46 = 0.804); the F1 and Youden values recompute (0.788 vs. 0.706 and 0.465 vs. 0.455 on the primary family; 0.698 vs. 0.651 and 0.422 vs. 0.363 on the second, the latter derived from the reported 0.588/0.833 and 0.529/0.833 recall/suppression pairs); the stratum-weighted recalls recompute to the overall values (0.784×37 + 0.667×3 + 0.727×11 = 39; 0.649×37 + 0.000×3 + 0.273×11 = 27; 0.703×37 + 0.333×3 + 0.545×11 = 33; core 8 + 0 + 0 = 8); the sampled Wilson intervals reproduce (8/51, 39/51, 21/30, 29/30, 30/51, 37/51, 19/32); every exact McNemar p-value reproduces (0.0013 for 16/2; 0.0225 for 11/2; 0.0625 for 5/0; 0.041 for 15/5; 0.0075 for 15/3; 0.39 for 8/4; 0.34 for 7/3; 0.11 for 8/2; 0.58 for 8/5); and the Holm multipliers match the declared seven-test family (0.0013×4 = 0.0052; 0.0225×4 = 0.090; 0.0075×3 = 0.023; 0.041×2 = 0.083; the maximal ten-test family gives 0.0013×7 ≈ 0.0092). The RQ1 funnel sums (119 chain verdicts → 96 distinct candidates → 60 defect-confirming → 19 + 11 + 5 + 25 = 60 → 44 deduplicated → five registrations), and the pack-rebuild arithmetic is exact (2,106 − 674 − 463 = 969; 969 − 302 = 667; 667 + 7 = 674 = 22 + 652). What I could not verify is limited to quantities requiring per-case data (the 43.3% and 32% audit rates, the three-run agreement rates, the second family's forced-only p = 0.75); those are declared in the artifact.
   - **3.3** Section 5 measures rather than merely discloses the judgment-side threats: an expectation-framing scan of all 81 rebuilt packs found 19 expectation-flavored phrases, all inside verbatim server responses; the four assembler provenance notes were removed before re-adjudication; ten packs carrying an embedded maintainer-cognition section were cleaned and the 13 affected runs (three core, six source-only, four flat) re-adjudicated; seven candidates' own issue numbers found in the runtime cognition files were removed and the affected verdicts replaced; and the protocol–pool co-evolution is bounded from two directions (the ef/nprobe carve-out flips no verdict on this pool; excluding the 16 objective-constraint-driven confirmations lowers recall to 0.451). The remaining irreducible threats — single backbone, training-data contamination, adjudication disagreement — are stated without softening.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The full-vs-flat separation is the paper's flagship comparison, and it is a single-backbone result: on the primary family it survives Holm correction (raw p = 0.0013; adjusted 0.0052), but the same pair re-run three times on the second family does not separate (discordant 8/5, p = 0.58; §4.3 "Backbone replication"), and the headline recall-only comparison does not survive on the primary family either (raw p = 0.0225; Holm-adjusted 0.090). The paper then writes that "the routing advantage measured on the primary family is a property of that backbone under this protocol, not a law of the protocol itself" — which reads a non-significant, same-direction difference (full 0.588 vs. flat 0.529, i.e., +3 true positives) as evidence of backbone-specificity. At n = 81, that null is underpowered for the claim it is asked to support. The genuinely informative evidence for the mechanism sits in the same paragraph: routing rates fall from 19.8% (primary) to 10.3% (second), consistent with a smaller, harder-to-detect advantage rather than its absence. Reframing the sentence and leaning on the routing-rate covariate is a revision action; left as is, it invites the reading that the paper's central mechanism claim was falsified by its own second family, when what was observed is a non-detection.
   - **3.5 [major, fixable]** The middle reading — the one the paper calls "deployment-faithful" — is produced by the authors: "the authors then adjudicated the routed cases of *both* arms against their pack materials only, without consulting the issue trackers, playing the channel's designated human role (non-blind; disclosed)" (§4.3). This produces the 0.647 recall / 0.917 precision joint result (Table 6), one of the three numbers carried into the abstract. Because the adjudicators wrote the ledger and the packs, and because part of one arm's cases "reuse the adjudications recorded in an earlier pass of this same worksheet", the number is not an independent measurement of the routing channel's value. The three-reading bracket (forced 0.529 below, convention 0.765 above) mitigates but does not remove the concern: if the middle reading is biased upward, the bracket's center moves. Independent, ideally arm-blinded adjudication of the routed cases — 21 for the full stage and 11 for the flat judge, 32 in total — with an agreement statistic would settle it, and the case lists are small enough to make that feasible in a revision.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** The protocol–pool co-evolution threat is bounded by excluding the 16 objective-constraint-driven confirmations (recall falls from 0.765 to 0.451), but no symmetric exclusion exists for the flat judge ("its single-prompt design records no perspective attribution", §5), so the bounding is one-sided. Since the flat judge's dispatch retains the objective-constraint principle with four of the seven classes as examples (Appendix A.1/A.2), the cleaner sensitivity would be a flat-judge run with those red lines stripped — or a B-blind full stage — so both arms are measured on and off the same rule content.
   - **3.7 [minor, fixable]** Table 1 is the paper's motivating negative claim — that deterministic oracle families structurally miss documentation-implementation bugs — and its caption discloses that rows 2–6 are "argued from each family's anchoring mechanism" while only rows 1 and 7 are exercised directly. The argument is plausible as far as it goes (the exclusion is a claim about where each family gets its expectation), but for contribution 1 to land as an empirical as well as a conceptual point, exercising one argued row — a schema/property-based oracle, or a cross-vendor differential probe on the same versions — would test whether the residual is as empty in practice as the anchoring argument implies.
   - **3.8 [minor, fixable]** The seeded anchor is used to bound the direction of the submission filter (§4.2: "never-submitted registrations confirm at 0.594, a point estimate below the adjudicated pool's 63.0%"), but the two rates come from different instruments: 0.594 is the TestVDB stage's own re-confirmation rate on 32 non-submitted registrations (19/32), while 63.0% is the maintainers' confirmation rate on the submitted pool. On the submitted pool, the stage's own convention-level confirmation rate is 48/81 = 0.593 — essentially identical to the anchor — so the like-for-like comparison does not obviously point the same way as the cross-instrument one. One sentence distinguishing the measures (and repeating the pack/protocol caveat the paper already records for the anchor) would make the direction claim inspectable.

4. **Verifiability** — Excellent
   - **4.1** The replication package is declared (Data Availability) at an anonymized URL with an inventory that matches the claims: agent role definitions and the strategy-trigger registry, per-version knowledge bases and specifications, all generated scripts with raw HTTP logs, one evidence chain per candidate, the submission ledger for all 81 adjudicated plus 49 unadjudicated submissions, the original and rebuilt RQ2 packs with the pack-audit verdict records and per-case judge outputs for every arm and run, the RQ3 baseline reports with per-template logs, and the analysis scripts that recompute every rate, interval, and paired test. Per the rubric I judged the link as declared from the text and did not fetch, clone, or run anything.
   - **4.2** The text is procedure-complete in outline even without the artifact: §3 names the intermediate artifacts (`knowledge.json`, `specifications.json`, `evidence_chain.json`), the two extraction self-checks, the evidence-tier system, the five generation gates, the executor's log/flushed-marker discipline, and the auditor's four checks, four perspectives, rebuild bound, and fixed aggregation order; Appendix A reproduces both judging prompts, with the verbatim Chinese dispatch files declared authoritative. A reader can follow how each number was produced and where the raw material lives.
   - **4.3** The replication limits are disclosed where they matter: serving identifiers and run dates rather than weight snapshots (§4.1, §5); the second-family run orchestrated from that family's own session, so backbone and runtime co-vary (§4.3, §5); and the anchor measured on the original packs under the pre-audit protocol, so its absolute value is not comparable to the rebuilt-package numbers (§4.3).
   - **4.4 [minor, fixable]** One sample cannot be reconstructed from the text. §4.2's process ledger ends by reporting that 44 deduplicated candidates "register five new defect numbers", while §4.3's anchor is "32 candidates seeded from the reported Qdrant run's registrations that never entered the 81-pool (four further registrations, tracked to a ledger issue, were excluded)". The relationship between "registrations", "defect numbers", and candidates is never defined, so a reader cannot tell whether 36 registrations is consistent with five registered defect numbers, or how the sampled 32 relate to the 44. One sentence plus a pointer to the registry would close the gap.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1** The structure is complete and conventional (Introduction, Background, Approach, Evaluation, Limitations, Related Work, Conclusion; six tables; an appendix with the two prompts), and the captions carry real information: Table 1 states what is argued versus exercised; Tables 4–6 state the majority rule and the scoring convention; Table 6's caption matches the numbers the text quotes from it ("matching the caption of Table 6"). The "false-positive candidate" versus "False-Positive verdict" distinction is drawn explicitly rather than left to the reader, as is the three-valued verdict vocabulary.
   - **5.2 [major, fixable]** The paper's single biggest obstacle is compression. The abstract runs three paragraphs, the latter two carrying roughly two dozen numeric claims between them, and a reader meets the three scoring readings before §4.3 defines them; §4.3's load-bearing paragraphs are long multi-sentence blocks — the flagship-comparison paragraph ("Structure is a measured mechanism—and the decomposition locates it") spends nine sentences carrying the comparison, the discordance counts, the ground-truth decomposition, the F1/Youden comparison, the ordering of four arms, and the underpowering caveat, while the joint-adjudication paragraph spends eleven sentences on the hand-adjudication pass and its equal-treatment counterpart, with nearly every sentence carrying its own bracketed statistics. I was able to extract and check every claim (nothing is actually undetermined), which is why this lands at Adequate rather than Weak, but the paper asks a reader to hold four arms, three readings, and two backbones in mind while parsing nested em-dash clauses. A short reader's guide — one paragraph defining the three readings with pointers to Table 6 — plus splitting the longest sentences would do more for this paper's reception than any additional result. Readers less determined than a checker will lose the thread, and the loss will be attributed to the work rather than the prose.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** The evidence-absent paragraph (§4.3 "Where recall lives: the evidence-absent stratum") closes with "The documentation still constrains a minority of the defect surface", which contradicts the stratum counts it has just given: 37 of the 51 packs are evidence-present (3 weak, 11 absent), so by its own data the documentation constrains the majority of the pool's defect surface. The same sentence's "where it constrains nothing, no scoring of this stage is strong" also sits oddly beside the 0.727 convention score in that stratum (higher than the forced-verdict score of the evidence-present stratum, 0.649). The data support "the evidence-absent stratum does not lead under any scoring"; they do not support the minority phrasing.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** §4.1 glosses Qdrant v1.18.0 as "the instance our Qdrant defects were found on", but the running example the paper threads through §3 (Qdrant #10369) is on v1.19.0, and the Qdrant candidates span five versions (v1.12.1–v1.19.0, per the same subsection). §4.4's phrasing — "the same v1.18.0 instance the reported run's registrations came from" — is the accurate one and should be used consistently.
   - **5.5 [minor, fixable]** Terminology and notation nits: "documentation-implementation bugs" (§1) and "documentation-implementation inconsistency" (§2.2) alternate for the same object; the abstract uses "the deployment's counting convention" before §4.3 defines the three readings; Table 3's Other-row gloss "(Qdrant #9045; one of three)" is hard to parse (it means one of the three Other bugs); the §1 claim of "a controlled five-arm evaluation" undercounts the configurations actually run, since §4.3 additionally re-runs the full and flat arms on the second family.
   - **5.6 [minor, fixable]** I could evaluate only the caption of Figure 1 (the pipeline diagram), as the review copy carries the source rather than the rendered figure. Neither the RQ1 funnel nor the three scoring readings has a visual, although both are inherently graphical and both are among the paper's most re-reading-intensive material; a single small schematic of the three conventions against one pool would substitute for a great deal of prose.

### Questions for Authors

- **Q1:** In one sentence a non-specialist can check: what does the anchor inversion buy over MASTOR's source-grounded oracles and LogicHunter's documentation-retrieval oracle, beyond where the final authority sits? — *Intended effect: if the delta can be stated as something measurable (e.g., a defect class unreachable by both), item 2.3's provisional Adequate would move up; if it cannot, domain experts will likely rate Novelty Weak, and the paper should reposition before submission.*
- **Q2:** Is the anchor's 0.594 (the stage's own re-confirmation rate on 32 non-submitted registrations) comparable to the 63.0% maintainer rate on the submitted pool, given that the stage's convention-level confirmation rate on the submitted pool is 48/81 = 0.593? — *Intended effect: if the authors can give the like-for-like comparison (stage on submitted vs. stage on non-submitted) and it still points the same way, item 3.8 resolves; if it does not, the "filter selects stronger candidates" direction claim needs restating, and Soundness moves up.*
- **Q3:** Can the objective-constraint sensitivity be symmetrized — a flat-judge run without the four enumerated classes as automatic confirmations, or a B-blind full stage? — *Intended effect: if the gap survives symmetric exclusion, item 3.6's concern disappears and the headline gap is shown not to be manufactured by the B class; if it does not, the headline needs the same "measured mechanism" scoping the routing result already carries.*
- **Q4:** What would a powered second-family replication need, and is "backbone-specific" the right reading of a same-direction 8/5 discordance at p = 0.58 — versus "not established either way", with the routing-rate drop from 19.8% to 10.3% as the mechanism-consistent explanation? — *Intended effect: a reframing toward the covariate would remove the one place where the paper over-reads its own null, and would let item 3.4 drop from major.*
- **Q5:** Were the routed cases adjudicated without knowing which arm (and which run) each case came from, and were the 32 routed cases (21 full, 11 flat) adjudicated consistently with the four already recorded in an earlier pass of the same worksheet? — *Intended effect: an arm-blinded, independently executed pass would firm up the 0.647 reading, which is the item most likely to keep Soundness at Adequate; without it, the "deployment-faithful" label on item 3.5 remains contestable.*
