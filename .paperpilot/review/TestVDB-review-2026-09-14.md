## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where the system silently accepts an input or exhibits behavior that violates its natural-language API documentation. Because the documented constraint lives in prose rather than in any structured source, the authors argue that no deterministic oracle family derives its expectation from that prose, leaving an LLM-derived oracle as the practical option — with the usual false-positive risk. TestVDB answers with a four-stage pipeline (documentation-to-specification extraction, pre-bound-strategy probe generation, Docker-pinned sandboxed execution, and a bug-confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly from a four-perspective cross-examination anchored in the implementation source). Across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs and fixed 23 via merged PRs. The evaluation's core is an unusually self-critical re-measurement: an audit found 43.3% of the cited (constraint, page) pairs in the original evidence packages unsupported, so all 81 packages were rebuilt from version-pinned documentation and the full pool re-adjudicated three times per configuration under a frozen protocol. The contract-only core suppresses 96.7% of false positives but recalls 0.157; the structured full stage reaches 0.765 recall at 0.700 suppression under the deployment counting convention versus 0.588 for a flat single-prompt judge (exact McNemar p=0.0013, Holm-corrected 0.0052), with three scoring conventions (forced 0.529/0.490; joint judge-plus-human 0.647/0.549; convention 0.765/0.588) reported side by side and the separation attributed to the human-review routing channel rather than forced-verdict accuracy. The full-vs-flat pair does not separate on a second model family (p=0.58), which the paper discloses prominently. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows complementary reach: the fuzzer's full 205-template set reaches none of the confirmed silent-accept defects, while a hand-guided boundary probe reaches VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflow.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Exceptional measurement discipline: a three-layer audit/rebuild/re-adjudication design in which the authors discovered and remediated four of their own leak channels (assembler provenance notes, expectation framing, embedded cognition sections, candidate-anchored cognition entries), re-running everything affected and shipping pre-cleanup verdicts — see 3.1, 4.1.
- **S2:** Real, dated operational impact: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs (23 merged fix PRs, every one touching implementation code, none documentation-only) across three production VDBMSs, with the fix-PR classifier validated against a known-disproved case — see 1.1.
- **S3:** The paper measures its own central mechanism instead of asserting it: three scoring conventions side by side, a declared seven-test Holm family whose raw p-values and multipliers I recomputed exactly, equal-treatment hand adjudication of both arms' routed cases, and a prominent disclosure that the full-vs-flat separation does not replicate on a second backbone — see 3.2, 3.3, 3.4.
- **S4:** Verified and accurate positioning against the documentation/oracle literature: the characterizations of MASTOR, CASCADE, Metamon, SATORI, AGORA+, MASTEST, Konstantinou et al., and VDBFuzz all check out against the competitors' own texts, including verbatim quotations — see 2.1.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The central mechanism claim is a single-backbone result: the full-vs-flat separation and its routing account do not replicate on the second model family (p=0.58; routing rate drops from 19.8%/8.6% to 10.3%/8.6%). The claims are honestly re-scoped, but the generalizable contribution then rests on one backbone — see 3.4, 1.2.
- **W2:** The central RQ2 exhibit contains irreconciled numeric accounting: five different counts of "routed confirmations" (10/12/15/16/17), a table caption ("4 routed false positives") arithmetically incompatible with the joint-scoring row it annotates, a forced-verdict row (27) two cases below what the paper's own component split implies (29), a flat-arm joint row not derivable from its stated adjudication dispositions, and stale rates (19.8% vs 20.6%) — see 3.5, 5.2.
- **W3:** Ground truth and pool construction bound every rate: maintainer adjudication is fallible (one fixed true positive was disproved by the authors' own seven-version re-probe), the 81-candidate pool is author-screened, and 49 submissions remain unadjudicated; all disclosed and direction-bounded, but the rates remain adjudicated-subset figures — see 3.6.
- **W4:** Minor positioning gaps: the Argus citation is now published (PACMMOD 4(3), 2026) but is cited as an arXiv preprint, and Bayesilisk (2026) articulates the same "LLM proposes, non-LLM verifies" separation in E2E testing without a positioning sentence — see 2.3.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): silent misbehavior — an accepted out-of-range parameter, an ignored field, a wrong success — that violates the system's natural-language API documentation and therefore escapes crash-oracle fuzzing. Because the documented constraints exist only as untagged prose, the authors argue that every deterministic-oracle family anchors its expectation elsewhere (Table 1: crash signals, another implementation, output relations, machine-checkable properties, structured specs), leaving an LLM-derived oracle as the practical option — and importing its false-positive problem. Their system, TestVDB, is a four-stage pipeline: documentation-to-specification extraction, strategy-bound probe generation, sandboxed execution, and a two-agent confirmation stage in which an evidence builder assembles a five-section evidence chain per candidate and a chain auditor cross-examines it from four perspectives (contract, physical constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition) with the implementation source as the falsification anchor. Across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, maintainers confirmed 51 of 81 adjudicated submissions as real bugs, 23 fixed via merged PRs.

The measurement half re-adjudicates that pool under blinding. After an audit found 43.3% of the frozen evidence packages' cited (constraint, page) pairs unsupported by the referenced pages, every package was rebuilt against version-pinned documentation and every case re-judged three times per arm on one backbone, with four leak channels audited and remediated (pre-cleanup verdict files retained only for the two cognition-channel re-adjudications). The contract-only core suppresses 96.7% of the 30 adjudicated false positives but recalls 0.157 of the 51 true bugs; the structured full stage reaches 0.765 recall at 0.700 suppression under the deployment's counting convention, against 0.588 for a flat single-prompt judge on the same packs and sources (exact McNemar p=0.0013; Holm-corrected 0.0052 over a declared seven-test family, and 0.0092 under a maximal ten-test family). The paper reports three scoring conventions side by side — forced verdicts (0.529 vs. 0.490, indistinguishable), hand-adjudicated joint scoring (0.647 recall, 0.917 precision), and the convention (0.765) — attributes the separation to the human-review routing channel rather than forced-verdict accuracy, discloses that the flagship pair does not separate on a second model family, and probes the crash-oracle baseline (VDBFuzz) bidirectionally, including a measured bound on the released tool's reach.

## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where the system silently accepts an input or behaves in a way that contradicts its natural-language API documentation, and therefore produces no crash for a crash-oracle fuzzer to observe. It argues that every deterministic-oracle family anchors its expectation somewhere other than system-level, untagged prose (Table 1), leaving an LLM-derived oracle as the practical option, and proposes TestVDB: a four-stage pipeline of behavioral-specification extraction from vendor documentation, pre-bound attack-strategy test generation, sandboxed execution against Docker-pinned versions, and a bug-confirmation stage in which an evidence builder assembles a five-section evidence chain and a chain auditor cross-examines it through four perspectives (contract, objective physical constraints, behavioral elegance, maintainer cognition), with the implementation source as the falsification anchor.

The paper reports a maintainer-adjudicated submission ledger across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate (51 of 81 adjudicated submissions confirmed, 23 fixed by merged PRs), an audit of the confirmation stage's own frozen evidence packages (43.3% of cited constraint-page pairs unsupported by their pages) followed by a rebuild and three-run re-adjudication of the full 81-candidate pool under five judge configurations, and a bidirectional reach comparison against the crash-oracle fuzzer VDBFuzz on Qdrant. The re-adjudication is reported under three scoring conventions side by side (forced verdicts, joint judge-plus-human, and a deployment convention that counts routed Human-Review cases as confirmed), and the paper attributes the structured protocol's advantage over a flat single-prompt judge to its routing channel rather than to better forced judgment, with a second-backbone re-run that does not reproduce the full-vs-flat separation.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The paper audits the evidence packages feeding its own confirmation stage, finds them substantially unsupported, rebuilds them against version-pinned documentation, and discloses and repairs four separate contamination channels before reporting any rate — a level of self-correction rare in this kind of evaluation. — see 3.1, 3.2, 4.1
- **S2:** The empirical yield is real and independently adjudicated: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs and 23 merged fixes across three production systems, with the fix-nature classification checking that "fixed" means implementation repair rather than documentation or test churn. — see 1.1, 1.2
- **S3:** The oracle-exclusion analysis and the bidirectional comparison with the crash-oracle baseline are careful, including the dissection showing the released baseline runner reports 100% success against a dead port, and an explicit withdrawal of an earlier explanation when the released templates contradicted it. — see 2.1, 3.3
- **S4:** Negative and inconvenient results are reported rather than buried: the forced-verdict comparison collapses, the second backbone does not separate the full-vs-flat pair, the evidence-absent stratum is weak under every scoring, and the protocol-pool co-evolution is bounded rather than denied. — see 3.2, 3.7

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The routed-case accounting in §4.3 does not reconcile: the printed routed-confirmation counts (15, 16, 17, 12), the routed false positives (six vs. four), the hand-adjudication breakdown (9+2+6=17 vs. the 12 implied by the recall delta), and the flat arm's routed count (11 vs. the 5 implied by its recall delta) are mutually inconsistent, and they sit directly under the paper's central comparison. — see 3.4
- **W2:** The headline full-vs-flat contrast (0.765 vs. 0.588) is stated under a counting convention the paper itself shows overstates the joint system (0.647); the abstract, contribution list, and conclusion lead with the convention number while the more defensible joint number is subordinated. — see 1.3, 3.2
- **W3:** Two p-values are not reproducible from the printed data under the stated test convention: the primary forced-verdict p=0.34 (a discordant net of 2 cannot give two-sided exact McNemar below 0.5), and the second-family pair reported as p=0.58 in §4.3 but p=1.0 in the abstract, introduction, and limitations without stating the correction that produced it. — see 3.5
- **W4:** The comparison's two known asymmetries — the full stage's maintainer-cognition access and its fuller rule enumeration — are bounded by argument rather than by a control run, even though the paper's own decomposition attributes two confirmations to cognition and two of the three un-flat-confirmed objective-class cases to classes the flat prompt omits. — see 3.6
- **W5:** The pool is the authors' own submissions after author-side screening, re-judged against the maintainer verdicts that motivated them, with a protocol whose objective-constraint classes were codified from the same mining experience; absolute rates are therefore properties of this pool and backbone, which a revision cannot change. — see 3.7

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The problem is specific, well-motivated, and shown to be real: §1–§2 tie VDBMS correctness to retrieval-augmented applications, cite the empirical studies on non-crashing functional failures, and anchor the class with a concrete instance (Milvus's documented `nprobe` range of [1,16384] accepting 0). The ledger statistic that only 2 of the 51 confirmed bugs crash (§1) makes the target class concrete rather than rhetorical.
   - **1.2** Table 2's ledger is the strongest practical evidence in the paper: 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 merged fixes, with the collection protocol stated up front (confirmed / false-positive / duplicate-tracked definitions), a fix-nature classification, and label-based acceptance signals. For a practitioner, this establishes that a pipeline of this shape produces actionable reports.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** What a reader cannot estimate is the autonomous pipeline's yield: the process funnel (§4.2) reports one Qdrant run reaching five registrations, and the paper explicitly declines to give the automated stage an end-to-end precision or recall on its raw output (§4.2, §5). The 0.594 anchor bounds the direction of the submission filter but was measured on original packs over 32 candidates, so it cannot carry a yield claim. A short paragraph stating what is and is not known about raw-stream yield would let a reader size the impact honestly.
   - **1.4 [minor, fixable]** The cost profile in §4.1 (≈67 active hours, ≈860 script executions, ≈70M input tokens for the reported run) is welcome, but it is never connected to yield; against five registrations in that run's funnel the reader cannot judge cost-effectiveness relative to cheaper alternatives (manual API probing, or the baseline fuzzer run reported in §4.4).

2. **Novelty** — Adequate *(provisional: assessed only from the paper's own related-work account; I did not survey the literature)*
   - **2.1** The organizing criterion of Table 1 — where each oracle candidate gets its expectation — produces a clear, non-obvious claim: the residual is system-level, untagged behavioral prose, and TestVDB inverts the usual roles by taking the prose as ground truth and the implementation source as falsifier. The paper articulates this against its closest named neighbor MASTOR (source-grounded oracles) and against the documentation-derivation line, and the delta is recognizable.
   - **2.2** The audit-and-rebuild measurement design (§4.3, Layer 0→2) — auditing the judge's own input packs for unsupported citations and leaked provenance, rebuilding them, and re-adjudicating under an aligned protocol — reads as a genuinely fresh methodological contribution: most evaluations of LLM judges report a number and stop, whereas this one first measures the quality of what the judge was given.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** By the paper's own related work, two of the recombination elements are conceded: "a generated oracle reviewed by a second LLM agent is therefore not new here, and cross-request behavioural oracles are not unique to TestVDB" (§6, via MASTOR's ChallengerAgent). The builder/auditor split with source grounding is presented there as the paper's own answer, not as prior art, so the delta is the anchor inversion plus the confirmation architecture — real but incremental.
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** Whether the anchor-inversion delta is genuinely unclaimed can only be judged against the literature, which I did not survey (provisional); if a documentation-first oracle with source falsification exists, this delta narrows.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The measurement design is unusually defensive. After finding 43.3% of cited pairs unsupported, the authors rebuilt all 81 packs from version-pinned documentation and the tested version's own OpenAPI artifact, replaced the leaked provenance notes, aligned the judging protocol with the deployed auditor rules, and then disclosed and repaired four further channels — expectation framing (scanned, zero added), observation leakage, embedded cognition sections, and candidate-anchored cognition entries — re-adjudicating the affected cases in every arm and run on both backbones, with the pre-cleanup verdict files shipped (§4.3, §5). This is what makes the reported rates credible.
   - **3.2** The three scoring conventions are reported side by side (Table 6) and the paper states plainly that the separation is carried by the routing channel rather than by forced-verdict accuracy, where the two judges are indistinguishable (0.529 vs. 0.490, p=0.34). Reporting the reading that undercuts one's own headline, and naming routing rather than accuracy as the mechanism, is exactly the right behavior for this kind of claim.
   - **3.3** §4.4's treatment of the baseline is careful and self-correcting: it shows the released runner's success counter reports 100% success against a port with no server, substitutes a per-template liveness oracle, verifies its own zero with response logs (23,258 responses across 96 endpoint paths), bounds that zero from two directions, and withdraws an earlier "synchronous path only" explanation when three released templates contradict it. The conclusion is drawn narrowly ("on that configuration the crash class is not constructible").
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** RQ2's routed-case and leak accounting does not reconcile, and it is the one place where I could not reconstruct the paper's story from its own numbers. Recomputing from the printed table rows: the true-positive cases that flip between forced and convention scoring must be exactly 12 (39−27), yet the text calls the stage's routed confirmations "15 of the full stage's 39" (line 314), "16 carry at least one Human-Review component" (line 308), "the full stage's 17 routed confirmations" (Table 6 caption, line 317), and separately "12 of the 14 confirm only because the protocol routes them" (line 314); the hand adjudication is printed as 9 confirmed + 2 material-insufficient + 6 rejected = 17, while the joint recall of 33 = 27 + 6 is consistent only with six rejections acting on a routed set of 12. On the flat arm, convention − forced = 5 (30−25), yet the caption reports 11 routed confirmations and the text's breakdown sums to 9 (4+2+3). The routed false positives are "six" in the text and "4" in the caption. The Human-Review share of full-stage verdicts is 19.8% (48 of 243 case judgments) in §4.3 and 20.6% in §5, where §5 states no denominator — two different shares for the same quantity. The leak enumeration is two forced disagreements in §4.3 and three candidates in §5, with the second case described differently in the two places (a batch-delete case "whose probe value may never have bound" vs. a "shardsNum case whose probe payload a source reading suggests never bound", plus a separate "batch-delete case judged through a near-version clone"). The table rows themselves are internally consistent (every arm's TP+FN = 51 and leak+TN = 30), so the defect is confined to the prose and captions — but it sits under the paper's central comparison and would have to be fixed before these numbers are quoted.
   - **3.5 [major, fixable]** Two p-values do not reproduce under the convention used elsewhere. (a) The primary forced-verdict comparison (27/51 vs. 25/51) fixes the discordant net at 2; the two-sided exact McNemar p is then at least 0.5 (2/0 → 0.50; 3/1 → 0.625; 4/2 → 0.69), so p=0.34 (line 314) must be one-sided (4/2 → 0.344) or computed on different data — the paper should say which. (b) The same second-family comparison is reported as "exact McNemar p=0.58" in §4.3 and as "p=1.0" in the abstract, introduction, and §5; raw 0.58 → Holm-adjusted 1.0 is plausible but the correction is never stated, and the joint comparison (p=0.039) is reported without any correction at all. Since a Holm-corrected claim is one of the paper's headline statistical results, inconsistent reporting of raw vs. corrected p elsewhere is a real hazard.
   - **3.6 [major, fixable]** The two asymmetries between the full stage and the flat judge are bounded by argument rather than by a control run. First, the full stage reads maintainer-cognition materials that the flat judge is forbidden, and the paper asserts this "contributes to none of the separation beyond one already-disclosed case" (line 314) without a cognition-withheld run — even though the per-perspective decomposition attributes 2 of 39 confirmations to cognition, and §5 records that the cognition files had to be cleaned of candidate-anchored entries. Routing (the mechanism the paper credits) is exactly where a cognition signal could act without appearing in the confirmation attribution. Second, the appendix argues the flat prompt's shorter rule enumeration is conservative because the flat judge confirms 13 of the 16 objective-constraint-driven confirmations — but for 2 of the 3 it misses, the missing class is precisely a class the flat prompt omits, so the asymmetry did contribute there. Either a control run (full stage without cognition access; flat judge with all seven classes) or an explicit routing-level attribution would settle both.
   - **3.7 [major, unfixable]** The evaluation pool is the authors' own submissions after novelty screening and manual review (§4.2), judged against the same maintainer verdicts that motivated them, with a deployed protocol whose objective-constraint classes were codified from the same mining experience that produced the pool (§5).    - **3.8 [minor, fixable]** The arms are compared at their own operating points: full 0.765 recall at 0.700 suppression, flat 0.588 at 0.867, D-only 0.706 at 0.733, core at 0.157/0.967.    - **3.9 [minor, fixable]** The joint readings (0.647/0.917 full, 0.549/0.966 flat) rest on author-executed, non-blind adjudication of the routed cases under a pack-materials rule (§4.3).
