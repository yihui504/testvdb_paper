## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Reject

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where the system silently accepts an input or exhibits a behavior that violates its own API documentation (e.g., Milvus accepting `nprobe=0` against a documented range of [1, 16384]). It first argues structurally, via a candidate-by-candidate exclusion table, that every deterministic-oracle family (crash signals, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property/schema-based testing, and the recent REST-oracle line anchored in OpenAPI fields, traces, or source) cannot derive its expectation from natural-language prose, which forces an LLM-derived oracle. It then presents TestVDB, a four-stage multi-agent pipeline: extraction of typed, evidence-leveled, source-verified behavioral specifications from crawled documentation; probe generation under deterministically pre-bound attack strategies; sandboxed execution against Docker-pinned targets; and a bug-confirmation stage that assembles a five-section evidence chain per candidate and cross-examines it from four perspectives (contract, physical behavior, maintainer cognition mined from issue history, and implementation source), the last serving as the falsification anchor intended to break the self-referential loop of an LLM that both writes and judges the oracle.

Empirically, across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, 81 adjudicated upstream submissions yielded 51 maintainer-confirmed new bugs, 23 of them fixed by merged PRs that all modify implementation code, organized into an eight-pattern taxonomy dominated by silent acceptance. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz on Qdrant reports that 26,000 mutated crash-oracle requests reached 0 of 7 live silent-accept true positives, while TestVDB's boundary reasoning reaches one of VDBFuzz's crash-class integer overflows. The false-positive interception experiment (RQ2) and the equal-budget head-to-head (RQ3) are marked as pending re-runs and appear as TODO placeholders.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Verified real-world impact: 51 maintainer-confirmed new bugs with 23 implementation-changing merged fixes across three production VDBMSs, reported through a protocol that treats adjudication as revisable and audits fix nature — see 1.1, 3.1.
- **S2:** A well-posed, externally checkable problem formalization: the silent-majority premise rests on cited empirical studies I verified, and the structural oracle-exclusion analysis cleanly delimits where an LLM-derived oracle is forced — see 1.2, 2.1, 2.2.
- **S3:** The pipeline is described at followable depth, with one real bug threaded through all four stages as a running example — see 4.1.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The paper's distinctive claim — that the evidence-chain confirmation stage breaks the LLM's extraction-judgment self-reference — is completely unevaluated in this draft: RQ2 is a TODO, so the central mechanism has no supporting evidence — see 3.2.
- **W2:** RQ3's complementarity evidence is one-directional and thin (a single crash-class bug in the reverse direction, on a different version, with an equal-budget head-to-head pending), while the abstract already asserts the complementarity conclusion — see 3.3.
- **W3:** No variance or statistical treatment anywhere, despite the paper itself citing intra-judge self-inconsistency as motivation and running a single backbone under default (non-deterministic) sampling — see 3.4.
- **W4:** Related-work coverage gaps a program committee will notice: the nearest uncited mechanism (CASCADE), several uncited LLM-doc-oracle contemporaries, the classical documentation-code inconsistency line, and the canonical DBMS logic-bug line are all absent from the rendered References; Toradocu is also mischaracterized as LLM-based — see 2.3, 2.4.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Excellent
   - **1.1** The headline result (Section 4.2, Table 2) — 51 confirmed bugs of 81 adjudicated submissions, 23 fixed by merged PRs, on Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate — is the kind of impact this venue values: new, maintainer-validated defects in widely deployed infrastructure that backs retrieval-augmented LLM applications. The fix-nature audit (all 23 fix PRs modify implementation code, 15 add regression tests, none documentation-only) and the validity check applying the classifier to the disproved #9149 give the "fixed" count real weight.
   - **1.2** The problem motivation is externally checkable and checks out. I verified the cited empirical study (Xie et al., arXiv:2506.02617): its abstract states that of 1,671 analyzed bug-fix PRs across 15 VDBMSs, "more than half manifesting as functional failures," matching the paper's silent-majority premise verbatim. The cited testing roadmap (arXiv:2502.20812) does name oracle definition among the key challenges in its abstract, supporting the claim that this residual is a recognized open problem.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The practical significance of the *approach* (as opposed to the bug yield) is contingent on the pending RQ2: submission precision is 63.0% on the adjudicated subset only, 49 submissions still await maintainer response, and whether the confirmation stage keeps the false-positive burden tolerable for real deployment is exactly what the missing experiment measures. The paper is honest about this (Section 5), but a reader cannot yet judge deployability.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** The delta against the closest prior work holds, and is in fact stronger than the paper states. I read MASTOR (arXiv:2606.10465) in full: it generates oracles grounded in source-verified facts, explicitly demoting OAS-declared items that the source does not substantiate to unread "pending" fields, and its own limitations section states verbatim that it "cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code." TestVDB inverts this direction — documentation-derived specifications falsified against source — which is a genuine, articulable novelty, and Table 1's where-does-the-expectation-come-from framing is a clean way to delimit it.
   - **2.2** For the target domain the claim to be first is supported: the only dedicated VDBMS testing tool, VDBFuzz (ICSE 2026), is verified from its official abstract to be a crash-bug fuzzer via boundary-value input generation, and my scoped coverage searches surfaced no prior documentation-conformance testing tool for VDBMSs.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** Mischaracterization of the doc-oracle prior line. Section 2.4 and Related Work describe "a prior line [that] derives oracles from documentation with LLMs~\cite{toradocu16,augmentest25}." I read Toradocu (ISSTA 2016) in full: it uses the Stanford Parser, open information extraction, Levenshtein-distance lexical matching, and fixed textual patterns — classical NLP with no LLM anywhere (unsurprising for 2016). The differentiation argument ("keeps the LLM as the final arbiter") is predicated on this premise and needs rewording against the actual LLM-era members of that line (e.g., AugmenTest, and the uncited works in 2.4).
   - **2.4 [major, fixable]** Missing related work, verified at abstract level: (a) **CASCADE** (arXiv:2604.19400, 2026) generates unit tests from natural-language documentation and suppresses false positives by additionally generating code from the documentation, reporting an inconsistency only when the doc-generated code passes a test the real code fails — the nearest published mechanism to this paper's doc-derived-tests-plus-independent-falsifier design, and it is uncited; (b) the LLM-documentation-oracle contemporaries the field will expect to see positioned: Doc2OracLL (FSE 2025), ChatAssert (TSE 2025), Testora (ICSE 2026), Konstantinou et al. (arXiv:2410.21136, which asks precisely whether LLM oracles capture documented vs. actual behavior), RBCTest (arXiv:2504.17287, which found 46 spec-vs-response mismatches with an observation-confirmation scheme to reduce hallucination), and RESTOR (arXiv:2607.23963); none is cited; (c) the classical documentation-code inconsistency detection line — @tComment (ICST 2012), DocRef/"Detecting API documentation errors" (OOPSLA 2013), iComment (SOSP 2007), and Rubio-González and Liblit's error-code mismatch study (PASTE 2010), all documented in Toradocu's related-work section that I read — none cited; (d) the canonical DBMS logic-bug testing line (NoREC, TLP, DQE, DDLCheck, BuzzBEE), which is absent from the rendered References even though the paper claims the logic-bug-in-databases territory and its Table 1 discusses differential testing generically. These are position-and-cite fixes, not redesign, but their absence currently overstates the openness of the field.

3. **Soundness** — Weak
   - **3.1** What is present is reported soundly. The RQ1 collection protocol (Section 4.2) defines confirmed/duplicate/false-positive operationalizations, re-verifies every submission state as of September 2026, excludes pending submissions from all rates, and — notably — treats adjudication as revisable: the authors' own seven-version re-probe disproved #9149, whose "fix" PR turned out to be test-only, and they reclassified it. Internal arithmetic is consistent throughout (81 = 51 + 30; 23 + 18 + 7 + 3 = 51; the eight-pattern taxonomy sums to 51). The limitations section is candid about construct, internal, and external threats, including two judgment-side biases (expectation-framing, observation leakage) that many papers would omit.
   - **3.2 [major, fixable]** The central mechanism is unevaluated. The paper's claimed answer to the LLM false-positive problem — evidence chains cross-examined from four perspectives and grounded in source — is exactly what RQ2 would measure, and RQ2 is entirely a TODO (Section 4.3): the confusion matrix, precision/recall/suppression rates, CIs, and both attribution analyses are all placeholders. Contribution bullet 3 accordingly advertises an evaluation that does not exist in the draft. Until these numbers land, the paper's differentiation from the Toradocu/AugmenTest line ("grounds every confirmation in an evidence chain") is a design claim, not a finding, and I cannot assess it. This single gap is what holds Soundness at Weak; the disclosed framing and leakage biases also remain unquantified until this experiment reports.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** RQ3's complementarity evidence is asymmetric and thin. The systematic direction is structurally sound — a crash oracle cannot fire on an HTTP-200 silent accept, so VDBFuzz reaching 0 of 7 such bugs on v1.18.2 is expected by construction — but the reverse direction rests on a single bug (the `size=2^63` overflow, live only on v1.4.0), the two directions run on different versions with different protocols, the equal-budget head-to-head is a TODO, and VDBFuzz's configuration disclosure is limited to "default configuration" over 26,000 requests. I verified VDBFuzz's official abstract: it targets crashes arising from "boundary condition failures such as invalid configurations and mismatched data dimensions," i.e., its input space overlaps the very boundary strategies TestVDB uses — the paper would benefit from analyzing that overlap rather than only the crash-oracle structural argument. The paper's claim that "the two Qdrant crashes VDBFuzz reports in its own case study" are fixed in v1.18.2 is not verifiable from any public source I could reach (no OA PDF exists yet; the abstract names 19 crash bugs across 8 systems) — provisional. The abstract's present-tense assertion that "the two oracles reach complementary bug classes" runs ahead of this evidence.
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** No variance or statistical treatment anywhere in the paper. All agents run on a single backbone (GLM-5.2) under *default sampling*, i.e., non-deterministic decoding — while the paper itself cites Haldar et al. on intra-judge self-inconsistency as motivation for its design. There are no repeated runs of the pipeline, no stability measure for the confirmed-bug set, and no confidence interval on the 63.0% precision (Wilson CIs appear only inside the RQ2 TODO). For a paper whose thesis is LLM-judge reliability, unquantified run-to-run variance of its own judges is a notable gap; peers in this area typically pin temperature or repeat and report agreement.
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** Smaller soundness blemishes: (a) Qdrant #9045 is described as "panic on zero-length vector" in Table 3 but as "wait=false silently accepts a zero-length vector" in Section 4.4 — the same issue id is given two different manifestations, and this interacts with Table 1's claim that only 2 of the 51 confirmed bugs produce a crash or panic; the counts should be reconciled. (b) Section 2.4's factual premises "several serve no OpenAPI at all" and "cross-vendor behavior diverges by design~\cite{roadmap25}" are asserted without per-system evidence in the text; I could confirm from the roadmap's abstract that oracle definition is named a key challenge, but not the differential-testing-hardness specifics (provisional). (c) MeTMaP is cited as the exemplar of "existing VDBMS relations," but per its own title it targets false vector matching in LLM-augmented generation rather than VDBMS-level metamorphic relations; the row's argument survives, the citation fit is loose.

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate
   - **4.1** The method description is unusually concrete and followable end-to-end: the two-step extraction with named artifacts (`knowledge.json`, `specifications.json`), the constraint schema (constraint_id, type, evidence tier, level, source_url, source_verified), the five generation gates, the execution manifest with `.done` markers, the five-section evidence chain with its `verification_outcome` vocabulary, the four mechanical checks and four perspectives, and — a real strength — a single real bug (#10369) threaded through all four stages with concrete request/response detail. Judged from the text as the rubric requires, the link is declared but not reachable, and the content the package would carry (agent role prompts, the strategy registry, per-target specification sets) is not otherwise in the paper.
   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** Quantitative provenance is thin in places: no counts of extracted specifications or generated probes per system, no cost or wall-clock numbers (only the qualitative note that source grounding dominates), no decoding parameters beyond "default sampling," and no budget/configuration details for the 26,000-request VDBFuzz run. The main flow is reproducible in concept, but several numbers cannot currently be traced to a stated procedure.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Reject

### Summary

The paper targets documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems (VDBMSs): cases where the system silently accepts an input or evolves state in a way that violates its own natural-language API documentation (the running example: Milvus accepting `nprobe=0` although the documentation declares the range [1, 16384]). It first argues structurally, via a candidate-by-candidate exclusion table, that every deterministic-oracle family (crash, cross-vendor differential, metamorphic, property/schema-based, and the recent OpenAPI/trace/source-anchored REST oracle tools) cannot anchor an expectation in system-level prose, which forces an LLM-derived oracle for this bug class. It then presents TestVDB, a four-stage multi-agent pipeline: two-step behavioral specification extraction from crawled documentation (typed, evidence-tiered, source-verified constraints), test generation by attack agents under deterministically pre-bound strategies with a level split between endpoint and system constraints, Docker-pinned sandboxed execution with raw HTTP logging, and a bug-confirmation stage that splits evidence assembly (five-section evidence chains grounded in documentation, execution output, and implementation source) from cross-examination (four mechanical checks plus contract/physical/cognition/source perspectives).

The evaluation reports, across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, 51 maintainer-confirmed new bugs out of 81 adjudicated upstream submissions, 23 fixed via merged PRs, with a taxonomy of eight bug patterns dominated by silent acceptance. A false-positive-interception study (RQ2) that re-judges the 81-candidate pool with the confirmation stage alone is announced but all its numbers are pending (\todo placeholders, including an empty confusion-matrix table), and the comparison against the crash-oracle baseline VDBFuzz (RQ3) is a bidirectional probe on Qdrant only: VDBFuzz reached 0 of 7 live silent-accept true positives on v1.18.2, while TestVDB reproduces VDBFuzz's integer-overflow crash on v1.4.0 by contract reasoning; an equal-budget head-to-head rerun is also pending. All agents run on one LLM backbone (GLM-5.2).

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Strong, externally adjudicated practical yield with unusually careful ground-truth hygiene — 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs and 23 merged fixes across three production VDBMSs, with fix-PR classification proving all 23 fixes modify implementation code and a self-disproved candidate (#9149) transparently reclassified — see 1.1, 3.1.
- **S2:** The oracle-direction delta is real and verified against the closest competitors: source is used as a falsifier of documentation-derived specifications, the inverse of MASTOR's source-as-oracle-ground, and outside SATORI's/AGORA+'s per-field and traffic-anchored settings — see 2.1, 2.2.
- **S3:** The pipeline is described to a genuinely followable level of detail, with one real bug threaded through all four stages as a worked example — see 4.1.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The paper's central design claim — that the evidence-chain confirmation stage intercepts LLM false positives and breaks the extraction-judgment self-reference — is currently supported by zero evidence: RQ2 is entirely \todo, including all four cells of the confusion matrix. Assessment of the core mechanism is blocked by the pending experiment — see 3.2.
- **W2:** The RQ3 complementarity claim rests on a single-system, single-version, default-configuration probe (n=7 forward, n=1 reverse), and one supporting factual claim about VDBFuzz's own case study could not be verified from any open source — see 3.3.
- **W3:** Related-work coverage gaps inside the paper's own specialty: the LLM-documentation-oracle line (Testora, Doc2OracLL, ChatAssert, Konstantinou et al.) and the intra-system differential DBMS logic-bug line (NoREC/TLP/DQE, DDLCheck, BUZZBEE) are uncited in the text, and Table 1's differential row addresses only the cross-vendor variant — see 2.4, 2.5.
- **W4:** No run-to-run variance, cost, or candidate-denominator reporting: the pipeline's precision cannot be reconstructed from the text (how many candidates the confirmation stage emitted vs the 130 submitted), and no cost/wall-clock numbers are given although the cited competitors report them — see 3.4, 4.3, 4.4.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The problem is real and the yield is consequential. Section 2 motivates silent non-crashing bugs in VDBMSs via two empirical studies, and RQ1 (Section 4.2, Table 2) converts the motivation into 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs with 23 merged fixes. The fix-nature analysis ("every one of the 23 modifies implementation code, 15 also add regression tests, and none is documentation-only") and its validity check against the disproved #9149 are exactly the kind of evidence that separates actionable defect reports from report inflation. For an FSE audience this is a meaningful, practice-anchored contribution.
   - **1.2** The oracle analysis (Section 2.4, Table 1) is a compact, reusable map of which oracle family reaches which bug class. I verified its load-bearing rows against the actual competitor papers (see 2.1–2.3): the exclusions are argued from each tool's actual anchoring, not from strawmen, with one exception (the differential row, 2.5). This analysis has value to the subfield independent of the tool.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The practical significance is materially qualified by the pending RQ2: the whole point of putting an LLM in the oracle role is only defensible if the false-positive cost is controlled, and that is precisely the number the paper does not yet have (Section 4.3 is entirely \todo). Deployability of the approach for maintainers cannot be judged until the interception matrix lands.
   - **1.4 [minor, fixable]** Scope is bounded to three systems, one backbone, and the documentation-conformance slice of correctness (consistency vs correctness is carefully separated in Section 2.3 — good). The claims are mostly scoped honestly, so this bounds rather than undermines significance; extension to further systems/backbones is possible in revision.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** Checked the delta against MASTOR (fetched, arXiv 2606.10465): the paper calls it "the closest to this work," and its own limitations section states verbatim that "MASTOR infers semantics from implementation behavior. It cannot detect violations of intended requirements that are not reflected in code." TestVDB inverts this direction — documentation derives the expectation, source falsifies it — so the paper's characterization in Table 1 row 5 and Related Work is accurate and the delta is real and non-obvious. It also extends beyond MASTOR's Java/Spring response-side setting to black-box multi-request state behavior over HTTP. One caveat the paper should acknowledge: MASTOR also uses a multi-agent pipeline with a reviewer agent (ChallengerAgent), so the builder/auditor split as such is not the new element; the new element is what the auditor is anchored in.
   - **2.2** Checked the delta against SATORI (fetched, ASE 2025) and AGORA+ (official abstract; no OA full text exists): SATORI's oracle catalog is 17 unary response-field oracle types inferred by an LLM from OpenAPI field names, types, and natural-language descriptions, and its 18 real bugs include exactly the documentation-implementation flavor on the response side (undocumented enum values, values outside documented ranges). The paper's characterization ("its oracle catalog targets response-field properties, not behavioral conformance to system-level prose") is accurate; the genuinely open space TestVDB occupies — input-acceptance semantics and cross-request state prescribed by system-level prose — is correctly outside SATORI's anchor. AGORA+ infers invariants dynamically from observed traffic (Daikon/Beet, 106 invariant types), so "cannot reach inputs the traffic did not exercise" is a fair paraphrase. The deltas hold; I would still like the Related Work to state explicitly that SATORI already detects documentation-inconsistency bugs at the response-field level, since the intro's framing ("no deterministic-oracle candidate derives its expectation from documentation") can read as claiming the entire documentation-consistency territory is new.
   - **2.3** Checked the delta against VDBFuzz (ICSE 2026; no OA full text — abstract and artifact repo only): its oracle fires on crash/hang from boundary-value mutation of API templates (19 unknown crash bugs across 8 VDBMSs, 13 memory corruption + 6 runtime exceptions), so the paper's "an oracle that fires only on crashes cannot detect a non-crashing accept by construction" is structurally sound, and the input distributions actually overlap (both families probe boundary values) — the delta is purely the oracle, which the paper says correctly. The empirical side of this delta is RQ3 (see 3.3).
   - **2.4 [minor, fixable]** Mischaracterization of Toradocu (Section 2.4 and Related Work): "A prior line also derives oracles from documentation with LLMs [toradocu16, augmentest25]." Toradocu (ISSTA 2016, fetched full text) uses classical NLP — Stanford Parser, open information extraction, Levenshtein-distance matching to code elements — not LLMs; the runtime-validation half of the sentence does apply to it. The LLM members of that line are AugmenTest and later work; the citation bundle conflates them. Small, but a factual error about the paper's own foundational prior work.
   - **2.5 [major, fixable]** Missing related work, verified within my specialty: (a) the intra-system differential line for non-crashing DBMS logic bugs — NoREC (ESEC/FSE 2020, abstract verified: rewrites an optimized query into a non-optimizable equivalent evaluated by the same engine, 159 unknown bugs), TLP, DQE, DDLCheck, and BUZZBEE — is entirely uncited in the rendered text, and Table 1's differential row rebuts only cross-vendor differential testing ("expectation anchored in another implementation; cross-vendor behavior diverges by design"). NoREC-style intra-system equivalence is the closest deterministic prior art for the "silent majority" the introduction claims is unreachable, and the table's exclusion argument never addresses it (the honest argument is that these techniques detect result-correctness/optimization bugs and still derive nothing from documentation — but it must be made). (b) The LLM-documentation-oracle line beyond Toradocu/AugmenTest: Testora (ICSE 2026 — natural-language intent of code changes turned into oracles for behavioral-regression detection), Doc2OracLL (FSE 2025 — Javadoc-only LLM oracles, 19–94% more Defects4J bugs than prior methods), ChatAssert (TSE 2025), and Konstantinou et al. 2024 (LLM oracles capturing actual vs expected behavior — directly on this paper's central concern). Several of these are present in the bibliography file yet uncited in the text, so positioning them is a revision-scale fix, not new research.
   - **2.6** The most original component is the confirmation stage's external anchoring — maintainer-cognition mining from the target's own issue history, and source grounding as an information source independent of the documentation. This is a genuine design idea; its effectiveness is what RQ2 must show (see 3.2). Pre-bound strategies, evidence tiers, and builder/reviewer splits are individually familiar from property-based testing and prior oracle work, so novelty rests on the composition and the direction argument above — adequate, not exceptional.

3. **Soundness** — Weak

Ground truth is external (maintainer adjudication), re-verified as of September 2026, treated as revisable (the #9149 seven-version re-probe that reclassified a "fixed" candidate as test-only is disclosed and even reused as a validity check for the fix-PR classifier), and the 49 unresolved submissions are excluded from every reported rate. The paper cites LLM judge self-inconsistency (Haldar et al.) as motivation, runs every agent on a single backbone under "default sampling," and yet reports no repeated runs of the pipeline — not for RQ1 (was each target mined once?) and not for the (pending) RQ2 re-judging. For a paper whose core contribution is an LLM adjudication mechanism, run-to-run stability of that mechanism is a first-order measurement, and its absence is a notable gap even apart from the pending numbers.
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** The evidence-tier mechanism's promise is never cashed out. Section 3.2 introduces explicit/inferred-from-example/inferred-from-behavior/convention tiers precisely so that "test strength and report claims can be graded by how directly the documentation supports them," but RQ1 never reports the tier distribution of the 51 confirmed bugs. ## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Reject

### Summary

The paper addresses documentation-implementation bugs in vector database management systems: cases where a VDBMS silently accepts an input or behaves in a way that violates its own natural-language API documentation (e.g., Milvus accepting `nprobe=0` where the documentation declares `[1, 16384]`). The authors argue that because the expected behavior lives in prose, every deterministic-oracle family (crash signals, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property/schema-based checking) is structurally unable to supply the expectation, leaving an LLM-derived oracle as the practical option — and importing the LLM's false-positive problem with it.

Their system, TestVDB, is a four-stage pipeline: (i) a two-agent extraction stage crawls version-pinned documentation into structured, source-verified behavioral specifications typed by constraint category, evidence tier, and endpoint/system level; (ii) attack agents generate executable probes under deterministically pre-bound strategies rather than LLM-chosen ones; (iii) a sandbox executes probes against Docker-pinned systems with raw HTTP logging; (iv) a bug-confirmation stage splits evidence assembly from cross-examination, grounding each candidate in a five-section evidence chain (including implementation-source greps) that a chain auditor checks mechanically and from four perspectives (contract, physical behavior, maintainer cognition, source) — the implementation source serving as the falsification anchor that breaks the LLM's extraction-judgment self-reference. The evaluation reports, across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs out of 81 adjudicated submissions with 23 merged implementation fixes (RQ1), a bidirectional complementarity probe against the crash-oracle tool VDBFuzz (RQ3), and a false-positive interception evaluation (RQ2) whose numbers are explicitly pending a redone experiment.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** Strong, externally validated detection results — 51 maintainer-confirmed bugs, 23 merged fix PRs all verified to touch implementation code, with an unusually careful adjudication protocol including self-disproof of a mis-adjudicated candidate — see 1.1, 3.1.
- **S2:** Clean problem formalization: the consistency-vs-correctness distinction and the structural oracle-exclusion analysis (Table 1) make a readable case for why this bug class needs a documentation-derived expectation — see 2.1.
- **S3:** Methodological discipline visible throughout: pre-bound strategies, five generation gates, builder/judge separation with a rebuild loop, honest treatment of adjudication fallibility and of 49 unresolved submissions excluded from all rates — see 3.1, 4.1.
- **S4:** The running example (Qdrant #10369) threaded through all four stages makes the architecture concrete and checkable; prose quality and the threat-organized limitations section are above average — see 4.1, 5.1.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** RQ2 — the evaluation that would support the paper's central design claim (that the confirmation stage intercepts LLM-oracle false positives) — is entirely absent from the manuscript; abstract, contributions, and conclusion all carry pending-number TODOs — see 3.2.
- **W2:** The disclosed internal-validity threats (expectation-framing bias, observation leakage) plus a leftover internal note referencing "superseded" numbers indicate the confirmation-stage evidence base was previously compromised and is in flux — see 3.3.
- **W3:** No stability/variance analysis for a stochastic single-backbone pipeline: one LLM family, default sampling, apparently a single run, and no confidence intervals anywhere in the current text — see 3.4.
- **W4:** The RQ3 complementarity probe is not budget-matched (default VDBFuzz configuration, single-version pairings) and the planned head-to-head rerun is pending — see 3.5.
- **W5:** The manuscript is openly incomplete: an empty RQ2 results section, an internal NOTE-TO-SELF in Section 4.3, an unconfirmed artifact link, and missing process/cost statistics — see 5.2, 4.2, 4.3.

### Detailed Assessment

**1. Significance — Adequate**

- **1.1:** The practical yield in Section 4.2 (Table 2) is the paper's strongest asset: 51 confirmed bugs across three production VDBMSs, 23 fixed via merged PRs, fix-PR classification showing every fix modifies implementation code (15 also add regression tests, none documentation-only), and upstream acceptance signals (50/51 carrying official bug labels). This is externally validated impact on a real, timely problem — silent wrong-result bugs in infrastructure that LLM applications depend on. The fix-nature classifier is even validated against the disproved #9149 case, which is a level of self-scrutiny most testing papers do not reach. This approaches Excellent on practical impact alone.
- **1.2 [minor, unfixable]:** The research significance beyond the yield is bounded and currently contingent: the contribution the paper most wants credit for — a reliable LLM-oracle confirmation architecture — is exactly the part whose evaluation is pending (3.2), and the claims are scoped by the authors themselves to prose-documented APIs of three systems (Section 5, external validity). This boundedness inheres in the contribution as framed; the honest scoping is commendable, but field-wide impact should not be assumed.

**2. Novelty — Adequate (provisional — I have not surveyed the field for this review; this tier is assessed from the paper's own account of prior work)**

- **2.1:** The paper's own differentiation is coherent and, if accurate, a real delta: prior LLM-doc-oracle work (Toradocu, AugmenTest, as described in Sections 2.4 and 6) keeps the LLM as final arbiter; structured-source tools (AGORA+, SATORI, MASTOR, as described) anchor expectations per-field in traces/OpenAPI/source, with MASTOR encoding implemented behavior and thus structurally unable to catch documentation-code gaps. TestVDB's inversion — reading source as a falsifier of documentation-derived specifications rather than as an oracle generator — is a non-obvious conceptual move, and the structural exclusion table (Table 1) is a genuine framing contribution.
- **2.2 [minor, unfixable]:** At the component level the system is a composition of known techniques (multi-agent role prompts, boundary/state/semantic attack strategies, evidence chains, grep-based source localization); originality rests on the composition and the confirmation protocol rather than on any new technique. This inheres in the work.
- **2.3 [minor, fixable]:** The differentiation from the closest prior line (LLM-derived oracles from documentation) is conceptual only — no empirical comparison against any prior oracle tool is run on the same targets (RQ3 compares only against VDBFuzz, the crash-oracle baseline). A small head-to-head or worked contrast on shared targets would materially sharpen the novelty claim; as written, the reader takes the delta on faith.

**3. Soundness — Weak**

- **3.1:** What is present is methodologically careful. The RQ1 adjudication protocol (Section 4.2) defines confirmed/duplicate/false-positive operationally, re-verifies every submission state as of September 2026, treats adjudication as revisable (demonstrated by the seven-version re-probe that reclassified #9149), and excludes 49 unresolved submissions from all rates — a conservative choice. Internal arithmetic is consistent everywhere I checked (Table 2 row/column sums; the eight bug-pattern counts summing to 51; bug-status partition 23+18+7+3 = 51; 37/51 = 72.5% for the acceptance family). The 30 adjudicated false positives are reported rather than hidden, and are correctly identified as the population for RQ2.
- **3.2 [major, fixable]:** The paper's second and third contributions — "a false-positive interception evaluation" and the rigorous oracle comparison — are unsupported in the current manuscript. Section 4.3 (RQ2) contains a protocol description and four TODO placeholders where the confusion matrix, precision/recall, suppression rates, CIs, and both attribution analyses should be; the abstract and conclusion carry matching TODOs. This is not a peripheral ablation: the entire motivation (Sections 1–2) establishes that LLM oracles have a false-positive problem, and the confirmation stage is the proposed answer; without RQ2 there is no evidence the answer works. Note also that the end-to-end 63.0% submission precision does not substitute: there is no baseline condition showing what the false-positive rate would have been without the confirmation stage, so even the direction of the effect is unmeasured. The TODOs indicate the experiment is being redone, so this is recoverable in a revision cycle — but as submitted, a core claim outruns the evidence.
- **3.3 [major, fixable]:** Section 5 discloses two judgment-side internal-validity threats — expectation-framing bias in the text presented to judges, and observation leakage from unsanitized probe descriptions — and states both are "addressed in the redone RQ2 protocol." Combined with the leftover internal note in Section 4.3 referencing an "oracle ceiling 42/44 = 95.5%," "superseded revertb numbers [that] must not be cited," and a possible "pool recount," this tells the reviewer that a prior version of the central experiment existed, was methodologically compromised, and its replacement numbers are not yet in. Until the redone experiment lands and its leak-audit is described in the paper, no interception claim can be assessed.
- **3.4 [minor, fixable]:** All agents run on a single LLM backbone (GLM-5.2) under default sampling (Section 4.1), and the RQ1 results are presented as a single run with no repetition, no run-to-run agreement statistic, and no confidence intervals (Wilson CIs are promised only inside the RQ2 TODO). For a stochastic LLM-orchestrated pipeline, the reported yields and false-positive counts should be shown to be stable across runs, at least for one target; otherwise the reader cannot distinguish a representative run from a fortunate one. The single-backbone limitation is disclosed, but single-run variance is a separate issue that is not.
- **3.5 [minor, fixable]:** The RQ3 bidirectional probe (Section 4.4) is a reasonable structural argument and is honestly bounded — the authors attribute the VDBFuzz `wait=false` miss to that tool's templates rather than to crash oracles as a class, which is good epistemic hygiene. But the comparison is not budget-matched: VDBFuzz ran 26,000 mutated requests at default configuration on one version (v1.18.2), the reverse probes sit on different versions (v1.4.0, v1.18.0), no wall-clock or compute budget is reported for TestVDB's side, and the text does not state whether the 26,000-request run surfaced any crashes at all on v1.18.2. The planned equal-budget head-to-head (TODO) is the right fix; the current evidence supports "complementary in principle," not a measured comparison.
- **3.6 [minor, fixable]:** Internal inconsistency in the headline count: the abstract and Section 4.2 call the 51 "new bugs," yet the bug-status partition includes "3 tracked as duplicates" within the 51, and the collection protocol defines "duplicate = tracked to an existing issue" as a category distinct from "confirmed." Either the 51 includes 3 non-new bugs (making the new-bug count 48) or the duplicate category is being used in two senses; the paper should reconcile this before the headline number is quoted.

**4. Verifiability — Adequate**

- **4.1:** The methodology is described at an unusually checkable level for a multi-agent system: the knowledge.json/specifications.json record structure, the constraint typology and evidence tiers with the source-verification rule, the five generation gates, the five-section evidence-chain schema with labeled sources, the verdict taxonomy (Confirmed/Refuted/Neutral-with-human-routing), and the Docker-pinned execution regime with log/marker accounting. The #10369 running example carried through all four stages (Sections 3.2–3.5) lets a reader trace exactly what each stage would produce. The RQ2 protocol (frozen, leak-audited material packages) is also described, so the main flow is reproducible in concept.
- **4.2 [minor, fixable]:** The replication package (agent role definitions, knowledge bases, specifications, scripts with raw HTTP logs, evidence chains, submission ledger) is promised at an anonymous.4open.science link "upon publication," with a TODO to confirm the link. Until the artifact is reachable in reviewable form, the agent prompts — on which extraction quality and judgment behavior critically depend — cannot be inspected, and the RQ2 claims will rest entirely on the paper's description.
- **4.3 [minor, fixable]:** Key process statistics are absent: how many constraints were extracted per system and per evidence tier, how many probes were generated and what fraction passed the five gates, documentation-coverage figures for the endpoint-coverage check (the mechanism is described, no numbers reported), and any wall-clock/compute/cost figures (Section 3.5 says only that cost is "dominated by source grounding and live re-probes"). These quantities are needed both to assess practicality and to sanity-check the yields in Table 2; VDBFuzz's run configuration beyond "default" is likewise unreported.

**5. Presentation — Weak**

- **5.1:** Where complete, the writing is strong: consistent terminology (consistency vs. correctness defined once and used throughout), a logical section flow, the threat-to-validity-organized limitations section, and the threaded running example all make the paper easy to follow. Language is precise and largely free of undefined jargon.
- **5.2 [major, fixable]:** The manuscript is structurally incomplete as submitted. Red TODO markers appear in the abstract, the contributions list, Section 4.2, the whole of Section 4.3 (which is a protocol skeleton plus placeholders, with a confusion-matrix table left empty), Section 4.4, the conclusion, and the data-availability statement. Most seriously, Section 4.3 contains an internal authoring note ("NOTE-TO-SELF, delete before submission ... superseded revertb numbers must not be cited") that must never appear in a reviewed manuscript — it both signals unfinished state and discloses that superseded numbers exist. None of this is unfixable, but a submission cannot be evaluated as a complete paper in this state.
- **5.3 [minor, fixable]:** Minor corral: (a) Sections 2.3–2.4 pack several distinct arguments into long unbroken paragraphs that would benefit from structure; (b) "an agentic coding runtime" (Section 3.5) is left unspecified, which matters for reproducibility of the harness; (c) the September 2026 re-verification snapshot date should be kept current through any revision cycle so the ledger claim stays accurate.

### Questions for Authors

- **Q1:** What are the redone RQ2 numbers (confusion matrix, precision/recall, FP suppression with Wilson CIs), and is there — or could there be — a no-confirmation-stage baseline so the interception effect is measured rather than assumed? — would directly repair item 3.2, likely raising Soundness.
- **Q2:** Was RQ1 produced by a single pipeline run, and how much do extracted-constraint counts, candidate yields, and confirmation verdicts vary across repeated runs of the same target under default sampling? — addresses 3.4; absent stability evidence the variance concern keeps Soundness at Weak even after Q1 lands.
- **Q3:** Can you report constraints extracted per system (by tier), probes generated vs. gate-passing, documentation-coverage percentages, and per-target wall-clock/compute cost, and make the artifact link live for review? — addresses 4.2/4.3 and the practicality half of 1.1.
- **Q4:** In the planned RQ3 head-to-head, how will budget and wall-clock be equalized, and did the 26,000-request VDBFuzz run on v1.18.2 surface any crashes at all? — addresses 3.5.
- **Q5:** How do you reconcile "51 ... new bugs" with the "3 tracked as duplicates" inside the 51, given that your protocol lists duplicate as a non-confirmed adjudication outcome? — resolves the 3.6 inconsistency; if the headline becomes 48, several derived percentages shift.
