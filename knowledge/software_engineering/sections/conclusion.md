# Writing a Good SE Conclusion

## Core Principle
End strong. Reinforce contributions and leave readers with clear takeaways.

## What Makes It Good
### Concise Summary: Restate problem and approach in 2-3 sentences. Echo introduction's framing. Remind of key insight.
### Highlight Key Results: Pick most impressive numbers. Connect to practical impact. What does this mean for developers?
### Concrete Future Work: 2-3 specific directions, not generic. Connect to limitations. Things community can build on.
### Confident but Honest: Be confident about what you achieved. Don't oversell. Acknowledge scope.

## Language Patterns
**Summary**: "In this paper, we presented [TOOL], a technique for...", "We addressed the challenge of X by..."
**Results**: "Our evaluation on N real-world projects shows...", "We achieve X% improvement over state-of-the-art..."
**Impact**: "This enables developers to...", "Practitioners can use [TOOL] to..."
**Future work**: "In future work, we plan to extend [TOOL] to handle...", "An interesting direction is applying this to..."

## Artifact Mention
If you have a tool/artifact, mention availability: "Our tool and data are available at [URL]" / "[TOOL] is open-source and available at..."

## Common Pitfalls
- Just repeating the abstract word-for-word
- Vague future work ("improve performance")
- Introducing new claims not supported in paper
- Too long (half a page is typically enough)
