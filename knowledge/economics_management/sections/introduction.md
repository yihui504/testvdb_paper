# Writing a Good Economics/Management Introduction

## Core Principle
Reviewers want theoretical rigor and practical relevance. Open with a real phenomenon, ground it in theory, and state your contribution precisely.

## What Makes It Good
### Phenomenon-Driven Opening: Start with a concrete economic or business phenomenon. Use real data, policy events, or industry trends. Avoid generic openings.
### Clear Research Gap: Show what existing literature has and has not addressed. Be specific. Connect gap to theoretical importance.
### Precise Contribution Statement: Separate theoretical, empirical, and policy contributions. State findings briefly. Specify data and methodology used.
### Theory-First Framing: Name the theoretical lens early. Explain why this theory is appropriate. Avoid hypothesis-free empirical exercises.

## Language Patterns
**Opening with phenomenon**: "The reform of X policy in [year] raised questions about...", "Despite the rapid growth of Y, firms continue to face..."
**Research gap**: "While existing studies have examined X, the effect of Y remains underexplored...", "The literature has largely overlooked the role of Z..."
**Contribution**: "This paper contributes to the literature in three ways. First,...", "Using a [DID/IV/PSM] approach with data from [source], we find..."

## Common Pitfalls
- Generic opening paragraphs with no concrete content
- Stating gap as "no one has studied X" without explaining why it matters
- Listing contributions that are just activities ("We use panel data")
- Introducing terminology inconsistent with later sections
- AI-flavored language: overly polished, generic, lacking field-specific jargon
