# Content Analysis Guide - Education

## Purpose
Extract and organize key information from research materials. Focus on research design rigor, theoretical grounding, and statistical evidence.

## What to Extract
### 1. Research Context: Background (current state, policy context), Motivation (achievement gaps, pedagogical challenges), Challenge (confounds, ethical constraints, sample limitations)
### 2. Theoretical Framework: Named theory grounding the study (e.g., Zimmerman's SRL, Vygotsky's ZPD), Constructs with operational definitions and measurement instruments, Terminology consistency (check for mixed terms)
**Critical**: The theoretical source determines term choice. If citing Zimmerman's model, use "self-regulated learning" (SRL), not "autonomous learning". Pick one set of terms.
### 3. Research Design: Study type, Participants (sample size, demographics, selection), Intervention details (duration, specific lesson/text names, fidelity), Instruments (with reliability coefficients), Data collection timeline
### 4. Data & Results: Statistical tests used and why, For each finding: test name, statistic value, p-value, AND effect size, Mediation/moderation analyses, Qualitative data: coding scheme, inter-rater reliability
### 5. Metadata: Target venue, page/word limit, paper type

## Education Conventions
1. Report full statistics — "t(45) = 2.31, p = .025, Cohen's d = 0.68", not just "significant"
2. Terminology consistency — one term set throughout, determined by theoretical framework
3. Specific teaching cases — name the lesson, text, or unit
4. Distinguish interpretation from data
5. Qualify paradigm critiques — say "early cognitivism" not "cognitivism"
