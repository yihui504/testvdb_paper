# Writing a Good Education Introduction

## Core Principle
Education reviewers want both theoretical grounding and practical relevance. Lead with a real educational problem, then anchor it in theory.

## What Makes It Good
### Grounded Opening: Start with a concrete educational challenge. Use evidence: national assessment data, OECD/PISA results, policy documents.
### Specific Problem: Name the exact educational issue. Show evidence from prior research. Explain why current approaches are insufficient. Connect to both learners and educators.
### Theoretical Anchoring: Name the theoretical framework early. Use terminology that matches your cited theory. Show how theory motivates research questions.
### Clear Research Questions: State RQs or hypotheses explicitly. Each RQ should be answerable with methodology. Distinguish descriptive, correlational, and causal questions.

## Language Patterns
**Opening with impact**: "National assessments reveal that X% of students fail to reach...", "Despite widespread adoption of Y, student outcomes in Z remain..."
**Problem statement**: "Prior research has examined X, yet few studies address...", "A gap exists between the theoretical promise of X and its classroom implementation..."
**Research questions**: "This study investigates the following questions: (1)... (2)..."

## Common Pitfalls
- Starting too broadly ("Education shapes the future...")
- No clear theoretical framework
- Mixing terminology from different theoretical traditions
- Research questions that don't match stated methodology
- Claiming causal language for correlational designs
