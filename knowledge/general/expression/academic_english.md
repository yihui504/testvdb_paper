# Core Academic English Conventions

## Tense Usage
### Present Simple — established facts, general claims, paper's own goals
- Established knowledge: "X plays a critical role in Y."
- Stating what the paper does: "This paper investigates..." / "We propose..."
- Interpreting results: "The results indicate that..."
### Past Simple — specific completed actions, others' specific studies
- Your methods: "We collected 500 samples..." / "Participants completed a survey..."
- Others' specific findings: "Smith et al. (2020) found that..."
### Present Perfect — past work with current relevance, gap statements
- Literature with ongoing relevance: "Several studies have examined..."
- Gap identification: "However, few studies have addressed..."

### Tense by Section
| Section | Dominant Tense | Why |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| Introduction (background) | Present simple / present perfect | General truths, ongoing relevance |
| Introduction (gap) | Present perfect / present simple | What has (not) been done |
| Methodology | Past simple | Completed procedures |
| Results | Past simple | What was found |
| Discussion | Present simple + past simple | Interpret (present) + compare (past) |
| Conclusion | Present simple | General claims and implications |

## Active vs Passive Voice
**Use active for**: Claims and contributions / Interpretations / Decisions
**Use passive for**: Methods where agent is irrelevant / Emphasizing the object / Avoiding first person where discouraged
**Avoid**: Passive where active is clearer / Chains of passive

## Articles
**Definite article (the)**: Previously mentioned / Unique/specific / Superlatives
**Indefinite article (a/an)**: First mention / One of many
**Zero article**: Uncountable/abstract concepts / Plural generalizations / Fields/disciplines
**Common Errors**: ❌ "The previous research has shown" → ✅ "Previous research has shown" / ❌ "In the Section 3" → ✅ "In Section 3"

## Paragraph Structure
- Topic sentence: first sentence states main point
- Support: evidence, examples, data, reasoning
- Concluding/transition: connects to next paragraph
- One idea per paragraph
- Avoid single-sentence paragraphs

## Punctuation
### Semicolon: Joins related independent clauses / Separates items in comma-containing lists
### Colon: Introduces list, explanation, or elaboration (clause before must be complete)
### Comma: After introductory clauses / Before coordinating conjunctions joining independent clauses / NOT before "that" in restrictive clauses

## Adverb Placement
- Before the main verb: "We significantly improved..."
- After be/auxiliary: "The results are clearly consistent..."
- NOT between verb and direct object
- Sentence-initial for emphasis: "Importantly, this finding suggests..."
