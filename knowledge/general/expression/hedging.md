# Hedging and Tentative Language

Academic writing requires calibrating the strength of claims. Hedging signals epistemic modesty.

## Modal Verbs (strongest → weakest)
| Strength | Modal | Example |
|----------|-------|---------|
| Certainty | will | "This will lead to errors." |
| Strong probability | would / should | "This would suggest a causal link." |
| Moderate possibility | may | "This may indicate an underlying issue." |
| Weak possibility | might / could | "This might be attributable to noise." |

## Hedging Devices Beyond Modals
### Tentative verbs: suggest, indicate, imply, appear to, seem to, tend to
### Probability adverbs: possibly, probably, perhaps, apparently, arguably, potentially
### Limiting phrases: in some cases, under certain conditions, to some extent, in part
### Attributional hedges: According to X..., Based on these results..., Given the evidence...
### Approximators: approximately, roughly, about, around

## Boosting (the opposite)
Boosters strengthen claims. Use sparingly and only when warranted: clearly, obviously, certainly, undoubtedly, definitely, demonstrate, establish.
**When boosting is appropriate**: well-established facts, your own methodology choices, results with overwhelming statistical support.

## When NOT to Hedge
- Your own procedure: "We collected data from..." (not "We possibly collected...")
- Established facts: "Water boils at 100°C" (not "Water may boil...")
- Definitions: "Machine learning is a subset of AI" (not "appears to be...")
- Describing what you did: "We ran 5 experiments" (not "We may have run approximately 5")

## Over-hedging (avoid)
Stacking multiple hedges weakens writing:
- ❌ "It might possibly be suggested that this could perhaps indicate..."
- ✅ "This may indicate..."

## Hedging by Section
| Section | Hedging Level | Rationale |
|---------|--------------|-----------|
| Methods | Low | You know what you did |
| Results | Low-Medium | Report findings directly; hedge interpretations |
| Discussion | Medium-High | Interpretations are inherently uncertain |
| Introduction (gap) | Medium | "Few studies have addressed..." is already cautious |
| Conclusion | Medium | Balance confidence with scope limitations |
