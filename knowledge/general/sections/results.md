# Writing Good Results

## Rhetorical Model (Glasman-Deal)
The results section presents findings and guides interpretation.

### Element 1: Revisit Research Aim
Briefly remind the reader what you set out to investigate.
**Vocabulary**: "As stated in Section X, the aim was to...", "To address RQ1, we examined..."

### Element 2: Highlight Most Important Results + Commentary
Present key findings with commentary on significance. Lead with the most important result.
**Vocabulary (presenting)**: "Table 1 shows that...", "The most striking finding is..."
**Vocabulary (commenting)**: "This suggests that...", "Notably, X outperforms Y by Z%..."

### Element 3: Report Problems / Unexpected Findings
Acknowledge anomalies, unexpected results, or limitations.
**Vocabulary**: "Contrary to expectations, ...", "An unexpected finding was that...", "Performance degrades when..."

### Element 4: State Implications
Connect findings to the broader research question.
**Vocabulary**: "These findings suggest that...", "This result has implications for..."

## Data Commentary Language
**Sequence**: first, then, subsequently, followed by, prior to, after
**Frequency**: often, rarely, frequently, in most cases, in N out of M instances
**Quantity**: the majority, a significant proportion, approximately, roughly, on average
**Comparison**: higher/lower than, comparable to, in contrast to, outperforms, falls short of
**Causality**: due to, as a result of, caused by, attributed to, leads to, stems from
**Degree**: substantially, marginally, considerably, slightly, dramatically

## Common Pitfalls
- Cherry-picking favorable results
- Figures that require too much explanation
- Missing error bars or statistical tests
- Burying important findings in the middle of a paragraph
- Repeating methodology instead of presenting results
- Over-interpreting results (save deep interpretation for Discussion)
