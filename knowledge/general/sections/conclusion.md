# Writing a Good Conclusion

## Rhetorical Structure
The conclusion consolidates the paper's contribution. It should work as a standalone summary.

### Element 1: Restate Problem and Approach
Echo the introduction's framing at a higher level of confidence.
**Vocabulary**: "In this paper, we addressed the problem of...", "We have presented [method/tool], a technique for..."

### Element 2: Summarize Key Findings
Highlight the most important results — pick 2-3 numbers that demonstrate impact.
**Vocabulary**: "Our evaluation on N [subjects] shows...", "We demonstrated that...", "We achieved X% improvement over..."

### Element 3: State Significance
Answer "so what?" — what changed because of this work?
**Vocabulary**: "This work contributes to...", "The main takeaway is...", "This enables [practitioners/researchers] to..."

### Element 4: Future Directions
Suggest 2-3 specific next steps. Connect to limitations acknowledged in Discussion.
**Vocabulary**: "In future work, we plan to extend this to...", "A natural extension would be..."

## General Principles
- The conclusion is the last thing readers remember — end on a strong note
- No new results, arguments, or evidence — only synthesis
- Be confident about what you achieved, don't oversell
- Keep it proportional: conference ~1 paragraph, journal ~2-3 paragraphs

## Common Pitfalls
- Introducing new material not discussed elsewhere
- Simply copying the abstract word-for-word
- Being too vague or generic ("More work is needed")
- Ending weakly with a disclaimer instead of a contribution statement
