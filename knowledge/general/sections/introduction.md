# Writing a Good Introduction

## Rhetorical Model: CARS (Create A Research Space)
The CARS model (Swales, 1990) is the standard rhetorical framework for research article introductions across disciplines.

### Move 1: Establish Territory
Convince the reader the research area is important, active, and worth investigating.
**Steps**: Claim centrality or importance / Make topic generalizations / Review prior research
**Vocabulary**: "X has attracted considerable attention in recent years...", "A great deal of research has been devoted to...", "The role of X in Y has been widely investigated..."

### Move 2: Establish Niche
Show there is a gap, problem, or unanswered question that justifies your research.
**Strategies**: Counter-claiming / Indicating a gap / Question-raising / Continuing a tradition
**Vocabulary**: "However, little is known about...", "Few studies have addressed...", "It remains unclear whether..."

### Move 3: Occupy Niche
State what your paper does and why it matters.
**Steps**: State purpose or announce present research / Announce principal findings (optional) / Indicate structure
**Vocabulary**: "In this paper, we present...", "This study aims to...", "We propose..."

## General Principles
- The introduction sells your paper
- Start with something the reader already cares about; avoid cliches
- State the gap in ONE clear sentence
- Each contribution should be verifiable
- Order contributions by importance

## Common Pitfalls
- Burying the problem in background (Move 1 too long, Move 2 too late)
- Contributions that don't match paper content
- Overselling without evidence
- Missing the "so what?" factor
