# Writing a Good Discussion

## Rhetorical Model (Swales, 1990 — Eight Moves)
The discussion section interprets results and places them in broader context.

### Move 1: Background Information
Provide context needed to interpret the results.
**Vocabulary**: "As outlined in Section X...", "The purpose of this study was to..."

### Move 2: Statement of Results
Restate the key finding being discussed (not raw data — the interpreted finding).
**Vocabulary**: "The results demonstrate that...", "Our analysis reveals that..."

### Move 3: (Un)expected Results
Flag whether findings were expected or surprising.
**Vocabulary (Expected)**: "Consistent with our hypothesis, ..." / "As predicted, ..." / "In line with [cite], ..."
**Vocabulary (Unexpected)**: "Contrary to expectations, ..." / "Surprisingly, ..." / "An unexpected finding was..."

### Move 4: Reference to Previous Research
Compare your findings with prior work.
**Vocabulary (Agreement)**: "This finding is consistent with [cite], who also found..."
**Vocabulary (Disagreement)**: "This contrasts with [cite], who reported..." / "A possible explanation for this discrepancy is..."

### Move 5: Explanation
Provide a causal or theoretical explanation for why you got these results.
**Vocabulary**: "One possible explanation is that...", "This may be attributed to..."

### Move 6: Exemplification
Give a concrete example or case to illustrate the point.
**Vocabulary**: "For instance, in the case of...", "This is illustrated by..."

### Move 7: Deduction and Hypothesis
Draw broader conclusions or propose new hypotheses.
**Vocabulary**: "This suggests that, more generally, ...", "We hypothesize that..."

### Move 8: Recommendation
Suggest practical applications, policy implications, or future research directions.
**Vocabulary**: "Based on these findings, we recommend...", "Future work should investigate..."

## General Principles
- Discuss, don't just repeat
- Connect findings back to research questions
- Be honest about limitations
- Engage with prior work: agreements validate, disagreements reveal new insights

## Common Pitfalls
- Repeating the results section verbatim
- Overgeneralizing from limited data
- Hiding limitations in vague language
- Ignoring findings that contradict your hypothesis
