# Writing a Good Methodology

## Rhetorical Model (Glasman-Deal)
The methodology section should enable a competent reader to replicate your work.

### Element 1: Overview
Provide a high-level summary of what was done, materials/data used, and overall procedure.
**Vocabulary**: "The experiment/study/analysis was carried out using...", "The approach/method/procedure consists of three stages..."

### Element 2: Details + Justification
Describe each step in detail and justify key choices.
**Vocabulary (details)**: "Specifically, we set parameter P to V...", "The algorithm operates as follows..."
**Vocabulary (justification)**: "We chose X over Y because...", "Following [cite], we..."

### Element 3: Procedure
Describe step-by-step execution: what was done, in what order, under what conditions.
**Vocabulary**: "First, ... Next, ... Finally, ...", "Participants were asked to..."

### Element 4: Limitations
Acknowledge limitations of methodological choices.
**Vocabulary**: "A limitation of this approach is...", "This method does not account for..."

## General Principles
- Present steps in the order you perform them
- Show how each step connects to the next
- Use figures to illustrate complex procedures
- Parameters, configurations, versions, data sizes — include enough for replication

## Common Pitfalls
- Skipping "obvious" steps that outsiders wouldn't know
- Missing parameter values or software versions
- No justification for design choices
- Mixing methodology with results
