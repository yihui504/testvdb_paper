## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper introduces TestVDB, a system for testing documentation-implementation consistency in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs) — cases where the API silently accepts inputs or produces behaviors that violate its natural-language documentation (e.g., accepting `nprobe=0` or `ef=0` where documentation prescribes rejection). The authors observe that a substantial portion of VDBMS defects are not reachable by classical oracles (crash, differential, metamorphic, property-based), because the relevant accept/reject boundary is expressed in ambiguous natural-language prose rather than a formal schema. They place VDBMS documentation-implementation consistency in a regime where an LLM must play two roles — extracting behavioral claims from documentation and judging consistency — and argue this introduces a two-layer unreliability: a family-specific layer (LLM self-preference, mitigated by cross-model validation) and a task-intrinsic layer (ambiguous documentation yields the same wrong claim across families, which cross-model validation cannot break).

The core methodological contribution is **source-grounded falsification**: LLM-derived claims are treated as refutable hypotheses and falsified against the implementation's actual behavior (via a dev-reviewer agent that reproduces candidates and cross-checks them against source). A retrospective on 48 maintainer-adjudicated candidates (Milvus + Qdrant) shows the source-grounded dev-reviewer reaches 67% precision and 74% recall (3-run any-confirmed ensemble), versus 37% recall without the source anchor. An 18-clause probe on Milvus and Qdrant demonstrates that source-grounded falsification catches all over-strict clauses, while cross-model judging misses 3 of the 6 task-intrinsic ones. At scale, TestVDB surfaced 107 candidate issues across three VDBMSs (Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate); 49 are true-positive defects, with 15 fixed via merged PR.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The paper identifies and cleanly characterizes a real, previously under-addressed oracle gap — the documentation-implementation residual where deterministic oracles cannot adjudicate natural-language accept/reject boundaries (Table 1). The scoping discussion distinguishing this from result correctness is clear and well-motivated — see 1.1, 2.1.
- **S2:** The family-specific vs. task-intrinsic error decomposition is a genuinely novel diagnostic for LLM-as-judge reliability in testing pipelines. The 18-clause probe provides direct evidence that cross-model validation is insufficient where the documentation is the ambiguity source, which is a non-obvious result — see 3.1, 3.2.
- **S3:** The source-grounded falsification mechanism is a principled counter to task-intrinsic error: using source as an *asymmetric falsifier* of documentation-derived claims (rather than as the oracle itself) is a crisp delta against the closest competitor, MASTOR, whose summary itself confirms that LLM-derived oracles are evaluated against implementation behavior — see 3.2.
- **S4:** Real-world yield is substantial: 49 maintainer-acknowledged true-positive defects across three VDBMSs, with 15 merged-PR fixes, is evidence the method produces actionable findings at scale — see 1.2.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The 85% "documentation-implementation residual" headline is the composition of TestVDB's own findings, not a population estimate, and the paper does not adequately separate the rhetorical use of this number (in the abstract) from its proper scope — the abstract's "about 85% are documentation-implementation defects that differential, metamorphic, and property-based oracles cannot reach" reads as a population claim until the body's caveat is reached. This weakens Significance — see 1.3.
- **W2:** Single-run variance is severe (recall 15–78% across five runs; precision 50–73%), and the operating point chosen (any-confirmed union) inflates recall while trading precision in a way that the paper underplays. The 67%/74% headline rests on an ensemble rule whose behavior is not fully characterized (e.g., false-positive growth at 5 runs: 62% precision) — see 3.3.
- **W3:** The bidirectional VDBFuzz head-to-head is n=1 per direction. Even though the paper flags this as hypothesis-generating, the Discussion's structural-asymmetry hypothesis (§8) over-extends from these two cases. The "VDBFuzz's templates hardcode `wait=true`" observation is a limitation of VDBFuzz's current templates, not a structural property of crash oracles — and the paper mostly says this, but the rhetoric sometimes slips — see 3.4.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** VDBMSs are infrastructure for retrieval-augmented LLM applications, and silent-accept defects (e.g., a negative `score_threshold` that disables a filter and returns all matches) corrupt query semantics in ways that are hard to detect downstream. The paper cites direct evidence (the VDBMS bug study and roadmap) that this is a meaningful defect class. The problem is real and timely.
   - **1.2** The yield (49 true-positive defects, 15 merged-PR-fixed, across three widely-used VDBMSs) demonstrates practical impact beyond a benchmark exercise. These are not toy findings; maintainers have acknowledged them.
   - **1.3 [major, fixable]** The 85% residual is "the composition of our findings, not a population estimate" (§1, §6), but the abstract leads with it as the central quantitative hook: "about 85% are documentation-implementation defects that differential, metamorphic, and property-based oracles cannot reach." This framing overreaches: the proportion is determined by TestVDB's design (which targets this class), so the 85% is a lower bound on the class TestVDB looks for, not evidence of the class's prevalence in the wild. The body's caveat is correct but comes too late. *Fix: reframe the abstract's 85% explicitly as "of the defects TestVDB surfaced, about 85% are in this class" and consider demoting the number from its abstract headline position, or pair it with a population-level estimate (the paper acknowledges capture-recapture is future work).*
   - **1.4 [minor, fixable]** The paper scopes itself to documentation-implementation consistency and explicitly excludes result correctness (ANN recall, ranking). This is a reasonable narrowing, but the Significance claim could be strengthened by at least briefly indicating how large the documentation-implementation subset is estimated to be in the VDBMS defect population (the cited roadmap attributes ~43% to incorrect behavior, but the mapping from "incorrect behavior" to "documentation-implementation" is not made precise).

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** The delta against MASTOR (fetched; cached summary) is real and well-articulated. MASTOR reads source to *generate oracles that encode implemented behavior*; TestVDB reads source to *falsify documentation-derived claims*. The cached summary's own stated limitation confirms this asymmetry directly: "LLM-derived oracles are evaluated by mutation score against implementation mutants — i.e., oracles are judged against the implementation's own behavior, not against an external specification of correctness." TestVDB targets exactly this gap. The positioning in §3 and §7 is accurate, not a strawman.
   - **2.2** The delta against SATORI (fetched; ASE 2025) holds. SATORI is black-box over the OpenAPI Specification and targets response-field properties (format, length, numerical range). The paper correctly identifies OAS as a low-ambiguity structured source relative to VDBMS natural-language documentation. SATORI's authors call OAS prose "unstructured," but this is a relative claim — OAS schema elements (`type`, `format`, `minimum`, `maximum`) encode machine-checkable constraints that VDBMS documentation lacks. The source-ambiguity gap TestVDB identifies is genuine.
   - **2.3** The delta against AGORA+ (abstract-level, via SATORI's reporting) is reasonable but less directly verified. AGORA+ detects likely invariants from execution traces and targets response correctness; it does not address input-acceptance decisions on ambiguous documentation. Paper's Table 1 characterization (row 5) is consistent with SATORI's description of AGORA+.
   - **2.4** The delta against VDBFuzz (abstract-level; no indexed metadata located) is structurally clear — VDBFuzz is crash-oracle-based — but cannot be independently verified beyond the paper's description and §6 head-to-head. The paper's characterization is plausible and appropriately caveated.
   - **2.5** The documentation-derived oracle line (Toradocu, Doc2OracLL, Konstantinou et al., abstract-level) is correctly positioned: these extract from Javadoc/specifications and trust the LLM output without an independent falsifier. The Doc2OracLL abstract (FSE 2025) confirms this — "Javadoc comments alone can match or outperform approaches that utilize the MUT implementation," with no verification step against source. TestVDB's source-grounded falsification is a real delta.
   - **2.6 [minor, fixable]** The Novelty story would be sharper if the paper explicitly compared the *source-ambiguity spectrum* — OAS (SATORI) → Javadoc (Toradocu, Doc2OracLL) → natural-language VDBMS prose (TestVDB) — as a continuous axis, rather than only a binary "structured vs. ambiguous." This would make the contribution's placement clearer.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The 18-clause probe (Table 2, §6 RQ3) is well-designed: live-probe-confirmed over-strict clauses, an independent DeepSeek formalization, and a within-vendor contrast (Qdrant's optional-default parameters vs. explicit-minimum parameters) that isolates documentation style as the driver. The falsifiable prediction ("optional default + no explicit bound ⇒ over-formalization candidate") is a useful, testable generalization.
   - **3.2** The 67% precision / 74% recall retrospective is the strongest single piece of evidence for the source-grounded falsification claim. The 3-run any-confirmed ensemble is justified on falsification semantics (a candidate surviving any independent falsification is more likely a true defect), and the comparison against a no-source-anchor baseline (37% recall) isolates the contribution of source.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The single-run variance (recall 15–78% across five runs) is severe enough to undermine the operating-point claim. The any-confirmed union reaches 85% recall at 5 runs but at 62% precision — meaning roughly 38% of forwarded candidates are false positives at that operating point. The paper reports this honestly but does not adequately discuss *why* some runs are conservative (15% recall) while others are high (78%): is this temperature? prompt sensitivity? An unlabeled source of run-to-run variance is a significant methodological concern. *Fix: at minimum, characterize the source of variance (sampling temperature? retrieval ambiguity in the source-grounding step?), and justify the 3-run choice of operating point beyond convenience.*
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The VDBFuzz bidirectional head-to-head is n=1 per direction. The Discussion (§8) generalizes from these two cases to a "structural hypothesis" about crash-oracle asymmetry. The paper's own language ("we treat these as hypothesis-generating controlled cases rather than a generalized result") is appropriate in §6 but drifts in §8's "crash-focused fuzzing surfaces symptoms whose fixes can leave the documentation-implementation residual intact." The VDBFuzz direction (§6) is fairer — the `wait=true` hardcoding is explicitly called a template limitation — but the rhetoric in §8 should be tightened. *Fix: scope §8's structural claim to "the cases we observed," or run a third direction.*
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** The 10 stale-closed true positives are counted as true defects because the maintainer acknowledged rather than rejected them. This is a reasonable choice, but it inflates the yield precision (68.1%) relative to a stricter standard. A sensitivity analysis excluding these 10 would strengthen the claim.
   - **3.6** The threats-to-validity section is thorough on internal, external, and construct validity. The honesty about lacking a public VDBMS ground-truth defect catalog is appreciated.

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate
   - **4.1** The paper commits to releasing the artifact (48 candidates, all five dev-reviewer runs, corrected ground truth, per-issue mapping for the 85% composition, full prompts, target versions) "at a persistent URL upon acceptance." This is a pledge rather than a reachable link; per the dual-review convention, the bar is whether the link is *declared and reachable* — a pledge is below the bar, but the in-paper evidence (per-issue mapping description, corrected ground truth accounting, explicit supersession of the earlier 81% claim) is sufficient to follow conceptually.
   - **4.2** The dev-reviewer's three checks (independently reproducible, evidence sufficient, falsifiable) are described concretely enough to reimplement. The pipeline's five stages are clear.
   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** Some quantitative claims reference the artifact without in-text pointers: e.g., "$10 per target at current API pricing" and "$10^4$ LLM calls" are ballpark figures whose provenance is unclear until acceptance. A pointer to a per-token accounting (which the paper says is in the artifact) would help, but until the artifact is public, these numbers cannot be checked.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1 [minor, fixable]** Several paragraphs are heavily parenthetical, making the logic hard to follow. §6's RQ1 paragraph (single block, ~30 lines) interleaves yield precision, fault-model classification, the VDBFuzz head-to-head, the Qdrant `wait=true` investigation, and the structural hypothesis, in one unbroken paragraph. *Fix: split RQ1 into separate paragraphs per sub-question.*
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** §6's RQ3 paragraph is similarly dense: the behavior-level probe (the 11/11 idempotency-TI finding, described as "the headline finding of this probe") is buried after the parameter-TI discussion and the within-vendor contrast. If the behavior-TI rate (11/11, [74%, 100%]) is genuinely the headline, surface it earlier.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** Table 2's caption mentions that "the behavior over-strict (now eleven, all TI) and explicit-bound subtypes extend this probe to n=50," but Table 2 itself only shows the 18 clauses. Either include the behavior and explicit-bound rows in Table 2, or move the n=50 statement to the text where the extended probe is introduced.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** Notation inconsistency: `dev-reviewer-v2`, `GLM-5.2`, `DeepSeek V4 Pro` (in MASTOR's summary) — model names are inconsistent in capitalization and versioning across the paper. Standardize.
