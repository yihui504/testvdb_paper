## Reviewer 1: Domain Expert

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper introduces TestVDB, a system for testing documentation-implementation consistency in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs) — cases where the API silently accepts inputs or produces behaviors that violate its natural-language documentation (e.g., accepting `nprobe=0` or `ef=0` where documentation prescribes rejection). The authors observe that a substantial portion of VDBMS defects are not reachable by classical oracles (crash, differential, metamorphic, property-based), because the relevant accept/reject boundary is expressed in ambiguous natural-language prose rather than a formal schema. They place VDBMS documentation-implementation consistency in a regime where an LLM must play two roles — extracting behavioral claims from documentation and judging consistency — and argue this introduces a two-layer unreliability: a family-specific layer (LLM self-preference, mitigated by cross-model validation) and a task-intrinsic layer (ambiguous documentation yields the same wrong claim across families, which cross-model validation cannot break).

The core methodological contribution is **source-grounded falsification**: LLM-derived claims are treated as refutable hypotheses and falsified against the implementation's actual behavior (via a dev-reviewer agent that reproduces candidates and cross-checks them against source). A retrospective on 48 maintainer-adjudicated candidates (Milvus + Qdrant) shows the source-grounded dev-reviewer reaches 67% precision and 74% recall (3-run any-confirmed ensemble), versus 37% recall without the source anchor. An 18-clause probe on Milvus and Qdrant demonstrates that source-grounded falsification catches all over-strict clauses, while cross-model judging misses 3 of the 6 task-intrinsic ones. At scale, TestVDB surfaced 107 candidate issues across three VDBMSs (Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate); 49 are true-positive defects, with 15 fixed via merged PR.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The paper identifies and cleanly characterizes a real, previously under-addressed oracle gap — the documentation-implementation residual where deterministic oracles cannot adjudicate natural-language accept/reject boundaries (Table 1). The scoping discussion distinguishing this from result correctness is clear and well-motivated — see 1.1, 2.1.
- **S2:** The family-specific vs. task-intrinsic error decomposition is a genuinely novel diagnostic for LLM-as-judge reliability in testing pipelines. The 18-clause probe provides direct evidence that cross-model validation is insufficient where the documentation is the ambiguity source, which is a non-obvious result — see 3.1, 3.2.
- **S3:** The source-grounded falsification mechanism is a principled counter to task-intrinsic error: using source as an *asymmetric falsifier* of documentation-derived claims (rather than as the oracle itself) is a crisp delta against the closest competitor, MASTOR, whose summary itself confirms that LLM-derived oracles are evaluated against implementation behavior — see 3.2.
- **S4:** Real-world yield is substantial: 49 maintainer-acknowledged true-positive defects across three VDBMSs, with 15 merged-PR fixes, is evidence the method produces actionable findings at scale — see 1.2.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The 85% "documentation-implementation residual" headline is the composition of TestVDB's own findings, not a population estimate, and the paper does not adequately separate the rhetorical use of this number (in the abstract) from its proper scope — the abstract's "about 85% are documentation-implementation defects that differential, metamorphic, and property-based oracles cannot reach" reads as a population claim until the body's caveat is reached. This weakens Significance — see 1.3.
- **W2:** Single-run variance is severe (recall 15–78% across five runs; precision 50–73%), and the operating point chosen (any-confirmed union) inflates recall while trading precision in a way that the paper underplays. The 67%/74% headline rests on an ensemble rule whose behavior is not fully characterized (e.g., false-positive growth at 5 runs: 62% precision) — see 3.3.
- **W3:** The bidirectional VDBFuzz head-to-head is n=1 per direction. Even though the paper flags this as hypothesis-generating, the Discussion's structural-asymmetry hypothesis (§8) over-extends from these two cases. The "VDBFuzz's templates hardcode `wait=true`" observation is a limitation of VDBFuzz's current templates, not a structural property of crash oracles — and the paper mostly says this, but the rhetoric sometimes slips — see 3.4.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** VDBMSs are infrastructure for retrieval-augmented LLM applications, and silent-accept defects (e.g., a negative `score_threshold` that disables a filter and returns all matches) corrupt query semantics in ways that are hard to detect downstream. The paper cites direct evidence (the VDBMS bug study and roadmap) that this is a meaningful defect class. The problem is real and timely.
   - **1.2** The yield (49 true-positive defects, 15 merged-PR-fixed, across three widely-used VDBMSs) demonstrates practical impact beyond a benchmark exercise. These are not toy findings; maintainers have acknowledged them.
   - **1.3 [major, fixable]** The 85% residual is "the composition of our findings, not a population estimate" (§1, §6), but the abstract leads with it as the central quantitative hook: "about 85% are documentation-implementation defects that differential, metamorphic, and property-based oracles cannot reach." This framing overreaches: the proportion is determined by TestVDB's design (which targets this class), so the 85% is a lower bound on the class TestVDB looks for, not evidence of the class's prevalence in the wild. The body's caveat is correct but comes too late. *Fix: reframe the abstract's 85% explicitly as "of the defects TestVDB surfaced, about 85% are in this class" and consider demoting the number from its abstract headline position, or pair it with a population-level estimate (the paper acknowledges capture-recapture is future work).*
   - **1.4 [minor, fixable]** The paper scopes itself to documentation-implementation consistency and explicitly excludes result correctness (ANN recall, ranking). This is a reasonable narrowing, but the Significance claim could be strengthened by at least briefly indicating how large the documentation-implementation subset is estimated to be in the VDBMS defect population (the cited roadmap attributes ~43% to incorrect behavior, but the mapping from "incorrect behavior" to "documentation-implementation" is not made precise).

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** The delta against MASTOR (fetched; cached summary) is real and well-articulated. MASTOR reads source to *generate oracles that encode implemented behavior*; TestVDB reads source to *falsify documentation-derived claims*. The cached summary's own stated limitation confirms this asymmetry directly: "LLM-derived oracles are evaluated by mutation score against implementation mutants — i.e., oracles are judged against the implementation's own behavior, not against an external specification of correctness." TestVDB targets exactly this gap. The positioning in §3 and §7 is accurate, not a strawman.
   - **2.2** The delta against SATORI (fetched; ASE 2025) holds. SATORI is black-box over the OpenAPI Specification and targets response-field properties (format, length, numerical range). The paper correctly identifies OAS as a low-ambiguity structured source relative to VDBMS natural-language documentation. SATORI's authors call OAS prose "unstructured," but this is a relative claim — OAS schema elements (`type`, `format`, `minimum`, `maximum`) encode machine-checkable constraints that VDBMS documentation lacks. The source-ambiguity gap TestVDB identifies is genuine.
   - **2.3** The delta against AGORA+ (abstract-level, via SATORI's reporting) is reasonable but less directly verified. AGORA+ detects likely invariants from execution traces and targets response correctness; it does not address input-acceptance decisions on ambiguous documentation. Paper's Table 1 characterization (row 5) is consistent with SATORI's description of AGORA+.
   - **2.4** The delta against VDBFuzz (abstract-level; no indexed metadata located) is structurally clear — VDBFuzz is crash-oracle-based — but cannot be independently verified beyond the paper's description and §6 head-to-head. The paper's characterization is plausible and appropriately caveated.
   - **2.5** The documentation-derived oracle line (Toradocu, Doc2OracLL, Konstantinou et al., abstract-level) is correctly positioned: these extract from Javadoc/specifications and trust the LLM output without an independent falsifier. The Doc2OracLL abstract (FSE 2025) confirms this — "Javadoc comments alone can match or outperform approaches that utilize the MUT implementation," with no verification step against source. TestVDB's source-grounded falsification is a real delta.
   - **2.6 [minor, fixable]** The Novelty story would be sharper if the paper explicitly compared the *source-ambiguity spectrum* — OAS (SATORI) → Javadoc (Toradocu, Doc2OracLL) → natural-language VDBMS prose (TestVDB) — as a continuous axis, rather than only a binary "structured vs. ambiguous." This would make the contribution's placement clearer.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The 18-clause probe (Table 2, §6 RQ3) is well-designed: live-probe-confirmed over-strict clauses, an independent DeepSeek formalization, and a within-vendor contrast (Qdrant's optional-default parameters vs. explicit-minimum parameters) that isolates documentation style as the driver. The falsifiable prediction ("optional default + no explicit bound ⇒ over-formalization candidate") is a useful, testable generalization.
   - **3.2** The 67% precision / 74% recall retrospective is the strongest single piece of evidence for the source-grounded falsification claim. The 3-run any-confirmed ensemble is justified on falsification semantics (a candidate surviving any independent falsification is more likely a true defect), and the comparison against a no-source-anchor baseline (37% recall) isolates the contribution of source.
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The single-run variance (recall 15–78% across five runs) is severe enough to undermine the operating-point claim. The any-confirmed union reaches 85% recall at 5 runs but at 62% precision — meaning roughly 38% of forwarded candidates are false positives at that operating point. The paper reports this honestly but does not adequately discuss *why* some runs are conservative (15% recall) while others are high (78%): is this temperature? prompt sensitivity? An unlabeled source of run-to-run variance is a significant methodological concern. *Fix: at minimum, characterize the source of variance (sampling temperature? retrieval ambiguity in the source-grounding step?), and justify the 3-run choice of operating point beyond convenience.*
   - **3.4 [major, fixable]** The VDBFuzz bidirectional head-to-head is n=1 per direction. The Discussion (§8) generalizes from these two cases to a "structural hypothesis" about crash-oracle asymmetry. The paper's own language ("we treat these as hypothesis-generating controlled cases rather than a generalized result") is appropriate in §6 but drifts in §8's "crash-focused fuzzing surfaces symptoms whose fixes can leave the documentation-implementation residual intact." The VDBFuzz direction (§6) is fairer — the `wait=true` hardcoding is explicitly called a template limitation — but the rhetoric in §8 should be tightened. *Fix: scope §8's structural claim to "the cases we observed," or run a third direction.*
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** The 10 stale-closed true positives are counted as true defects because the maintainer acknowledged rather than rejected them. This is a reasonable choice, but it inflates the yield precision (68.1%) relative to a stricter standard. A sensitivity analysis excluding these 10 would strengthen the claim.
   - **3.6** The threats-to-validity section is thorough on internal, external, and construct validity. The honesty about lacking a public VDBMS ground-truth defect catalog is appreciated.

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate
   - **4.1** The paper commits to releasing the artifact (48 candidates, all five dev-reviewer runs, corrected ground truth, per-issue mapping for the 85% composition, full prompts, target versions) "at a persistent URL upon acceptance." This is a pledge rather than a reachable link; per the dual-review convention, the bar is whether the link is *declared and reachable* — a pledge is below the bar, but the in-paper evidence (per-issue mapping description, corrected ground truth accounting, explicit supersession of the earlier 81% claim) is sufficient to follow conceptually.
   - **4.2** The dev-reviewer's three checks (independently reproducible, evidence sufficient, falsifiable) are described concretely enough to reimplement. The pipeline's five stages are clear.
   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** Some quantitative claims reference the artifact without in-text pointers: e.g., "$10 per target at current API pricing" and "$10^4$ LLM calls" are ballpark figures whose provenance is unclear until acceptance. A pointer to a per-token accounting (which the paper says is in the artifact) would help, but until the artifact is public, these numbers cannot be checked.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1 [minor, fixable]** Several paragraphs are heavily parenthetical, making the logic hard to follow. §6's RQ1 paragraph (single block, ~30 lines) interleaves yield precision, fault-model classification, the VDBFuzz head-to-head, the Qdrant `wait=true` investigation, and the structural hypothesis, in one unbroken paragraph. *Fix: split RQ1 into separate paragraphs per sub-question.*
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** §6's RQ3 paragraph is similarly dense: the behavior-level probe (the 11/11 idempotency-TI finding, described as "the headline finding of this probe") is buried after the parameter-TI discussion and the within-vendor contrast. If the behavior-TI rate (11/11, [74%, 100%]) is genuinely the headline, surface it earlier.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** Table 2's caption mentions that "the behavior over-strict (now eleven, all TI) and explicit-bound subtypes extend this probe to n=50," but Table 2 itself only shows the 18 clauses. Either include the behavior and explicit-bound rows in Table 2, or move the n=50 statement to the text where the extended probe is introduced.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** Notation inconsistency: `dev-reviewer-v2`, `GLM-5.2`, `DeepSeek V4 Pro` (in MASTOR's summary) — model names are inconsistent in capitalization and versioning across the paper. Standardize.

## Reviewer 2: Area Specialist

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

TestVDB targets documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs): cases where the API silently accepts inputs or produces behaviors its natural-language documentation does not permit (e.g., `nprobe=0`, out-of-range HNSW `ef=0`, success returned where the documentation prescribes rejection). The paper motivates this class empirically — a large majority of VDBMS defects produce no crash, so existing fuzzers such as VDBFuzz cannot reach them — and then argues that differential, metamorphic, and property-based oracles also miss them, because the relevant accept/reject boundary is documented in prose rather than encoded in a machine-checkable schema.

The paper's central conceptual contribution is to map VDBMS documentation-implementation consistency into an "LLM-as-semantic-oracle" regime that prior REST-API oracle tools (MASTOR, SATORI, AGORA+) avoid by operating on low-ambiguity structured sources. In this regime the LLM must both extract behavioral claims from documentation and judge conformance; the resulting claims are unreliable in two layers — a family-specific self-preference layer that cross-model validation mitigates, and a task-intrinsic layer where multiple LLM families extract the same wrong clause from the ambiguous documentation, which cross-model validation cannot fix. The fix is **source-grounded falsification**: treat each LLM-derived behavioral claim as a refutable hypothesis and falsify it against the implementation's source code, where the documentation expresses intent and the source expresses actual behavior. TestVDB instantiates this as a multi-agent pipeline with a "dev-reviewer" agent that reproduces, source-grounds, and falsifies each candidate. Evaluation reports 107 issues submitted across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate (49 maintainer-acknowledged true positives, 15 fixed via merged PR), 67% precision / 74% recall on a controlled retrospective over 48 adjudicated candidates, and an eighteen-clause probe showing cross-model judging misses 3 of 6 task-intrinsic clauses while source-grounded falsification catches all 18.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The two-layer unreliability decomposition (family-specific vs. task-intrinsic) is a genuinely useful conceptual contribution — it cleanly separates "self-preference bias" (Panickssery et al.) from "intra-judge self-inconsistency" (Haldar & Hockenmaier) from the third, extraction-level task-intrinsic layer the paper identifies, and motivates why cross-model validation is necessary-but-insufficient — see 2.1, 2.3.
- **S2:** The directional asymmetry in source use against MASTOR is a real and well-scoped novelty claim: both papers are source-grounded, but MASTOR reads source to encode what the implementation does (so cannot detect doc-code gaps), while TestVDB reads source to falsify claims extracted from documentation (so targets exactly the gap) — see 2.2.
- **S3:** Honest framing of the 85% residual as "composition of our findings, not a population estimate" and of the bidirectional VDBFuzz comparison as n=1-per-direction hypothesis-generating, with explicit limitations on transfer and on treating the implementation as correct — see 1.2, 3.2.
- **S4:** Within-vendor parameter-style contrast (Milvus and Qdrant both: optional-default parameters over-formalize, explicit-bound parameters do not; Weaviate all-explicit-bounds produces zero over-strict) yielding a falsifiable prediction — see 2.4.

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The headline precision/recall (67% / 74%) comes from a 3-run any-confirmed ensemble compared against a single-run no-source baseline; the any-confirmed union favors recall by construction and single-run recall ranges 15–78%, which is large variance — see 1.3.
- **W2:** The RQ3 task-intrinsic probe is small (18 parameter clauses + 11 behaviors + 21 explicit-bound negatives) and concentrated on two vendors for the parameter-TI claim; the 6/18 TI rate has wide Wilson CI [16%, 56%] — the within-vendor contrast is suggestive but not definitive — see 2.3.
- **W3:** The paper's framing of the LLM regime dichotomy (structured source → transcription; NL documentation → interpretation) does a lot of the novelty work; the boundary is less clean than stated, since SATORI also reads NL field descriptions from OpenAPI, and the paper does not engage with where RESTGPT/LlamaRestTest-style LLM rule extraction from NL API descriptions sits — see 2.5.
- **W4:** Closed-source-VDBMS exclusion and the "treat implementation as correct" assumption are real scope limits; a single implementation bug falsifies a clause whose documentation is right, and the paper's only mitigation is to flag it as a limitation — see 1.5.

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The paper tackles a real, well-motivated problem: the empirical bug study and the VDBMS roadmap it cites both attribute a large share of VDBMS defects to incorrect behavior, and the paper documents 49 maintainer-acknowledged documentation-implementation defects (15 merged-PR fixes) across three production VDBMSs. This is meaningful industry impact — these are not toy defects.
   - **1.2** The paper is appropriately careful about what its yield numbers mean: the 85% residual is explicitly framed as the composition of TestVDB's findings (biased toward documentation-implementation defects by design) rather than a population estimate, and the Wilson 95% CI on yield precision (68.1% [56.6%, 77.7%]) is reported with a worst-case bound (45.8%) under the conservative all-pending-as-FP assumption.
   - **1.3 [major, fixable]** The most-cited practical number — "67% precision / 74% recall (3-run ensemble)" — is presented as the operating point, but the union (any-confirmed) rule is biased toward recall by construction (a candidate survives if any run confirms it). This is a defensible design choice for a falsifier (under-confirmation is costlier than forwarding an FP for human triage), but it makes the headline number an upper bound on recall under that operating point, not an intrinsic property of the dev-reviewer. The single-run recall band of 15–78% (5 independent runs) is more representative of intrinsic variance, and should be the headline rather than the union. The paper reports both but headlines the more favorable one.
   - **1.4** The crash-oracle asymmetry discussion (§7) is a thoughtful structural argument for why the two reachability directions differ, and the #7967/#9045 root-cause case (a `debug_assert` skipped in release builds, fix recurred across versions until the API-boundary patch) is concrete and useful.
   - **1.5 [major, unfixable]** Source-grounded falsification requires source access (closed-source VDBMSs excluded) and treats the implementation as correct — an implementation bug wrongly falsifies a clause whose documentation is right. The paper flags this honestly in §7, but it bounds the approach's generality and is a genuine limitation of the contribution rather than a fixable defect.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate
   - **2.1** The two-layer unreliability decomposition (family-specific vs. task-intrinsic) is a useful and non-obvious conceptual move. It correctly maps the family-specific layer onto Panickssery et al.'s self-preference phenomenon (verified: Panickssery 2024 arXiv:2404.13076 establishes that LLM evaluators recognize and favor their own outputs, with self-recognition linearly correlated with self-preference) and cleanly separates it from Haldar & Hockenmaier's intra-judge self-inconsistency (verified: Haldar EMNLP 2025 Findings arXiv:2510.27106 reports low intra-rater reliability across runs). The paper correctly identifies a third axis — extraction-level stability across families — that neither prior work covers and that cross-model validation cannot address.
   - **2.2** The directional asymmetry in source use against MASTOR (verified: Deng et al. arXiv:2606.10465, fetched full text) holds. MASTOR's pipeline extracts oracles from source via transitive import closure, with OAS-anchored endpoint discovery and a precision-biased `*_pending` field for OAS items source does not substantiate (§4.3.3). It encodes implemented behavior as the oracle; it does not extract documentation claims and check them against code. TestVDB extracts from documentation and uses source to falsify. The novelty is real, but the paper should be more precise that it is a re-targeting of source grounding rather than a new mechanism for source grounding itself.
   - **2.3** The within-vendor parameter-style contrast is the strongest evidence for the task-intrinsic phenomenon being tied to documentation style rather than to vendor identity: on both Milvus and Qdrant, parameters with optional defaults (`ef`, `nprobe`, `level`, `replicaNumber` / `timeout`, `group_size`, `score_threshold`) over-formalize, while parameters with explicit documented bounds (`M`, `nlist`, `dimension` / `shard_number`, `replication_factor`, `write_consistency_factor`) do not. Weaviate's all-explicit-bound documentation produces zero over-strict clauses on parameters but contributes the idempotency-behavior phenomenon — a clean cross-vendor confirmation.
   - **2.4** The 11/11 behavior-TI rate (idempotency behaviors where documentation states an explicit error condition) is reported separately from the 6/18 parameter-TI rate, with the mechanistic reason noted (parameter docs state a default that only sometimes invites a bound; behavior docs state an explicit error condition that both families over-formalize). This separation is correct and the paper is right not to pool them as a single headline.
   - **2.5 [major, fixable]** The paper's dichotomy between the "structured-source regime" (MASTOR/SATORI/AGORA+, low ambiguity, transcription) and the "NL-documentation regime" (TestVDB, high ambiguity, interpretation) is doing significant novelty work, and the boundary is less clean than stated. SATORI (verified: arXiv:2508.16318) reads NL field descriptions from OpenAPI to infer behavior — it does transcribe from a structured frame, but it is not pure schema transcription. The paper does not engage with the LLM-rule-extraction line (RESTGPT, LlamaRestTest, Kim et al. 2023–2025) which extracts rules from NL descriptions in OpenAPI; these sit on the structured side of the dichotomy but extract from NL. The paper's Related Work covers Toradocu/Doc2OracLL/AugmenTest/ChatAssert/Testora well, but the boundary case of LLM extraction from OpenAPI NL descriptions deserves explicit positioning.
   - **2.6** The "novelty gate" stage of the pipeline (§4) is mentioned but its behavior is not characterized — how many candidates it removes, against what baseline (known-issues DB, dedup heuristic). This is minor but leaves a gap in the pipeline's contribution decomposition.

3. **Soundness** — Adequate
   - **3.1** The central RQ3 claim — "source-grounded falsification resolves task-intrinsic errors that cross-model validation cannot" — is supported by the eighteen-clause probe: cross-model judging misses 3 of 6 TI clauses; source-grounded falsification contradicts all 18. The logic is sound: source expresses actual behavior, so an implementation that accepts the value an over-strict clause rejects contradicts the clause directly. This is the strongest single claim in the paper and it is well-defended.
   - **3.2 [major, fixable]** The RQ3 probe is small. Eighteen parameter clauses across two vendors (Milvus 13, Qdrant 5) with a Wilson 95% CI of [16%, 56%] on the TI rate is wide. The 11-clause behavior probe at 11/11 [74%, 100%] is striking but n=11 with no failures is statistically thin. The 21-clause explicit-bound negative check at 0/21 [0%, 16%] is well-sized for the specificity claim. The paper acknowledges this in Threats ("the over-strict subset remains the most contingent finding; the retrospective and the yield are the broader evidence base, and a larger head-to-head study is ongoing") but the headline framing leans on RQ3.
   - **3.3** The retrospective (RQ2) is well-designed for the question it asks: 48 maintainer-adjudicated candidates (27 TP, 21 FP; Milvus 32, Qdrant 16) with a 3-run ensemble, single-LLM baseline (no source anchor), and a per-anchor ablation isolating the source-grounded and threat-model anchors. The κ=1.0 cross-model agreement on 20 stratified candidates is a useful construct-validity check (with the caveat that 20 is small and the sample is diversity-stratified, not random).
   - **3.4** Comparison fairness: the VDBFuzz head-to-head (§6) is honestly reported as n=1 per direction, and the paper does not generalize from it — "we treat these as hypothesis-generating controlled cases rather than a generalized result." The Qdrant v1.18.2 run of VDBFuzz's default configuration with 26,000+ mutated requests and 0 crashes is a fair budget allocation; the version-state explanation (case-study crashes already fixed in v1.18.2) is appropriate. The reverse-direction caveat about `?wait=true` being hardcoded in VDBFuzz's templates is concrete and checkable.
   - **3.5 [minor, fixable]** The 85% residual composition is asserted to be auditable in the artifact ("the per-issue mapping — all 107 submissions assigned to one of these three fault models with a one-line rationale — is in the artifact"). This is good practice, but the paper would be stronger with a brief inline table or example mappings; currently the reader must take the 85%/10%/5% split on faith.
   - **3.6** The "no public ground-truth defect catalog for VDBMSs" caveat in Threats is the right honest scoping for why the dev-reviewer is evaluated against maintainer adjudication rather than against a curated catalog; the absence of an a priori defect sample is a limitation the paper flags ("a full-space estimation with capture-recapture or an unbiased defect sample is future work").

4. **Verifiability** — Adequate
   - **4.1** The paper promises a persistent-URL artifact on acceptance, including: the 48 candidates, all five dev-reviewer runs, the corrected ground truth (27 TP + 21 FP), the 107-issue fault-model mapping with rationales, the full prompt set, per-token accounting, and the target version matrix. This is the right set of artifacts for the claims.
   - **4.2** Specific URLs the paper relies on are checkable: the Qdrant issue #9045 (footnote 4) and #7967 (footnote 7) are live GitHub issues; the GLM backbone via the bigmodel Anthropic-compatible API is documented. The VDBFuzz `?wait=true` template claim (footnote 5) is verifiable against VDBFuzz's released Qdrate suite and the paper explicitly invites this check.
   - **4.3 [minor, fixable]** The artifact is "released at a persistent URL upon acceptance" — this is a common but suboptimal practice; an anonymous preview link or Zenondo-with-embargo would let reviewers verify the per-issue 107 mapping and the 5-run stability analysis that the Threats section leans on. Without it, several quantitative claims (the 85/10/5 composition, the 5-run recall band) are unverifiable from the paper alone.
   - **4.4** The prompts for the 20 agents are promised in the artifact but not summarized in the paper — a brief prompt skeleton for the dev-reviewer's three-check procedure would help readers evaluate whether the source-grounding procedure is reproducible across LLM backbones other than GLM-5.2.

The duplication is redundant and the abstract would benefit from compression.
   - **5.2 [minor, fixable]** Section §6 (Evaluation) packs a large amount of quantitative detail into paragraph form, making it hard to extract the key numbers.    - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** Table 1 (Oracle candidates) is well-constructed but row 5 ("REST doc/spec-derived oracles (AGORA+, SATORI, MASTOR)") groups three tools that differ meaningfully in their source-of-evidence (AGORA+ traces, SATORI OpenAPI, MASTOR source code). The paper does distinguish these in §3, but the table row invites the misreading that they are equivalent.

   - **5.5 [minor, fixable]** Typos / minor: "Milvus and Qdrant's search parameters" possessive inconsistency; "the large majority of the 50" in Table 1 caption (earlier text says 49 TP) — be consistent about 49 vs. 50.
   - **5.6 [minor, fixable]** The conclusion's headline ("67% precision and 74% recall") should match the body's framing — the body reports 67% precision / 74% recall at 3-run ensemble on the 48-candidate retrospective, with 65% accuracy; the conclusion omits accuracy and the ensemble qualifier.

### Questions

- **Q1 (from 1.3):** What is the rationale for headlining the 3-run any-confirmed ensemble as the operating point rather than the per-run median or majority vote? The paper reports majority voting (≥3 of 5) at 64% precision / 26% recall — the precision is within the single-run band but the recall is unusable. ## Reviewer 3: General Reviewer

**Overall Recommendation:** Weak Accept

### Summary

The paper addresses a real testing gap: documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs), where the API silently accepts inputs that violate its own natural-language documentation (e.g., accepting `nprobe=0` or `ef=0` documented as "default 1"). Because these accept/reject decisions cannot be mechanically checked—the documented boundary is prose, not a formal grammar—classical oracles (crash, differential, metamorphic, property-based) cannot reach them, and the human oracle does not scale. The authors argue, with a worked-out oracle-exclusion table, that this residual forces an LLM into the testing pipeline as both extractor of behavioral claims and direct semantic judge.

The LLM-as-oracle introduces two reliability layers. The family-specific layer (self-preference) is mitigated by cross-model validation; the task-intrinsic layer—where ambiguous documentation causes different LLM families to converge on the same wrong clause—is not. The paper's contribution is *source-grounded falsification*: treating LLM-derived behavioral claims as refutable hypotheses and checking them against the implementation's actual behavior via a dev-reviewer agent. TestVDB instantiates this and surfaced 107 candidate issues across Milvus, Qdrant, and Weaviate, with 49 maintainer-acknowledged true positives. A controlled retrospective on 48 adjudicated candidates reports 67% precision / 74% recall with source grounding (vs. 37% recall without), and an eighteen-clause probe (plus extensions to behavior-level and explicit-bound subtypes, n=50) shows source-grounded falsification catches all task-intrinsic errors while cross-model judging misses half of them.

### Core Strengths

- **S1:** The oracle-exclusion argument (Table 1) is coherent, self-contained, and does the load-bearing work of motivating an LLM oracle. It walks each classical oracle family through what it reaches and why the documentation-implementation residual escapes it; this is exactly the kind of internal-coherence reasoning a general reviewer can fully evaluate. — see 1.1, 2.1
- **S2:** The decomposition of LLM unreliability into family-specific vs task-intrinsic layers, with the cross-model judging probe that empirically shows the second layer survives the first, is a clean and falsifiable conceptual move. The within-vendor contrast (Qdrant's optional-default search parameters over-strict vs. its explicit-minimum collection parameters not) is the most convincing single piece of evidence. — see 2.2, 2.3
- **S3:** Information sufficiency for Verifiability is genuinely strong: pin versions, per-anchor ablation, 5-run variance honestly disclosed, per-issue fault-model mapping promised in the artifact, Wilson CIs on rates, and the `wait=false` / VDBFuzz template-detail forensic is the kind of self-checking a reviewer expects. — see 4.1, 4.2

### Core Weaknesses

- **W1:** The headline precision/recall (67% / 74%) rests on n=48 with a wide single-run recall band of 15–78% across five runs, and the union ensemble rule is the only operating point that reaches the headline. The paper is transparent about this, but the operating-point selection is post-hoc and the union rule is precisely the one that maximizes recall by construction. — see 3.1, 3.2
- **W2:** The 85% "documentation-implementation residual" figure is presented prominently in the Abstract and Contributions, but the paper carefully admits in §6 (Discussion) and §5 (RQ1) that it is "the composition of TestVDB's findings, biased toward documentation-implementation defects by design, not an estimate of the true defect distribution." The framing in the Abstract does not carry that caveat, and a general reader will read 85% as a population estimate. — see 3.3
- **W3:** The source-grounded dev-reviewer is implemented on a single LLM family (GLM-5.2). The κ=1.0 DeepSeek cross-model check on 20 candidates is reassuring on the *agreement* axis, but the paper does not claim it bounds recall, and §6 explicitly admits no public ground-truth defect catalog exists. This caps the strength of the central RQ2/RQ3 claims. — see 3.4

### Detailed Assessment

1. **Significance** — Adequate
   - **1.1** The paper targets a genuinely under-servedred defect class. The Introduction and §2 (Background) make a self-contained case that the VDBMS oracle problem is acute, that ~43% of VDBMS bugs per the cited roadmap~\cite{roadmap25} are behavioral, and that crash-only fuzzers (VDBFuzz~\cite{vdbfuzz26}) miss the silent-accept subset. A general reviewer can verify this argument from the paper's own content.
   - **1.2** The 49 maintainer-acknowledged true positives (15 merged-PR-fixed, 16 open fix-PRs, 18 acknowledged-but-unfixed) are concrete impact: third-party maintainers acted on the findings, which is a strong external signal of significance.
   - **1.3 [minor, fixable]** The Discussion §6 explicitly limits the claim to "structurally similar documentation regimes" and lists transfer targets (REST APIs without OpenAPI coverage, config validation, policy-as-code) as *future work* with no testing. The paper does not over-claim here, but the abstract framing of "the testing of LLM-dependent systems" is broader than what the VDBMS-only evaluation supports. Trimming the abstract's closing line would tighten the significance claim to match the evidence.

2. **Novelty** — Adequate *(provisional — assessed from the paper's own claims; I did not survey the field)*
   - **2.1** The delta over MASTOR~\cite{mastor26} is clearly articulated in §4 (Design) and §7 (Related Work): both are source-grounded, but MASTOR reads source to *encode implemented behavior* as the oracle, whereas TestVDB reads source to *falsify documentation-derived claims*. The asymmetry (documentation = intended, source = actual, gap = target) is a non-obvious reframing of what "source as oracle" means, and the paper states it cleanly.
   - **2.2** The task-intrinsic vs family-specific decomposition is the conceptual novelty. The within-vendor Qdrant contrast (search parameters with optional defaults over-formalized, collection parameters with explicit minimums not) is, as far as the paper itself demonstrates, the original empirical observation; the falsifiable prediction that "an optional-default parameter with no explicit bound is a candidate for over-formalization" is the kind of transferable artifact a general reviewer can judge on its face.
   - **2.3 [minor, fixable]** §7's positioning against Toradocu~\cite{toradocu16}, Doc2OracLL~\cite{doc2oracll25}, AugmenTest~\cite{augmentest25}, Konstantinou et al.~\cite{konstantinou24}, ChatAssert~\cite{chatassert24}, and Testora~\cite{testora26} is well-differentiated, but the "we differ by using source, not runtime behavior, as an independent verification source" claim is asserted rather than traced to where ChatAssert/Testora's runtime-feedback loop is shown insufficient for task-intrinsic errors. A single sentence with the specific failure mode would harden this. Provisional because I have not read these competitors directly.
   - **2.4 [major, fixable]** The novelty of the *family-specific vs task-intrinsic* split depends on it being a real, exploitable distinction rather than a relabeling of "hard cases for cross-model judging." The 18-clause probe is small (6 TI clauses), and the RQ3 scaling extensions bring it to 29 TI + 21 explicit-bound negatives, which is better but still bounded by what the paper itself calls "the phenomenon rather than sampling effort." The transferability of the explicit-bound predictor beyond three vendors is untested. The conceptual claim survives but the empirical ceiling is visible.

3. **Soundness** — Weak
   - **3.1** The central RQ2 claim (source grounding drives recall from 37%→74%) is supported by a controlled retrospective on 48 adjudicated candidates, with a 3-run any-confirmed ensemble. The methodology is appropriate to the question and the ablation (clean-reproduction anchor alone, source anchor alone, threat-model anchor alone, union) is the right design to isolate the source anchor's contribution. The main claim is defensible *at the reported operating point*.
   - **3.2 [major, fixable]** The single-run recall band of 15–78% across five runs is disclosed honestly, but the operating-point selection raises a soundness concern: the union (any-confirmed) rule is *defined* as the rule that maximizes recall by surfacing any candidate that any run confirms, and majority voting (≥3 of 5) is dismissed as "recall too low to be useful" at 26%. The paper argues under-confirmation is costlier than forwarding false positives for triage, which is a legitimate operational stance, but it is also the stance that produces the headline number. A simple fix: present the full precision-recall curve across operating points (1-of-5, 2-of-5, ..., 5-of-5) rather than narrating two points, and justify the operating-point choice with a decision-theoretic argument (false-positive triage cost vs. missed-defect cost).
   - **3.3 [major, fixable]** The 85% composition figure is, as the paper admits in §5 and §6, a property of TestVDB's design (it targets documentation-implementation defects) rather than an estimate of the VDBMS defect population. This is fine in the body. But the Abstract says "about 85% are documentation-implementation defects that ... standard oracle families cannot reach" without that framing, and Contributions bullet 2 ("Quantification of the documentation-implementation residual") reads as a population-level result. Either reframe in the Abstract ("of the issues TestVDB surfaced, about 85% were documentation-implementation defects by our classification, biased by design toward this class") or move the number to RQ1 only.
   - **3.4 [major, unfixable]** The source-grounded dev-reviewer uses a single LLM family (GLM-5.2) for the source-anchor results. The DeepSeek cross-model κ=1.0 check on 20 candidates addresses family-specificity of the *verdict* but explicitly does not bound recall, and §6 admits no public ground-truth defect catalog exists. This is an inherent limitation of the study design and cannot be patched in revision; it caps the strength of RQ2/RQ3 claims to "consistent within the family GLM-5.2 on the studied candidates."
   - **3.5** The bidirectional VDBFuzz head-to-head is appropriately caveated as "n=1 per direction" and "hypothesis-generating controlled cases rather than a generalized result." This is honest and the right framing.
   - **3.6 [minor, fixable]** The "specificity check" on 21 explicit-bound parameters (0/21 over-formalized, Wilson 95% CI [0%, 16%]) is a strong negative control that supports the explicit-bound predictor, but the paper does not report how these 21 were selected (random, stratified, exhaustive of explicit-bound parameters in three vendors?). If cherry-picked, the negative control is weaker than it looks. A single sentence on selection protocol fixes this.

4. **Verifiability** — Excellent
   - **4.1** The paper provides substantial information for a reader to follow the methodology: pinned Docker versions (Milvus 2.6.19, Qdrant v1.18.2, Weaviate v1.38.2), the full per-anchor ablation, the 5-run ensemble, Wilson CIs on all reported rates, and a version matrix (Table 2) for the bidirectional reachability probe. Section §4 names the three checks the dev-reviewer applies and the three anchors they draw on, which is enough to conceptually reproduce the pipeline.
   - **4.2** The paper repeatedly commits to artifact release: "the per-issue mapping—all 107 submissions assigned to one of these three fault models with a one-line rationale, plus a maintained count of each—is in the artifact"; "the 48 candidates, all five dev-reviewer runs, and the corrected ground truth (27 TP + 21 FP, after the Qdrant reclassification) are in the artifact"; "the full prompts, target versions, and per-token accounting are in the artifact, which we will release at a persistent URL upon acceptance." No artifact link is declared in the paper itself, but the commitment is consistent and specific.
   - **4.3** The `#9045` / `#7967` Qdrant forensic is verifiable from the linked GitHub issues, and the `wait=true` template detail in VDBFuzz's released suite is the kind of checkable forensic that supports the head-to-head claim. This is the level of detail Verifiability wants.
   - **4.4 [minor, fixable]** The contract-formalizer agent and its prompt are referenced (RQ3 scaling passes) but not described in §3 (Implementation) beyond "20 agents defined by a task-structured role prompt." A reader cannot reproduce the formalization step without the prompt; the artifact commitment covers this, but a one-paragraph description of the formalizer's role would let a reviewer assess the step from the paper alone.

5. **Presentation** — Adequate
   - **5.1** The structure is sound and follows the standard technical-paper arc (Introduction → Background → §3 LLM role → §4 Design → §5 Implementation → §6 Evaluation → Related Work → Discussion → Conclusion). Section numbering is logical and cross-references are consistent.
   - **5.2** The abstract is dense but well-constructed; it states the problem, the method, the headline numbers, and the conceptual contribution in roughly the right proportions. The Discussion §6 paragraph on crash-oracle asymmetry is one of the better-written parts of the paper and earns its place.
   - **5.3 [minor, fixable]** Table 2 (bidirectional reachability) is labeled "Each direction is $n{=}1$" in the caption, but Table 1 (the yield table) has no version label per row and the reader has to dig into RQ1 prose to find which version each VDBMS was tested on. A column in Table 1 with "version tested" would close this.
   - **5.4 [minor, fixable]** The Abstract reports "67% precision and 74% recall (3-run ensemble on 48 adjudicated candidates)" but the body reports these as the *any-confirmed union* operating point of a 5-run study; the abstract does not flag the operating-point selection. Adding "(union ensemble)" or "(see §6 for the full PR curve)" in the abstract would prevent a reader from taking the headline at face value.
   - **5.5 [minor, fixable]** Table 2 (the E2 over-strict table) lists eighteen rows but the caption mentions "The behavior over-strict (now eleven, all TI) and explicit-bound subtypes extend this probe to $n{=}50$ (see text, RQ3)." This is a table-caption digression; either include the behavior rows in a supplementary table or remove the forward-reference from the caption.
   - **5.6 [minor, fixable]** The Conclusion repeats the abstract numbers almost verbatim ("107 candidate issues across three VDBMSs, 49 true-positive defects (15 merged-PR-fixed, 16 with open fix-PRs, 18 acknowledged but unfixed), with the source-grounded dev-reviewer reaching 67% precision and 74% recall on Milvus and Qdrant"). A reader does not need the same enumeration twice; the Conclusion could be tightened to the conceptual claim and the boundary ("the testing of LLM-dependent systems is heading" closing line is well-aimed).
   - **5.7 [minor, fixable]** Notation is mostly consistent, but §3 uses both "dev-reviewer" and "source-grounded falsifier" interchangeably before the term is formally introduced in §4; defining "dev-reviewer" on first use in §3 (or forwarding the reader to §4) would tighten the introduction of the term.
