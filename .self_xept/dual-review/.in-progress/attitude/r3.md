## Reviewer 3 — Friendly
> Confidence: 4/5 · Overall: ACCEPT (8/10)

**Summary**
TestVDB proposes an LLM-driven pipeline to detect documentation-implementation defects in vector databases by extracting behavioral claims from natural-language documentation, generating tests, and adjudicating responses against implementation source. The work addresses a genuine gap in current VDBMS fuzzing and introduces a source-grounded falsifier to mitigate LLM false positives, demonstrating practical impact with 49 maintainer-acknowledged defects.

**Strengths**
1. **Novel problem framing.** The identification of "documentation-implementation defects" as a distinct class that escapes crash-oracle fuzzing is well-motivated and concrete. The \#49823 example (Milvus silently accepting nprobe=0 despite documented range [1,16384]) clearly demonstrates why current VDBMS fuzzers miss this defect type.

2. **Solid empirical contribution.** 49 maintainer-acknowledged true-positive defects (15 with merged PRs) across three production VDBMSs is strong evidence that the defect class is real and the approach reaches it. The yield precision of 68.1% (maintainer-adjudicated) is reasonable for a first-generation LLM-derived oracle.

3. **Well-diagnosed false-positive problem.** Section 4 (False-Positive Problem) clearly articulates two failure modes (hallucination in extraction, self-preference in judgment) and provides concrete evidence that multi-perspective judging is insufficient (15% recall). The source-grounded falsifier is a principled response to these diagnoses.

4. **Threats-to-validity section is honest.** The paper explicitly acknowledges limitations: single-backbone evaluation (GLM-5.2 only), lack of cross-family generalization, implementation-as-correct assumption, and VDBMS-only evaluation. The external validity threat correctly flags Weaviate as yield-only and calls for non-VDBMS case studies.

5. **Good comparative analysis with VDBFuzz.** Section 7 (RQ3) provides a clean bidirectional probe showing VDBFuzz reaches 0 of TestVDB's 14 silent-accept true positives on Qdrant v1.18.2, and TestVDB reaches one of VDBFuzz's crash-class defects (size overflow) by contract reasoning. The \#9045 case study (wait=false accepts zero-length vector) illustrates why crash-focused patching can miss documentation-implementation residuals.

**Weaknesses**
1. **[minor, fixable]** **Post-hoc operating-point selection weakens RQ2 claims.** Table 4 reports the 3-run union as the headline operating point (65% accuracy, 67% precision, 74% recall), but the text (page 12) acknowledges this is a post-hoc selection across four operating points justified by falsifier semantics. The Wilson CIs [49%,81%] for precision and [55%,87%] for recall do not account for this selection. While the bootstrap validation (2000 resamples) provides wider CIs ([53%,83%] and [71%,96%]), the operating-point selection is not pre-registered. This weakens the statistical claim but does not invalidate the qualitative finding that source grounding lifts recall above 37%. Fix by pre-registering operating-point selection criteria in a replication or by explicitly stating the post-hoc nature earlier in the RQ2 abstract (first sentence of RQ2).

2. **[minor, fixable]** **Cross-family generalization is acknowledged but not scoped.** Page 14 reports that cross-model re-runs (DeepSeek, Qwen, LongCat) show family-specific verdicts (κ = 0.14–0.51) and lower recall (18–56% vs. 85%), confirming backbone-dependence. The external validity threats correctly flag this, but the discussion (Section 8) could more clearly bound the claim: "Source-grounded falsification improves recall relative to documentation-only adjudication within a single LLM family; cross-family robustness is an open question." Currently, the text buries this scope limitation. Fix by adding one sentence in Section 8 (Discussion) explicitly stating the single-family scope.

3. **[minor, fixable]** **Weaviate evaluation is yield-only in the controlled retrospective.** RQ2 reports the 48-candidate retrospective as covering "Milvus and Qdrant" (page 13), but RQ1 reports Weaviate as having 13 true positives of 30 submitted. The controlled retrospective (Table 5) excludes Weaviate, so the dev-reviewer's precision/recall numbers do not apply to Weaviate. The threats section correctly notes this under external validity, but it should be surfaced earlier in RQ2. Fix by adding one sentence in the RQ2 abstract (first paragraph) acknowledging that Weaviate coverage is yield-only in the retrospective.

4. **[minor, fixable]** **Variance disclosure could be more prominent.** RQ2 (page 12) reports that single-run recall varies widely (15–78%) and justifies the any-confirmed ensemble as the operating point. However, the abstract (page 1) reports only the 3-run union headline (67%/74%) without mentioning single-run variance. This could mislead readers about stability. Fix by adding one sentence in the abstract: "Single runs vary widely (recall 15–78%), so we report the 3-run any-confirmed ensemble as the operating point."

5. **[minor, fixable]** **External validity mini-case is anecdotal.** Section 8 (Discussion) describes a portability probe on CouchDB and Elasticsearch as evidence that the pipeline transfers to non-VDBMS targets. However, the results (zero silent-accept defects found in both mature systems) are anecdotal—n=2 targets, no defect discovery. The text correctly frames this as "probing method portability rather than defect detection" (page 19), but the external validity threats would be stronger with at least one non-VDBMS defect detection. Fix by explicitly stating the n=2 limitation in the threats paragraph on external validity (page 16): "Our non-VDBMS probes (CouchDB, Elasticsearch) validate method portability but do not establish defect-detection efficacy beyond VDBMSs (n=2, zero defects found)."

**Questions for Authors**
1. The 3-run union operating point is justified by falsifier semantics: "a candidate that survives any independent falsification is more likely a true defect." Could you clarify why the 5-run union (85% recall, 62% precision) was not selected as the headline despite higher recall? Is the precision trade-off (67%→62%) the sole reason, or does wall-clock cost also factor in?

2. Table 6 (ablation) shows source-grounding alone suppresses 9/12 false positives (75%) on a 12-FP/4-TP control, but the text notes the threat-model anchor is "unstable on state and concurrency FPs." Could you characterize which FP types the threat-model anchor misses? Would a more comprehensive threat model (e.g., with maintainer-acknowledged patterns from all three VDBMSs) stabilize this anchor?

3. The bidirectional probe with VDBFuzz (RQ3) finds that VDBFuzz reaches 0 of TestVDB's 14 silent-accept true positives on Qdrant v1.18.2. Is this 26,000-request budget representative of VDBFuzz's typical configuration? If VDBFuzz were run with a larger budget or expanded templates, could it reach silent-accept defects, or is the crash-oracle limitation structural?
