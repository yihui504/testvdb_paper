## Reviewer 3 — Friendly
> Confidence: 4/5 · Overall: Accept (7/10)

**Summary**
This paper addresses a real and underexplored problem in VDBMS testing: logical bugs that manifest as silent accepts rather than crashes. The authors propose TestVDB, an LLM-based pipeline that extracts behavioral claims from documentation and validates them against running VDBMS instances. The key innovation is a source-grounded falsifier (dev-reviewer) that mitigates two LLM reliability issues—hallucination in extraction and self-preference in judgment—by cross-checking against implementation source. Evaluation across three VDBMSs (Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate) surfaces 49 maintainer-acknowledged true positives, with a controlled retrospective showing the dev-reviewer lifts recall from 37% to 74% at 67% precision.

**Strengths**
1. **Clear problem definition and practical motivation.** The distinction between crash-based logical bugs and documentation-implementation defects is well-drawn, and the empirical grounding (cite to bugstudy25 and roadmap25) establishes this as a genuine gap. The framing that "the absence of a crash is the wrong signal" for this defect class is compelling.

2. **Methodological innovation in LLM reliability.** The dev-reviewer's source-grounded falsification is a substantial contribution over naive multi-perspective judging. By identifying two specific failure modes (extraction hallucination, judgment self-preference) and introducing an implementation-anchor as an independent signal, the paper moves beyond generic "LLM-as-judge" pattern-matching into a principled reliability strategy.

3. **Rigorous comparative evaluation.** The RQ2 retrospective (48 maintainer-adjudicated candidates) with ablations, operating-point analysis, and cross-model validation provides strong evidence for the dev-reviewer's value. The bidirectional VDBFuzz probe (Section 7) is particularly well-executed, demonstrating complementary coverage rather than a competitive claim.

4. **Concrete artifacts and practical impact.** The 49 true positives, 15 merged-PR fixes, and detailed yield breakdown by vendor show the approach works in practice. Table 2's per-vendor specificity (Milvus's optional-default parameter defects vs. Qdrant's search-parameter issues) adds useful nuance beyond aggregate numbers.

5. **Honest limitation discussion.** Section 8.1 (non-VDBMS transfer) and Section 7's threat modeling (implementation-as-correct assumption, single-backbone GLM-5.2) show appropriate boundary-setting. The CouchDB/Elasticsearch probes, while yielding no defects, demonstrate pipeline portability.

**Weaknesses**
1. **[minor, fixable]** **Cross-family generalization under-explored.** Section 7 reports that DeepSeek/Qwen/LongCat show low agreement (κ=0.14–0.51) and lower recall than GLM-5.2, but treats this as an open question. A suggested improvement: pre-register a small cross-family validation set (e.g., 10 candidates) and report whether the dev-reviewer's three-check falsification (independent of backbone choice) shows higher cross-family agreement than the raw LLM judgment. This would clarify whether the reliability gain is architecture-specific or falsifier-specific.

2. **[minor, fixable]** **Post-hoc operating point selection.** The 3-run union is chosen as the headline metric after examining four operating points (Table 5). While the paper flags this as post-hoc, the Wilson CIs do not account for selection. A suggested improvement: add a Bonferroni-corrected CI to Table 5 (as briefly mentioned in text but not visualized) or pre-register the ensemble strategy in a future iteration to strengthen the recall claim.

3. **[minor, fixable]** **Construct validity framing for RQ3.** The VDBFuzz comparison (Section 7) is well-executed, but the paper could more explicitly situate it as a "complementarity hypothesis" rather than a "superiority" claim. The current text ("VDBFuzz misses a TestVDB silent-accept defect") is empirically correct but reads as competitive. A suggested improvement: frame RQ3 around "orthogonal coverage of residual defect classes" and explicitly call out that VDBFuzz's template limitation (wait=true only) is a fixable gap rather than an inherent oracle weakness.

4. **[minor, fixable]** **Threat-model anchor under-specification.** Section 6.2 mentions a threat-model anchor as an optional third check but provides limited detail on how maintainer-acknowledged by-design patterns are encoded. A suggested improvement: append a small example (e.g., "Qdrant's idempotent delete pattern") to Figure 4 or Table 6 to illustrate how threat-modeling falsifies specific FP classes.

**Questions for Authors**
1. The CouchDB/Elasticsearch probes found no silent-accept defects. Do you hypothesize that mature REST APIs have stricter validation, or was the probe coverage insufficient? Would a targeted probe on newer/less-mature VDBMSs (e.g., Chroma, pgvector) yield higher defect density?

2. Cross-model agreement is low (κ=0.14–0.51). Is the variance primarily from extraction hallucination (different backbones formalize different claims) or judgment self-preference (different backends confirm different subsets)? A within-backbone ablation holding extraction constant but varying judgment could isolate this.

3. The dev-reviewer treats implementation-as-correct as a bounding assumption. Have you observed cases where a confirmed TP was actually a documentation bug rather than an implementation bug? Quantifying this false-negative mode (if any) would strengthen the validity discussion.
