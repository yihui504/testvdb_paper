## Three Key Strengths

### 1. **Well-Defined Target Problem with Clear Oracle Characterization**
The paper makes a compelling case that documentation-implementation defects are a systematic blindspot for existing VDBMS testing approaches. The oracle-exclusion argument in Table 1 is carefully structured — each candidate oracle is evaluated against the specific residual it cannot reach, and the LLM-derived oracle emerges from this elimination rather than being asserted as the default solution. This is a strong methodological choice. The distinction between consistency (correctness against documentation) and result correctness (ANN math) is also crisp and prevents scope creep.

### 2. **Novel Source-Grounded Falsification Mechanism**
The dev-reviewer's three-check falsification framework (independent reproducibility, evidence sufficiency, falsifiability against source) is a thoughtful mitigation for the dual failure modes of LLM-based testing (hallucination at extraction and self-preference at judgment). The ablation study showing source grounding alone suppresses 75% of false positives, with the threat-model anchor providing orthogonal coverage, is particularly convincing. This addresses the central reliability concern for LLM-as-judge systems without defaulting to brittle ensemble voting.

### 3. **Practical Impact Beyond Theoretical Framing**
With 49 maintainer-acknowledged true positives, 15 merged-PR fixes, and a concrete bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz that demonstrates complementary coverage, the work establishes real-world relevance. The CouchDB mini-case, while limited in scope (n=1 target), provides initial evidence that the pipeline architecture transfers beyond VDBMSs without claiming premature generalization. This balances ambition with due caution.

## Minor Revisions Requested

### 1. **Post-Hoc Operating Point Selection Requires Clearer Framing**
The 3-run union ensemble is selected as the headline operating point after examining four alternatives (Table 3). While the falsifier semantics provide a principled justification, the paper currently flags this as post-hoc in a single sentence (lines 251-252) but does not explicitly state whether any pre-registered hypothesis or power calculation guided the design. Recommend adding one sentence in Section 4.2 (RQ2) to clarify whether: (a) this is a hypothesis-generating exploratory analysis; or (b) there was a pre-registered decision rule (e.g., "select the operating point that maximizes recall subject to precision ≥60%"). The current phrasing ("we flag it as a post-hoc operating point") leans toward (a), but making this explicit would align with SE reproducibility expectations.

### 2. **External Validity Mini-Case Needs Explicit Limitation Statement**
The CouchDB mini-case (Section 7, lines 351-352) is valuable as a portability probe but is currently framed in a way that a casual reader might interpret as stronger empirical support for generalization than intended. Recommend adding one explicit limitation clause to the last sentence of that paragraph, such as: "This single non-VDBMS probe demonstrates pipeline portability but does not establish efficacy; defect detection on non-VDBMS targets remains an open question." This sharpens the boundary between what is demonstrated (portability) and what is claimed (transferability on structural grounds only).

### 3. **Cross-Family Generalization Results Should Appear Earlier in the Validity Section**
The cross-model re-run results (κ=0.14 for DeepSeek, κ=0.51 for LongCat vs. GLM single-run) appear in the Internal Validity subsection (line 332) but are not explicitly connected back to the External Validity threat in line 330 ("result correctness of vector search is out of scope"). Recommend adding one bridge sentence: "The cross-family non-generalization we observe (κ<0.5 for two independent families) bounds the External Validity claim to the GLM-5.2 backbone unless cross-family calibration is demonstrated." This explicitly ties the internal result to the external limitation.

## Optional Suggestions (Not Blocking)
- The paper would benefit from a figure illustrating the three-check falsification flow with concrete example artifacts (request/response snippets, source code excerpt), but this is not required for acceptance.
- The discussion of why majority voting collapses recall (line 156) could be expanded with one concrete example of a conservative run, but the current description is sufficient for clarity.

---

## Summary
This paper tackles a real and under-addressed problem with a carefully engineered solution and credible empirical validation. The strengths are substantive, and the requested revisions are minor clarification points that would tighten an already solid contribution. I recommend acceptance with the above minor revisions.
