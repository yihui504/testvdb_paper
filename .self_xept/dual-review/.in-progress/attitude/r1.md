# Reviewer 1 (Objective)

## Summary

This paper presents TestVDB, a pipeline for detecting documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs) using LLM-derived behavioral claims as test oracles. The authors target a defect class where VDBMSs silently accept inputs that violate their API documentation (e.g., accepting nprobe=0 when documentation specifies [1, 16384]). They argue that because VDBMS documentation is natural-language prose, deterministic oracles (crash detection, differential testing, metamorphic relations, property-based testing) cannot adjudicate these accept/reject decisions, leaving LLMs as the practical oracle. The authors identify two false-positive failure modes (hallucination in extraction, self-preference in judgment), show that multi-perspective judging is insufficient, and introduce a dev-reviewer agent that uses implementation source as an independent falsifier. On three VDBMSs (Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate), TestVDB surfaced 107 issues with 49 maintainer-acknowledged true positives (15 fixed via merged PR). On a 48-candidate retrospective, the dev-reviewer reaches 67% precision and 74% recall against 37% recall without source grounding. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows complementary coverage.

## Strengths

1. **Problem clarity.** The defect class (documentation-implementation consistency) is well-defined and motivated. The distinction from result correctness is explicit, and the practical impact (49 acknowledged defects, 15 fixed) demonstrates relevance.

2. **Oracle exclusion argument.** Table 1 systematically walks through why deterministic oracles miss the documentation-implementation residual. The structural argument (rows 1-5 keep deterministic oracles, row 6 enters LLM regime) is clear.

3. **Evaluation with maintainer ground truth.** The 49 maintainer-acknowledged true positives across three production VDBMSs provide stronger validation than self-adjudication. The 15 merged-PR fixes show practical impact.

4. **False-positive diagnosis.** The two-mode failure analysis (hallucination in extraction, self-preference in judgment) is well-motivated by LLM literature. The multi-perspective judging baseline (80% precision, 15% recall) quantitatively demonstrates why voting alone is insufficient.

5. **Source-grounded falsification.** The dev-reviewer design (three checks: reproducibility, evidence sufficiency, falsifiability) is a reasonable approach to breaking self-preference. The ablation (Table 4) showing source alone suppresses 75% of false positives on a 12-FP/4-TP control is convincing.

6. **Bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz.** The n=1 controlled cases (#9045 silent-accept vs. VDBFuzz crash-template coverage, and the reverse direction on size overflow) provide concrete evidence of complementary coverage between documentation-based and crash-based oracles.

7. **External validity mini case.** The CouchDB pilot (5 extracted claims, 1 silent-accept that is graceful) is a valuable signal that the pipeline ports to non-VDBMS REST APIs, even if the claim of transferability remains structural.

## Weaknesses

1. **[CRITICAL, unfixable] Post-hoc operating point selection without statistical correction.** The headline 67% precision / 74% recall (3-run union) is selected post-hoc from four operating points (Table 3) after observing results. The Wilson CIs do not account for this selection. While the authors flag this as a limitation and apply a Bonferroni correction, the correction is illustrative only ("would widen the CI to roughly [44%, 84%] and [51%, 89%]") rather than being the primary reported result. This weakens the quantitative claim that source grounding lifts recall above the 37% baseline.

2. **[HIGH, fixable] Cross-family generalization is unproven.** The full independent re-run (DeepSeek and LongCat-2.0 on all 48 candidates) shows family-specific verdicts (kappa = 0.14 DeepSeek, 0.51 LongCat vs. GLM single-run). The abstract claims "LLM-derived oracles" as a general category, but the evaluation is GLM-5.2-only. The cross-model pilot (kappa=1.0 on six candidates) is contradicted by the full re-run. The paper should either qualify claims to "within single LLM families" or provide evidence that the dev-reviewer design stabilizes verdicts across families.

3. **[HIGH, partially fixable] No ground-truth defect catalog for recall estimation.** The authors explicitly state "we did not estimate recall because no public ground-truth defect catalog exists." This limits the interpretability of the 74% recall figure. Is 74% low or high relative to the true defect population? The authors could mitigate by (a) reporting the 35 still-pending submissions as a sensitivity bound, or (b) conducting a capture-recapture or manual defect-injection study to estimate the denominator.

4. **[MEDIUM, fixable] Vendor-specific source-grunting value is not decomposed.** The recall collapse without source grounding is vendor-specific (Milvus 80% to 5%, Qdrant 57% unchanged). The paper explains this as "parameter validation logic lives in source rather than contract" for Milvus vs. "HTTP responses directly expose accept/reject" for Qdrant, but this is not operationalized. A systematic analysis of which VDBMS architectural patterns make source grounding valuable would strengthen the claim.

5. **[MEDIUM, fixable] Construct validity: implementation-as-correct assumption.** The dev-reviewer treats the implementation as ground truth for falsification. The authors acknowledge this bounds the approach but do not quantify how often implementation bugs would wrongly suppress correct-documentation claims. The 15 merged-PR fixes are used as weak evidence ("holds often enough to be useful"), but this is not a systematic estimate. A threat-model case study of one implementation-bug-false-positive would clarify the boundary.

6. **[MEDIUM, unfixable] External validity beyond VDBMSs is structural claim only.** The CouchDB mini case (5 claims, 1 graceful accept) is insufficient to support the broad transferability claim to "REST APIs without OpenAPI, configuration validation, policy-as-code." The pipeline ports, but the defect yield is negative relative to VDBMSs (0 defects vs. 49). The paper should either qualify the external validity claim or provide at least one non-VDBMS case study with positive yield.

7. **[LOW, fixable] RQ3 bidirectional probe is n=1.** The VDBFuzz comparison reports n=1 for each direction (size overflow on v1.4.0, #9045 on v1.18.0). The authors treat this as "hypothesis-generating controlled cases rather than a generalized result," which is appropriate framing. However, given that complementary coverage is a key claim, a small n=3 or n=5 per direction would strengthen the evidence.

## Questions

1. On the post-hoc operating point selection: Would the main claims hold if the Bonferroni-corrected CIs ([44%, 84%] precision, [51%, 89%] recall) were the primary reported numbers instead of the uncorrected Wilson CIs?

2. On cross-family generalization: If the dev-reviewer is family-specific (kappa=0.14 DeepSeek), what design changes would stabilize verdicts across families? Or should claims be scoped to "within-family" evaluation?

3. On recall estimation: Could you report a sensitivity analysis treating the 35 still-pending submissions as false positives (worst-case bound) to contextualize the 74% recall?

4. On vendor-specific source-grunting value: Can you operationalize which architectural features (parameter validation in source vs. response-exposed accept/reject) predict source-grunting utility? This would help readers assess transferability.

5. On the implementation-as-correct assumption: Do you have any example of a confirmed TP where the documentation was wrong (not the implementation)? How many such cases would you expect to observe given your yield?

6. On external validity: The CouchDB mini case yields 0 defects vs. 49 on VDBMSs. Is this because CouchDB validates inputs more strictly (as you suggest), or because the sample size (5 claims) is too small to detect defects? How many claims would you need to detect at least one defect with 80% power given the VDBMS yield rate?

## Scores

- **Soundness:** 3/5 — The approach is sound, but the post-hoc operating point selection and unaddressed cross-family generalization weaken the quantitative claims.
- **Significance:** 4/5 — The problem is significant (49 acknowledged defects, 15 fixed), and the defect class is well-motivated. The oracle exclusion argument is clear.
- **Novelty:** 4/5 — LLM-derived oracles are not novel, but applying them to documentation-implementation defects in VDBMSs and using source-grounded falsification to break self-preference is a reasonable contribution.
- **Presentation:** 4/5 — The paper is well-structured. Tables and figures are clear. The writing is precise with no em-dashes or emojis.

**Overall:** 6/10 — The paper addresses a significant problem with a reasonable approach and strong maintainer-validation (49 TPs). However, the post-hoc operating point selection, unproven cross-family generalization, and lack of recall estimation weaken the quantitative claims. The external validity beyond VDBMSs is not empirically demonstrated. I lean toward ACCEPT with major revisions to address the post-hoc selection and cross-family generalization issues.

**Confidence:** 4/5 — I am familiar with VDBMSs, LLM-as-judge literature, and testing oracles. The evaluation design is clear, but the statistical reporting issues limit confidence in the quantitative claims.
