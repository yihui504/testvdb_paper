# Reviewer 1: Objective

## Summary

This paper introduces TestVDB, a system for detecting documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs). The authors characterize a defect class where VDBMSs silently accept inputs that violate their API documentation (e.g., accepting nprobe=0 when documentation specifies [1, 16384]). Because these boundaries are specified in natural-language prose, the authors argue that deterministic oracles cannot handle this regime, leaving an LLM as the practical oracle. They identify two false-positive failure modes: hallucination in claim extraction and self-preference bias in judgment. The core contribution is a dev-reviewer agent that acts as a source-grounded falsifier, reproducing candidates and cross-checking against implementation source. Across 107 submitted issues, maintainers acknowledged 49 as true-positive defects (15 fixed via merged PR). On a 48-candidate retrospective, the dev-reviewer achieves 67% precision and 74% recall versus 37% recall without source grounding. A bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz explores complementary coverage.

## Strengths

1. **Clear problem framing.** The paper distinguishes documentation-implementation consistency from correctness and argues persuasively that crash-oracle fuzzers miss a prevalent defect class (44 of 49 true positives produce no crash). The oracle-exclusion argument (Table 1) is methodical and helpful.

2. **Real-world validation.** The authors submit 107 issues to three production VDBMSs, achieving 49 maintainer-acknowledged true positives with 15 merged-PR fixes. This establishes practical impact beyond a toy benchmark.

3. **Diagnosis of failure modes.** The two-mode false-positive analysis (hallucination in extraction, self-preference in judgment) is well-motivated by prior work (Ji et al. 2023, Panickssery et al. 2024) and explains why multi-perspective judging collapses recall.

4. **Source-grounded falsification mechanism.** The dev-reviewer's three-check approach (reproducible, evidence-sufficient, falsifiable) is principled, and the Qdrant #9255 reversal example clearly illustrates how it catches assertion-depends-on-unrequested-field false positives that documentation-only judges miss.

5. **Bidirectional comparison with VDBFuzz.** The Qdrant probe reaches a crash-class defect (integer overflow on size=2^63) by contract reasoning and shows VDBFuzz misses a silent-accept defect (#9045) under current templates. This establishes concrete complementarity rather than abstract claims.

## Weaknesses

### **[major, fixable]** Post-hoc operating point selection without pre-registration

The paper selects the 3-run union ensemble as the headline operating point post-hoc across four alternatives (single run, 3-run union, 5-run union, 5-run majority). The authors acknowledge this is "post-hoc" and that Wilson CIs "do not account for this selection," but this weakens RQ2's validity claim. Table 2 presents four operating points without justifying why 3-run union is principled beyond "falsifier semantics" and "knee of precision-recall trade-off." A reviewer reasonably asks: was 3-run chosen because it maximizes recall at acceptable precision? If so, the paper overfits to the 48-candidate set. The fix is straightforward: pre-register the operating-point selection rule (e.g., "choose the highest-recall ensemble with precision >= 60%") or report all four and let readers decide. The current presentation appears to optimize after seeing the data.

### **[major, fixable]** Limited external validity beyond VDBMSs

The Discussion section (Section 6) claims transferability to "REST APIs without OpenAPI coverage, configuration validation, and policy-as-code checks" "on structural grounds only" and explicitly states "we have not tested these transfers." This is a scope overreach. The core technical novelty—source-grounded falsification of LLM-derived claims—does intrinsically generalize to any system with (a) ambiguous natural-language documentation and (b) available source code. But the paper provides zero empirical validation outside VDBMSs, and RQ3 is a single bidirectional probe on Qdrant (n=1 per direction). The Threats to Validity section acknowledges this ("Transfer... is claimed on structural grounds only and is not empirically tested"), but the Abstract and Conclusion language ("marks where an LLM-derived oracle needs an independent falsifier") suggests a universal boundary without evidence. The fix is to narrow the claim to VDBMSs or add one non-VDBMS case study (e.g., a REST API with prose documentation). As-is, the contribution boundaries are inflated.

### **[major, fixable]** Unaddressed selection bias in yield calculation

The paper reports a yield precision of 68.1% (49 true positives of 72 adjudicated) and a worst-case bound of 45.8% (assuming 35 pending are false positives). This calculation assumes the 72 adjudicated submissions are representative of the 107 total, but the submission process itself is biased by TestVDB's design toward documentation-implementation defects. The authors acknowledge this in the Discussion ("Our 107-submission yield is biased by the tool's design toward documentation-implementation defects, so the proportion we observe is not an estimate of the distribution in VDBMSs at large"), but they do not address how the bias affects precision. For example, if the tool systematically under-reports certain defect classes (e.g., concurrency issues), the 49/107 TP count is not a reliable precision metric. The fix is to report precision stratified by defect class or by submission trigger (e.g., "boundary-test-triggered" vs. "state-test-triggered"), or to acknowledge that precision is conditional on the tool's search strategy and not a property of the method alone. The current presentation treats 68.1% as a method-level precision estimate, which it is not.

### **[minor, fixable]** Missing statistical power characterization for RQ3

The bidirectional probe on Qdrant reports n=1 per direction (v1.4.0 and v1.18.0). The authors correctly state "each direction is n=1; we treat these as hypothesis-generating controlled cases rather than a generalized result," but this limits RQ3's contribution. A reviewer reasonably asks: is one crash-class defect and one silent-accept defect sufficient to claim "complementary coverage"? The authors could strengthen this by (a) characterizing the space of reachable defects (e.g., "VDBFuzz reaches X crashes under template Y, TestVDB reaches Y silent-accepts under contract Z") or (b) framing RQ3 as a qualitative case study rather than a quantitative RQ. As-is, RQ3 is underpowered for the claims it supports ("complementary coverage").

### **[minor, fixable]** Insufficient detail on dev-reviewer source-grounding implementation

The dev-reviewer is the core technical contribution, but Section 4 and the pipeline description (Section 3) lack concrete implementation details. The paper states the dev-reviewer "greps the clone to trace constants and call chains" and uses "a GLM-5.2 backbone under default sampling," but does not specify: (1) What source-language features does it handle? (C++ for Milvus, Rust for Qdrant, Go for Weaviate—the paper mentions these targets but not whether dev-reviewer generalizes across languages); (2) What is the prompt structure for source-grounded falsification? (The authors promise "full prompts... in the artifact," but a high-level sketch would help readers assess feasibility); (3) How does the dev-reviewer avoid hallucinating constraints from source code? (Section 4 mentions "falsifiable" checks but not whether source-grounding itself is LLM-derived). The fix is to add a subsection in Section 4 with implementation details or to move Figure 2 (dev-reviewer three-check pipeline) earlier and annotate it with source-language coverage. The current presentation leaves the core mechanism vague.

### **[minor, fixable]** Overconfident construct validity claims

The paper claims "the dev-reviewer with a 3-run any-confirmed ensemble reaches 65% accuracy, 67% precision... and 74% recall" (RQ2) and later states "a cross-model check on twenty candidates agrees with GLM-5.2 on all twenty (Cohen's kappa = 1.0; Wilson 95% CI on agreement [83%, 100%]), so the verdict does not appear strongly family-specific." However, the 20-candidate check is a "diversity-stratified, non-random sample" (Threats to Validity) and measures agreement, not recall. The paper does not estimate recall because "no public ground-truth defect catalog for VDBMSs exists." This creates a construct validity gap: the paper reports high agreement but cannot assess whether dev-reviewer verdicts are correct relative to an external ground truth. The fix is to acknowledge that agreement is a proxy for reliability, not correctness, or to report inter-rater reliability between dev-reviewer and maintainer adjudication on the 48-candidate set (which the paper has data for). The current presentation conflates agreement with accuracy.

## Questions

1. **Pre-registration and operating points:** What was the pre-specified selection rule for the 3-run union ensemble? If none, could you re-run RQ2 with a pre-registered rule (e.g., "highest-recall ensemble with precision >= 60%") and report whether the operating point changes?

2. **External validity:** Could you provide at least one non-VDBMS case study (e.g., a REST API with prose documentation) to empirically validate the structural claim that source-grounded falsification generalizes beyond VDBMSs?

3. **Yield precision bias:** You acknowledge submission bias in the Discussion, but can you report precision stratified by submission trigger (e.g., boundary-test vs. state-test vs. semantic-test) or by defect class (e.g., parameter-range vs. concurrency) to show whether precision is uniform across the tool's search strategy?

4. **RQ3 statistical power:** Could you characterize the space of reachable defects more quantitatively (e.g., "VDBFuzz reaches X crashes under template Y, TestVDB reaches Y silent-accepts under contract Z") to strengthen the "complementary coverage" claim beyond n=1 cases?

5. **Dev-reviewer implementation details:** What source languages does the dev-reviewer currently support, and what is the high-level prompt structure for source-grounded falsification? Does it use retrieval-augmented generation over the source clone, or does it feed entire source files into context?

6. **Construct validity and agreement vs. accuracy:** You report Cohen's kappa = 1.0 on 20 candidates between GLM-5.2 and DeepSeek, but do you have inter-rater reliability data between dev-reviewer verdicts and maintainer adjudication on the full 48-candidate set? This would strengthen the claim that dev-reviewer aligns with maintainer judgment.

## Scores

- **Soundness:** 7/10 — The methodology is sound and the empirical evaluation (107 submissions, 49 TPs, 15 merged PRs) is substantive. However, post-hoc operating-point selection (Weakness 1) and unaddressed yield-precision bias (Weakness 3) reduce confidence. The pre-registration fix would raise this to 8/10.

- **Significance:** 8/10 — The problem is significant (VDBMSs are infrastructure for RAG applications, logical bugs are prevalent) and the practical impact (15 merged-PR fixes across three production systems) is strong. Source-grounded falsification is a useful technique for LLM-derived oracles.

- **Novelty:** 7/10 — The combination of (a) documentation-implementation defects as a VDBMS target class, (b) LLM-derived oracles for natural-language API boundaries, and (c) source-grounded falsification to suppress false positives is novel. However, AGORA+, SATORI, and MASTOR (Related Work) already explore source-code-derived oracles; TestVDB's novelty is the target class (prose documentation) and the falsification mechanism, not the use of source per se.

- **Presentation:** 7/10 — The paper is well-structured and the oracle-exclusion table (Table 1) is helpful. However, the dev-reviewer mechanism lacks implementation details (Weakness 5) and the Discussion overgeneralizes beyond VDBMSs (Weakness 2). The writing is clear but occasionally dense (Section 4 could be expanded).

- **Overall:** 7/10 — Accept with revisions. The core contribution (source-grounded falsification for LLM-derived oracles applied to documentation-implementation defects) is valuable, and the empirical evaluation is substantive. The major weaknesses are fixable: pre-register the operating-point selection, narrow the generalization claim or add a non-VDBMS case study, and address yield-precision bias. The minor weaknesses (RQ3 power, dev-reviewer details, agreement vs. accuracy) are polish items.

- **Confidence:** 4/5 — I am confident in the technical assessment and the fixability of the major weaknesses. I have not verified the reproducibility of the 107-submission yield or the 48-candidate retrospective (no artifact is provided), but the paper describes the methodology clearly enough to assess validity.
