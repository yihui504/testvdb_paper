# Reviewer 1 (Objective)

## Summary

This paper introduces TestVDB, a system for detecting documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs). The authors identify a defect class where VDBMSs silently accept inputs that violate their API documentation (e.g., accepting nprobe=0 when the documentation specifies [1, 16384]). Because the boundary is natural-language prose, deterministic oracles cannot adjudicate these accept/reject decisions, leaving an LLM as the practical oracle. The authors diagnose two false-positive failure modes (hallucination in extraction, self-preference in judgment), show that multi-perspective judging is insufficient, and introduce a dev-reviewer agent that falsifies LLM verdicts against implementation source. TestVDB surfaced 107 issues with 49 maintainer-acknowledged true positives (15 merged-PR fixes) across three VDBMSs, reaches 67% precision and 74% recall on a 48-candidate retrospective against 37% recall without source grounding, and a bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz shows complementary coverage.

The problem is well-motivated. VDBMS defects are costly (empirical study shows 50%+ are functional failures), existing crash-oracle fuzzers miss the silent-accept majority, and the natural-language boundary creates a genuine oracle gap that deterministic methods cannot cross. The source-grounded falsifier is a sound countermeasure to the two documented failure modes. The evaluation includes maintainer acknowledgment (49 TPs with 15 fixes), a controlled retrospective (48-candidate subset with vendor balance), and a bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz that makes the complementarity concrete.

## Strengths

1. **Problem formulation.** The documentation-implementation defect class is well-specified, motivated by empirical evidence (bug study, roadmap), and cleanly separated from correctness (the paper targets consistency, not result correctness). The oracle-exclusion argument (Table 1) systematically walks through why standard oracles miss the residual, establishing an LLM as the necessary oracle.

2. **False-positive diagnosis.** The two-mode failure analysis (hallucination in extraction, self-preference in judgment) is grounded in established LLM biases and directly tied to the pipeline. The insufficiency of multi-perspective judging is demonstrated, not claimed, with concrete numbers (80% precision, 15% recall).

3. **Source-grounded falsifier.** The dev-reviewer is a well-structured solution with three clear checks (reproducible, evidence-sufficient, falsifiable) and three anchors (clean-reproduction, source-grounded, threat-model). The #9255 reversal example illustrates falsification in action, showing how the dev-reviewer catches an assertion-depends-on-unrequested-field false positive that multi-perspective judging misses.

4. **Evaluation breadth.** Three VDBMSs, maintainer acknowledgment, a controlled retrospective, and a bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz. The yield (107 submitted, 49 acknowledged) shows practical impact, and the 15 merged-PR fixes demonstrate that the surfaced defects are actionable.

5. **VDBFuzz comparison.** The bidirectional probe is hypothesis-generating but concrete: TestVDB reaches VDBFuzz's crash-class defect by contract reasoning (Qdrant size=2^63), and VDBFuzz misses TestVDB's silent-accept defect (wait=false zero-length vector) under its current templates. The #9045 root cause analysis explains why crash-oracle patching can miss documentation-implementation residuals.

6. **Honest scope limitations.** The paper explicitly separates consistency from correctness, bounds the implementation-as-correct assumption, acknowledges cross-family generalization is open, and flags the post-hoc operating-point selection with Bonferroni-corrected CIs.

## Weaknesses

**[MEDIUM, partially addressable]** Post-hoc operating point selection inflates recall claims. The 3-run union is selected from four operating points after observing the data. The Wilson CIs do not account for this selection, and the Bonferroni-corrected CIs ([44%, 84%] precision, [51%, 89%] recall) materially weaken the headline 67%/74% claims. The paper flags the selection, but the headline numbers remain uncorrected. A pre-registered operating point (e.g., "we will use 3-run union because under-confirmation is costlier for a falsifier") would have been stronger.

**[MEDIUM, fixable]** Cross-family generalization is unaddressed despite being a claimed limitation. The abstract states "cross-family generalization is an open question," and the full independent cross-model re-run shows family-specific verdicts (kappa = 0.14 DeepSeek, 0.51 LongCat vs. GLM). However, the paper does not attempt even a minimal cross-family validation (e.g., a 10-candidate subset run on two families) to bound the sensitivity. Given that the entire approach relies on LLM reliability, this gap weakens the generalizability claim.

**[LOW, fixable]** Weaviate external validity is yield-only. The controlled retrospective covers Milvus and Qdrant, with Weaviate included only in the overall yield (30 submitted, 13 acknowledged). No 48-candidate retrospective data is presented for Weaviate, so the 67%/74% precision/recall claims are vendor-specific to Milvus and Qdrant. The paper flags this in threats, but the framing ("three VDBMSs" in the abstract) suggests broader applicability than the retrospective supports.

**[LOW, partially addressable]** CouchDB portability framing is weak. The Discussion claims portability to non-VDBMS targets via a CouchDB case study, but CouchDB rejected all invalid probes with 400 errors, surfacing no silent-accept defects. The pipeline ran, but the result validates strict input validation rather than defect detection. The contrast sharpens the motivation (VDBMSs have weaker validation), but the portability claim is not empirically demonstrated.

## Questions

1. **Operating point pre-registration:** Did you consider pre-registering the 3-run union as the operating point before observing the retrospective results? If so, why not report the pre-registered analysis?

2. **Cross-family validation:** What is the minimum cross-family validation that would address the open question? A 10-candidate subset on two families? A full re-run on a single alternative family?

3. **Weaviate retrospective:** Why is Weaviate excluded from the 48-candidate retrospective? Is there a technical reason (e.g., lack of maintainer adjudication, insufficient candidate count), or was it a resource constraint?

4. **CouchDB framing:** The CouchDB case study shows no defects surfaced. Do you have plans to test on a non-VDBMS target that does surface silent-accept defects, to empirically demonstrate portability beyond VDBMSs?

5. **Recall estimation:** The paper states no public ground-truth defect catalog exists for VDBMSs, so recall cannot be estimated. Have you considered capture-recapture or unbiased defect sampling as a future direction, as mentioned in the threats?

## Scores

- **Originality:** 3/5 — The problem class is novel, the false-positive diagnosis is sound, and the source-grounded falsifier is a well-motivated solution. However, LLM-as-judge bias and documentation-derived oracles are established areas.

- **Significance:** 4/5 — VDBMSs are infrastructure for retrieval-augmented LLMs, and the 49 maintainer-acknowledged defects with 15 fixes show practical impact. The boundary between structured-source extraction and natural-language interpretation is a meaningful contribution.

- **Correctness:** 4/5 — The approach is sound, the evaluation includes maintainer acknowledgment and a controlled retrospective, and the VDBFuzz probe is concrete. The post-hoc operating point selection and unaddressed cross-family generalization weaken but do not invalidate the claims.

- **Clarity:** 4/5 — The paper is well-structured, with clear motivation, a systematic oracle-exclusion argument, and concrete examples (#49823 path, #9255 reversal). Some framing issues (CouchDB portability, Weaviate retrospective scope) could be sharper.

- **Reproducibility:** 3/5 — The paper states artifact release upon acceptance, with full prompts, target versions, and per-token accounting. However, single-vendor reliance (GLM-5.2) and the lack of cross-family validation limit the ability to assess reproducibility across LLM families.

- **Overall:** 7/10 — A solid contribution that identifies a real defect class, provides a sound solution with empirical support, and demonstrates practical impact. The post-hoc operating point selection, unaddressed cross-family generalization, and vendor-specific retrospective scope are material weaknesses but not fatal.

- **Confidence:** 4/5 — I am familiar with LLM-as-judge bias, documentation-derived oracles, and VDBMS testing. I have not implemented or evaluated TestVDB directly, but the paper provides sufficient detail to assess the approach and evaluation.
