## Reviewer 2 — Strict
> Confidence: 5/5 · Overall: WEAK ACCEPT (3/10)

**Summary** The paper identifies a real problem—silent-accept documentation-implementation defects in VDBMSs—and proposes TestVDB, an LLM-based pipeline that extracts behavioral claims from documentation and validates them against implementation. The core idea (source-grounded falsification) is sound, but the evaluation has critical methodological weaknesses that undermine the quantitative claims: post-hoc operating-point selection, an underpowered VDBFuzz comparison, and insufficient external validity beyond three VDBMSs.

**Strengths**
1. **Clear problem isolation.** The distinction between crash-oracle fuzzers (VDBFuzz) and documentation-implementation defects is well-motivated, and Table 1's exclusion argument is persuasive: the residual (accept/reject decisions from natural-language docs) genuinely forces an LLM-derived oracle.

2. **Source-grounded falsification.** The dev-reviewer's three-check design (reproducibility, evidence sufficiency, falsifiability) is methodologically stronger than pure runtime validation. The #9255 reversal example (projection artifact) is concrete evidence of independent verification value.

3. **Real-world impact.** 49 maintainer-acknowledged defects across three production VDBMSs, with 15 merged-PR fixes, establishes that the target class is non-empty and the tool surfaces issues maintainers accept.

**Weaknesses**

1. **[major, fixable]** Post-hoc operating-point selection invalidates the 67% precision / 74% recall headline. The paper reports four operating points (Table 3), selects the 3-run union as "headline" post-hoc based on the precision-recall knee, and then presents Wilson CIs that do not account for this selection. The text acknowledges the post-hoc choice and provides bootstrap CIs ([53%, 83%] / [71%, 96%]), but these are reported in-text rather than in the table, and the abstract leads with the 67%/74% figures without qualifying them as a selected operating point. This is a garden-of-forking-paths issue: any of four operating points could have been chosen post-hoc to optimize a metric, and the uncertainty estimates must incorporate that selection. Fix: reframe the headline as "up to 74% recall" with clear qualification, or pre-register the 3-run union as the primary endpoint in a registered protocol and re-run.

2. **[major, fixable]** The VDBFuzz comparison is underpowered and structurally biased toward TestVDB. The systematic direction (VDBFuzz on v1.18.2, 26k requests, 0 of 14 TPs reached) is strong evidence of complementarity, but the two controlled cases are n=1 each. The framing of v1.4.0 and v1.18.0 as "n=1 controlled mechanism cases" acknowledges the limitation but does not mitigate it. A crash-class defect reached via contract reasoning on v1.4.0 is single-case evidence, not a mechanism validation. More critically, the paper does not report VDBFuzz's crash coverage on v1.18.2 beyond the two known fixed crashes—without a baseline crash rate on that version, the 0/14 figure is incomparable. Fix: run VDBFuzz on v1.18.2 to establish a crash baseline, and add at least 3-5 controlled crash-class cases with multiple versions to make the mechanism claim generalizable.

3. **[major, fixable]** External validity is limited to VDBMSs and the evidence for cross-family generalization is weak. Section 7 (Discussion) claims transferability to non-VDBMS REST APIs based on two mini-probes (CouchDB 3.4.3, Elasticsearch 8.11.0) that found zero silent-accept defects. This is negative evidence, not a portability demonstration. The cross-model re-run (DeepSeek, Qwen, LongCat) shows κ = 0.14–0.51 vs. GLM and lower recall (18–56% vs. 85%), which the paper interprets as "backbone-dependent" but is actually evidence that the approach does not generalize across LLM families. The single-backbone limitation is acknowledged (Construct Validity), but the implications are understated: if source-grounded falsification is backbone-dependent, the dev-reviewer's performance is contingent on a specific model family, not a general solution. Fix: remove or heavily qualify the portability claim, and add at least one successful non-VDBMS case study (e.g., a documentation-implementation defect found in CouchDB/Elasticsearch) to substantiate transferability.

4. **[minor, fixable]** Selection bias in the 48-candidate retrospective. The paper acknowledges the set is "maintainer-adjudicated but non-random" and did not pre-register candidates, but does not quantify the bias. The retrospective is the backbone of the RQ2 quantitative claims; if the candidate set was enriched for higher-quality cases (e.g., prioritized by the novelty gate or by maintainer responsiveness), the precision/recall estimates are biased upward. Fix: report the selection process for the 48 candidates in detail (how many candidates total were considered, how the 48 were sampled, what triage filters were applied), or use capture-recapture to estimate the false-negative rate.

5. **[minor, fixable]** The "implementation-as-correct" assumption is not stress-tested. The paper acknowledges this as a limitation (Section 7) but provides no empirical quantification: how many maintainer-rejected TPs were due to implementation bugs vs. documentation errors? The 15 merged-PR fixes are cited as evidence the assumption "holds often enough," but without a denominator (how many confirmed TPs were rejected as wont-fix without source explanation?), this is anecdotal. Fix: add a breakdown of rejected TPs by reason (implementation bug vs. documentation error vs. other) and report the false-negative rate for implementation-as-correct.

**Questions for Authors**
1. Why not pre-register the 3-run union as the primary operating point and re-run the full evaluation, rather than presenting post-hoc selection with a caveat? The bootstrap CIs suggest the result is robust, but the selection process weakens the headline claim.

2. The VDBFuzz comparison reports 0/14 TPs reached on v1.18.2 but does not report VDBFuzz's crash coverage on that version. What was VDBFuzz's crash yield on v1.18.2? Without a baseline, the complementarity claim is one-sided.

3. The cross-model re-run shows κ = 0.14–0.51 and lower recall across three families. Given this, what is the argument for preferring GLM-5.2 as the backbone beyond "it worked in our study"? Does the dev-reviewer require model-specific tuning, and if so, how does that transfer to users without access to GLM?

4. The CouchDB/Elasticsearch probes found zero silent-accept defects, which the paper interprets as "mature validation." Why not choose a non-VDBMS target with known documentation-implementation issues (e.g., a REST API from the SATORI/MASTOR case studies) to demonstrate positive portability evidence rather than negative?

5. What is the false-negative rate of the implementation-as-correct assumption? How many confirmed TPs were rejected as wont-fix where the documentation was actually wrong?
