## Reviewer 2 — Strict
> Confidence: 4/5 · Overall: Weak Accept (3.5/10)

**Summary**
TestVDB targets documentation-implementation defects in VDBMSs using an LLM-derived oracle with a source-grounded falsifier (dev-reviewer) to address false positives from hallucination and self-preference bias. The approach yields 49 maintainer-acknowledged true positives from 107 submissions (68% adjudicated precision) and lifts recall from 37% to 74% on a 48-candidate retrospective, but the evaluation has methodological limitations that constrain confidence.

**Strengths**
1. The oracle-exclusion argument (Table 1) is well-structured and convincingly motivates why LLM-derived oracles are necessary for documentation-implementation defects.
2. The dev-reviewer's three-check falsification design (independent reproduction, evidence sufficiency, falsifiability) is methodologically sound and breaks self-preference bias by anchoring on implementation source.
3. The bidirectional VDBFuzz probe (RQ3) cleanly demonstrates complementary coverage: VDBFuzz's crash oracle cannot reach silent-accept defects by construction, and TestVDB reaches a crash-class defect through contract reasoning.
4. Threats to validity are disclosed, including post-hoc operating point selection and lack of pre-registration, which is often omitted in LLM-as-judge work.

**Weaknesses**

1. **[major, fixable]** Post-hoc operating point selection without proper statistical correction inflates Type I error risk.
   - Section 4 (RQ2) reports four operating points and selects the 3-run union as the "post-hoc headline" (line 234), yet the Wilson CIs in Table 3 do not account for this selection across four points.
   - The Bonferroni correction mentioned widens the precision CI to [44%, 84%] and recall to [51%, 89%], which overlaps substantively with the single-LLM baseline's 56% precision and the multi-perspective baseline's 80% precision.
   - Fix: Pre-register the operating point selection rule or apply the Bonferroni-corrected CIs as the primary confidence intervals in all reported results, not as a footnote.

2. **[major, fixable]** The 48-candidate retrospective is non-random and unaudited for selection bias.
   - Line 214 states the retrospective is "maintainer-adjudicated but non-random, and we did not pre-register the candidate set."
   - No evidence is provided that the 48 candidates (27 TP, 21 FP) are representative of the 107 submissions or the defect population.
   - Table 3 shows single-run recall spanning 15--78%, suggesting the candidate set may be cherry-picked or ordered in a way that inflates the union ensemble's performance.
   - Fix: Perform a capture-recapture analysis or audit the candidate set against the full 107-submission pool to establish representativeness, or limit claims to the 48-candidate set without population-level inference.

3. **[major, fixable]** Cross-family generalization is claimed as an "open question" but the evidence undermines confidence in the GLM-5.2 backbone.
   - Line 292 reports full independent cross-model re-runs (DeepSeek, Qwen-3.8-Max, LongCat-2.0) with κ = 0.14, 0.37, 0.51 vs. GLM single-run, and all three recall fewer defects (18--56%) than GLM-5.2's 85% (5-run union).
   - The framing "cross-family generalization is an open question" minimizes the strength of this negative result. The data suggests the approach is backbone-dependent, not merely an open question.
   - Fix: Recast the limitation as "source-grounded falsification is backbone-dependent and cross-family robustness is not established," and weaken claims about the dev-reviewer's general applicability until cross-family validation is demonstrated.

4. **[major, fixable]** The RQ3 VDBFuzz comparison is asymmetric and conflates template coverage with oracle capability.
   - The systematic direction (VDBFuzz v1.18.2 on 26,000 requests, 0 of 14 TPs reached) is well-controlled, but the controlled cases are n=1 each and isolate crash-class mechanisms rather than establish general template limitations.
   - Line 310 states "we read the reverse direction as a limitation of VDBFuzz's current templates and input coverage, not a property of crash oracles as a class," but the paper's title and abstract frame VDBFuzz as a crash-oracle approach, not a template-coverage issue.
   - Fix: Separate the claims: (a) crash oracles cannot detect silent-accept defects by construction (well-supported), and (b) VDBFuzz's current templates have coverage gaps (weakly supported by n=1 cases). Recast RQ3 as primarily demonstrating (a), with (b) as preliminary evidence.

5. **[minor, fixable]** External validity claims about portability beyond VDBMSs are weakly substantiated.
   - Section 6 (Discussion) claims transferability to "structurally similar documentation regimes" based on two probes (CouchDB 3.4.3, Elasticsearch 8.11.0) that surfaced no silent-accept defects because both APIs strictly validate inputs.
   - This is evidence of mature validation rather than portability. The claim "the extraction, probing, and falsification steps all port across non-VDBMS targets" overstates the evidence from two negative probes.
   - Fix: Downgrade the portability claim from "structurally similar documentation regimes" to "the pipeline mechanics execute end-to-end on non-VDBMS targets, though defect detection efficacy is untested."

6. **[minor, unfixable]** The implementation-as-correct assumption (Section 6) bounds the approach but is not empirically quantified.
   - The paper states "an implementation bug can wrongly falsify a clause whose documentation is right" but reports no false-negative rate where the dev-reviewer wrongly suppressed a true defect because the implementation was buggy.
   - This is inherent to the source-grounded falsification design and cannot be fixed without a ground-truth catalog of documentation errors, which does not exist.
   - Acknowledged as a limitation, but worth noting as a structural bound on recall estimation.

**Questions for Authors**
1. Why was the 48-candidate retrospective set not selected randomly from the 107 submissions, and what audit was performed to assess selection bias?
2. Given the Bonferroni-corrected CIs [44%, 84%] for precision and [51%, 89%] for recall, how do you justify the uncorrected CIs as the primary reported values?
3. The cross-family re-runs show κ = 0.14--0.51 and substantially lower recall (18--56%); what theoretical or empirical basis is there to expect source-grounded falsification to generalize across families?
4. How do you reconcile the RQ3 framing ("how many defects detected by TestVDB can the existing crash-oracle approach reach") with the n=1 controlled cases that primarily demonstrate template coverage gaps rather than oracle limitations?
