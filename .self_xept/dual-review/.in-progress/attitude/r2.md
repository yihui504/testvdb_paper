# Reviewer 2: Strict/Critical Evaluation

## Summary

This paper introduces TestVDB, a system that uses LLMs to detect documentation-implementation defects in Vector Database Management Systems (VDBMSs). The core innovation is a "dev-reviewer" agent that uses source code as a ground truth to falsify LLM-derived claims, addressing two false-positive modes (extraction hallucination and self-preference bias). The authors report 107 submitted issues across three VDBMSs with 49 maintainer-acknowledged true-positive defects (15 fixed via merged PR), and on a 48-candidate retrospective, the dev-reviewer achieves 67% precision and 74% recall against a 37% recall baseline without source grounding.

The paper addresses a real and important problem: VDBMSs suffer from silent-accept defects that crash-oracle fuzzers miss. The oracle-exclusion argument (Table 1) is thorough and convincingly shows why LLM-derived oracles are the practical option for natural-language documentation boundaries. However, the evaluation exhibits several methodological weaknesses that undermine confidence in the claims.

## Strengths

1. **Oracle problem characterization is solid.** Table 1's systematic exclusion of deterministic oracles is well-structured and compelling. The distinction between consistency (accept/reject) and correctness (mathematical result) is clear and appropriately bounds the scope.

2. **Dev-reviewer falsifier design is principled.** Using implementation source as an independent ground truth to break LLM self-preference is logically sound and well-motivated. The three-check structure (reproducibility, evidence sufficiency, falsifiability) is a reasonable decomposition of the confirmation problem.

3. **Bidirectional VDBFuzz probe is hypothesis-generating.** The #9045 case (wait=false silent accept) effectively illustrates how crash-oracle approaches miss documentation-implementation defects, and the root-cause analysis (debug_assert vs. API-level check) is informative.

## Weaknesses

### **[CRITICAL, Not fixable in revision] Post-hoc operating point selection without pre-registration**

The RQ2 evaluation reports four operating points (single run, 3-run union, 5-run union, 5-run majority) and selects the 3-run union as the headline. The authors acknowledge this is "post-hoc" and that Wilson CIs do not account for selection across operating points. This is a fundamental validity threat. The paper justifies the selection by "falsifier semantics" (any-confirmed union because under-confirmation is costlier), but this is a post-hoc rationalization, not a pre-registered analysis rule. The retrospective is already non-random and maintainer-adjudicated, so adding another layer of selection (picking the best-looking operating point after seeing the data) compounds the problem. The 3-run union is not uniquely justified by theory—if falsifier semantics truly demanded union semantics, why report 5-run and majority at all? The operating point section reads like optimization rather than principled selection.

**Severity:** CRITICAL. The headline precision/recall numbers are not reliable because they were selected after seeing the data. This is a selection bias that undermines the central quantitative claim.

**Fixability:** Not fixable without new data. Pre-registration was the solution; absent that, any post-hoc justification is inherently ad hoc.

---

### **[CRITICAL, Partially fixable] Insufficient baseline on multi-perspective judging**

The paper claims multi-perspective judging is "insufficient" because it reaches only ~15% recall. However, the baseline is not rigorously evaluated. Section 3.3 reports "about 80% precision but only about 15% recall" and states "the 15% figure is the highest recall we could reach by tuning the voting threshold; higher thresholds traded recall for at most a few points of precision." But there is no systematic sweep or threshold curve. What voting rules were tried? What does the precision-recall curve look like? The paper mentions "majority-of-four rule" but does not explore whether weighted voting, confidence thresholds, or other ensemble methods could improve recall. The claim that multi-perspective judging is structurally insufficient because judges "read the same ambiguous documentation" is plausible, but without a thorough exploration of the design space, this is under-justified. The comparison to dev-reviewer (which does explore multiple operating points) is methodologically asymmetric.

**Severity:** HIGH. The baseline is under-explored, so the claim that multi-perspective judging is fundamentally insufficient is weaker than it should be.

**Fixability:** Partially. A threshold sweep and PR curve could be added, but the fundamental asymmetry (deep exploration of dev-reviewer vs. shallow exploration of baseline) cannot be fully corrected post-hoc.

---

### **[HIGH, Fixable] VDBFuzz comparison is severely underpowered**

The RQ3 bidirectional probe between TestVDB and VDBFuzz is n=1 in each direction. The paper acknowledges "each direction is n=1" and treats these as "hypothesis-generating controlled cases rather than a generalized result." But the headline claim about "complementary coverage" and the abstract's assertion that "a bidirectional probe against VDBFuzz on Qdrant shows TestVDB reaches a crash-class defect... while VDBFuzz misses a TestVDB silent-accept defect" imply a broader generalization than n=1 supports. The #9045 case is illustrative, but it is a single data point. VDBFuzz's template suite is implicitly treated as static (the paper notes that "every points-upsert template uses wait=true"), but there is no exploration of whether VDBFuzz could be extended or configured to probe wait=false. The comparison is between TestVDB (a general-purpose framework) and VDBFuzz in a specific default configuration, which is an unfair comparison. The claim would be stronger if VDBFuzz were given a comparable budget and configuration exploration.

**Severity:** HIGH. The RQ3 claim is overgeneralized from a single example.

**Fixability:** Fixable in principle by expanding the VDBFuzz configuration space (multiple templates, larger budget), but time/resource constraints may make this impractical for revision.

---

### **[MEDIUM, Partially fixable] Single LLM backbone limits cross-family generalization**

The paper uses a single LLM family (GLM-5.2) for all results, with only a cross-model check on 20 candidates using DeepSeek. The authors report "agrees with GLM-5.2 on all twenty (Cohen's κ = 1.0)" and note that "moderate family-specificity remains statistically compatible" given the Wilson CI on agreement. But 20 candidates is a small control, and the check measures agreement, not recall. The paper does not estimate recall on DeepSeek because "no public ground-truth defect catalog for VDBMSs exists." However, the paper itself has constructed a maintainer-adjudicated 48-candidate ground truth—why not run DeepSeek on all 48 and report recall? The paper claims the approach is "not strongly family-specific when source evidence is explicit," but the evidence for this is limited. The cross-model check is better than nothing, but it is not sufficient to support broad generalization claims about LLM-as-judge reliability.

**Severity:** MEDIUM. The cross-family check is underpowered.

**Fixability:** Partially. Running DeepSeek on all 48 candidates would strengthen the claim, but resource constraints may be limiting.

---

### **[MEDIUM, Fixable] Ablation design does not isolate the three-check contribution**

The three-condition ablation (Table 3) reports FP suppression rates for Clean-reproduction only (2/12), Source-grounded alone (9/12), Threat-model alone (6/12), and Union (11/12). However, the ablation does not isolate the three-check contribution. The ablation varies the anchor configuration (source vs. threat vs. union), not the check count (1-check vs. 2-check vs. 3-check). To truly isolate the three-check contribution, the ablation should compare:
- 1-check (reproducibility only)
- 2-check (reproducibility + evidence sufficiency)
- 3-check (reproducibility + evidence + falsifiability)

The current ablation shows that source grounding is the dominant anchor, but it does not show whether all three checks are necessary or whether some checks are redundant. The paper's prose (Section 4) presents the three-check structure as a design contribution, but the evaluation does not validate that each check adds incremental value.

**Severity:** MEDIUM. The ablation is misaligned with the design's claimed structure.

**Fixability:** Fixable by re-designing the ablation to isolate each check's contribution, but this may require re-running experiments.

---

### **[MEDIUM, Fixable] No statistical power analysis for sample size**

The 48-candidate retrospective is maintainer-adjudicated but non-random. The paper acknowledges this and states that "capture-recapture or unbiased defect-sample estimation is future work." However, there is no power analysis or justification for why 48 candidates are sufficient to estimate precision/recall with meaningful confidence intervals. The Wilson CIs are wide (precision: [49%, 81%]; recall: [55%, 87%]), which reflects high uncertainty. The single-run recall band (15-78%) is also extremely wide, suggesting high variance. The paper does not discuss whether the sample size is adequate for the claims being made, nor does it address how many candidates would be needed to tighten the CIs to a usable range.

**Severity:** MEDIUM. The sample size is under-justified.

**Fixability:** Partially. Power analysis could be added post-hoc, but it cannot fix the underlying small sample.

---

### **[LOW, Fixable] Multi-perspective baseline is under-documented**

Section 3.3 mentions "four specialized judges (documentation, evidence, severity, novelty)" but does not detail their prompts, decision criteria, or how they differ. The prose states each reads "the documentation independently and voting on each candidate under a majority-of-four rule," but the mechanics are opaque. Do judges see the raw response? Do they see the extracted claim? Do they see each other's votes? The lack of detail makes it difficult to assess whether the multi-perspective baseline is a fair comparison or a straw man. The Related Work section discusses LLM-as-judge reliability but does not connect it to the specific multi-perspective design used here.

**Severity:** LOW. This is a documentation issue rather than a fundamental flaw.

**Fixability:** Fully fixable by adding a brief appendix or supplementary section detailing the multi-perspective design.

---

## Questions

1. **On operating point selection:** You report four operating points and select the 3-run union post-hoc. If you had pre-registered a different operating point (e.g., 5-run majority), how would the headline claims change? Would the core conclusions still hold?

2. **On multi-perspective baseline:** You explored multiple dev-reviewer operating points but only one voting rule (majority-of-four) for multi-perspective judging. Did you explore unanimity, supermajority, or weighted voting? If not, why is the baseline exploration limited?

3. **On VDBFuzz comparison:** You claim VDBFuzz "misses" #9045 under its "current templates." Did you explore whether extending or reconfiguring VDBFuzz's templates (e.g., adding wait=false variants) would allow it to detect this defect? The comparison is between TestVDB (general framework) and VDBFuzz (specific default config)—is this a fair comparison?

4. **On cross-model validation:** Why limit the DeepSeek cross-model check to 20 candidates? If the maintainer-adjudicated 48-candidate set exists as ground truth, why not run DeepSeek on all 48 and report full precision/recall rather than just agreement?

5. **On ablation design:** Table 3 varies anchor configuration (source vs. threat vs. union) but does not isolate the three-check contribution (1-check vs. 2-check vs. 3-check). Do you have data on whether each of the three checks adds incremental value, or are some checks redundant?

6. **On sample size:** The Wilson CIs are wide (precision ±16%, recall ±16%). Did you conduct a power analysis to determine whether 48 candidates are sufficient? How many candidates would be needed to achieve ±5% precision on recall estimates?

## Scores

- **Originality:** 4/5 - The dev-reviewer falsifier design is novel and well-motivated. The oracle-exclusion argument is thorough but the core novelty is the source-grounded falsification approach.

- **Significance:** 3/5 - The problem is important (VDBMS silent-accept defects), but the methodological weaknesses (post-hoc selection, underpowered baselines) undermine confidence. The practical impact (49 acknowledged defects, 15 merged PRs) is non-trivial.

- **Validity:** 2/5 - Multiple threats to internal validity (post-hoc operating point selection, non-random sample, insufficient baseline exploration). External validity is limited to two VDBMSs for controlled evaluation. Construct validity is moderate (single LLM family, limited cross-model check).

- **Clarity:** 4/5 - The writing is clear and well-structured. The oracle-exclusion table (Table 1) is excellent. The main weaknesses are in evaluation methodology, not exposition.

- **Reproducibility:** 3/5 - The paper mentions an artifact (prompts, target versions, per-token accounting) and promises release upon acceptance. However, the LLM prompt details are not in the paper, and the 48-candidate ground truth is not publicly available, so independent verification is currently impossible.

## Overall

**3/5 (Weak Accept)**

## Confidence

**High (85%)** - The methodological weaknesses are clear and well-documented in the review. The core issue (post-hoc selection) is unambiguous and fundamentally undermines the quantitative claims. The other weaknesses (baseline underpower, small sample) are also significant and independently problematic.

## Top 3 Weaknesses (for consensus)

1. **Post-hoc operating point selection without pre-registration** - CRITICAL, not fixable. The headline precision/recall numbers are compromised by selection bias.

2. **Insufficient baseline on multi-perspective judging** - HIGH, partially fixable. The multi-perspective baseline is under-explored, making the claim that it is "structurally insufficient" weaker than it should be.

3. **VDBFuzz comparison is severely underpowered** - HIGH, fixable in principle but likely impractical for revision. The n=1 probe is overgeneralized in the abstract and RQ3 claim.
